# Vertical batch furnace: anneal and alloy

An anneal furnace is the batch furnace run without an oxidant or a
precursor: the wafers are heated in nitrogen, argon or a
hydrogen-bearing gas to repair, densify, diffuse or passivate, rather
than to grow or deposit a film. In a 130 nm flow its main task is the
hydrogen alloy — a long, low-temperature soak in forming gas that
passivates the silicon–oxide interface — and it is the batch alternative
to rapid thermal annealing for higher-temperature anneals. This page
describes the class in general, lists representative 200 mm-era models,
and then says what SkyWater has published about its own furnaces and
which SKY130 steps this reference assigns to the class. The anneal
physics (diffusion, activation, the alloy) is on the
{ref}`category page <category-anneal>`; the furnace hardware shared with
oxidation is described on the
{ref}`oxidation furnace page <machine-vertical-furnace-oxidation>`.

## What the machine class is and how it works

The hardware is an oxidation furnace's tube, heater, boat and loading
area with a different gas panel: nitrogen, argon and a hydrogen or
forming-gas line in place of the oxygen and torch. Wikipedia's
description of furnace annealing covers the purposes — "activate
dopants, change film to film or film to wafer substrate interfaces,
densify deposited films … repair damage from implants, move dopants" —
and the reason furnaces lost the high-temperature anneals: "the
relatively long thermal cycles of furnaces" cause dopants, "especially
boron, to diffuse further than is intended".[^wiki-furnace] What remains
for the furnace are anneals where a long time is harmless or wanted:
low-temperature sinters and alloys, densification, and anneals whose
extra diffusion a flow can tolerate.

### Inert anneals: nitrogen and argon

An inert anneal in the furnace follows the oxidation ramp and cycle
described on the oxidation page, with no oxidant admitted. The choice
between the two inert gases is not neutral. Ohashi et al. compared
high-temperature N₂ and Ar anneals after the sacrificial oxidation of a
shallow-trench flow: both rounded the trench corners, but "With the N₂
annealing, however, the hump in the Id-Vg curve increased, and the
time-dependent dielectric breakdown (TDDB) characteristics were
degraded", while "With the Ar annealing, there was no hump, and the TDDB
characteristics improved"; they concluded that "Ar annealing after gap
filling is promising".[^ohashi-2007] The ambient in which an oxide is
annealed and cooled also sets its interface-state density: oxides
"annealed and cooled from any oxidation temperature in nitrogen or argon
exhibit large interface state densities as oxidized", which a later
hydrogen anneal reduces.[^razouk-1979]

For implant anneals the furnace competes with the lamp-heated RTA. The
diffusion that makes a furnace unattractive for shallow junctions has a
mechanism: implanted boron and phosphorus "exhibit transient enhanced
diffusion (TED) during annealing which arises from the excess
interstitials generated by the implant",[^stolk-1997] and a slow ramp
spends time in the temperature range where that diffusion is
active (category page). A well anneal is the case where the furnace
remains a reasonable choice, because the wells are meant to diffuse; a
published flow anneals its wells "at approximately 900° C. … for
approximately 30 minutes",[^pat-vt-lsi] another "at 1000 C for 10 sec in
an RTA (Rapid Thermal Anneal) apparatus".[^pat-well-hynix]

### The hydrogen alloy

The alloy anneal passes hydrogen through the finished or nearly finished
dielectric stack to the Si/SiO₂ interface, where it terminates silicon
dangling bonds (P_b centres) and lowers the interface-trap density. Reed
and Plummer measured the kinetics with rapid thermal processing and
capacitance–voltage measurements and proposed "a two-reaction model
involving atomic hydrogen dimerization and hydrogen/interface trap
reactions" that "predicts anneal kinetics over a temperature range of
170–500 °C".[^reed-1988] Stesmans found that both the P_b0 and P_b1
defects of (100) silicon passivate in molecular hydrogen with mean
activation energies near 1.5 eV, so that "All three interface defects thus
passivate comparably".[^stesmans-1996] The reverse reaction limits the
temperature: Brower measured the dissociation of hydrogen-passivated
defects in vacuum anneals "at temperatures ranging from 500 to 595 °C",
with "An activation energy of 2.56±0.06 eV".[^brower-1990] Atomic
hydrogen both passivates and depassivates, "keeping the steady-state P_b
density at a low value".[^cartier-1993]

The furnace suits the alloy because the anneal is long and cool: tens of
minutes at a few hundred degrees for a full batch (category
page).[^txt-01][^txt-02] Hydrogen is
supplied as forming gas, a mixture of hydrogen and nitrogen of which
"Typical forming gas formulations (5% H₂ in N₂) are not explosive", and
"a high-temperature anneal in forming gas assists in silicon-silicon
dioxide interface passivation",[^wiki-fg] or as hydrogen diluted at the
tool; a University of Illinois patent anneals in "10% by volume hydrogen
in nitrogen … for a period of about 1 hour at a temperature of about 400°
C."[^pat-deuterium-uiuc]

### Deuterium

The same furnace can sinter in deuterium. Lyding, Hess and Kizilyalli
reported that "replacing hydrogen with deuterium during the final wafer
sintering process greatly reduces hot electron degradation", with
"Transistor lifetime improvements by factors of 10–50";[^lyding-1996] in a
multilevel-metal process an optimised deuterium post-metal anneal gave "a
50-100 fold improvement in transistor channel hot carrier lifetime", and
the benefit remained "even if the post-metal anneal is followed by the
final SiN cap wafer passivation process".[^kizilyalli-1998] Brower had
already found that the dissociation "rate is reduced if the samples are
passivated with deuterium instead of hydrogen".[^brower-1990]

### Horizontal and vertical anneal furnaces

Alloy and sinter tubes were long the domain of horizontal furnaces, in
which "the boat entered the oxidation chamber from the side" and held
"the wafers vertically, beside each other"; vertical furnaces replaced
them for oxidation because of convection and particles.[^wiki-thox] A
low-temperature anneal is less sensitive to either, and horizontal
furnaces stayed in production: in November 2004 Aviza Technology
announced the sale of its "THERMCO HORIZONTAL FURNACES" to Tetreon
Technologies.[^aviza-news]

## Representative 200 mm-era models

* **SVG Thermco, later Aviza Technology.** The AVP-8000, whose listed
  applications include "anneal and cure from 100 to >1200°C";[^aviza-avp]
  the AVP/RVP vertical furnaces;[^aviza-vert] and the Thermco horizontal
  furnaces sold on to Tetreon Technologies in 2004.[^aviza-news]
* **Tokyo Electron.** The Alpha-8 series;[^tel-alpha8se] its ALPHA-8SE i
  successor lists "oxidation/annealing" among its
  applications.[^tel-telindy]
* **ASM International.** The A400, whose processes include "diffusion
  processes such as wet oxidation and anneal processes".[^asm-a400]
* **Expertech (originally SVG).** The VTR vertical reactor, whose
  "Typical diffusion/oxidation" load is "150 product, test, and filler
  wafers".[^expertech-vtr]

the rapid thermal processors that took over most activation anneals.

## Consumables and facilities

The gases are listed in the {ref}`materials index <materials-index>`;
what is specific to an anneal and alloy furnace is summarised here. None
of the SkyWater sources describes the fab's hydrogen supply or furnace
exhaust.

* **Gases.** Nitrogen and argon for inert anneals and purges;[^ohashi-2007]
  forming gas or hydrogen for the alloy,[^wiki-fg] which the University
  of Illinois example meters as "10% by volume hydrogen in
  nitrogen";[^pat-deuterium-uiuc] deuterium only where a deuterium sinter
  is run.[^kizilyalli-1998]
* **Hydrogen safety.** Premixed forming gas of about 5 % hydrogen is not
  explosive;[^wiki-fg] richer hydrogen needs the interlocks, purges and
  exhaust treatment of a flammable gas. The step pages describe purging
  the tube of oxygen before hydrogen is admitted.
* **Quartzware, boats and heater.** As on the
  {ref}`oxidation furnace <machine-vertical-furnace-oxidation>`, with less
  wear at alloy temperatures than at oxidation temperatures.
* **Monitor wafers.** MOS capacitor or transistor monitors for
  interface-trap density, since the purpose of the alloy is electrical;
  the ALLY pages name charge pumping and C–V.

## Process-integration notes for SKY130

These notes connect the machine class to the step pages; they add no
SKY130 conditions of their own. SKY130's anneal temperatures, times and
ambients are not public.

* **Two alloys.** SKY130 has an alloy after the local-interconnect
  contact etch ({ref}`ALLY1 <step-096>`) and a final alloy after the last
  metal ({ref}`ALLY <step-170>`). The first sees bare silicon and poly at
  the contact bottoms and must not deactivate the boron in the p⁺
  regions — "a deactivation process of the boron acceptor by hydrogen with
  the formation of a B−H⁺ pair";[^sah-1983] the second has its
  temperature capped by the aluminium metallisation (category
  page).[^txt-02]
* **Hydrogen and the SONOS stack.** The {ref}`ALLY1 <step-096>` page notes
  that hydrogen reaches the charge-trapping nitride of the memory cells;
  Maes, Usmani and Heyns found that a high-temperature hydrogen anneal
  after nitride deposition reduced the threshold-voltage decay of MNOS
  transistors at 125 °C "by more than 20%".[^maes-1981]
* **The only listed hydrogen anneal.** The step pages assign both alloys
  to the furnace as inferences because the furnaces' "H2 and forming gas
  alloy" is the only hydrogen-bearing anneal on SkyWater's list; the
  Heatpulse lists "NH3, Ar, N2, O2".[^skw-01]
* **Densifying the trench fill.** The {ref}`FILOX <step-011>` page offers
  a furnace or RTP densification of the HDP oxide as optional; an argon
  anneal after gap fill is one published choice.[^ohashi-2007] SkyWater
  lists argon and nitrogen anneals to 1150 °C.[^skw-01]
* **A furnace well anneal is the weak option.** The
  {ref}`RTAI <step-034>` page reads the pre-gate anneal as an RTA, from
  the Cypress integration patent, and grades the furnace as weak; a
  furnace anneal of the wells would add diffusion, which transient
  enhanced diffusion makes hard to predict.[^stolk-1997]
* **Deuterium is not on the list.** A deuterium post-metal anneal
  improves hot-carrier lifetime in multilevel-metal CMOS,[^kizilyalli-1998]
  but SkyWater lists only "H2 and forming gas alloy";[^skw-01] whether
  SKY130's final anneal uses deuterium is not public.
