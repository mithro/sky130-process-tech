## What this step is

`P1M` prints the gate. The wafer arrives from {ref}`BFR <step-060>`
carrying the complete gate stack — gate oxide, 0.18 µm of doped
poly,[^pdk-03][^pdk-04] the nitride cap of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` and
the oxide cap of {ref}`POC <step-059>` — and `P1M` coats it with resist,
exposes it through the *Poly 1* {term}`reticle` and develops it, leaving
resist lines wherever poly is to remain: every transistor gate, every
poly interconnect, every precision-resistor body and the gate of every
{term}`SONOS` cell. The etch that transfers the pattern is
{ref}`P1ME <step-062>`. There is no dedicated strip step after it in the
step list used in this reference, so we infer the resist is removed as
part of the etch sequence.

The PDK's mask table lists "Poly 1, P1M" among the masks used in
SKY130,[^pdk-05] and the drawn layer is `poly` (GDS 66:20,
"Polysilicon"), with a `gate` purpose (66:9) and a `resistor` purpose
(66:13).[^pdk-06] The geometry that makes this the most demanding
lithography of the process is public: the minimum poly width (poly.1a)
is 0.150 µm and the minimum poly-to-poly spacing (poly.2) is
0.210 µm;[^pdk-periph] the assumptions table gives the same pair for
"Poly 1" as the endcap/gap criteria 0.15 µm and 0.21 µm
(P1G).[^pdk-03] The gate must extend beyond the active area by
0.130 µm (poly.8, the endcap), the active must extend beyond the gate
by 0.250 µm (poly.7, the minimum drain), poly on field must keep
0.075 µm from active (poly.4), and a {term}`poly resistor` must be at least
0.330 µm wide (poly.3).[^pdk-periph] The 0.150 µm line is the drawn
gate length of the 1.8 V transistors; whether the printed and etched
gate is shorter than drawn — normal at this node, as discussed below —
is not public.

The layer prints on a smooth, planar surface: the poly was deposited
on planarised {term}`STI` ({ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>`) and gate oxide, the caps
are conformal, and the only topography is the small field-oxide step
(0.07 µm above the silicon surface under poly[^pdk-03]) at every
active edge. That step, and the highly reflective poly beneath the
caps, are what the reflectivity control of the stack has to handle.

## Step category

`P1M` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *critical-layer* type. Of the 36 masks in the flow it is
the one with the smallest drawn line, and the one whose {term}`CD`
maps most directly onto a device parameter: the printed and etched
poly width *is* the transistor gate length. The category page gives
the resolution relation CD = {term}`k1`·λ/{term}`NA`; at 248 nm with
a lens of NA 0.6–0.7, a 0.150 µm line is k₁ ≈ 0.36–0.42, within reach
of a KrF tool with resolution enhancement, whereas at the i-line
365 nm it would need k₁ ≈ 0.25–0.29, below what production i-line
lithography achieved ({ref}`category-lithography`;[^mack-2007]
[^lin-2002]). We infer that `P1M` is a **248 nm (KrF)** layer, as
{ref}`FOM <step-004>` was inferred to be; no SkyWater statement
assigns any layer to a tool.

## Why this step exists

The gate length set here is the single most influential dimension in
the process. It fixes the threshold voltage roll-off, drive current,
off-state leakage and short-channel behaviour of every transistor,
and its variation — from field to field, from wafer to wafer and along
the edge of a single line — is a first-order term in circuit
variability. Orshansky, Milor and Hu characterised the spatial
intrafield gate-CD variation of a lithography tool and its impact on
circuit performance, and showed that mask-level correction can
compensate the systematic part;[^orshansky-2004] Zhang, Poolla and
Spanos treated across-wafer CD uniformity as a joint property of the
lithography and etch sequence.[^zhang-2007] The random part — line-edge
roughness — is set largely by the resist and its processing: Lin,
Sooriyakumaran and Huang traced its material origin in chemically
amplified positive resists,[^lin-2000] Gallatin related it to resist
blur,[^gallatin-2005] and Asenov, Kaya and Brown simulated the
threshold-voltage and leakage fluctuations that gate line-edge
roughness introduces in sub-100 nm MOSFETs.[^asenov-2003]

ITRS 2001 gives the era's targets for this layer at the 130 nm node: an
ASIC/low-power gate of 130 nm in resist and 90 nm after etch, a CD
control of 15.9 nm (3σ) on the half-pitch, an {term}`overlay` requirement of
46 nm and a reticle magnification of 4×.[^itrs-03] SKY130's drawn gate
of 0.150 µm is slightly relaxed against that roadmap entry, consistent
with a 1.8 V core supply rather than a scaled one, but the same tool
class and the same techniques apply.

Overlay matters here as much as CD. The gate has to land on the
active area with its 0.130 µm endcap on both sides,[^pdk-periph] and
a misregistration between poly and active converts directly into
source/drain asymmetry, endcap loss and, at the limit, a gate that
fails to cut off the channel. The later self-aligned steps — the tip
and {term}`halo` implants ({ref}`NTM <step-064>` onwards) and the {term}`spacers <spacer>`
({ref}`SPE <step-077>`) — take the etched gate as their reference, so
whatever overlay error `P1M` has with respect to active is inherited
by every junction. The poly resistors have the complementary
sensitivity: their value goes as 1/W, so the CD uniformity of this
layer is also the matching budget of the `res_high_po` and
`res_xhigh_po` devices ({ref}`RPM <step-049>`).

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic KrF gate-level litho sequence for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe, tool, resist and reticle
technology are not public):

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake and {term}`HMDS` vapour prime on
   the coat/develop track; the surface is the oxide cap of
   {ref}`POC <step-059>`, on which resist adheres well and which keeps
   the nitride from poisoning a chemically amplified resist
   ({ref}`POC <step-059>`).
2. **Anti-reflective coating.** Poly under thin dielectric caps is a
   strongly reflective, interference-prone stack at 248 nm, so the
   resist is either spun over an organic bottom {term}`BARC` or
   relies on the cap films as an inorganic {term}`ARC`, or
   both.[^wiki-arc][^mack-2007] Brunner's treatment of the optimisation
   of the optical properties of resist processes[^brunner-1991] is the
   classic account of the swing-curve problem; {ref}`GATENIT
   <step-058>` and {ref}`POC <step-059>` discuss the inorganic route.
   Which SKY130 uses is not public; that the flow contains a dedicated
   ARC etch after the tunnel mask ({ref}`TUNARCE <step-036>`) shows
   that organic ARCs are in use somewhere in the process.
3. **Resist coat.** A chemically amplified positive KrF resist
   ({term}`CAR`;[^ito-2005][^wiki-resist]), soft-baked. The PDK's
   assumptions table gives a generic "Photoresist thickness" of
   1.14 µm,[^pdk-03] which for a 0.15 µm line would be an {term}`aspect ratio`
   near 8:1; we infer that the gate level uses a thinner resist than
   that generic figure, as was normal for critical KrF layers, whose
   resists were typically a few hundred nanometres thick to keep
   collapse and depth-of-focus within limits.[^levinson-2005]
4. **Exposure.** Step-and-scan (or step-and-repeat) exposure through
   the Poly 1 reticle on a KrF tool. Two resolution enhancements were
   standard on gate levels of this generation and are the likely, but
   unconfirmed, choice here: an {term}`attenuated phase-shift mask <attenuated PSM>` — the
   "attenuated shifter" that ITRS 2001 lists as a primary {term}`PSM`
   choice[^itrs-03] and whose principle goes back to Levenson's
   phase-shifting mask[^levenson-1982] and Lin's account of the
   attenuated variant[^lin-1993][^wiki-psm] — and optical proximity
   correction of the mask data, so that isolated gates, dense gates
   and line ends all print to size.[^otto-1994][^wiki-opc] Off-axis
   (annular) illumination and, at later nodes, sub-resolution assist
   features complete the toolkit.[^zhang-2005] Lens aberrations
   become visible at this k₁ as CD differences between orientations
   and pitches.[^brunner-1997]
5. **Post-exposure bake.** The {term}`PEB` drives the acid-catalysed
   deprotection of the CAR; its temperature and the delay between
   exposure and bake are controlled to seconds and tenths of a degree,
   because both move the CD ({ref}`category-lithography`).
6. **Develop** in 2.38 % TMAH,[^wiki-tmah] rinse and dry.
7. **{term}`Resist trim <resist trim>` (generic practice).** At the 130 nm node the resist
   line was routinely narrowed after develop by a short isotropic
   plasma trim, in the etcher or on a dedicated tool, to reach a gate
   length below what the lithography prints: ITRS 2001's "in resist"
   versus "after etch" gate columns quantify the practice,[^itrs-03]
   Montgomery et al. describe the roughness and collapse problems of
   post-trim resist lines,[^montgomery-2005] and Kang, Joung and Mun
   describe a gate-CD control step using plasma treatments of an
   organic BARC in the gate etch.[^kang-2007] Whether SKY130 trims its
   gate, and by how much, is not public; the PDK gives drawn
   dimensions only.
8. **Metrology and inspection.** After-develop CD on the {term}`CD-SEM`
   (ADI), overlay to the reference layer on box-in-box or grating
   targets, and after-develop inspection; dose and focus are separated
   from the CD signature and fed back to the exposure tool as
   corrections.[^ausschnitt-1999][^starikov-1992] Lithography is the
   one category in which a failing wafer can be stripped and reworked
   before any irreversible step ({ref}`category-lithography`).

**Alignment.** The poly must register to the active area. The only
earlier layers with topography are the STI ({ref}`FOM <step-004>`,
{ref}`STIE <step-006>`), since the implant masks leave no marks, so
we infer that `P1M` aligns to STI marks, whose quality after the STI
polish ({ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>`) is one of the known contributors to
poly-to-active overlay error.[^starikov-1992][^van-haren-2019] The
later self-aligned masks then align either to poly or to the same
STI marks, a choice (the alignment tree) that is not public.

**Reticle tone.** Poly is drawn where it remains, so with a positive
resist the reticle is clear-field (bright-field): chrome or attenuating
MoSi lines on a clear background. Neither tone nor reticle technology is
stated publicly.

## Machines typically used

* **KrF (248 nm) step-and-scan systems**, 200 mm, NA 0.6–0.8: ASML
  PAS 5500/750E ("130 nm resolution while using standard 248 nm
  light", NA 0.7)[^asml-750e] and PAS 5500/800 (NA 0.80, "120 nm
  resolution");[^asml-800] Nikon NSR-S204B (248 nm, 4:1, NA
  0.55–0.68);[^nikon-s204b] Canon FPA-3000EX4 (248 nm, 5×,
  NA 0.6).[^tolpygo-2014]
* **Coat/develop track** linked to the exposure tool: TEL CLEAN TRACK
  ACT 8,[^tel-act8] SVG/ASML 90S, DNS/SCREEN, Sokudo.
* **CD-SEM**: KLA-Tencor 8100 series,[^gce-kla8100] Hitachi
  S-9200/S-9260,[^semimarket-s9200] Applied Materials
  VeraSEM/Verity.
* **Optical overlay metrology**: KLA-Tencor 5xxx/Archer series.
* **After-develop inspection**: KLA-Tencor 2xxx bright-field
  inspectors.[^wiki-kla]
* **Reticles** are made at a mask shop and are not fab equipment
  ({ref}`category-lithography`).

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML {term}`DUV` {term}`stepper` / DUV scanner.** SkyWater lists "ASML DUV
  stepper" and "ASML DUV scanner" alongside its i-line tools and, as
  later additions, "ASML 193nm single stage scanner – 90nm CD" and
  "ASML 193nm twin stage scanner – sub 65nm CD", with a maximum field
  of 26 mm × 32 mm.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the vendor and
  wavelength classes; **inference** for the assignment of `P1M` to the
  248 nm tools (the design-rule argument above). The 193 nm scanners
  could print this layer too, but the capability list is a 2020s
  snapshot that includes tools added for the 90 nm process, so they
  are not evidence for how the 130 nm gate was, or is, printed
  ({ref}`references-public-sources`).
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ/Lithius.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong; which track serves the DUV tools is not public.
* **CD metrology — AMAT Verity (multiple versions) and VeraSEM;
  overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer.**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; that the gate level is measured on them is an inference
  from their function.
* **Reticle supply.** No public SkyWater source names its mask
  supplier or the reticle technology (binary or attenuated PSM) of the
  gate level.
* **Resist supply.** SkyWater's S-1 names The Dow Chemical Company,
  JSR Corporation and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo America as photoresist
  suppliers and Air Products (Moses Lake) for developer,[^sec-01]
  without tying them to a layer.

## Resources required

* **KrF chemically amplified positive resist** (poly(hydroxystyrene)
  platform[^ito-2005]) from the suppliers named above.[^sec-01]
* **Organic BARC** (if used) and its casting solvent.
* **HMDS**; **PGMEA/ethyl lactate** for edge-bead removal and
  back-rinse; **cyclohexanone** or similar for rework strips
  ({ref}`category-lithography`).
* **Developer** — aqueous 2.38 % TMAH,[^wiki-tmah][^microchemicals-dev]
  supplied by Air Products.[^sec-01]
* **KrF excimer laser gases** (krypton, fluorine/neon premix) and
  laser chamber and optics replacements.
* **The Poly 1 reticle** with pellicle, cleaned and re-pelliclised
  periodically;[^wiki-mask] mask blanks to SEMI P1.[^semi-p1]
* **DI water, nitrogen**; **CD-SEM and overlay reference wafers**.

## Open questions

* The exposure wavelength and tool are inferred from the 0.150 µm
  rule, not stated; whether the layer is now printed on the 193 nm
  scanners is unknown.
* Whether the Poly 1 reticle is an attenuated phase-shift mask, and
  whether and how its data are OPC-corrected, is not public.
* Whether an organic BARC is used over the oxide cap, or the
  nitride/oxide caps serve as the ARC, is not public.
* Resist type and thickness for the gate level are not public; the
  1.14 µm in the assumptions table is a generic figure.
* Whether a resist trim is used, and hence what the printed and
  etched gate lengths are relative to the 0.150 µm drawn width, is
  not public.
* The assumptions table carries a second "Poly 1" row with "N/A" and
  0.14 under the P1MCD/P1MCDSP variables;[^pdk-03] the table does not
  explain it and it is not used on this page.
* The alignment tree — which marks `P1M` and the later self-aligned
  masks use — and the mask supplier are not public.
