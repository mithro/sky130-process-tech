| **Category** | {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` |

## What this step is

`LI1ME` is the etch that turns the blanket titanium nitride of
{ref}`LITIN <step-101>` into the `li1` wiring. Through the resist
pattern of {ref}`LI1M <step-102>` it removes the 0.1 µm TiN
film[^pdk-04] from everywhere the resist is open, stopping on the
{term}`cap oxide` of {ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>` and — wherever a tungsten plug
lies outside the {term}`LI` pattern, which the rules forbid (licon.4,
li.5[^pdk-periph]) but {term}`overlay` can cause — on the top of a tungsten
plug. What remains is a network of 0.17 µm-wide TiN lines and pads
(li.1[^pdk-periph]), each sitting on its plugs, separated by 0.17 µm
spaces (li.3) that must be etched completely clean: a TiN residue
between two LI lines is a short, and a TiN stringer along a step is
a leakage path. The resist is stripped afterwards and the wafer
cleaned before the nitride cap of {ref}`LINIT <step-104>` seals the
lines.

The public evidence for how it is done is the capability list:
SkyWater names two metal etchers with TiN among their materials —
"Lam 9600, Al, TiW, TiN, Pt" and "Lam 2300 Versys, Al, TiW, TiN, Nb,
Pt".[^skw-01] The etch chemistry, {term}`endpoint` and {term}`over-etch` are not
public.

## Step category

`LI1ME` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *metal* class
— the first metal etch in the flow, ahead of the aluminium etches
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` to {ref}`MM5E <step-163>`, which also end on
a TiN or Ti:W cap. The category page gives the chemistries: TiN
etches in chlorine (Cl₂/BCl₃, the aluminium chemistry, in which
TiCl₄ is volatile) and in fluorine (SF₆, CF₄, in which TiF₄ is
volatile), with the choice set by what must survive underneath. What
is specific to this instance is that the film is thin, the stop is
oxide *and* tungsten, and the etch must be anisotropic at 0.17 µm
spaces without any of the sidewall passivation that an aluminium
etch gets from its own by-products. Fluorine chemistries etch oxide
and tungsten readily; chlorine chemistries etch neither quickly,
which is why, we infer, a chlorine-based etch on a metal etcher of
the kind SkyWater lists is the most likely route.

## Why this step exists

The TiN sheet must become wires, and the wires must be clean,
straight and the right width:

* **CD control.** The PDK's "LI1CD add/drop" entries of 0.01 and
  0.04 µm[^pdk-03] are, we infer, the mask sizing that compensates
  the {term}`etch bias` of this step, and the "Li1 proximity correction"
  entry[^pdk-03] the lithographic part; at 0.17 µm the sum of etch
  bias and litho bias must be reproducible to a few tens of
  nanometres. A conductive TiN line's resistance scales with its
  width, so {term}`CD` variation here is resistance variation in the `li1`
  resistors the PDK offers (li.7[^pdk-periph]).
* **Selectivity to oxide.** The etch stops on the cap oxide with
  the gate caps not far beneath (on the {ref}`CMPP <step-090>`
  cap-stop reading, only the cap oxide separates them); every
  nanometre of over-etch into the oxide thins the gate-to-LI
  dielectric. Chlorine plasmas etch SiO₂ slowly without fluorine,
  which is the {term}`selectivity` mechanism; Flamm and Donnelly set out the
  design of such etchants.[^flamm-1981]
* **Selectivity to tungsten.** A plug top exposed by overlay must
  not be hollowed out; tungsten etches quickly in fluorine but
  slowly in chlorine at low temperature — another argument for
  chlorine (inference).
* **Residue and stringers.** TiN is a hard, refractory film, and the
  polish before it ({ref}`WCMPLI <step-100>`) may have left plug
  recess or oxide {term}`erosion` for the film to step down into; the
  over-etch has to clear TiN from those steps without the
  {term}`loading effect` making the open field lose its stop.
  Gottscho, Jurgensen and Vitkavage treat microscopic uniformity in
  plasma etching,[^gottscho-1992] and Oehrlein and Kurogi the
  sidewall chemistry that decides whether a thin-film etch is
  vertical.[^oehrlein-1998]
* **Precedent.** The TiN local-interconnect process of Tang et al.
  at Texas Instruments patterned its TiN with a plasma etch selective
  to the titanium {term}`silicide` beneath;[^tang-1987] Douglas's TI patent
  claims a fluorine-deficient CF₄ plasma that etches TiN selectively
  with respect to titanium silicides,[^pat-tin-etch-ti] and Holloway
  et al.'s patent the hard-mask patterning process.[^pat-li-ti-holloway]
  Min et al. characterise TiN etching in a Cl₂/Ar inductively coupled
  plasma,[^min-2008] and Bell, Anderson and Light the BCl₃/Cl₂
  aluminium chemistry of the metal etchers on which TiN is
  normally etched.[^bell-1988]
* **Charging.** Once the LI lines are isolated, each is an antenna
  connected through its plugs to gate poly; the PDK's "LI1 thickness
  for antenna ratio calculations"[^pdk-03] and li.2 (a maximum
  length-to-width ratio of 10 for LI without a licon or mcon[^pdk-periph])
  exist because a plasma etch on a conductor tied to a gate can
  charge the gate oxide — the electron-shading mechanism Hashimoto
  described.[^hashimoto-1994]

Without `LI1ME` the {term}`local interconnect` would be a short across the
whole die.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic TiN line etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Chamber.** Single-wafer high-density metal etcher — a
   transformer-coupled ({term}`TCP`) or inductively coupled reactor with
   independent bias, helium backside cooling and optical emission
   endpoint (Lam's TCP source of the Ogle patent;[^pat-tcp-lam]
   SkyWater names a "Lam 9600" and a "Lam 2300 Versys",[^skw-01] and
   Lam's fiscal-2003 Form 10-K lists the 2300 among its etch
   products[^lam-10k]).
2. **BARC open.** If a {term}`BARC` was used, a short O₂-based or
   Cl₂/O₂ step opens it.
3. **Main etch.** Cl₂ with BCl₃ (which scavenges oxygen and reduces
   the surface oxide on the TiN) and argon or nitrogen at a few to
   tens of mTorr, moderate bias, wafer at 40–80 °C (typical
   industry conditions for the aluminium/TiN chemistry[^bell-1988][^nojiri-2015]);
   the TiN etches as TiCl₄. A fluorine-based alternative (SF₆ or CF₄
   with argon) etches TiN faster but attacks the oxide stop and the
   tungsten; Douglas's fluorine-deficient CF₄ recipe is one way to
   tame it.[^pat-tin-etch-ti]
4. **Endpoint.** Optical emission on a titanium or TiCl line as the
   film clears, then a timed over-etch of a few tens of per cent to
   remove stringers at plug steps; the etch is short (a 0.1 µm film
   at typical rates clears in well under a minute).
5. **Post-etch treatment.** An in-situ passivation step (H₂O vapour
   or O₂/CF₄ plasma) to remove residual chlorine that would
   otherwise corrode the TiN and any exposed tungsten, followed by
   resist strip — oxygen plasma {term}`ash` — and a solvent or
   dilute-amine clean for the chlorinated residue
   ({ref}`category-strip`).
6. **Metrology.** Final CD by {term}`CD-SEM` on dense and isolated lines;
   residue inspection; cross-section SEM for profile and stringers
   during development; LI {term}`sheet resistance` and line-to-line leakage
   on test structures ({ref}`category-test`). The published SKY130
   {term}`test tile` has an "LI sheet resistance and electrical
   linewidth" pair of 2.6 × 452.785 and 0.17 × 426.04 lines (the sheet
   gives no unit; we read micrometres from the rest of the column),
   an "LI to LI / LICON to LICON bridging test structure
   (15232 cells)" and an "Ultra long local interconnect 1 line
   integrity test; ~81600 squares […]".[^raw-data-testtile-pads]

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam 9600 / Lam 2300 Versys.** SkyWater lists "Lam 9600, Al, TiW,
  TiN, Pt" and "Lam 2300 Versys, Al, TiW, TiN, Nb, Pt".[^skw-01]
  Strength: **strong** for the tools and for TiN being among their
  etched materials; assignment to `LI1ME` is an **inference** — the
  list names no steps — but the LI etch is the one TiN-only etch in
  the flow, and these are the only tools listed with TiN.
* **AMAT DPS II** ("HBR, Cl2, NF3, CF4, CHF3, O2"[^skw-01]) carries
  chlorine and fluorine and could etch TiN. Strength: medium.
* Strip and clean: **GaSonics PEP, Iridia, Mattson Aspen II** ashers;
  **EKC265/EKC270** solvent bench.[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence.

  etcher reading is an inference from the oxide and tungsten stops
  and from SkyWater's TiN-capable tools.

  is stripped and the chlorine residue removed, are not public.

  is not public.
