## What this step is

`RPM` is the first lithography step printed on the gate film. The
wafer arrives from {ref}`SAGD <step-048>` covered by a blanket, undoped
amorphous-silicon layer; `RPM` coats it with photoresist, exposes it
through the *resistor protect* {term}`reticle` and develops it, leaving
islands of resist over the future poly-resistor bodies and bare
silicon everywhere else. The resist then masks the gate-doping implant
{ref}`P1I <step-050>` so that the resistor bodies stay undoped, and is
removed at {ref}`P1IS <step-051>`.

The PDK's mask table lists "Resistor Protect, RPM" as a mask used in
SKY130.[^pdk-05] The drawn layer is `rpm` (GDS 86:20, described as
"300 ohms/square polysilicon resistor implant"); a second drawn layer,
`urpm` (GDS 79:20, "2000 ohms/square polysilicon resistor implant"),
marks the ultra-high-resistance flavour; and the generated mask layer
is `crpm` (GDS 96:0, "Resistor Protect mask").[^pdk-06] The two layers
correspond to the two precision-resistor families in the device
list: the "P+ poly precision resistors" (`res_high_po`, 300 Ω/sq) and
the "P- poly precision resistors" (`res_xhigh_po`, 2000 Ω/sq), for
which "a separate implant is used to set the sheet
resistance".[^pdk-07] The extraction table gives the same two values
as 319.8 Ω/sq ("XHR poly resistor") and 2000 Ω/sq ("UHR poly
resistor"), against 48.2 Ω/sq for gate poly.[^pdk-08]

Two facts fix what the mask must do. First, both resistor flavours are
*p-type* poly while the gate is *n-type* — the PDK calls the generic
{term}`poly resistor` "N+ doped gate poly".[^pdk-07] Second, the PDK
says that for the P− poly resistors "a separate implant is used to set
the sheet resistance to 2000 ohm/sq",[^pdk-07] and both flavours are
p-type, so their doping does not come from the n-type gate implant,
which this reference describes next ({ref}`P1I <step-050>`). We
therefore infer that `RPM` protects the resistor bodies (`rpm` *and*
`urpm` regions) from the n-type gate implant, and that the p-type
resistor doping is supplied later by {ref}`PRI <step-053>` and
{ref}`UPRI <step-056>` through the *reverse* masks
{ref}`RRPM <step-052>` and {ref}`URPM <step-055>`. Whether the `urpm`
regions are covered by this reticle or only by their own later mask is
not public; the reading above is the only one in which the 2000 Ω/sq
film is never n-doped.

## Step category

`RPM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *implant-block* type, like {ref}`LVTNM <step-014>` and the
other well and channel masks: a relaxed-geometry image whose job is to
cover the right places with enough resist. Its minimum feature is a
1.27 µm line with a 0.84 µm space (the assumptions table's RPMCD /
RPMCDSP),[^pdk-03] confirmed by the periphery rules rpm.1a (minimum
width 1.270 µm) and rpm.2 (minimum spacing 0.840 µm).[^pdk-periph]
What is specific to this instance is the surface it prints on —
bare amorphous silicon rather than oxide — and the fact that the
pattern it must eventually agree with, the poly, does not yet exist.

## Why this step exists

A precision resistor needs a {term}`sheet resistance` that is high enough to
give useful values in a small area, low enough in temperature and
voltage coefficient to be predictable, and stable against later
processing. Heavily doped n⁺ gate poly at 48.2 Ω/sq[^pdk-08] is none
of those things: its resistance is too low for analogue use and its
temperature coefficient is that of a degenerate semiconductor. Lu et
al. showed how the resistance of a poly resistor depends on grain
structure and doping and how it can be optimised for monolithic
use,[^lu-1981] and Lane and Wrixon set out the design of thin-film
poly resistors for analogue ICs.[^lane-1989] The PDK offers the
300 Ω/sq flavour in five fixed widths from 0.35 µm to 5.73 µm with
slot-contact ends,[^pdk-07][^pdk-periph] which is a considerable
investment in a matched device. The resistor body itself must be at
least 0.330 µm wide (poly.3) and keep 0.480 µm from other
poly (poly.9).[^pdk-periph]

`RPM` is what keeps that device possible. If the resistor bodies
received the gate implant, no later p-type implant of practical dose
could raise them to 300 Ω/sq, let alone 2000 Ω/sq, because
{term}`counter-doping` a degenerately doped film requires an even larger dose
and gives a resistance that depends on the difference of two large
numbers. The design rules reflect the boundary the mask draws: `rpm`
must enclose the precision resistor by at least 0.200 µm (rpm.3), poly
must not straddle `rpm` (rpm.8) and must keep 0.200 µm from it
(rpm.7), and `rpm` must keep 0.200 µm from `nsdm` and may not overlap
it (rpm.6), but the resistor must
be enclosed by `psdm` by 0.110 µm (rpm.4).[^pdk-periph] A poly line
that crossed the `rpm` edge would have one segment doped n⁺ and one
segment doped p — a resistor in series with a p–n junction — which is
why the rules forbid it.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic implant-mask litho sequence for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab:

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake and {term}`HMDS` vapour prime on
   the track; the surface is silicon, on which resist adheres well.
2. **Resist coat.** A conventional i-line (DNQ/novolac) positive resist
   of about 1 µm; the PDK's assumptions table gives a general
   "Photoresist thickness" of 1.14 µm.[^pdk-03] Because the implant it
   masks ({ref}`P1I <step-050>`) is a shallow poly-doping implant at
   tens of keV (industry-typical, see that page), a micrometre of resist
   stops the beam with a large margin; the thick-resist problems of MeV
   implant masks[^pat-resist-zilog] do not arise.
3. **Exposure.** i-line step-and-repeat exposure. A 1.27 µm / 0.84 µm
   pattern is far above the i-line resolution limit (the category page
   gives {term}`CD` = {term}`k₁ <k1>`·λ/{term}`NA`), so we infer the layer is printed on the i-line
   tools, as for the well masks; no SkyWater statement assigns layers
   to tools. Amorphous silicon is strongly absorbing at 365 nm, so
   substrate reflectivity is moderate and an anti-reflective coating is
   optional for a layer this relaxed.[^wiki-arc]
4. **Alignment.** The poly has not been patterned, so the reticle aligns
   to the {term}`STI`/active marks from {ref}`FOM <step-004>` (inferred;
   the alignment tree is not public). The 0.200 µm enclosure of
   rpm.3[^pdk-periph] is the {term}`overlay` budget between this layer
   and the later {ref}`P1M <step-061>`, both registered to the same
   reference.
5. **Post-exposure bake, develop** in 2.38 % TMAH[^microchemicals-dev] (0.26 N; our arithmetic from TMAH's
   molar mass of 91.15 g/mol,[^wiki-tmah]
   taking the solution's density as about 1 g/mL), rinse,
   hard bake. Implant resists are often given a UV or thermal
   hardening step to reduce outgassing in the implanter.[^txt-02]
6. **Inspection.** Overlay to the active layer on an optical overlay
   tool and a coarse CD check; a full {term}`CD-SEM` measurement is
   unnecessary at this geometry.

**Reticle tone.** Resist must *remain* over the resistors, so with a
positive resist the reticle is mostly clear with opaque islands. The
reverse mask {ref}`RRPM <step-052>` is the complementary tone. Neither
tone is stated publicly.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`i-line <machine-i-line-stepper>` {term}`stepper`**, 200 mm (ASML PAS 5500/100–/275, Nikon NSR-2205i,
  Canon FPA-3000i), or a {ref}`KrF tool <machine-duv-krf-stepper>` if the fab prefers to keep all
  layers on one platform (category page).
* **{ref}`Coat/develop track <machine-coat-develop-track>`** (TEL, DNS/SCREEN, SVG/ASML, Sokudo).
* **{ref}`Overlay metrology <machine-cd-sem-overlay-metrology>`** tool; CD-SEM for sampling.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML i-line stepper or scanner.** SkyWater lists "ASML I-line
  stepper" and "ASML I-line scanner" alongside its {term}`DUV` tools.[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for the tools; **inference** for the assignment of
  this layer to i-line (design-rule argument above).
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ Lithius.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong; which track serves which tool is not public.
* **Overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer; CD — AMAT Verity.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence (SkyWater statement); use at this
  mask is an inference.

## Resources required

* **i-line positive photoresist** ({ref}`lithography materials <material-lithography-materials>`; DNQ/novolac[^wiki-dnq]); SkyWater's
  2021 S-1 names Dow, JSR and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo as resist
  suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **HMDS** adhesion promoter; **edge-bead remover** and rinse solvents.
* **Developer** — aqueous 2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH.[^wiki-tmah][^microchemicals-dev]
* **{ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>`**, {ref}`nitrogen <material-process-gases>`.
* **The RPM reticle** — a chrome-on-quartz photomask of relaxed
  geometry, which we infer needs no {term}`OPC`.

## Open questions

* Whether the RPM reticle covers both `rpm` and `urpm` regions, or only
  `rpm`, is not public; the reading here keeps both p-type films free
  of the n-type gate implant.
* Reticle tone, resist type and thickness, and the exposure tool are
  not public; i-line is inferred from the 1.27 µm / 0.84 µm geometry.
* Which alignment marks the layer uses (STI/active is the only pattern
  present) is not stated.
* Whether any resistor bodies other than the two precision flavours
  (for example the {term}`SONOS` or varactor poly) are also protected here is
  not public.
