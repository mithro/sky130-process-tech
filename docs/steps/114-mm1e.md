## What this step is

`MM1E` is the metal-1 etch. Through the resist lines of
{ref}`MM1 <step-113>` a chlorine plasma removes the whole
metal-1 stack of {ref}`TIAL6 <step-112>` — on the 2013 Cypress
description of S8TNV-5R at this fab, 300 Å of TiW over 3 200 Å of
Al–0.5%Cu over 100 Å of Ti;[^cyp-qtp-113005] on the stack qualified in
2013–2014, 500 Å of TiN over 90 Å of Ti over the same alloy over
250 Å of TiN on 150 Å of Ti[^cyp-qtp-123907]
({ref}`overview-metal-cap`) — stopping on the
{ref}`NILD2 <step-105>` oxide and on the tops of the tungsten plugs
of {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`, so that what remains is the `met1` wiring
(GDS 68:20[^pdk-06]) at 0.140 µm minimum width and space.[^pdk-periph]
Because chlorine-etched aluminium corrodes in air, the etch cannot
simply end: it is followed, in the same tool or immediately after,
by a passivation step, a resist strip and a wet clean; this reference
treats those as part of this step.

The etch is *subtractive*: SKY130's aluminium levels are patterned by
etching the blanket metal, whereas a copper back end is patterned by
etching trenches in the dielectric and filling them ({term}`damascene`). The
distinction matters here because the etch, not a polish, sets the
line profile, and because the spaces it opens — 0.14 µm wide and
0.36 µm deep, an {term}`aspect ratio` above 2.5:1 — are what
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>` must then fill. SkyWater lists two metal
etchers by name, "Lam 9600, Al, TiW, TiN, Pt" and "Lam 2300 Versys,
Al, TiW, TiN, Nb, Pt",[^skw-01] whose material lists match this stack.

## Step category

`MM1E` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *metal,
chlorine-chemistry* class — the category page's "Aluminium–copper
with Ti/TiN or Ti:W caps" entry — and the first of the five metal
etches ({ref}`MM2E <step-125>` to {ref}`MM5E <step-163>`). What is
specific to metal 1 is the geometry: the tightest pitch in the metal
system, 0.28 µm, shared with metal 2 (m2.1, m2.2)[^pdk-periph], a thin
stack, and a floor that is partly tungsten plug, partly oxide. What is
specific to the class is that aluminium etches *spontaneously* in
chlorine — AlCl₃ is volatile enough at the modest wafer temperatures
of an etch chamber — so {term}`anisotropy` comes entirely from a
sidewall passivation film, and that the etch product chemistry
leaves the lines liable to corrode.[^hess-1982][^nojiri-2015]

## Why this step exists

The etch turns the resist image into wires, and its details decide
several things the design rules assume:

* **Line width and profile.** The etched width, not the resist width,
  is what the PDK's 0.140 µm[^pdk-periph] describes; the PDK's
  physical-criteria table carries a "Min process bias 3s tolerance"
  of 0.032 µm,[^pdk-03] a public trace of the etch-plus-litho bias
  budget. A vertical profile with straight sidewalls gives the
  {ref}`NILD3 <step-115>` fill its best chance; a re-entrant or
  footed profile leaves voids or stringers.
* **Complete clearing.** The films etch differently: W etches in
  chlorine[^fischl-1987] and TiW in chlorine–fluorine
  mixtures,[^liu-2007-tiw] TiN in Cl₂/Ar,[^min-2008] Al–Cu in
  Cl₂/BCl₃,[^schaible-1978][^hess-1982] but the copper chlorides are
  not volatile and are removed only by ion bombardment, leaving
  copper-rich residues that Lee, Eldridge and Schwartz identified as
  corrosion sites.[^lee-1981] Any metal left between lines is a
  short; any {term}`over-etch` into the oxide or the plug tops is lost
  margin.
* **Corrosion.** Chlorine left in the sidewall polymer and the
  resist reacts with atmospheric moisture to form HCl, which attacks
  aluminium galvanically at the copper precipitates;[^lee-1981]
  Wai and Ling describe how a production line prevents it through
  the post-etch and polymer-clean sequence.[^wai-2017] This is the
  reason the etch, passivation, strip and clean are treated as one
  time-critical sequence.
* **Charging.** Metal etch is a classic source of plasma-charging
  damage: a long line connected to a gate collects current from the
  plasma while it is being etched, and the gate oxide sees the
  result. Fang and McVittie described thin-oxide damage from gate
  charging during plasma processing,[^fang-1992] and Hwang and Giapis
  explained the "notching" at the foot of lines by pattern-dependent
  charging in high-density plasmas.[^hwang-1997] The PDK's antenna
  rules chapter[^pdk-11] exists because of this step and its
  successors.

Without `MM1E` metal 1 would be a continuous sheet shorting every
contact together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic aluminium-stack etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Chamber.** A high-density plasma metal etcher — inductively or
   transformer-coupled, with separate bias, a cooled electrostatic
   chuck and helium backside cooling — with a clean-room-integrated
   passivation chamber and downstream asher on the same platform.
   Lam's {term}`TCP` source[^pat-tcp-lam] and Applied Materials' DPS coil
   reactor[^pat-dps-amat] are the two 200 mm-era designs; Lam's 10-K
   describes its 9600/2300 metal etch lines,[^lam-10k] both of which
   SkyWater's own capability page also names.[^skw-01]
2. **BARC and cap open.** The organic {term}`BARC` (if used) is opened
   in an O₂/N₂ or Cl₂-containing step, then the refractory cap is
   broken through. A TiW cap is opened with a chlorine/fluorine
   mixture — Fischl and Hess give the chlorine etching of
   tungsten-based films,[^fischl-1987] Liu and Kuo that of TiW in
   CF₄/Cl₂[^liu-2007-tiw] — while a TiN cap can be cleared in chlorine
   alone, as Min et al. characterise in a Cl₂/Ar plasma,[^min-2008] and
   so need not be a separate step. Which cap this level carries is not
   public ({ref}`overview-metal-cap`).
3. **Main etch.** Cl₂/BCl₃ with N₂ (or CHF₃ or CH₄) at a few to some
   tens of mTorr. Chlorine etches aluminium as AlCl₃; BCl₃ scavenges
   water and oxygen, breaks the native Al₂O₃ and supplies boron-
   chloride species to the sidewall; nitrogen or a carbon source
   builds the sidewall film that makes the profile
   vertical.[^schaible-1978][^hess-1982][^chen-1989] Cooperberg,
   Vahedi and Gottscho's profile simulation of Cl₂/BCl₃ aluminium
   etching shows how these knobs set the sidewall.[^cooperberg-2002]
   The etch rate is high — hundreds of nanometres per minute
   (industry-typical[^txt-02]) — so the 0.36 µm stack clears in well
   under a minute.
4. **Endpoint.** Optical emission on the atomic aluminium line near
   396 nm or the AlCl band, which drops as the aluminium clears
   (Bawolek's Monte Carlo treatment covers emission-endpoint behaviour
   in general[^bawolek-1987]; industry practice[^nojiri-2015]).
5. **Over-etch.** A timed step with reduced power and a more
   passivating chemistry to remove the Ti underlayer and any
   stringers at plug edges, with {term}`selectivity` to the oxide and
   to the tungsten plug tops; this is where {term}`notching` and micro-
   trenching from charging appear.[^hwang-1997]
6. **Passivation.** Without an air break, an H₂O-vapour or O₂/CF₄
   plasma that converts residual AlCl₃ on the sidewalls to oxide and
   fluoride and removes chlorine from the resist (industry
   practice;[^nojiri-2015][^txt-02] Wai and Ling report the production
   view[^wai-2017]).
7. **Strip and clean.** Oxygen or O₂/N₂ downstream plasma
   {term}`ash` — SkyWater lists GaSonics, Iridia and Mattson tools
   with N₂/O₂ and CF₄/H₂ options[^skw-01] — then a solvent or
   semi-aqueous polymer clean (SkyWater lists "EKS265, EKC270
   solvents" under "Batch Rotational"[^skw-01]; amine-based
   EKC265/EKC270-type solvents) within a time limit of
   the etch, followed by DI rinse and dry. HF is not used on
   aluminium (inference).
8. **Metrology.** Line {term}`CD` by {term}`CD-SEM` after etch; profile by
   cross-section SEM on monitors; metal-1 {term}`sheet resistance` and
   serpentine/comb structures at {term}`e-test` (the PDK's
   125 mΩ/sq[^pdk-08] is the resulting sheet resistance); corrosion
   inspection after a queue-time delay. The published SKY130
   {term}`test tile` measures "M1 sheet resistance and electrical
   linewidth" on 2.595 µm and 0.18 µm lines with force and sense pads,
   and "M1 / M2 step coverage/bridging over cell topo." with 0.14 µm
   serpentines and combs.[^raw-data-testtile-pads]

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam 9600 and Lam 2300 Versys.** SkyWater lists "Lam 9600, Al,
  TiW, TiN, Pt" and "Lam 2300 Versys, Al, TiW, TiN, Nb, Pt" as its
  metal etchers.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the tools and for
  the material set (aluminium with TiW and TiN); which runs metal 1
  is not public — the 2300 is the newer platform and the natural
  candidate for the tightest layer (inference).

  wet bench and batch rotational tools with "EKS265, EKC270
  solvents".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for existence; assignment to
  this step is an inference.

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Cl₂ <material-etch-gases>` and BCl₃**, **{ref}`N₂ <material-process-gases>`** (and possibly CHF₃ or CH₄) for the etch;
  **CF₄ or SF₆** for the cap breakthrough; **Ar** and **He** (backside
  cooling) — industry practice;[^nojiri-2015] SkyWater lists no gases
  for its metal etchers, but names Cl₂, CF₄ and CHF₃ on its other
  etchers.[^skw-01]
* **H₂O vapour or O₂/CF₄** for passivation; **O₂/N₂** and **{ref}`H₂/N₂ <material-anneal-ambients>`**
  for the ash.[^skw-01]
* **Amine or semi-aqueous polymer-removal solvent** ({ref}`wet chemicals <material-wet-chemicals>`; EKC265/EKC270
  class; SkyWater lists "EKS265, EKC270 solvents"[^skw-01]) and {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>`.
* **{ref}`Chamber consumables <material-hardware-consumables>`** — liners, coil windows, {term}`electrostatic chuck`
  parts — replaced often, since AlCl₃ and polymer coat the chamber;
  **{ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** with the metal stack for rate and profile.
* **Chlorine abatement** on the exhaust.
