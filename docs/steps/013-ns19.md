## What this step is

`NS19` (nitride strip) removes the isolation nitride that has served
as hard mask, oxidation barrier and polish stop since
{ref}`ISONIT <step-003>`. After the polish at
{ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>` the wafer surface is a mosaic of nitride
islands and oxide-filled trenches; `NS19` dissolves the nitride in hot
phosphoric acid, leaving the active areas covered only by the thin pad
oxide from {ref}`BOX <step-002>` and the field oxide standing slightly
proud of them. It is the last step of the isolation module; from
{ref}`LVTNM <step-014>` onwards the flow is about wells and channels.

Wikipedia's STI outline lists this as "Removal of the protective
nitride" followed by "Adjusting the oxide height to Si" (WIKI-STI).
The pad oxide is not necessarily removed here: in most flows it stays
on as a screen oxide for the first well implants and is replaced by a
fresh sacrificial oxide before gate oxidation. The PDK's final
field-oxide step of 0.07 µm above the silicon under poly (PDK-03) is
the net result of the nitride thickness, the polish and all the oxide
recess that follows this step.

## Step category

`NS19` is filed under {ref}`Resist strip / clean <category-strip>`
because it is a wet, blanket, selective film removal; no photoresist is
involved. It is the only hot-phosphoric nitride strip in the baseline
flow (later nitrides — spacers, the LI cap, passivation — stay in the
device). The "19" in the code is not explained by any public source
(see open questions).

## Why this step exists

The nitride has done its job and would be harmful if left:

* it is an oxidation barrier, so the gate oxide could not be grown
  through it;
* it is a poor implant screen and a charge-trapping dielectric, so
  wells and channels could not be implanted through it reproducibly;
* its tensile stress on the active islands would remain in the device.

The strip must be *selective*: it has to remove ~100 nm or more of
nitride while barely touching the trench oxide (which is exposed) and
the pad oxide (which protects the silicon). Hot phosphoric acid is the
one wet chemistry that does this. The 1967 paper that established it
reports that "Refluxed boiling phosphoric acid at 180°C was found to
be a useful etchant for silicon nitride films. The etch rate is
100 Å/min", with deposited oxide etching at 0–25 Å/min and silicon at
3 Å/min (VGH-1967) — a selectivity of at least 4 : 1 against
deposited oxide and about 30 : 1 against silicon. Wikipedia likewise notes that silicon
nitride is "attacked by dilute HF and hot H₃PO₄" (WIKI-SIN).

Two things can go wrong: over-etching the exposed field oxide (which
recesses it and creates divots at the active edge where the pad oxide
meets the trench liner), and leaving nitride residue (which blocks
later oxidations locally). Both are controlled by the acid's water
content and temperature and by the pre-strip treatment.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic nitride strip for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab:

1. **Pre-strip oxide breakthrough.** The polish leaves a thin oxidised
   or slurry-modified skin on the nitride, and the exposed nitride may
   carry a native oxide. A short dilute-HF dip (seconds) removes it so
   that the phosphoric acid can attack the nitride uniformly. This dip
   also slightly recesses the field oxide, and its length is part of
   the field-oxide height budget.
2. **Hot phosphoric acid.** Batch immersion in 85 % H₃PO₄ held at
   150–180 °C; the boiling point and hence the etch rate depend on the
   water content, so production baths are run under reflux with
   controlled water addition to keep the composition constant
   (VGH-1967, TXT-02). At ~100 Å/min a 150 nm nitride needs about
   15 min plus over-etch. Etch time is set by the post-CMP nitride
   thickness map from {ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>`.
3. **Rinse.** Hot then cold DI-water cascade rinse — phosphoric acid
   is viscous and must be rinsed thoroughly.
4. **Post-strip clean.** SC-1/SC-2 (WIKI-RCA) to remove particles and
   metals introduced by the acid bath, then spin or IPA dry.
5. **Inspection.** Optical inspection for nitride residue; oxide-height
   and pad-oxide thickness by ellipsometry on monitor structures.

SkyWater's capability page lists the exact bench: "Akrion Gamma Batch
Wet Bench" with sulphuric, SC1, phosphoric and BOE chemistries
(SKW-01). Phosphoric acid appears on no other tool on the list, so the
Akrion bench is the site's hot-phosphoric strip tool by elimination.

Dry (plasma) nitride removal is possible but is not used for this
step in production, because it has no useful selectivity to the
exposed field oxide.

## Machines typically used

* **Batch wet bench with a heated, refluxed phosphoric-acid tank**
  and quartz or PFA carriers: Akrion Gamma/GAMA, DNS (SCREEN) WS-series,
  SCP/FSI Mercury-type spray tools (for the HF and clean steps),
  Semitool, Santa Clara Plastics.
* **Ellipsometer / reflectometer** for oxide height.
* **Bright-field inspection** for residue.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Akrion Gamma batch wet bench (phosphoric).** SKW-01 lists it with
  "phosphoric" among its chemistries. Strength: strong (SkyWater
  statement) that a hot-phosphoric capable bench exists; the
  assignment to `NS19` follows from it being the only phosphoric tank
  listed.
* **DNS / FSI Mercury HF/SC1/SC2** for the pre- and post-strip cleans
  (SKW-01). Strength: strong for existence.
* **SEZ 223 / DaVinci single-wafer HF** as an alternative for the
  oxide breakthrough (SKW-01, SKW-07). Strength: strong for existence.

## Resources required

* **Phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄, 85 %)**, high-purity electronic grade, and
  **DI water** for composition control (VGH-1967).
* **Dilute HF** for the pre-strip breakthrough (WIKI-SIN).
* **SC-1 / SC-2 chemicals** (NH₄OH, HCl, H₂O₂) (WIKI-RCA).
* **DI water** for cascade rinsing; **nitrogen** and **IPA** for
  drying.
* **Bath heaters, reflux condensers, quartz tanks** — hot phosphoric
  is one of the most maintenance-intensive baths in the fab (TXT-07).
* SkyWater's filings name KMG Chemicals and EMD Performance Materials
  among its chemical suppliers (SEC-01, SEC-02) without tying them to
  a step.

## Open questions

* The meaning of "19" in `NS19` is not public; it may be a recipe or
  historical revision number, or a bath/tank identifier, but that is
  speculation.
* Whether the pad oxide is removed here or kept as the well-implant
  screen, and when the sacrificial oxidation before gate oxide occurs,
  cannot be resolved from the public step list (no explicit
  sacrificial-oxide step is listed between `NS19` and
  {ref}`GOX100 <step-043>`).
* Bath temperature, acid concentration control method and etch time
  for SKY130 are not public.
* Whether a divot-control treatment (for example a nitride liner or a
  deliberately thinned pad oxide) is used is not public.
