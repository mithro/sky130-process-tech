## What this step is

`LICM1` is the lithography for the local-interconnect contacts. On
the flat, annealed dielectric of {ref}`RTAD2 <step-092>` it coats,
exposes and develops a resist whose openings are the holes —
0.17 µm squares at minimum — that {ref}`LICM1E <step-094>` will etch
through the {term}`cap oxide` and the phosphosilicate glass down to the
poly heads, the source/drain diffusions and the well taps. Those
holes become the tungsten plugs (`licon1`) that connect the
transistors to the titanium-nitride {term}`local interconnect`, and they are
the smallest holes in the flow: the layer's minimum feature is the
same 0.17 µm as the `mcon` contact above it,[^pdk-03] and the drawn
`licon1` maps to a 0.08 µm "Standard Licon bottom CD" on the
wafer.[^pdk-03]

The PDK's mask table lists "Local Intr Cont.1, LICM1" as used in
SKY130;[^pdk-05] the drawn layer is `licon1` (GDS 66:44, "Contact to
local interconnect") and the generated mask layer `clicm1` (43:0,
"Local interconnect contact mask") with "mask add" (106:43) and
"mask drop" (106:42) purposes,[^pdk-06] so the {term}`reticle` is derived
from the drawn layer by Boolean operations. The rules head the
`licon` section "Defines contacts between poly/diff/tap and Li1" and
give: 0.170 µm minimum and maximum width and length (licon.1),
0.170 µm spacing (licon.2), only minimum square licons except in the
seal ring (licon.3), "Licon1 must overlap li1 and (poly or diff or
tap)" (licon.4), 0.040 µm enclosure by diffusion (licon.5a), 0.050 µm
enclosure of a poly licon by poly (licon.8), 0.055 µm spacing from a
diffusion licon to a gate (licon.11), "Every source_diff and every
tap must enclose at least one licon1" (licon.16), "Licons may not
overlap both poly and (diff or tap)" (licon.17) and the nitride-cut
rules licon.13, licon.15 and licon.18.[^pdk-periph] Precision
resistors use *slotted* licons 0.190 µm wide and 2.000 µm long
(licon.1b, licon.1c), spaced 0.350 or 0.510 µm (licon.2b–2d).[^pdk-periph]
The minimum-CD table adds a 0.17/0.17 µm slotted entry
(`LICM1SLCD`) and a 0.19/0.35 µm core entry (`LICM1CD`,
`LICM1CDSP`),[^pdk-03] and the physical criteria a "Licon1 etch
angle" of 10° and a 0.15 µm "min. etch and fill capability for
isolation, licon, and met1".[^pdk-03]

## Step category

`LICM1` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *contact-hole* type — the first of the flow's three
hole layers (`licon1`, `mcon` at {ref}`CTM1 <step-107>`, and the
{term}`vias <via>`) and, at 0.17 µm holes on 0.17 µm spaces (a 0.34 µm pitch),
printed at a {term}`k1` close to that of the 0.15 µm poly gate
(poly.1a[^pdk-periph]) — harder, because it is a hole. Hole layers
differ from line layers in every lithographic respect: a hole is a
two-dimensional feature whose aerial image has markedly lower contrast
than an equal-width line, the dose latitude is narrower, the depth of
focus is smaller,
and isolated and dense holes print differently enough to need
{term}`OPC`. The surface is flat — the whole point of
{ref}`CMPP <step-090>` — so this layer, unlike {ref}`NPCM <step-078>`,
does not fight topography; it fights resolution.

## Why this step exists

Every transistor terminal, every resistor end and every well tap in
the PDK is reached through a `licon1`; licon.16 makes a contact
mandatory on every source and tap.[^pdk-periph] The reasons the
layer looks as it does:

* **Contact resistance and the 0.17 µm square.** The PDK's
  extraction tables give 15 000 mΩ (15 Ω) per licon,[^pdk-08] a
  value set by the contact area, the {term}`silicide` formed in its bottom
  ({ref}`CSIL <step-098>`) and the doping beneath (the specific
  contact resistivity Berger's models describe[^berger-1972]). Only
  minimum squares are allowed (licon.3) because a fixed hole size is
  what makes the etch, the {term}`liner` and the tungsten fill reproducible;
  larger holes fill differently and dish in the polish.
* **The enclosure rules.** licon.5a (0.040 µm by diffusion) and
  licon.8 (0.050 µm by poly) are the {term}`overlay` budget of this layer
  against the active and poly patterns plus the etch's lateral
  growth; licon.11 (0.055 µm from a diffusion contact to a gate) is
  the margin that keeps the contact etch off the {term}`spacer` and the gate
  edge.[^pdk-periph] Levinson treats how overlay budgets are
  allocated among layers.[^levinson-2005]
* **Slotted resistor contacts.** The precision resistors are
  contacted by long slots rather than rows of squares (licon.1b,
  licon.1c) so that the contact resistance of the resistor heads is
  uniform and small compared with the body; Tsang et al. analysed
  the resistance variation of high-value {term}`poly resistor` banks, in
  which the contact head is a term.[^tsang-2014]
* **Lithographic difficulty.** ITRS 2001 gives contact holes their
  own lines in the lithography tables (165 nm in resist and 150 nm
  after etch in 2001), noting "the particular challenges associated
  with imaging contact holes": "contacts have very small process
  windows and large mask error factors".[^itrs-03] At 0.17 µm on 248 nm, contact
  printing relied on attenuated phase-shift masks and off-axis
  illumination: Chen, Wang and Chu optimised an attenuated {term}`PSM` for
  contact-hole printing[^chen-1999-psm] and Yang and Dai measured
  the process window of 0.2 µm contacts with off-axis illumination
  and an attenuated PSM as a function of resist and oxide
  thickness.[^yang-1998] Levenson introduced phase shifting[^levenson-1982]
  and Lin surveyed its variants.[^lin-1993] The "mask add" and "mask
  drop" purposes of `clicm1`[^pdk-06] are, we infer, where the
  proximity corrections of the kind Otto et al., Rieger and
  Stirniman, and Cobb and Zakhor describe are
  applied.[^otto-1994][^rieger-1994][^cobb-1995]

Without `LICM1` nothing on the wafer could be connected to anything
else.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic contact-hole lithography sequence for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Surface preparation.** The annealed cap oxide is flat;
   dehydration bake and {term}`HMDS` prime on the track.[^txt-02]
2. **BARC and resist.** An organic bottom anti-reflective coating
   ({term}`BARC`) to suppress the standing waves and swing that a
   thick oxide stack over reflective poly and silicon would otherwise
   cause;[^wiki-arc] then a 248 nm chemically amplified positive
   resist of the order of 0.4–0.6 µm (industry-typical for a contact
   layer[^txt-05][^mack-2007]), thin enough for resolution but thick
   enough to survive a deep oxide etch — the PDK's nominal 1.14 µm
   "Photoresist thickness"[^pdk-03] is, we infer, the implant-layer
   value. Sturtevant et al. describe the substrate-contamination
   sensitivity of chemically amplified resists that makes the BARC
   doubly useful.[^sturtevant-1994]
3. **Exposure.** At 0.17 µm feature and space, an i-line {term}`stepper` at
   {term}`NA` 0.6 would need k₁ = 0.17 × 0.6 / 0.365 ≈ 0.28, below the "0.4
   for production" limit,[^wiki-litho] so i-line is excluded; a
   248 nm tool at NA 0.6–0.7 gives k₁ ≈ 0.41–0.48, workable for
   holes only with an attenuated phase-shift mask
   ({term}`attenuated PSM`) and off-axis
   illumination.[^chen-1999-psm][^yang-1998] ITRS 2001 lists
   "248 nm + PSM" and "193 nm" as the exposure options for the 130 nm
   node, and only 248 nm had "a mature infrastructure".[^itrs-03] We therefore infer a
   **DUV (KrF) exposure with resolution enhancement** for `LICM1`;
   whether on a stepper or a scanner is not public — SkyWater lists
   both.[^skw-01] Brunner sets out how lens aberrations, to which
   small holes are most sensitive, enter the budget.[^brunner-1997]
4. **Alignment.** To poly for the poly contacts and to active for
   the diffusion contacts (licon.8, licon.5a[^pdk-periph]); van Haren
   et al. treat the alignment-mark placement side of the overlay
   budget.[^van-haren-2019]
5. **Post-exposure bake** (the {term}`PEB` that drives the
   chemically amplified resist's deprotection), **develop** in 2.38 % (0.26 N)
   TMAH,[^txt-02] rinse, dry.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** After-develop {term}`CD-SEM` of dense and
   isolated holes; overlay on box-in-box targets to poly and active;
   the dose/focus window characterised as Bossung and Ausschnitt
   describe;[^bossung-1977][^ausschnitt-1999] after-develop
   inspection for missing or bridged holes, which become open or
   shorted contacts.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`DUV (KrF, 248 nm) stepper or scanner <machine-duv-krf-stepper>`**, 200 mm: ASML PAS 5500/300–/750
  series,[^asml-750e][^asml-800] Nikon NSR-S20x, Canon FPA-3000EX
  ({ref}`category-lithography`).
* **{ref}`Coat/develop track <machine-coat-develop-track>`** (TEL, DNS/SCREEN, Sokudo) with BARC and {term}`DUV`
  resist modules and PEB plates.
* **{ref}`Overlay metrology <machine-cd-sem-overlay-metrology>`** (KLA-Tencor 5xxx/Archer) and **CD-SEM**.
* **Attenuated phase-shift reticle** (MoSi on quartz).[^wiki-psm]

## Resources required

* **248 nm chemically amplified positive photoresist** ({ref}`lithography materials <material-lithography-materials>`) and **organic
  BARC**; SkyWater's 2021 S-1 names Dow, JSR and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo as
  resist suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **HMDS**, **2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH developer**,[^txt-02] edge-bead remover,
  rinse solvents, {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>` and {ref}`nitrogen <material-process-gases>`.
* **The LICM1 reticle** — generated from `clicm1` (43:0) with its
  add/drop corrections;[^pdk-06] an attenuated phase-shift blank on
  a quartz substrate (SEMI P1 class[^semi-p1]) if the resolution
  enhancement inferred above is used.

## Open questions

* The exposure tool (stepper or scanner), wavelength, illumination,
  reticle type and resist are not public; the DUV-with-RET reading
  is an inference from the 0.17 µm rules.
* How the drawn 0.17 µm `licon1` becomes a 0.08 µm bottom {term}`CD`
  (etch taper at 10°, {term}`resist trim`, mask bias) is not stated; the
  PDK gives only the end values.[^pdk-03]
* Whether the "mask add"/"mask drop" purposes carry OPC, {term}`SRAFs <SRAF>` or
  only Boolean sizing is not public.
