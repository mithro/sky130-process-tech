## What this step is

`2PSDI` is a second p-type implant, described in this reference as
made through the *same* resist as {ref}`PSDI <step-082>`, before the
strip {ref}`PDIS <step-084>`: the PDK's mask list has a single "P+
Implant, PSDM".[^pdk-05] On that reading, whatever it is, it lands in
exactly the regions the first implant did — the PMOS source/drains,
the p⁺ taps, the PNP emitters and collectors, the NPN base contacts,
the P⁺ diffusion resistors, the p-diodes and the precision-resistor
heads opened by the {term}`nitride cut` — and differs from the first only in
species, energy, dose, tilt or twist. Nothing public says which.
This page sets out what a second p-type implant under a source/drain
mask usually is, and what the PDK's own tables make more or less
likely for SKY130.

The PDK's implant-angle table lists three angles: 0° for "High
current" implants, 7° for the tip implant, and 40° with a 23° twist
for the high-voltage tip.[^pdk-03] Its physical-criteria table gives
a "Photoresist thickness for HV Tip Implants" of 0.3 µm, shadowing
allowances for the N-tip masks (`NTM shadowing` 0.16 µm, `HVNTM
shadowing` 0.232 µm) and, notably, an `HVPTM shadowing` of 0.089 µm
and a "pseudo-shadowing" of 0.045 µm.[^pdk-03] An HVPTM — a
high-voltage P-tip mask — appears in no public mask list; its
shadowing allowance
in the PDK is the one public trace of a tilted p-type implant in the
family of flows the tables were written for.

## Step category

`2PSDI` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*source/drain* class, sharing a mask with {ref}`PSDI <step-082>`.
Two implants under one mask is a common pattern: the well pairs
{ref}`NWI <step-018>`/{ref}`NWI2 <step-019>` and
{ref}`PWI <step-027>`/{ref}`PWI2 <step-028>` chain energies to shape a
profile, and the tip/halo pairs ({ref}`ASTI <step-065>`/
{ref}`BHI <step-066>`) put two species at two angles through one
resist. This is the only source/drain implant in the flow with such a
partner; the N⁺ side has one implant ({ref}`NSDI <step-086>`).

## Why this step exists

Three readings are consistent with the public evidence. They are not
exclusive, and none is stated by any public source.

* **A profile-shaping pair (BF₂ then B, or two energies).** A
  molecular BF₂⁺ implant gives a shallow, self-amorphised, highly
  active surface layer, and a following B⁺ implant at higher energy
  gives the depth and the low sheet resistance the contact needs; or
  two boron energies do the same. Ogura et al.'s original "double
  implanted LDD" used two implants to grade a junction,[^ogura-1982]
  and on the n-type side Lee and Lee's arsenic-plus-phosphorus
  double implant[^lee-1999-edl] and Augendre et al.'s As/P
  co-implantation[^augendre-2001] show the same logic at
  0.25–0.13 µm. The trade-offs between B and BF₂ that Bourdelle et
  al. measured for thin-oxide PMOS[^bourdelle-2000] and the fluorine
  effects Wang et al. found[^wang-1997] are what such a pair is
  tuned around; Josse et al. describe optimising the whole
  source/drain-plus-spike-anneal module for a 0.13 µm
  platform.[^josse-2002] On this reading `2PSDI` runs on the same
  high-current tool at 0° tilt.[^pdk-03]
* **The PMOS extension, made after the spacer.** The PDK's PMOS
  cross-section shows a "P−" region under the gate edge,[^pdk-07]
  yet the flow has no PMOS tip mask. A lighter boron implant at a
  large tilt, under the source/drain resist, can reach under the
  spacer to form an extension after the spacer — the large-angle
  tilted implant that Lin et al. used for a punch-through stopper is
  the same geometry.[^lin-1997] The PDK's 40°/23° "HV tip" angles and
  its `HVPTM shadowing` allowance[^pdk-03] show that tilted p-type
  tips are within the family's vocabulary; whether the 1.8 V PMOS
  gets one this way, and whether this step is it, is not public.
  Such an implant would be shadowed by the roughly 0.4 µm-tall (0.18 µm poly plus the ~0.2 µm cap[^pdk-03]) capped gates
  and by the resist — the shadowing that Krieger et al.
  analysed[^krieger-1989] and that the PDK's shadowing allowances
  budget for — and would more naturally run on a medium-current
  tool with tilt: SkyWater's "Axcelis 8250 Mid current B11, BF2, As,
  ESC chuck, E shower, 1e11 to 1e14, 0-60 deg tilt".[^skw-01]
* **A device-specific implant.** A second implant tuned for one
  device family — the 5 V PMOS with its thicker oxide and longer
  channel, the PNP emitter, or the diffusion resistor — is possible
  under a shared mask only if the first implant does no harm there;
  the mask sharing argues against a strongly different dose, so this
  reading is the weakest of the three.

Whichever reading is right, the gate is untouched: on the reading
used throughout this reference the PMOS gate is capped and n⁺ doped
({ref}`P1I <step-050>`, {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`), and no p-type
source/drain implant reaches it. That is also why a
boron-penetration problem of the kind Pfiester et al. described for
p⁺ gates[^pfiester-1990] does not arise in this flow.

Without `2PSDI`, on the first reading, the P⁺ junction would be
either too shallow for its contact or too deep for its channel; on
the second, the PMOS would have no extension at all.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic description for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public); the two main readings differ in
tool and geometry:

* **As a deep-junction component.** Boron (B⁺ from BF₃[^wiki-bf3])
  at tens of keV and of the order of 10¹⁵ cm⁻² (industry-typical[^txt-01][^txt-02])
  at 0° tilt,[^pdk-03] on the same batch high-current implanter as
  {ref}`PSDI <step-082>`, immediately after it without unloading; the
  amorphous layer from the BF₂ implant suppresses channelling of the
  boron that follows, which is one reason for the order. Germanium
  pre-amorphisation is the alternative route to the same
  end.[^ozturk-1988] Wafer cooling, flood-gun charge control and
  resist outgassing are as on the {ref}`PSDI <step-082>` page
  ({ref}`category-implant`; Smith,[^smith-1983] Dixon et
  al.,[^dixon-1996] Horsky[^horsky-1998]).
* **As a tilted extension.** Boron or BF₂ at low energy and of the
  order of 10¹³–10¹⁴ cm⁻² (industry-typical for an extension or
  pocket[^txt-01]), at a large tilt with a quad twist so that both
  gate edges of every orientation are reached, on a single-wafer
  medium-current tool with an {term}`electrostatic chuck` and electron
  shower; the tilt is limited by the resist height; we read the PDK's
  thinner resist budget for its HV tip implants (0.3 µm[^pdk-03],
  against the nominal 1.14 µm[^pdk-03]) as a response to that, though
  the PDK does not say so. Adachi et al.
  describe forming PMOS extensions with pre-amorphisation and sub-keV
  boron, the low-energy alternative to tilt.[^adachi-2001]
* **Monitoring.** Sheet resistance on monitors after a monitor
  anneal for the first reading; thermal-wave measurement[^smith-1985]
  is usable for a lighter tilted implant. The e-test PMOS parameters
  — the −1.05 V long-channel threshold and the short-channel
  behaviour of `pfet_01v8`[^pdk-07] — are the ultimate monitor of the
  extension, whichever step forms it.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`High-current batch implanter <machine-high-current-implanter>`** (Axcelis/Eaton GSD, Applied
  Materials xR/Quantum, Varian VIISta 80[^mezack-2000]) for a
  deep-junction component.
* **{ref}`Medium-current single-wafer implanter <machine-medium-current-implanter>`** with tilt and twist
  (Axcelis/Eaton 8250, Varian E220/E500, Applied Materials xR LEAP)
  for a tilted extension component.[^current-1996]
* **{ref}`Four-point probe <machine-sheet-resistance-metrology>`**; **thermal-wave** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD high-dose implanter** ("Hi dose") — "B11, BF2, P, As
  2-180kev, 5e12 to 5e16, tilt/twist".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the
  tool; assignment is an **inference** and depends on which reading of the
  step is right.
* **Axcelis 8250 medium-current** — "B11, BF2, As, ESC chuck, E
  shower, 1e11 to 1e14, 0-60 deg tilt".[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  the tool and for its tilt range; assignment is an inference that
  applies only on the tilted-extension reading. The Eaton 8250HT
  covers "3keV to 750keV" at beam currents "between 4µA and
  3,500µA".[^axcelis-8250]

## Open questions

* What `2PSDI` is — a second energy or species for the deep P⁺
  junction, a tilted PMOS extension, or a device-specific implant —
  is not public; this page gives the readings, not an answer.
* Where the "P−" extension in the PDK's PMOS cross-section[^pdk-07]
  is formed, and whether SKY130 includes an HVPTM-type p-tip implant
  (no public mask list includes such a mask[^pdk-05]), are open
  questions.
* Species, energy, dose, tilt and twist are not public; the values
  given are industry-typical for each reading.
* Which implanter runs the step follows from which reading is
  right, and is therefore also open.
