## What this step is

`WCMP5` is the tungsten polish that turns the blanket film of
{ref}`WDEP5 <step-147>` into via-3 plugs. The wafer is pressed
face-down on a rotating pad flooded with an oxidising, abrasive slurry
until the tungsten and the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN5 <step-146>` are
gone from the field and the polish lands on the
{ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` cap oxide; tungsten survives only inside the
vias. What is left is a planar oxide surface studded with 0.20 µm
tungsten squares (via3.1[^pdk-periph]) — the `via3` plugs, "Contact
from metal 3 to metal 4"[^pdk-06] — some landing on metal-3 lines and
some on the top plates of the first {term}`MiM capacitor` (the PDK's `cap_mim`
cross-section draws both[^pdk-07]). On this surface the metal-4 stack
of {ref}`WTIAL4 <step-149>` will be sputtered. It is the fifth and
last tungsten polish in the step list used in this reference, after
{ref}`WCMPLI <step-100>`, {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`,
{ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>` and {ref}`WCMP4 <step-133>`.

SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP" with the applications "oxide",
"nitride", "niobium", "aluminum", "tungsten", "high selectivity
tungsten" and "copper";[^skw-01] which tungsten process runs this step
is not public. The dielectric the polish stops on is 0.39 µm thick
over metal 3, the via-3 height of the PDK's stack diagram,[^pdk-04]
and thinner over the capacitor plates (our estimate at
{ref}`CMPM3 <step-142>`); the metal deposited next is the 0.845 µm
metal 4,[^pdk-04] which, like metal 3, carries a MiM capacitor built on
its unpatterned stack ({ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>` to
{ref}`CAP2ME <step-153>`). The PDK's `via3` is 3 410 mΩ,[^pdk-08]
measured on plugs that have been through this step.

## Step category

`WCMP5` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *metal-clearing* type: it removes two metals completely
and stops on a third material, with {term}`plug recess`, oxide
{term}`erosion` in dense arrays, tungsten {term}`coring` and corrosion
as the failure modes. {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` sets out the slurry
electrochemistry and endpoint methods in full and
{ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>` what a via level changes; Kaufman et al.'s
oxidation-and-abrasion mechanism is the physics.[^kaufman-1991] What
is specific to this instance is what lies above and below. Below,
some plugs sit on capacitor plates, so the dense via arrays that
contact large capacitors — the published test tile has a
"Via-3 chain, 5754 via-3, M4-CAPM over M3" and a 1 120-via "Via-3
array, M4-M3 near CAPM"[^raw-data-testtile-pads] — are also where
oxide erosion is greatest (inference). Above, the surface this polish leaves becomes,
under the metal-4 stack, the base of the second MiM capacitor's
bottom plate: the flat, topography-free bottom electrode the
top-plate-first construction relies on ({ref}`CAPILD <step-135>`) is
only as flat as this polish makes it (inference).

## Why this step exists

The plug process needs the tungsten removed from everywhere but the
vias, and the result flat:

* **Isolation.** Blanket tungsten shorts every via-3 plug, every
  metal-3 line and every capacitor top plate together; the polish
  makes them separate conductors, as IBM's metal/insulator CMP patents
  first described.[^pat-cmp-ibm-1988][^pat-cmp-ibm-1990]
* **A flat base for metal 4 and the second capacitor.** Any recessed
  plug or residual step prints through the sputtered metal-4 stack;
  under the {ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>` dielectric such a step is a
  place where a few tens of nanometres of capacitor dielectric would thin or
  crack (inference from the construction). Wang et al. characterised
  an integrated tungsten CMP process for via plugs[^wang-2002-wcmp]
  and Lee et al. the consumables for a plug polish.[^lee-2001-wcmp]
* **Via resistance.** Over-polish recesses the plug — Vlassak's
  contact-mechanics model of {term}`dishing` and erosion[^vlassak-2001]
  and Nguyen et al.'s dishing model[^nguyen-2000] describe the
  geometry — and reduces the contact area for the metal-4 bottom
  layer; under-polish leaves residue that shorts. The 3 410 mΩ per
  `via3` of the PDK's tables[^pdk-08] is the result.
* **Oxide loss over dense vias and capacitors.** Dense via-3 arrays
  erode faster than the field, the pattern-density dependence Stine et
  al. characterised;[^stine-1998] whatever is lost thins the dielectric
  between metal 3 (or a capacitor plate) and metal 4 locally. Shiratani
  and Kumazawa describe a low-erosion tungsten CMP
  process,[^shiratani-2006] and Grover et al. how slurry rheology
  shifts the oxide-to-tungsten balance.[^grover-1998]

Without `WCMP5` there would be no via-3 plugs, only a tungsten sheet
shorting metal 3 and the capacitor plates together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic tungsten via-plug polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the sequence is that of
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`.

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher — the Mirra
   architecture[^pat-cmp-mirra] — with a carrier head, retaining ring,
   multi-zone membrane, stacked polyurethane pad, diamond conditioner
   and an in-situ endpoint window on some platens.[^pat-cmp-window]
2. **Slurry.** Acidic (pH 2–4, industry-typical[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]),
   with alumina or silica abrasive and an oxidiser — ferric nitrate,
   hydrogen peroxide with an iron catalyst, or potassium
   iodate;[^kaufman-1991][^kneer-1997][^stein-1999][^pat-cmp-cabot]
   Krishnan, Nalaskowski and Cook review the chemistry.[^rev-02] The
   "high selectivity tungsten" of SkyWater's list[^skw-01] we read as
   a slurry removing tungsten many times faster than oxide
   (inference), which would suit a level where oxide loss over the
   capacitors matters.
3. **Recipe.** A first platen removes the bulk tungsten; a second
   clears the last tungsten and the TiN liner and lands on the cap
   oxide with a short, low-pressure over-polish; a third buffs.
   Down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens of rpm are
   typical.[^txt-05] Elbel et al. give a production description of
   tungsten CMP and its defects.[^elbel-1998]
4. **Endpoint.** Motor-current or optical endpoint as the pad passes
   from tungsten to oxide;[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm] Sue
   et al. describe tungsten CMP endpoint detection.[^sue-1999] The
   over-polish is then timed.
5. **{term}`Post-CMP clean <post-CMP clean>`.** Immediately: a double-sided brush scrub with
   dilute NH₄OH or a TMAH-based chemistry — Jolley reports TMAH as a
   post-tungsten-CMP clean[^jolley-1998] — then spin-rinse-dry; Sun,
   Han and Keswani review brush scrubbing.[^sun-2017]
6. **Metrology.** Plug recess and oxide erosion by profilometry or AFM
   on via-3 array test structures; remaining oxide over metal-3 pads
   by reflectometry; residual-metal and scratch inspection on the KLA AIT class (our
   reading of "AIT" in SkyWater's job posting);[^job-06] via-3 chain
   resistance and capacitor leakage at {term}`e-test`.

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Tungsten CMP slurry <material-cmp-consumables>`** — alumina or silica abrasive with ferric
  nitrate, hydrogen peroxide ({ref}`wet chemicals <material-wet-chemicals>`) or iodate oxidiser (industry
  practice[^rev-02][^pat-cmp-cabot]).

  category page) and **diamond pad conditioners**.[^wiki-cmp]

  clean; **PVA brushes**.
* **Carrier-head consumables**; **slurry supply and {ref}`waste treatment <material-hardware-consumables>`**
  for metal-laden acidic CMP waste.[^txt-07]
