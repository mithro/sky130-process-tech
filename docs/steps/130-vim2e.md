## What this step is

`VIM2E` etches the via-2 holes. Through the 0.20 µm resist openings
of {ref}`VIM2 <step-129>` a fluorocarbon plasma cuts the
{ref}`NCAPOX4 <step-128>` cap and the {ref}`NILD4 <step-126>` oxide —
0.42 µm in all, the via-2 height of the PDK's stack diagram[^pdk-04]
— down to the top of the metal-2 lines, producing the holes that
{ref}`TIN4 <step-131>` will line and {ref}`WDEP4 <step-132>` will fill
to make the `via2` plug, "Contact from metal 2 to metal 3".[^pdk-06]
The resist is stripped afterwards; the step list used in this
reference shows no separate strip between `VIM2E` and `TIN4`, so on
our reading the strip and post-etch clean belong to this step, as at
{ref}`VIME <step-119>`.

The etch repeats the via-1 etch one level up with a deeper, wider
hole: 0.20 µm squares (via2.1a) on a 0.200 µm space (via2.2), with
metal 2 required to enclose them by only 0.040 µm (via2.4),[^pdk-periph]
etched 0.42 µm deep[^pdk-04] — an {term}`aspect ratio` of about 2.1:1
as drawn, against 1.8:1 at via 1 — and landing on the same kind of
floor, the 300 Å titanium–tungsten cap over Al–0.5%Cu of the metal-2
stack.[^cyp-qtp-113005] The deeper hole means more oxide to clear,
more sidewall for fluorocarbon polymer to accumulate on, and a longer
etch before the cap is reached; the smaller enclosure means a larger
share of vias whose floor is partly the oxide beside a 0.14 µm
metal-2 line (inference). The PDK's `via2` is 3 410 mΩ against the
4 500 mΩ of a `via`,[^pdk-08] which on our reading reflects the larger
bottom area rather than any difference in the etch.

## Step category

`VIM2E` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *dielectric,
fluorocarbon* class and the second of the four via etches;
{ref}`VIME <step-119>` sets out what distinguishes the class within
the flow — the stop layer is a metal that fluorine can etch, since
tungsten forms volatile WF₆ (Petri, Henry and Sadeghi[^petri-1992])
and titanium TiF₄, so the etch must reach the TiW in a polymerising,
fluorine-lean regime and stop on time — and {ref}`CTME <step-108>` the
underlying surface chemistry.[^flamm-1981][^winters-1992] What is
specific to this instance is the depth. At 0.42 µm and 2.1:1 the
conductance limits Coburn and Winters described[^coburn-1989] and the
aspect-ratio-dependent etching ({term}`ARDE`) of Gottscho, Jurgensen
and Vitkavage's review[^gottscho-1992] matter more than at via 1, and
the over-etch needed to clear the deepest, most polymer-starved vias
is a larger fraction of the cap's tolerance.

## Why this step exists

The via-2 hole sets the resistance and reliability of every
connection between metal 2 and metal 3; the etch decides the same
four things as at via 1:

* **A clean, complete landing on TiW.** Polymer or oxide left on the
  floor raises the via resistance and, as Bui et al. showed for the
  anti-reflective cap under a tungsten-plug via, degrades its
  electromigration performance;[^bui-1994] a deeper via with more
  sidewall polymer is more prone to it.
* **Not punching through.** If the 300 Å TiW[^cyp-qtp-113005] is
  consumed, fluorine reaches the aluminium and forms involatile AlF₃
  — fluorine does not etch aluminium[^hess-1982] — leaving a
  resistive fluoride and a cap too thin to serve as shunt and
  barrier; Texas Instruments' patent on an etch stop for a
  "fluorine-bearing" via etch states the problem.[^pat-etchstop-ti]
  With a longer over-etch to budget for, the selectivity mechanism
  Schaepkens et al. describe — a thicker steady-state fluorocarbon
  film on the non-oxide surface[^schaepkens-1999] — has more work to
  do here.
* **Tolerating a partly unlanded via.** With only 0.040 µm of
  enclosure (via2.4),[^pdk-periph] the oxide beside a line etches on
  while the cap holds, and the trench exposes the aluminium sidewall
  to the liner and the tungsten precursor; the etch's over-etch and
  the liner's coverage are budgeted together (inference from the
  rule).
* **A via that tungsten will fill.** Residue, moisture or outgassing
  from the dielectric sidewall can stop the tungsten of
  {ref}`WDEP4 <step-132>` nucleating — the poisoned via of
  Kobayakawa et al. and Romero et al.[^kobayakawa-1991][^romero-1991]
  that a UMC patent addresses by densifying the exposed
  dielectric.[^pat-via-poison-umc] The 0.42 µm sidewall exposes more
  of the NILD4 stack, including whatever the PDK's "NILD4_C" (k 3.5,
  0.030 µm)[^pdk-04] is, than via 1 exposes of NILD3.

Without `VIM2E` the resist pattern of {ref}`VIM2 <step-129>` would be
stripped without effect and metal 3 would sit on unbroken oxide.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic via etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's
recipe is not public); the shared physics is set out at
{ref}`CTME <step-108>` and {ref}`VIME <step-119>`.

1. **Chamber.** A single-wafer dielectric etcher — medium-density
   capacitively coupled or high-density inductively coupled, with
   separate bias, helium backside cooling and optical-emission
   endpoint ({ref}`category-etch`); Lam's Exelan was launched for
   this generation in 2001.[^lam-exelan]
2. **BARC open.** The organic {term}`BARC` of {ref}`VIM2 <step-129>` is
   opened with a short O₂/N₂ or CF₄-containing step.
3. **Main oxide etch.** A polymerising fluorocarbon — C₄F₈, C₂F₆ or
   CHF₃ with argon and a little O₂ or CO — at tens of mTorr and high
   bias.[^oehrlein-1994b][^perry-2001][^standaert-1998] The 0.42 µm
   depth[^pdk-04] and 2.1:1 ratio put the etch where {term}`ARDE`
   between isolated vias and dense arrays,[^coburn-1989][^gottscho-1992]
   or its inverse,[^doemling-1996] must be characterised and the
   over-etch sized for the slowest via.
4. **Endpoint and over-etch.** Via layers expose little of the wafer,
   so the emission signal is weak — Wodecki describes endpoint on
   low-open-area dielectric etches[^wodecki-1999] — and the etch is
   run by time from the thickness the cap and polish left
   ({ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>`, {ref}`NCAPOX4 <step-128>`), with an
   over-etch sized to clear the deepest vias without consuming the
   TiW, whose fluorine-lean protection fails if the chemistry runs
   fluorine-rich.[^schaepkens-1999][^petri-1992]
5. **Strip and clean.** Oxygen or O₂/N₂ downstream {term}`ash` —
   Gasonics, Iridia or Mattson class in SkyWater's list[^skw-01] —
   then a solvent or semi-aqueous clean ("EKS265, EKC270 solvents" on
   SkyWater's wet-bench list,[^skw-01] EKC265/270-class; SkyWater
   writes "EKS265") to remove the fluorocarbon
   polymer and the fluoride residue from the via floor without
   attacking the TiW or the exposed aluminium of an unlanded via; no
   HF (inference).
6. **Queue time.** Limited between clean and {ref}`TIN4 <step-131>`,
   with a degas before the liner, against the moisture uptake of the
   open via (industry practice;[^txt-05] the poisoned-via literature
   is the reason[^kobayakawa-1991]).
7. **Metrology.** Top and bottom CD by {term}`CD-SEM`, profile and cap
   loss by cross-section SEM on monitors, via-2 chain resistance at
   {term}`e-test`; the PDK's 3.4 Ω `via2`[^pdk-08] is the number such
   chains give.

## Machines typically used

* **Dielectric etcher**, 200 mm single-wafer: Lam Exelan / 4520XLE,
  Applied Materials eMax / MxP+ / Super-e, TEL DRM / Unity
  ({ref}`category-etch`); high-density alternatives (AMAT IPS, Lam
  TCP 9100).
* **Resist asher** (Gasonics Aura, Mattson Aspen) and **wet bench or
  single-wafer clean** for post-etch residue.
* **CD-SEM**, **cross-section SEM**, **e-test** for via chains.

## Resources required

* **C₄F₈, C₂F₆, CHF₃, CF₄**, **Ar**, **O₂** (and possibly CO or N₂)
  for the oxide etch (industry practice;[^nojiri-2015] SkyWater lists
  CF₄, CHF₃, C₂F₆ and O₂ on its etchers[^skw-01]).
* **Helium** for backside cooling; **NF₃** or O₂-based chamber clean.
* **O₂/N₂** (and forming gas or CF₄) for the ash;[^skw-01] **amine or
  semi-aqueous solvent** (EKC265/270 class) and DI water for the
  post-etch clean.[^skw-01]
* **Chamber consumables**; **monitor wafers** with blanket oxide and
  TiW for rate and selectivity checks.

## Open questions

* The etch chemistry, chamber, endpoint scheme and over-etch of
  `VIM2E`, the TiW consumed, and the resulting profile are not
  public.
* Whether the deeper via-2 etch uses a different recipe from the
  via-1 etch, or the same recipe run longer, is not public.
* How a via that lands partly off a 0.14 µm metal-2 line is
  tolerated is inferred from the 0.040 µm enclosure rule.[^pdk-periph]
* Which etcher runs the step is not public; SkyWater's list names no
  dedicated dielectric etcher.[^skw-01]
* The step list used in this reference has no separate strip step
  after `VIM2E`; this page treats the resist strip and post-etch
  clean as part of the etch step.
