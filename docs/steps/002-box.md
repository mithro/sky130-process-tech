## What this step is

`BOX` grows the first film on the bare wafer: a thin thermal silicon
dioxide, usually called the *pad oxide* or *base oxide*, over the whole
surface. It is the bottom layer of the two-layer pad-oxide/nitride
stack that every shallow-trench-isolation ({term}`STI`) module starts with
(Wikipedia lists "Stack deposition (oxide + protective nitride)" as the
first STI step[^wiki-sti]). The oxide is sacrificial and never appears
in the finished device; whether it is removed straight after the
nitride strip at {ref}`NS19 <step-013>` or kept as the {term}`screen oxide` for
the first well implants is not stated in any public source (see
{ref}`NS19 <step-013>`).

Precisely: after the incoming clean, the wafers are oxidised in a
furnace in dry O₂ (or a dilute steam ambient) to a thickness of the
order of 10–20 nm. That figure is taken from a Cypress patent on the
embedded-SONOS flow, which describes "pad oxide 209 … silicon dioxide
(SiO₂) having a thickness of from about 10 nanometers (nm) to about
20 nm",[^pat-04] and from an AmberWave Systems STI patent (assigned to
TSMC in 2010) giving "Pad oxide 26 may have a thickness T4 of, e.g.,
50-200 Å".[^pat-sti-amberwave] No SkyWater document states the SKY130
pad-oxide thickness; those ranges are what the era's practice looked
like.

## Step category

`BOX` belongs to {ref}`Thermal oxidation <category-oxidation>`. What
distinguishes it from the later gate oxidations
({ref}`GOX100 <step-043>`, {ref}`LVGOX <step-047>`) is that its
electrical quality is almost irrelevant; what matters is a controlled,
uniform thickness and a clean interface, because the oxide's job is
mechanical and chemical rather than electrical.

## Why this step exists

The pad oxide performs four functions in the STI module:

1. **Stress buffer.** {term}`LPCVD` silicon nitride, deposited next at
   {ref}`ISONIT <step-003>`, is under high tensile stress; put directly
   on silicon it generates dislocations during the high-temperature
   steps that follow. A thin oxide between the two relieves that
   stress. This is the classic LOCOS-era reason for a pad oxide and it
   carries straight over to STI.[^txt-03][^txt-05]
2. **Etch stop and screen.** At {ref}`NS19 <step-013>` the nitride is
   removed in hot phosphoric acid, which attacks nitride at about
   100 Å/min but deposited oxide at only 0–25 Å/min;[^vgh-1967] the pad
   oxide is what stops the strip at the silicon surface. It also
   screens the silicon during the deep N-well implant
   ({ref}`DNI <step-008>`), which is performed while the pad-oxide/
   nitride stack is still in place.
3. **Adhesion and cleanliness.** It gives the nitride a surface to
   adhere to and keeps the silicon under the active areas covered from
   `BOX` until it is finally removed before gate oxidation (the exact
   point is not public).
4. **Corner engineering.** The pad-oxide thickness and any deliberate
   undercut of it before the liner oxidation influence how the top
   corner of the trench rounds. A Spansion STI patent, now held by
   Cypress's successor, describes removing "approximately 100-300 Å of
   the pad oxide 26 in the undercut" before liner oxidation for that
   reason.[^pat-sti-cr]

Without a pad oxide, the nitride would crack or generate defects, and
the nitride strip would have no stop.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic recipe for a 200 mm fab of the 130 nm era:

* **Pre-clean.** Standard RCA-type clean ({term}`SC-1`, dilute HF,
  {term}`SC-2`)[^wiki-rca] on a batch wet bench, then spin or IPA dry.
* **Furnace load.** Of the order of 100–150 wafers in a quartz boat in
  a vertical furnace; push into a tube idling at a few hundred °C
  below the process temperature in nitrogen.[^txt-07]
* **Ramp and oxidise.** Ramp to the oxidation temperature and switch to
  the oxidising ambient. Thermal oxidation of silicon "is usually
  performed at a temperature between 800 and 1200 °C".[^wiki-thox] For
  a pad oxide of 10–20 nm a *dry* oxidation (Si + O₂ → SiO₂) at about
  900 °C is typical, because dry oxide is denser and the thin target is
  easy to hit; some fabs use a short wet oxidation
  (Si + 2 H₂O → SiO₂ + 2 H₂) at lower temperature.[^wiki-thox] The
  growth rate follows the {term}`Deal–Grove model`.[^wiki-thox][^txt-01]
* **Silicon consumption.** "For every unit thickness of silicon
  consumed, 2.17 unit thicknesses of oxide will appear" and "46% of the
  oxide thickness will lie below the original surface",[^wiki-thox] so
  a 15 nm pad oxide consumes about 7 nm of silicon.
* **Anneal and unload.** A brief nitrogen anneal at temperature, ramp
  down, pull.
* **Metrology.** Thickness and uniformity by spectroscopic ellipsometry
  on monitor and product wafers.

SkyWater's own capability list describes its furnaces as offering
"wet oxidation to 1150C" and "dry oxidation to 1150C",[^skw-01] so both
ambients are available on site.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`Vertical batch diffusion furnace <machine-vertical-furnace-oxidation>`** with quartz tube, boat and
  O₂/H₂/N₂ gas panel; optionally a torch or pyrogenic steam generator
  for wet oxidation. Representative 200 mm-era models: the
  SVG/Thermco–ASML–Aviza AVP/RVP vertical furnaces, Kokusai (Hitachi
  Kokusai) DD-series, TEL Alpha-8 series, ASM A400.
* **{ref}`Batch wet bench <machine-wet-bench>`** for the pre-clean (SC-1/HF/SC-2).
* **Spectroscopic ellipsometer** for thickness.

  being listed only for these two benches.

## Resources required

* **Oxygen** (dry oxidation) and, if a wet step is used, **hydrogen**
  and oxygen for pyrogenic steam.[^wiki-thox]
* **Nitrogen** for purge, ramp and anneal.
* **SC-1 / SC-2 chemicals** (NH₄OH, HCl, H₂O₂) and **dilute HF** for the
  pre-clean.[^wiki-rca]
* **Quartz consumables** — tube, boat, baffles.
* **Monitor wafers** for thickness control.

SkyWater's filings name Air Products and Praxair (2021 S-1) and Linde and
Airgas (fiscal 2023 10-K) as gas suppliers[^sec-01][^sec-02] without tying
them to a step.
