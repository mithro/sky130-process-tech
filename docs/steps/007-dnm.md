## What this step is

`DNM` is the second mask step of the flow. It coats the wafer — which
at this point has open, unfilled isolation trenches and a nitride/pad-
oxide stack over the active areas — with a thick photoresist, exposes
it through the *deep N-well mask* and develops it, opening windows over
the regions that are to receive the high-energy phosphorus implant at
{ref}`DNI <step-008>`. The resist is removed at
{ref}`DNIS <step-009>`.

The PDK's mask table lists "Deep N-Well, DNM" as used in
SKY130.[^pdk-05] The drawn layer is `dnwell` (GDS 64:18, "Deep n-well
region") and the generated mask layer is `cdnm` (GDS 48:0, "Deep nwell
mask").[^pdk-06] The design rules make it a coarse layer: minimum deep
N-well width 3.000 µm (dnwell.2), minimum spacing between deep N-wells
6.300 µm (dnwell.3, which the PDK marks "Rule exempt inside UHVI"), and
the deep N-well "must be enclosed by nwell by
atleast" 0.400 µm (nwell.5), with a minimum enclosure of an N-well
*hole* by deep N-well of 1.030 µm outside the UHVI region
(nwell.6).[^pdk-periph] The last two rules describe the structure: a
buried N-type plate (`dnwell`) whose edges are tied up to the surface
by a ring of ordinary N-well, enclosing an island of P-well that is
thereby isolated from the p-substrate.

## Step category

`DNM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step. It is an *implant* mask rather than an *etch* mask: the resist
must be thick enough to stop MeV phosphorus ions, but the pattern is
coarse and the {term}`CD` tolerance loose. It is also the first layer that has
something to align to — the trenches cut at {ref}`STIE <step-006>`.

## Why this step exists

The deep N-well is an option that lets designers isolate a P-well from
the common p-substrate. The PDK device pages describe its uses
directly: the 1.8 V NMOS "can be made either with or without the DNW
under the p-well"[^pdk-07] (and its cross-section drawing labels a
"Deep N-well" under the "P-well"); the "20V isolated NMOS FET … is
built over a Deep N-Well. This permits the p-well to be isolated from
the substrate and permit 'high-side' usage (where the PW body is held
above ground)"; and "The NPN uses the deep n-well as the
collector".[^pdk-07] The WOSET 2020 paper on the open PDK lists "deep
n-well" among the process options.[^ann-15] RF NMOS devices are
required to be enclosed by deep N-well (dnwell.6),[^pdk-periph] because
a deep N-well suppresses substrate-noise coupling — the topic of
several published 130 nm-era studies.[^chew-2002][^yang-2005]

A Cypress {term}`SONOS` patent describing "a deep N-well 210 in the second
region 208 over or in which a high-voltage (HV) MOS transistor 214 …
will be formed"[^pat-04] suggests that in the Cypress lineage the deep
N-well was, first of all, a high-voltage-device feature.

## Why here? The placement of DNM/DNI/DNIS between trench etch and liner oxidation

In a textbook {term}`STI` flow the sequence is pad oxide → nitride → active
litho → nitride and trench etch → liner oxidation → fill → {term}`CMP` →
nitride strip, with all well implants afterwards.[^wiki-sti][^rev-01]
A Hynix triple-well patent, for example, implants its MeV phosphorus
deep N-well "after a field oxide layer 32 for isolation between
devices is formed … through a shallow trench isolation (STI)
process".[^pat-dnw-hynix] SKY130 instead inserts the deep N-well mask,
implant and strip *between* the trench etch and the liner oxidation.
No public document explains why. Our interpretation, stated as an
inference:

1. **{term}`Thermal budget <thermal budget>` for free.** A MeV phosphorus implant leaves a heavy
   damage band around its {term}`projected range` and needs a substantial
   high-temperature anneal to remove it and to spread the well
   laterally so that it merges with the N-well ring. The liner
   oxidation at {ref}`LINOX <step-010>` (900–1100 °C in comparable
   flows[^pat-sti-cr][^pat-sti-lattice]) and the subsequent oxidations
   are the hottest steps left in the flow. Placing the implant before
   them gives the deep N-well the longest possible drive without
   adding a dedicated anneal. Implants placed after the wells and
   before the gate oxide, by contrast, see only the pre-gate anneal
   ({ref}`RTAI <step-034>`).
2. **Alignment marks exist only after the trench etch.** On this
   reference's reading (see {ref}`FOM <step-004>`) `FOM` is the zero
   layer; the etched trenches are the first topography a
   {term}`stepper` can align to, and any earlier mask would need a
   separate zero-mark step.
3. **The implant can be done while the active silicon is still
   protected.** The nitride/pad-oxide stack is still in place over the
   active areas and the trench floors are bare; a MeV implant passes
   through both with only a small range shift, and the sacrificial
   films absorb any resist-related contamination before being
   stripped at {ref}`NS19 <step-013>`.
4. **Resist thickness.** A thick implant resist is easier to pattern
   on a wafer that has only ~0.3 µm of topography than on a wafer with
   the filled, polished STI plus later films; and because the deep
   N-well geometry is coarse (3 µm minimum width), the loss of
   resolution from a thick resist and the trench topography does not
   matter.

Placing the implant *before* the trench etch would give a flatter
implant surface but would need a zero-layer mark; placing it *after*
the STI module (the conventional order) would forgo the liner-
oxidation anneal. The SKY130 order is therefore a deliberate trade
that favours thermal budget and mark availability over surface
planarity. This is our reading, not a documented fact.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic recipe for a deep-well implant litho at a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab:

1. **Track preparation.** {term}`HMDS` prime; no {term}`BARC` is needed for a coarse
   implant layer, though one may be used for reflectivity control over
   the nitride.
2. **Thick resist coat.** Implant resists for MeV phosphorus are
   typically 2–3 µm thick; the stopping power of resist is lower than
   that of silicon, so the mask must exceed the ion range with margin
   (industry-typical[^txt-02]). (For comparison the PDK's generic
   "Photoresist thickness" is 1.14 µm,[^pdk-03] and its HV-tip implant
   resist is 0.3 µm — the deep-well resist would be at the thick end
   of the range.)
3. **Exposure.** i-line (365 nm) exposure is adequate for 3 µm
   features.[^wiki-litho] The relaxed rules mean the layer can be
   printed on an i-line stepper, freeing the KrF tools for critical
   layers — an inference consistent with SkyWater having both
   classes.[^skw-01]
4. **Develop, inspect.** TMAH develop; {term}`overlay` to the FOM trenches is
   checked on the overlay tool; CD is checked loosely.
5. **Optional resist hardening.** A UV cure or hard bake improves the
   resist's resistance to the implant heating and outgassing.

## Machines typically used

* **i-line stepper**, 200 mm (ASML PAS 5500/100–/275, Nikon NSR-2205i,
  Canon FPA-3000i); a KrF tool could equally print it.
* **Coat/develop track** with thick-resist capability.
* **Overlay metrology** tool (first use in the flow).

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML i-line stepper or i-line scanner.** SkyWater lists
  both.[^skw-01] Strength: strong for existence; the assignment of DNM
  to i-line is an inference from the 3 µm design rules.
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL Lithius Pro Z.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence.
* **Overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer.**[^skw-01] Strength: strong.

## Open questions

* The reason for placing the deep N-well between trench etch and liner
  oxidation is not documented anywhere public; the four arguments
  above are our inference.
* Whether DNM is printed on i-line or KrF, and its resist thickness,
  are inferred from the design rules.
* Whether every SKY130 wafer receives the DNM/DNI/DNIS steps or only
  lots with deep N-well content (the WOSET paper lists it as an
  option[^ann-15]) is unknown; a foundry MPW wafer carries every
  option.
