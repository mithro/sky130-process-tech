## What this step is

`MM3` is the metal-3 lithography. A resist is coated, exposed and
developed so that resist remains wherever the layout draws `met3`
(GDS 70:20, "Metal 3"[^pdk-06]) and is cleared everywhere else; the
exposed metal is removed at {ref}`MM3E <step-140>`. What lies under
the resist is unlike any earlier metal mask. The metal-3 stack of
{ref}`WTIAL3 <step-134>` has been blanket on the wafer for four steps
while the first {term}`MiM capacitor` was built on it: the dielectric of
{ref}`CAPILD <step-135>`, the plate film of {ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>`
(TiW, as assumed there), and
the plate pattern of {ref}`CAPM <step-137>` and
{ref}`CAPME <step-138>`. The resist is therefore printed on a
reflective metal sheet that carries, on our reading of the preceding
pages, a thin dielectric film and, here and there, plate islands
of the order of 0.1 µm high (inferred at {ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>`).
Every capacitor plate must end up inside a metal-3 shape: metal 3 is
the capacitor's bottom electrode, drawn as "M3 (plate 1)" under
"CAPM" in the PDK's `cap_mim` cross-section.[^pdk-07]

The PDK's mask table lists the `MM3` mask three times — "Metal 3-TLM",
"Metal 3-S8TM" and "Metal 3-PLM" — and flags only the PLM variant as
used in SKY130;[^pdk-05] the minimum-CD table gives the three variants
0.36 µm, 0.8 µm and 0.3 µm respectively as `MM3CD`/`MM3CDSP`.[^pdk-03]
The periphery rules for the flow described here give "Width of
metal 3" 0.300 µm (m3.1) and "Spacing of metal 3 to metal 3"
0.300 µm (m3.2) — a 0.6 µm pitch, a little over twice the 0.28 µm
of metals 1 and 2 —
with 0.400 µm to or from "huge_met3" features (m3.3c, m3.3d), a
minimum area of 0.240 µm² (m3.6), an enclosure of the via-2 plugs
beneath of 0.065 µm (m3.4), a minimum oxide {term}`pattern density` of 0.7
(m3.pd.1) checked in 700 µm windows stepped by 70 µm, and a `cmm3
waffleDrop` fill check; the via-3 plugs that will later land on metal
3 must be enclosed by it by 0.060 µm (via3.4) and by 0.090 µm on one
of two adjacent sides (via3.5).[^pdk-periph] The metal being
patterned is thicker than below — 0.845 µm on the PDK's stack
diagram[^pdk-04] against 0.36 µm for metals 1 and 2 — and its sheet
resistance is 47 mΩ/sq (limits 38–56 mΩ/sq).[^pdk-07][^pdk-08]

## Step category

`MM3` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *line-and-space* type printed on metal, like
{ref}`MM1 <step-113>` and {ref}`MM2 <step-124>`, which set out the
imaging and reflectivity arguments at length. It is, however, no
longer a critical layer in the resolution sense. At 0.30 µm on a
248 nm tool of {term}`NA` 0.70 (ASML's PAS 5500/750E[^asml-750e])
the process factor {term}`k₁ <k1>` is {math}`k_1 = 0.30 \times 0.70 / 0.248 \approx 0.85`, and even on an
i-line tool of NA 0.6, {math}`k_1 = 0.30 \times 0.60 / 0.365 \approx 0.49`,
above the production boundary the category page quotes[^wiki-litho]
(our arithmetic). SkyWater lists both "ASML I-line" and "ASML DUV"
steppers and scanners;[^skw-01] which prints metal 3 is not public,
and either is plausible for a 0.3 µm line (inference; the ITRS 2001
lithography chapter assigns the cheaper exposure tools to the
non-critical layers of a generation[^itrs-03]).

What is specific to this instance is the substrate. Like
{ref}`MM4 <step-154>`, it is printed over a partly built device: across the
wafer the resist sees TiW-capped aluminium under a few tens of
nanometres of capacitor dielectric, and over each capacitor a plate
on that dielectric — two different thin-film stacks with different
reflectivities (Brunner's {term}`swing-curve <swing curve>` analysis[^brunner-1991]) — and
a plate edge a fraction of a micrometre high. The metal-3 stack
itself is also thicker, so the {ref}`MM3E <step-140>` etch needs more
resist to survive it (inference from the stack thickness; see
*How it is typically performed*).

## Why this step exists

Metal 3 is the flow's first coarse-pitch, low-resistance routing
level and, in the same pattern, the bottom plate of every `capm`
capacitor. The mask fixes:

* **Line width and resistance.** At 0.3 µm width and 47 mΩ/sq[^pdk-07]
  a metal-3 wire has less than two-fifths the resistance per square
  of metals 1 and 2 (125 mΩ/sq[^pdk-08]); Bohr's argument that
  interconnect limits performance[^bohr-1995] and Stamper, Fuselier
  and Tian's account of wiring RC in multilevel aluminium[^stamper-1998]
  are why an aluminium back end puts its thick, wide metals on top.
  The space sets the gap {ref}`NILD5 <step-141>` must fill.
* **The bottom plate of the MiM capacitor.** The plate edge printed
  at {ref}`CAPM <step-137>` must lie inside a metal-3 shape, and the
  PDK's `capm` rule set includes a minimum enclosure of the top plate
  by the metal beneath (rule text: met2) and a minimum spacing of
  bottom plate to bottom plate (capm.3, capm.2b; values not
  published).[^pdk-periph]
  The overlay of this mask to the `capm` pattern therefore enters a
  device rule, not only a wiring rule. In the top-plate-first
  construction of the Newport Fab patent the bottom plate is likewise
  patterned from the interconnect metal *after* the top plate is
  defined,[^pat-mim-newportfab] and Ng et al. review the integration issues of MiM
  capacitors in Al–Cu and copper back ends.[^ng-2005]
* **Overlay to two earlier layers.** The metal must enclose the
  via-2 plugs by 0.065 µm (m3.4)[^pdk-periph] and the capacitor plates
  by the unpublished capm.3 value (rule text: met2); metal 3 is printed after a
  non-wiring mask ({ref}`CAPM <step-137>`), so the alignment tree —
  whether `MM3` aligns to {ref}`VIM2 <step-129>` targets under the
  metal, to the `capm` layer, or to both — decides which enclosure
  carries the larger error (inference; Levinson treats overlay
  budgets[^levinson-2005]).
* **Reflectivity control on a mixed substrate.** The TiW cap is a
  partial anti-reflective layer — Rocke and Schneegans documented
  that role for a refractory cap on aluminium[^rocke-1988] — but the
  thin capacitor dielectric over it and the plates change the reflectance
  locally; an organic {term}`BARC` spun over the whole surface
  levels the optical conditions and planarises the plate steps
  (industry practice;[^brunner-1991][^mack-2007] SKY130's scheme is
  not public).
* **Fill for the polish.** The `cmm3` waffle-drop fill and the 0.7
  minimum oxide pattern density[^pdk-periph] exist for
  {ref}`CMPM3 <step-142>`; the assumptions table gives metals 3 and 4
  an {term}`"Oxide Bias" <oxide bias>` of 1.15 against 0.6 for metals 1 and 2,[^pdk-03]
  the public trace of a different fill or polish rule for the thicker
  levels (inference).

Without `MM3` there would be no metal-3 wiring and no bottom plates;
metal 3 would remain a sheet shorting every via-2 plug and every
capacitor together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic metal-line lithography sequence for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's is not public); {ref}`MM1 <step-113>`
describes the shared steps at greater length.

1. **Surface preparation.** The wafer comes from the
   {ref}`CAPME <step-138>` strip and clean; a dehydration bake, then
   an organic {term}`BARC` spun to a thickness chosen from the
   swing-curve minimum over the mixed plate/dielectric
   surface.[^brunner-1991][^wiki-arc] A spin-on BARC also partly
   planarises the plate edges (industry practice[^mack-2007]).
2. **Resist coat.** A positive resist — chemically amplified for
   248 nm[^ito-2005][^wallraff-1999] or DNQ/novolak for
   i-line[^dammel-1993] — thicker than at metals 1 and 2, because the
   0.845 µm stack[^pdk-04] must be etched through before the resist
   is consumed; aluminium etches in Cl₂/BCl₃ only one to a few times
   faster than resist (industry-typical[^nojiri-2015]). The PDK's
   1.14 µm "Photoresist thickness"[^pdk-03] is an antenna-rule
   assumption and need not describe this layer.
3. **Exposure.** KrF or i-line with conventional or mild off-axis
   illumination; at {math}`k_1` 0.5–0.85 no phase shifting is needed,
   and {term}`OPC`, if used, is modest (inference from the
   geometry[^itrs-03][^wong-2001]). The metal-3 `cmm3` fill[^pdk-periph]
   is on the reticle.
4. **Post-exposure bake and develop.** {term}`PEB`, 0.26 N TMAH
   develop, rinse and dry.[^txt-02]
5. **Alignment.** To marks under the opaque metal stack — the via-2
   level or the metal-2 targets it was aligned to — read through the
   topography the plugs print into the sputtered film, and, on our
   reading, checked against the `capm` pattern, whose TiW edges are
   visible on the surface (industry practice[^levinson-2005]; SKY130's
   alignment tree is not public).
6. **Inspection and metrology.** Line {term}`CD` by {term}`CD-SEM`;
   {term}`overlay` to via 2 and to `capm` on the KLA 5200/5300/Archer
   class;[^skw-01] after-develop inspection for bridging and for resist
   defects over the plates; dose and focus tracked by Ausschnitt's
   method.[^ausschnitt-1999] Rework is possible, but a solvent or ash
   rework now also exposes the capacitor dielectric and the plates
   (inference).

## Machines typically used

* **KrF step-and-scan or stepper** (ASML PAS 5500/750E or
  /800,[^asml-750e][^asml-800] Nikon NSR-S204B[^nikon-s204b]) or an
  **i-line stepper** (ASML PAS 5500 i-line models[^asml-30]), 200 mm
  ({ref}`category-lithography`).
* **Coat/develop track** with BARC and PEB modules (TEL
  ACT/Lithius,[^tel-act] DNS, Sokudo).
* **CD-SEM**, **overlay tool**, **after-develop inspection**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML I-line or DUV stepper / scanner.** SkyWater lists "ASML
  I-line stepper", "ASML I-line scanner", "ASML DUV stepper" and "ASML
  DUV scanner".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for existence; which
  class prints the 0.3 µm metal-3 lines is **not public** and either
  is plausible from the {math}`k_1` arithmetic above.
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL Lithius Pro Z.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence.
* **CD — AMAT Verity, VeraSEM; overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong.

## Resources required

* **Positive photoresist** (248 nm chemically amplified or i-line
  DNQ/novolak); SkyWater's S-1 names Dow, JSR and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo as
  photoresist suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **Organic BARC**, **0.26 N TMAH developer** (Air Products per the
  S-1[^sec-01]), edge-bead remover, rinse solvents, DI water and
  nitrogen.[^txt-02]
* **The MM3 reticle** (the PLM variant flagged for SKY130[^pdk-05]),
  generated from the drawn `met3` layer (70:20) with its `cmm3`
  fill.[^pdk-06][^pdk-periph]
* **Exposure-tool consumables** (KrF laser gas or mercury lamps).

## Open questions

* Whether `MM3` is exposed on an i-line or a DUV tool, with what
  resist, BARC and reticle type, is not public.
* The alignment tree — to via 2, to `capm`, or both — and the values
  of the `capm` enclosure and spacing rules[^pdk-periph] are not
  public.
* The PDK does not define "TLM", "S8TM" or "PLM" in the mask table's
  three `MM3` entries.[^pdk-05] That "S8TM" is a 2 µm thick-metal-3
  option follows from the assumptions table's 2 µm "S8TM* flow"
  thickness and 0.8 µm `MM3CD`[^pdk-03] (inference). The mask table
  flags the "-PLM" entries as used in SKY130[^pdk-05] and the rule
  tables name an "SP8P*/SKY130P* (PLM) CADflow";[^pdk-periph] this
  reference reads PLM as the label of the five-metal P flow (inference).
* Whether the metal under the resist is 0.845 µm thick as the PDK's
  diagram shows[^pdk-04] or the 0.765 µm of the Cypress S8TNV
  report[^cyp-qtp-113005] is discussed as an open question at
  {ref}`MM3E <step-140>`.
* Why the "Oxide Bias" rises to 1.15 for metals 3 and 4[^pdk-03] is
  not public.
