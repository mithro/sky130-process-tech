## What this step is

`NWI` is a high-energy N-type implant — phosphorus, we infer on range grounds — through the resist windows
opened at {ref}`NWM <step-017>`. It forms the main body of the N-well:
a region about a micrometre deep whose doping peaks well below the
surface and falls off towards it — a *retrograde* well. The PDK's
process assumptions describe the result: "n-well peak concentration"
6.0 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³, a "background concentration" of 8.0 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³ and
a baseline N-well vertical dimension of 1.1 µm (PDK-03). The second
implant of the pair, {ref}`NWI2 <step-019>`, follows immediately
through the same resist, and the P-channel threshold implant
{ref}`LVTPI <step-020>` after that.

The implant passes through the pad oxide over the active areas and
through the field oxide over the trenches; at these energies the
difference in range is small compared with the well depth, so the
well is essentially continuous under active and field alike (TXT-01,
ch. 8).

## Step category

`NWI` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*well* class: phosphorus at hundreds of keV to about 1 MeV and a dose
of order 10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻² from a high-energy implanter (category page).
It is the second MeV-class implant of the flow after
{ref}`DNI <step-008>`, and shallower than it.

## Why this step exists

Every PMOS transistor in SKY130 sits in this well (WIKI-CMOS;
PDK-07), and the 16 V extended-drain NMOS use lightly doped N-well as their drain extension (PDK-HV; PAT-DEMOS-TI) — we infer the same for the 20 V family, whose HV methodology is not separately documented. Beyond simply
being N-type, the well's *profile* is engineered:

* **Latch-up.** The parasitic thyristor of CMOS runs through "the
  source of the NMOS, the p-substrate, the n-well and the source of
  the PMOS" (WIKI-LATCHUP). A heavily doped buried layer gives "a low
  resistance path and suppress[es] parasitic bipolar gain for latchup
  protection" (HOOK-2003), which is how a bulk process without an
  epitaxial layer (SKW-02) meets its 0.23 µm latch-up spacing
  (PDK-03).
* **Punch-through and short-channel control.** A doping peak beneath
  the channel stops the drain depletion region from reaching the
  source without raising the surface doping that sets the threshold;
  ITRS 2001 notes that "the retrograde well profile must be less than
  0.5 times the drain extension depth to improve short channel
  effects" (ITRS-01). The IBM well patent puts it plainly: "The
  concentration of dopant is higher at the bottom of the well and
  lower at the upper surface of the well. This doping profile helps
  prevent latch up by raising the threshold voltage" (PAT-WELL-IBM).
* **Well-to-well isolation.** The PDK's assumptions list an
  "n-well - n-well" punch-through spacing of 0.835 µm for 3.3 V
  (PDK-03); the buried peak sets this.

Retrograde wells by MeV implantation replaced diffused wells in the
0.35–0.18 µm generations precisely because they need no long
high-temperature drive-in and keep the surface lightly doped
(MORRIS-2000; BORLAND-1998). Without `NWI` there would be no PMOS
body and no isolation ring for the deep N-well.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic retrograde N-well implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab. SKY130's values are not public; the figures are typical or from
public patents.

* **Species.** Phosphorus (³¹P⁺) from phosphine (WIKI-IMPLANT).
  Phosphorus is preferred over arsenic for wells because its range at
  a given energy is much longer (category page). Where the implanter
  cannot reach the energy singly charged, doubly charged P⁺⁺ is used —
  a UMC patent implants "double charge … P++ … at an energy within a
  range from about 380 keV to about 400 keV" for a retrograde well
  (PAT-UMC-DC).
* **Energy.** Set by the wanted peak depth. Published retrograde
  N-wells of the 0.25–0.13 µm era: 500, 275 and 130 keV phosphorus in
  a Harris twin-well flow (PAT-TWIN-HARRIS); 700 keV at 2.0 × 10¹³
  cm⁻² plus 120 keV at 2.0 × 10¹² cm⁻² in a Hynix flow
  (PAT-WELL-HYNIX); 850 keV at 5.2 × 10¹³ cm⁻², 550 keV at
  1.25 × 10¹² cm⁻² and 50 keV at 5 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² at IBM (PAT-WELL-IBM);
  a "middle n-well" at "about 500 keV to about 600 keV" and
  5 × 10¹²–2 × 10¹³ cm⁻² in a Hynix triple-well (PAT-DNW-HYNIX). For a
  1.1 µm well depth (PDK-03) the deepest `NWI` energy is plausibly in
  the 500 keV–1 MeV band (inference from range tables, TXT-01, ch. 8).
* **Dose.** 10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻² per energy (the patents above), giving a
  peak of a few 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ — consistent with the PDK's 6 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³
  (PDK-03).
* **Tilt.** Either the conventional 7° with twist to avoid channelling
  (WIKI-IMPLANT; TXT-02) or 0° to use channelling for depth; the
  well-edge scattering that tilt causes is the origin of the well
  proximity effect (HOOK-2003).
* **Wafer handling.** Batch spinning-disc end station; the long
  implant heats the resist, so disc cooling and a hard-baked resist
  matter (category page).
* **Anneal.** At {ref}`RTAI <step-034>`; a Cypress patent on the
  SONOS/logic integration states that "a rapid thermal anneal is
  performed after implanting both the n-well and p-well" (PAT-03).
* **Monitoring.** Sheet resistance of monitor wafers after a monitor
  anneal; thermal-wave damage on product.

## Machines typically used

* **High-energy ion implanter**, 200 mm, batch end station: Axcelis
  (Eaton) GSD/HE ("10 stage LINAC with energies up to 3 MeV",
  AXCELIS-GSD-PAGE), Varian VIISta 3000, Genus/AMAT Quantum, Nissin
  EXCEED-HE (TXT-09; category page).
* A **medium-current** tool with doubly charged ions can reach the
  lower end of the range (PAT-UMC-DC).
* **Four-point probe** and **thermal-wave** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD high-current/high-energy implanter.** SKW-01 lists
  "Axcelis GSD High current/energy B11, BF2, P, As, 10-3000kev, 1e11
  to 5e15, tilt/twist" — phosphorus, the full energy band and the
  dose window of a well implant. Strength: **strong** for the tool;
  assignment to `NWI` is an **inference** from its being the only
  listed tool above 750 keV.
* **Axcelis 8250 medium-current** (SKW-01) is listed without phosphorus (its species list is "B11, BF2, As") and is limited in energy, so
  it is unlikely for this step. Strength: strong for existence.
* Unverified job-board snippets mention "Axcelis GSD300E" and
  "Purion" implanters (public-sources §4); weak.

## Open questions

* The SKY130 N-well energies and doses are not public; only the
  resulting peak concentration and depth are (PDK-03).
* Whether `NWI` is the deep or the shallow member of the pair, and
  whether doubly charged ions are used, is unknown.
* Tilt angle (0° channelled versus 7°) is not public.
* The species (phosphorus rather than arsenic) is inferred from the required range, not stated publicly.
