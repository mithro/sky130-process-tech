## What this step is

`FOM` is the first photolithography step of the flow. It coats the
nitride-covered wafer with photoresist, exposes it through the *field
oxide mask* and develops it, leaving resist over the future active
(transistor and tap) areas and opening it over the future field
(isolation) areas. The pattern is transferred into the nitride at
{ref}`STINITE <step-005>` and into the silicon at
{ref}`STIE <step-006>`.

The PDK's mask table lists "Field Oxide, FOM" as a mask used in SKY130
(PDK-05). In layout terms the mask is derived from the drawn active
layers: `diff` (GDS 65:20, "Active (diffusion) area (type opposite of
well/substrate underneath)") and `tap` (GDS 65:44, "Active (diffusion)
area (type equal to the well/substrate underneath) (i.e., N+ and P+)")
(PDK-06). The generated mask layer itself is `cfom` (GDS 22:20 drawing,
with 22:21 "mask add", 22:22 "mask drop" and 22:24 "waffle drop"
purposes), and `fom` 22:23 is the dummy/waffle layer (PDK-06). The
"waffle" purposes exist because large field regions are filled with
dummy active squares — the layers page defines "fom_waffles" of
0.5 × 0.5, 1.5 × 1.5, 2.5 × 2.5 and 4.08 × 4.08 µm (PDK-06) — to keep
the CMP at {ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>` uniform. Because no other pattern
exists on the wafer yet, `FOM` also prints the alignment marks that
every later mask registers to.

## Step category

`FOM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step. Compared with the other 30-odd mask steps of SKY130 it is unusual
in three ways: it is the *zero* layer (nothing to align to), it prints
on a nitride/oxide stack rather than on resist-friendly oxide or
poly, and it defines the tightest *space* rule of the front end.

## Why this step exists

Every transistor needs an island of silicon surrounded by insulator.
`FOM` draws those islands. The minimum drawn active width in SKY130 is
0.150 µm and the minimum active-to-active spacing (which becomes the
minimum trench width) is 0.270 µm (PDK-PERIPH, rules difftap.1 and
difftap.3). Getting these right sets transistor width, source/drain
area and hence capacitance, and the isolation leakage between
neighbours. The mask also determines where the tap contacts to the
wells sit, and it contains the fill "waffles" that keep the field-oxide
polish flat.

A wafer without this step would have no isolation and could carry no
circuit; a mis-sized or mis-aligned `FOM` propagates into every device
parameter.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic litho sequence for an active layer at a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab:

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake and HMDS vapour prime on
   the coat/develop track.
2. **Anti-reflective coating.** Nitride on oxide on silicon is a
   strongly reflective, interference-prone stack at 248 nm, so an
   organic bottom anti-reflective coating (BARC) is spun on first; a
   BARC "may be applied before the photoresist is applied, to avoid
   reflections from occurring under the photoresist" (WIKI-LITHO). Some
   fabs use a thin oxide or oxynitride cap on the nitride instead.
3. **Resist coat.** Chemically amplified DUV resist, soft-baked. The
   PDK's own assumptions table gives a "Photoresist thickness" of
   1.14 µm (PDK-03), which is a plausible thickness for the etch layers
   of this generation (the same table gives 0.3 µm for HV tip implants).
4. **Exposure.** Step-and-repeat or step-and-scan exposure through the
   FOM reticle. The wavelength question is discussed below.
5. **Post-exposure bake, develop** in aqueous TMAH developer, rinse,
   hard bake.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** CD-SEM measurement of the printed
   active width/space, and — since this is the first layer — overlay
   is measured only from the second layer onwards.

**Which wavelength?** Wikipedia gives the mercury i-line at 365 nm and
the KrF excimer line at 248 nm (WIKI-LITHO), and resolution scales as
CD = k₁·λ/NA. A 0.15 µm line/0.27 µm space at 365 nm with a 0.6 NA
i-line lens would need k₁ ≈ 0.25, below what production i-line could
achieve; at 248 nm with NA 0.6–0.7 (the lens range of 130 nm-era KrF
tools, TXT-05) the same feature needs k₁ ≈ 0.4, comfortable for KrF. The 2001 ITRS likewise places 248 nm on the
critical layers of the 130 nm node (ITRS-03). We therefore infer that
`FOM` is a **248 nm (KrF) layer** in SKY130, even though the active
layer is often described as "non-critical" relative to poly and
contact: it is less critical than gate (no 0.13 µm lines), but its
0.27 µm space is far beyond i-line. The relaxed masks of the isolation
module — {ref}`DNM <step-007>` with a 3 µm minimum width — are the
ones that can go on the i-line tools.

**Mask polarity.** Whether the FOM reticle is clear-field with resist
protecting active, or the reverse with a negative-tone resist, is not
public; the name "field oxide mask" describes what is *created*, not
the reticle tone.

## Machines typically used

* **DUV (KrF, 248 nm) stepper or scanner**, 200 mm. Representative
  130 nm-era models: ASML PAS 5500/300–/750 series, Nikon NSR-S204/S205,
  Canon FPA-3000EX4/EX5.
* **i-line stepper** as the fallback if the layer were relaxed
  (ASML PAS 5500/100–/275, Nikon NSR-2205i, Canon FPA-3000i).
* **Coat/develop track** linked to the exposure tool (TEL, DNS/SCREEN,
  SVG/ASML, Sokudo).
* **CD-SEM** and **overlay metrology** tools.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML DUV stepper / DUV scanner.** SKW-01 lists "ASML DUV stepper"
  and "ASML DUV scanner" alongside "ASML I-line stepper" and "ASML
  I-line scanner". Strength: strong for the vendor and wavelength
  classes; **inference** for the assignment of FOM to the DUV tools
  (based on the design-rule argument above, not on any SkyWater
  statement).
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ/Lithius** (SKW-01).
  Strength: strong; which track serves which exposure tool is not
  public.
* **CD metrology — AMAT Verity CD-SEM, VeraSEM; overlay — KLA
  5200/5300/Archer** (SKW-01). Strength: strong.

## Resources required

* **Photoresist** — chemically amplified KrF resist (or i-line
  novolac resist if the layer were relaxed). SkyWater's S-1 names
  The Dow Chemical Company, JSR Corporation and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo
  America as photoresist suppliers (SEC-01).
* **BARC** — organic bottom anti-reflective coating.
* **HMDS** adhesion promoter.
* **Developer** — aqueous TMAH (2.38 %) is the industry standard
  (TXT-02).
* **Edge-bead remover / rinse solvents**, DI water.
* **The FOM reticle** — a chrome-on-quartz photomask, with OPC as
  required; the 0.13 µm STI paper cited below reports "re-designs of
  the STI layout with Optical Proximity Correction (OPC) tagging"
  being needed at this node (THUNG-2016).
