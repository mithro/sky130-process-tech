## What this step is

`FOM` is the first photolithography step of the flow. It coats the
nitride-covered wafer with photoresist, exposes it through the *field
oxide mask* and develops it, leaving resist over the future active
(transistor and tap) areas and opening it over the future field
(isolation) areas. The pattern is transferred into the nitride at
{ref}`STINITE <step-005>` and into the silicon at
{ref}`STIE <step-006>`.

The PDK's mask table lists "Field Oxide, FOM" as a mask used in
SKY130.[^pdk-05] The mask-level layer is `cfom` "mask" 23:0, described
as "Field oxide mask";[^pdk-06] in layout terms we read the mask as
derived from the drawn active layers — `diff` (GDS 65:20, "Active
(diffusion) area (type opposite of well/substrate underneath)") and
`tap` (GDS 65:44, "Active (diffusion) area (type equal to the
well/substrate underneath) (i.e., N+ and P+)")[^pdk-06] — an inference:
the PDK names the layers but does not publish the mask-generation
expression. The drawn-side purposes are `cfom` 22:20 drawing, 22:21
"mask add", 22:22 "mask drop" and 22:24 "waffle drop", and `fom` 22:23
is the dummy/waffle layer.[^pdk-06] The "waffle" purposes exist because
large field regions
are filled with dummy active squares — the layers page defines
"fom_waffles" of 0.5 × 0.5, 1.5 × 1.5, 2.5 × 2.5 and
4.08 × 4.08 µm[^pdk-06] — to keep the {term}`CMP` at {ref}`CMPNIT <step-012>`
uniform. Because no other pattern exists on the wafer yet in the flow
described here, this reference takes `FOM` to also print the alignment
marks that every later mask registers to (a separate zero-layer step
would also be consistent with industry practice; see open questions).

## Step category

`FOM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step. Compared with the other 35 mask steps of SKY130 it is unusual
in three ways: it is the *zero* layer (nothing to align to), it prints
on a nitride/oxide stack rather than on resist-friendly oxide or
poly, and it sets the minimum isolation width (difftap.3, 0.270 µm;
poly.2 at 0.210 µm and licon.2 at 0.170 µm are tighter
spaces[^pdk-periph]).

## Why this step exists

Every transistor needs an island of silicon surrounded by insulator.
`FOM` draws those islands. The minimum drawn active width in SKY130 is
0.150 µm and the minimum active-to-active spacing (which becomes the
minimum trench width) is 0.270 µm (rules difftap.1 and
difftap.3).[^pdk-periph] The PDK's assumptions table gives the mask's
own minimum CD and space as `FOMCD` 0.14 µm and `FOMCDSP` 0.27 µm,[^pdk-03]
slightly tighter than the drawn rule. Getting these right sets transistor width,
source/drain area and hence capacitance, and the isolation leakage
between neighbours. The mask also determines where the tap contacts to
the wells sit, and it contains the fill "waffles" that keep the
field-oxide polish flat.

A wafer without this step would have no isolation and could carry no
circuit; a mis-sized or mis-aligned `FOM` propagates into every device
parameter.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic litho sequence for an active layer at a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab:

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake and {term}`HMDS` vapour prime on
   the coat/develop track.
2. **Anti-reflective coating.** Nitride on oxide on silicon is a
   strongly reflective, interference-prone stack at 248 nm, so an
   organic bottom anti-reflective coating ({term}`BARC`) is spun on first; a
   BARC "may be applied before the photoresist is applied, to avoid
   reflections from occurring under the photoresist";[^wiki-litho] the
   article ties BARC to "45 nm and below", but organic BARCs were
   already standard for 248 nm imaging on reflective stacks at this
   node.[^txt-05] Some fabs use a thin oxide or {term}`oxynitride` cap
   on the nitride instead.
3. **Resist coat.** Chemically amplified {term}`DUV` resist, soft-baked. The
   PDK's own assumptions table gives a "Photoresist thickness" of
   1.14 µm,[^pdk-03] which is a plausible thickness for the etch layers
   of this generation (the same table gives 0.3 µm for HV tip implants).
4. **Exposure.** Step-and-repeat or step-and-scan exposure through the
   FOM {term}`reticle`. The wavelength question is discussed below.
5. **Post-exposure bake, develop** in aqueous TMAH developer, rinse,
   hard bake.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** {term}`CD-SEM` measurement of the printed
   active width/space, and — since this is the first layer — {term}`overlay`
   is measured only from the second layer onwards.

**Which wavelength?** Wikipedia gives the mercury i-line at 365 nm and
the KrF excimer line at 248 nm,[^wiki-litho] and resolution scales as
{term}`CD` = {term}`k₁ <k1>`·λ/{term}`NA`. A 0.15 µm line/0.27 µm space at 365 nm with a 0.6 NA
i-line lens would need k₁ ≈ 0.25, below what production i-line could
achieve; at 248 nm with NA 0.6–0.7 (the lens range of 130 nm-era KrF
tools[^txt-05]) the same feature needs k₁ ≈ 0.4, comfortable for KrF.
ITRS 2001 likewise lists "248 nm + PSM" (with 193 nm) as the
exposure options for the 130 nm node.[^itrs-03] We therefore infer that `FOM` is a **248 nm
(KrF) layer** in SKY130, even though the active layer is often
described as "non-critical" relative to poly and contact: it is less
critical than gate (no 0.13 µm lines), but its 0.15 µm minimum width
(k₁ ≈ 0.25 at i-line) is beyond production i-line, even though the
0.27 µm space alone (k₁ ≈ 0.44 at NA 0.6) would not be. The relaxed
masks of the isolation module — {ref}`DNM <step-007>` with a 3 µm
minimum width — are the ones that can go on the i-line tools.

**Mask polarity.** Whether the FOM reticle is clear-field with resist
protecting active, or the reverse with a negative-tone resist, is not
public; the name "field oxide mask" describes what is *created*, not
the reticle tone.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`DUV (KrF, 248 nm) <machine-duv-krf-stepper>` {term}`stepper` or scanner**, 200 mm. Representative
  130 nm-era models: ASML PAS 5500/300–/750 series, Nikon NSR-S204/S205,
  Canon FPA-3000EX4/EX5.
* **{ref}`i-line stepper <machine-i-line-stepper>`** as the fallback if the layer were relaxed
  (ASML PAS 5500/100–/275, Nikon NSR-2205i, Canon FPA-3000i).
* **{ref}`Coat/develop track <machine-coat-develop-track>`** linked to the exposure tool (TEL, DNS/SCREEN,
  SVG/ASML, Sokudo).
* **{ref}`CD-SEM <machine-cd-sem-overlay-metrology>`** and **overlay metrology** tools.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML DUV stepper / DUV scanner.** SkyWater lists "ASML DUV
  stepper" and "ASML DUV scanner" alongside "ASML I-line stepper" and
  "ASML I-line scanner".[^skw-01] Strength: strong for the vendor and
  wavelength classes; **inference** for the assignment of FOM to the
  DUV tools (based on the design-rule argument above, not on any
  SkyWater statement).
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ Lithius.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong; which track serves which exposure tool is not
  public.
* **CD metrology — AMAT Verity CD-SEM, VeraSEM; overlay — KLA
  5200/5300/Archer.**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence (SkyWater statement); use at this mask is an inference.

## Resources required

* **Photoresist** ({ref}`lithography materials <material-lithography-materials>`) — chemically amplified KrF resist (or i-line
  novolac resist if the layer were relaxed). SkyWater's 2021 S-1 names
  The Dow Chemical Company, JSR Corporation and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo
  America as photoresist suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **BARC** — organic bottom anti-reflective coating.
* **HMDS** adhesion promoter.
* **Developer** — aqueous 2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH is the industry
  standard.[^txt-02]
* **Edge-bead remover / rinse solvents**, {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>`.
* **The FOM reticle** — a chrome-on-quartz photomask, with {term}`OPC` as
  required; the 0.13 µm {term}`STI` paper cited below reports "re-designs of
  the STI layout with Optical Proximity Correction (OPC) tagging"
  being needed at this node.[^thung-2016]

## Open questions

* Whether FOM is exposed on the KrF or the i-line tools at SkyWater is
  an inference from the design rules; no public statement assigns
  layers to tools.
* Reticle tone, resist type and whether a BARC or an inorganic
  anti-reflective cap is used are not public.
* This page assumes the zero-layer alignment marks are formed in FOM
  itself; a separate zero-mark step would also be consistent with
  industry practice.
