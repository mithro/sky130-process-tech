## What this step is

`TIAL6` deposits the metal-1 film stack. Onto the polished oxide and
tungsten plugs left by {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` a sputtering cluster
tool lays down — in one vacuum sequence, on our reading — three
films: a thin titanium bottom layer, the aluminium–copper alloy that
carries the current, and a thin titanium–tungsten cap. The stack is
blanket; it becomes wiring only when {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` and
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` pattern and etch it into the `met1` layer
(GDS 68:20, "Metal 1").[^pdk-06] This reference describes the stack,
bottom to top, as titanium, aluminium–copper and TiW, on the public
sources below, and reads the titanium as collimated (inference; see
below).

Unusually for this flow, the composition of the stack has a public
source. Cypress qualification reports for products made in the
Bloomington fab on the S8 process — the process that became
SKY130[^pdk-02] — describe the metallisation. A 2013 report for a
64 K serial nvSRAM family on "S8TNV-5R" technology at "Cypress
Semiconductor — Bloomington, MN", design rule "0.13 µm", gives
"Metal 1: 100A Ti / 3200A Al-0.5%Cu / 300A TiW", the same for
metal 2, and "Metal 3: 150A Ti / 7200A Al-0.5%Cu / 300A
TiW".[^cyp-qtp-113005] The three films sum to 3 600 Å, exactly the
0.36 µm that the PDK's process stack diagram labels for
`met1`[^pdk-04] and close to the 0.35 µm "Metal 1 thickness for
antenna ratio calculations" of its assumptions table.[^pdk-03] A
second Cypress report, from 2014, records the qualification of a
"Metal Stack Change from Ti/AlCu/TiW to Ti/TiN/AlCu/Ti/TiN in CMI
Fab 4" for the S8DI, S8TNV and S8P technologies (excluding top
metal), with the new metal 1 as "150A Ti/250A TiN/3200A Al 0.5%
Cu/90A Ti/500A TiN".[^cyp-qtp-123907] Two older Fab 4 reports show
the lineage of the sandwich: a 0.18 µm derivative with "150Å Ti /
4,200Å Al / 300Å TiW"[^cyp-qtp-014807] and a 0.42 µm process with
"TiW, AlCu, TiW / 500A, 6000A, 300A".[^cyp-qtp-030204]

Which of the two S8 stacks the SKY130 flow as run today uses is not
public. SkyWater's capability page lists {term}`PVD` "TiW", "ESC TiN",
"Imp TiN" and "Collimated Ti" and metal etchers for "Al, TiW,
TiN";[^skw-01] and the PDK's 0.36 µm[^pdk-04] matches the Ti/AlCu/TiW
sum rather than the 4 190 Å of the TiN version. Cypress's 2014
notification of the change describes the TiW-based stack as Cypress's
Minnesota process, to be aligned "with the industry-wide Best Known
Method Titanium Nitride (TiN) based metal stack",[^cyp-pin145273] so
the TiW stack was the Minnesota fab's own before 2014. We therefore
describe the Ti/AlCu/TiW stack and read the PDK's numbers as derived
from it (inference). The PDK's metal-1 {term}`sheet resistance` of
125 mΩ/sq[^pdk-08] is consistent: 0.32 µm of aluminium alloy at that
sheet resistance implies a resistivity of about 4.0 µΩ·cm, the
expected range for sputtered Al–0.5%Cu with thin refractory layers in
parallel (typical industry value;[^txt-02] our arithmetic).

**On the bottom titanium.** The Cypress reports give the bottom layer
as "Ti".[^cyp-qtp-113005] The step list used in this reference labels
it "CoTi" and does not explain the label; we read the layer as
collimated titanium. SkyWater lists "Collimated Ti" among its PVD
films,[^skw-01] and *collimated* titanium — sputtered through a
honeycomb filter so that only near-normal atoms reach the
wafer[^rossnagel-1991] — is a standard way to put a thin, uniform Ti
layer at the bottom of contacts and under aluminium (inference). A
cobalt–titanium bilayer is not supported by any public description
of an aluminium stack, although SkyWater does list "Cobalt" as a PVD
film (for {term}`salicide`, we infer).[^skw-01] The metal-2
equivalent is {ref}`TIAL12 <step-123>`.

## Step category

`TIAL6` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *PVD, multi-layer metal* class; the category page's description
of magnetron sputtering, Al–Cu, Ti and Ti:W films applies. What is
specific to this instance is that it is the first *wiring* metal —
the {term}`local interconnect` below it is titanium nitride, chosen for its
etch and thermal behaviour rather than its conductivity (12 800 mΩ/sq
against 125 mΩ/sq[^pdk-08]) — and that the three films must be
deposited in one vacuum sequence, since an oxidised interface between
Ti and Al, or between Al and TiW, would raise the {term}`via` resistance and
weaken the cap.

## Why this step exists

Metal 1 is the first level that can carry current any distance; the
flow needs it, and it needs each of the three films:

* **Al–Cu.** Aluminium is the conductor; the half per cent of copper
  is there for {term}`electromigration`. Ames, d'Heurle and Horstmann showed
  in 1970 that copper doping suppresses aluminium
  electromigration,[^ames-1970] and the mechanism — copper at grain
  boundaries slowing aluminium diffusion, until it is depleted — has
  been studied ever since;[^wiki-em] Vaidya and Sinha related the
  lifetime of Al–0.5%Cu lines to their texture and grain
  structure,[^vaidya-1981] and Knorr and Rodbell showed how strongly
  a (111) texture helps.[^knorr-1996] The ITRS 2001 interconnect
  chapter still treats aluminium metallisation as an alternative to
  copper at the 130 nm generation,[^itrs-02] and Bohr's 1995 argument
  that interconnect, not the transistor, limits performance is why
  the thickness and resistivity of this film matter.[^bohr-1995]
* **Ti underlayer.** A thin titanium film under the aluminium does
  three things: it wets the tungsten plug and the oxide so the alloy
  adheres; it gives the Al–Cu a strong (111) texture — Kamoshida and
  Ito obtained highly (111)-textured Al–Cu on a plasma-treated Ti
  underlayer and better electromigration endurance with
  it[^kamoshida-1997] — and, by reacting to TiAl₃ during later
  anneals, it forms a conductive shunt that keeps a line alive after
  a void opens, the effect Blech first exploited with TiN
  underlayers.[^blech-1976] Domenicucci et al. studied Ti–AlCu–Ti
  lines over tungsten with exactly this structure.[^domenicucci-1996]
* **TiW cap.** Titanium–tungsten (about 10 wt.% Ti, the composition
  of the sputter targets[^pat-tiw-hitachi]) was the industry's
  aluminium barrier and cap long before TiN: Ghate et al. described
  Ti:W barrier metallisation for integrated circuits in
  1978,[^ghate-1978] Nowicki et al. studied its interaction with
  aluminium,[^nowicki-1978] and Olowolafe et al. measured the Al/TiW
  reaction kinetics and how copper and interface oxides slow
  them.[^olowolafe-1985] On top of the alloy it serves as an
  anti-reflective layer for the {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` exposure — the
  role Rocke and Schneegans documented for TiN caps, together with
  {term}`hillock` suppression[^rocke-1988] — as a hard surface for the
  resist and etch, as a hillock and corrosion barrier, and as the
  landing pad the {ref}`VIME <step-119>` via etch stops on. Dalton
  describes a TiW-capped aluminium interconnect of the same
  style.[^dalton-1990]

Without `TIAL6` there is no metal 1; the `mcon` plugs would end in
air.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic Ti/Al–Cu/TiW deposition for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public beyond the layer thicknesses in
the Cypress reports[^cyp-qtp-113005]):

1. **Cluster tool.** A multi-chamber PVD platform — SkyWater's "AMAT
   PVD Metal" with "Sputter etch, degas"[^skw-01] — so that {term}`degas`,
   pre-clean and the three depositions happen without an air break.
   The Endura is the 200 mm-era Applied Materials
   platform.[^amat-endura]
2. **Degas and pre-clean.** A vacuum bake to drive moisture from the
   polished oxide, then a light argon {term}`sputter etch` to remove the
   native oxide from the tungsten plug tops and any post-CMP residue;
   too aggressive a pre-clean sputters oxide onto the plugs and
   raises contact resistance (industry practice[^txt-05]).
3. **Ti, 100 Å.** Titanium sputtered in argon; collimated
   deposition[^rossnagel-1991] or ionised PVD[^rossnagel-1998] if the
   film must also reach into any recess left over the plugs. The
   thickness is that of the Cypress S8 report.[^cyp-qtp-113005]
4. **Al–0.5%Cu, 3 200 Å.** Sputtered from an Al–0.5 wt.% Cu target at
   a wafer temperature of roughly 150–300 °C (industry-typical;[^txt-02]
   "Aluminum both pure and Cu doped" is on SkyWater's list[^skw-01]).
   Temperature and pressure set the grain size and texture through
   Thornton's structure-zone relations;[^thornton-1974][^ohring-2002]
   a warm deposition improves {term}`step coverage` and grain size but must
   stay below the point where hillocks and Cu precipitation
   degrade the film.[^rocke-1988] The 3 200 Å is the Cypress S8
   value;[^cyp-qtp-113005] the plugs are already flat, so no reflow
   or "hot Al" step is needed here, although a Fab 4 derivative of
   the 0.18 µm generation was qualified with "Hot Al".[^cyp-qtp-014807]
5. **TiW, 300 Å.** Sputtered from a Ti:W target (10 wt.% Ti[^pat-tiw-hitachi]),
   typically in argon with the chamber kept free of nitrogen unless
   a nitrided TiW(N) is wanted; thickness per the Cypress
   report.[^cyp-qtp-113005] The film's reflectivity at 248 nm is what
   the {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` {term}`BARC` is tuned against.
6. **Metrology.** Sheet resistance of the stack by
   {term}`four-point probe`; thickness by XRF or profilometry on
   monitors; reflectivity; stress by wafer bow; particle counts.
   The PDK's 125 mΩ/sq[^pdk-08] is the sheet resistance such a stack
   should read after the back-end anneals.

## Machines typically used

* **PVD cluster tool with Ti, Al–Cu and TiW chambers**, 200 mm:
  Applied Materials Endura,[^amat-endura] Novellus INOVA, Ulvac and
  Anelva sputtering systems ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **Degas and sputter-etch pre-clean chambers**.
* **Four-point probe**, **XRF**, **reflectometer**, **stress gauge**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **AMAT PVD Metal platform.** SkyWater lists "AMAT PVD Metal" with
  "Sputter etch, degas", "Aluminum both pure and Cu doped", "TiW",
  "ESC TiN", "Imp TiN", "Collimated Ti", "WN", "Cobalt",
  "Niobium".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the vendor and for
  each of the three films of this stack (Ti, Al–Cu, TiW); the
  platform model (Endura[^amat-endura]) and the assignment of
  "Collimated Ti" to this step's bottom layer are **inferences**.
* **Metal etchers "Lam 9600, Al, TiW, TiN, Pt" and "Lam 2300 Versys,
  Al, TiW, TiN, Nb, Pt"**[^skw-01] corroborate that TiW-capped
  aluminium is etched in the fab (strength: strong for the material
  set, used at {ref}`MM1E <step-114>`).

## Resources required

* **Sputter targets** — titanium, Al–0.5 wt.% Cu and Ti:W (10 wt.%
  Ti[^pat-tiw-hitachi]); SkyWater's filings name Honeywell Electronic
  Materials and JX Metals as sputter-target suppliers.[^sec-01][^sec-02]
* **Argon** for sputtering and pre-clean; **nitrogen** for venting and,
  if used, reactive steps; gas suppliers per the filings.[^sec-01][^sec-02]
* **Chamber shields, clamps and electrostatic-chuck consumables**;
  targets are consumed on a kilowatt-hour schedule.
* **Monitor wafers** for sheet resistance, reflectivity and particles.
