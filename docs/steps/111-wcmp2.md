## What this step is

`WCMP2` is the tungsten polish that turns the blanket film of
{ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>` into plugs. The wafer is pressed face-down on
a rotating pad flooded with an oxidising, abrasive slurry until the
tungsten and the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>` are gone from
the field and the polish lands on the {ref}`NILD2 <step-105>` oxide;
tungsten survives only inside the contact holes. What is left is a
planar oxide surface studded with 0.17 µm tungsten squares[^pdk-periph]
— the `mcon` plugs, "Contact from local interconnect to
metal1"[^pdk-06] — ready for the metal-1 stack of
{ref}`TIAL6 <step-112>` to be sputtered over it. The "2" distinguishes
the step from the local-contact polish {ref}`WCMPLI <step-100>`; the
same polish recurs at every via level ({ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>` and
after).

SkyWater's capability page lists "AMAT Mirra CMP" with the
applications "oxide", "nitride", "niobium", "aluminum", "tungsten",
"high selectivity tungsten" and "copper".[^skw-01] The distinction
between "tungsten" and "high selectivity tungsten" is, on our reading,
between a conventional tungsten slurry that also removes oxide at a
useful rate and one that stops sharply on the oxide; which is used at
this step is not public.

## Step category

`WCMP2` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *metal-clearing* type: unlike the oxide polishes of the
flow ({ref}`CMPL <step-106>`, {ref}`CMPM <step-116>`), which remove a
fixed thickness of one material, it removes two metals completely
and must stop on a third material. The category page's comparison
table sets it against the STI and ILD polishes: an acidic slurry of
alumina or silica with an oxidiser, a stop on oxide, and failure
modes of plug recess, oxide {term}`erosion` in dense arrays, tungsten
"coring" and corrosion. Kaufman et al.'s 1991 paper — tungsten is
oxidised by the slurry to a soft WO₃ that the abrasive strips, so
that removal proceeds only where the pad touches — is the founding
description of the mechanism.[^kaufman-1991]

## Why this step exists

The plug process needs the tungsten removed from everywhere but the
holes, and it needs the result flat:

* **Isolation.** Blanket tungsten shorts every contact to every other;
  the polish is what makes them separate conductors. Before CMP the
  industry used a plasma etch-back ({ref}`category-etch` mentions
  the SF₆/Ar alternative), which left recessed plugs and tungsten
  stringers; polishing, which IBM patented for metal/insulator
  structures in the late 1980s,[^pat-cmp-ibm-1988][^pat-cmp-ibm-1990]
  gives a plug flush with the oxide.
* **A flat base for metal 1.** The 0.14 µm lines of
  {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` must be printed and etched on this surface;
  a plug standing proud or a recessed hole under a line would show up
  as a focus error, a reflective notch or a thinned metal. The
  planarity requirement is the same one that made CMP the standard
  after Kaanta et al.[^kaanta-1987]
* **Contact resistance.** Over-polish recesses the plug below the
  oxide (Vlassak's contact-mechanics model of {term}`dishing` and
  erosion[^vlassak-2001] describes the geometry), which reduces the
  area the metal-1 bottom layer contacts and adds a step for it to
  cover; under-polish leaves TiN or tungsten residue that shorts
  neighbouring lines. The 152 000 mΩ per `mcon` of the PDK's
  tables[^pdk-08] is measured on plugs that have been through this
  step.
* **Oxide loss.** Whatever oxide the polish removes from the field
  after the metal clears reduces the NILD2 thickness between `li`
  and `met1` — the 0.265 µm the stack diagram labels[^pdk-04] — and
  with it the capacitance between the two levels; the "Oxide Bias for
  MM1" of 0.6 and "Min pattern density for oxide" of 0.75 in the
  PDK's assumptions[^pdk-03] are, on our reading, density controls
  for the oxide polishes rather than this one, but the pattern-density
  dependence Stine et al. characterised[^stine-1998] applies to
  tungsten erosion equally.

Without `WCMP2` there would be no plugs, only a tungsten sheet.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic tungsten plug polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher — the Mirra architecture
   Applied Materials patented as "continuous processing"[^pat-cmp-mirra]
   — with a carrier head, retaining ring and multi-zone membrane, a
   stacked polyurethane pad, a diamond conditioner and, on some
   platens, an in-situ endpoint window.[^pat-cmp-window]
2. **Slurry.** Acidic (pH 2–4, industry-typical[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]),
   with alumina or silica abrasive and an oxidiser — ferric nitrate,
   hydrogen peroxide with an iron catalyst, or potassium iodate.
   Kaufman et al. describe the oxidation/abrasion cycle;[^kaufman-1991]
   Kneer et al.[^kneer-1997] and Stein et al.[^stein-1998] measured
   the electrochemistry of tungsten under polishing conditions, and
   Stein, Hetherington and Cecchi the kinetics in iodate
   slurries;[^stein-1999] Cabot's patent on a peroxide-plus-ferric
   catalyst slurry for tungsten, titanium and TiN is the commercial
   form.[^pat-cmp-cabot] The "high selectivity tungsten" of
   SkyWater's list[^skw-01] we read as a slurry formulated to remove
   tungsten tens of times faster than oxide.
3. **Recipe.** A first platen removes the bulk tungsten at high rate;
   a second clears the last tungsten and the TiN liner — whose
   removal rate differs from tungsten's and can leave a "TiN halo"
   if the selectivity is wrong — and lands on oxide with a short,
   low-pressure over-polish; a third platen buffs with dilute slurry
   or DI water. Down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens of
   rpm are typical.[^txt-05] Elbel et al. give a production
   description of tungsten CMP, its removal-rate behaviour and its
   defects.[^elbel-1998]
4. **Endpoint.** Motor-current (friction) endpoint, which changes as
   the pad passes from tungsten to oxide, or optical endpoint through
   a pad window;[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm] Sue et al.
   describe tungsten CMP endpoint detection specifically.[^sue-1999]
   The over-polish is then timed.
5. **Post-CMP clean.** Immediately, before the slurry dries: a
   double-sided brush scrub with dilute NH₄OH or a TMAH-based
   chemistry — Jolley reports TMAH as a post-tungsten-CMP cleaning
   mixture[^jolley-1998] — to remove alumina particles and iron
   residues, sometimes with a dilute HF step, then spin-rinse-dry;
   Sun, Han and Keswani review brush scrubbing.[^sun-2017] Tungsten
   plugs exposed to a wet, oxidising slurry can corrode (the category
   page's "corrosion" failure mode), so the clean also passivates.
6. **Metrology.** Plug recess and oxide erosion by profilometry or
   AFM on test arrays; remaining oxide thickness by reflectometry;
   residual-metal and scratch inspection by patterned-wafer
   inspection (the KLA AIT class SkyWater's job posting
   names[^job-01]); contact-chain resistance at {term}`e-test`.

## Machines typically used

* **Rotary multi-platen CMP polisher**, 200 mm: Applied Materials
  Mirra,[^pat-cmp-mirra][^chiphistory-mirra] Ebara EPO/F-REX,[^ebara-frex]
  SpeedFam-IPEC, Strasbaugh ({ref}`category-cmp`).
* **Post-CMP brush scrubber** (OnTrak/Lam Synergy, SEZ/Lam DaVinci).
* **Profilometer/AFM**, **reflectometer**, **patterned-wafer
  inspection**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Applied Materials Mirra CMP.** SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP"
  with "tungsten" and "high selectivity tungsten" among its
  applications.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** (SkyWater statement);
  which of the two tungsten processes runs this step is not public.
  Unverified job-board snippets recorded in the public-sources
  inventory also mention "AMAT Mirra and Mirra Mesa" (weak).
* **Post-CMP clean.** SkyWater lists the "SEZ223, Davinci" single-wafer
  tools[^skw-01] and a technician profile names "the SEZ etcher
  tool";[^skw-07] a dedicated brush scrubber is not named (open
  question).
* **Defect inspection — KLA AIT / SP1** per a SkyWater job
  posting.[^job-01] Strength: medium.

## Resources required

* **Tungsten CMP slurry** — alumina or silica abrasive with ferric
  nitrate, hydrogen peroxide or iodate oxidiser (industry
  practice[^rev-02][^pat-cmp-cabot]).
* **Polishing pads** (polyurethane) and **diamond pad conditioners**.[^wiki-cmp]
* **DI water**, **dilute NH₄OH or TMAH**, possibly **dilute HF**, for
  the post-CMP clean;[^jolley-1998] brush consumables.
* **Carrier-head consumables** — membranes, retaining rings.
* **Slurry supply and waste treatment** — metal-laden acidic CMP
  waste requires its own treatment line.[^txt-07]
