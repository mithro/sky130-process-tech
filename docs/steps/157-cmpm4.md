# Step 157 — CMPM4: CMP over metal4

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step number** | 157 of 171[^steps-sheet] |
| **Step code** | `CMPM4` |
| **Category** | {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>` |
| **Phase** | BEOL — MiM capacitors, metal 3–5, via 3–4 |
| **Previous step** | {ref}`NILD6 <step-156>` |
| **Next step** | {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` |

## What this step is

`CMPM4` polishes the inter-level dielectric ({term}`ILD`) over metal 4
flat. The {ref}`NILD6 <step-156>` oxide arrives following the metal-4
topography — the 0.845 µm step of the lines,[^pdk-04] softened, and on
some lines the smaller step of a `cap2m` capacitor plate — thick over
wide lines, capacitor arrays and dense buses, thinner over open field.
The wafer is pressed against a pad flooded with alkaline silica slurry
(industry-standard for ILD; SKY130's is not public) until that
topography is gone and a planar oxide surface remains at a controlled
height above the metal. As at {ref}`CMPM <step-116>`,
{ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>` and {ref}`CMPM3 <step-142>` it is an
*oxide-on-oxide* polish stopped by removal amount, not by a stop layer;
the cap of {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` then restores a fixed thickness
before the via-4 mask ({ref}`VIM4 <step-159>`). The finished dielectric
above metal 4 is, per the PDK's stack diagram, the 0.505 µm via-4
height.[^pdk-04]

`CMPM4` is the last of the four back-end oxide polishes in the step
list used in this reference ({ref}`CMPM <step-116>`,
{ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>`, {ref}`CMPM3 <step-142>`, here). The step list used in this reference has
no CMP step after it: no tungsten liner, fill or plug polish follows the
via-4 etch, which leads straight to the metal-5 deposition
({ref}`VIM4E <step-160>`, {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`, where the reading of
how via 4 is filled is set out). SkyWater lists "AMAT
Mirra CMP" with "– oxide" among its applications.[^skw-01] The PDK's
metal-4 rules are written around this polish: a "Min
MM4_oxide_Pattern_density" of 0.7 (m4.pd.1) checked in 700 µm square
windows (m4.pd.2a) stepped by 70 µm (m4.pd.2b), and a `cmm4 waffleDrop`
algorithm that flags any 700 µm × 700 µm window whose metal density
falls below stated levels for its fill coverage;[^pdk-periph] the
assumptions table gives an "Oxide Bias for MM4" ({term}`oxide bias`) of 1.15, the same as
for MM3.[^pdk-03]

## Step category

`CMPM4` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *inter-level dielectric* type: plasma oxide, no stop layer,
silica slurry at pH 10–11, with thickness non-uniformity, scratches and
{term}`pattern-density <pattern density>` steps as the failure modes. {ref}`CMPM <step-116>` sets
out the mechanism, the pattern-density models and the recipe at length,
and {ref}`CMPM3 <step-142>` what a thick metal and a capacitor level add.
Two things are specific to this instance. The target above the metal is
thicker — 0.505 µm against 0.39 µm over metal 3[^pdk-04] — which leaves
more oxide over the `cap2m` plates than over the `capm` plates below
(our arithmetic; see below). And, on our reading of the via-4 rules and the fill reading set out at
{ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`, the dielectric this
polish leaves is what via 4 passes through before metal 5 is sputtered
into it, with no plug polish afterwards to even out a non-uniform via
depth (inference).

## Why this step exists

A {term}`subtractive <subtractive metallisation>` aluminium back end accumulates topography with every
level; this polish removes the metal-4 topography and fixes:

* **Planarity for lithography.** The {ref}`VIM4 <step-159>` and
  {ref}`MM5 <step-162>` exposures need a surface flat within their depth
  of focus ({ref}`category-lithography`); Sivaram et al. modelled the
  removal rate and planarity of ILD CMP,[^sivaram-1992] Burke gave an
  early semi-empirical planarisation model,[^burke-1991] and Boning et
  al. and Chang et al. the statistical metrology of the thickness
  variation that results.[^boning-1994][^chang-1995] IBM's four-level
  fully planarised interconnect of 1991 showed this polish, repeated at
  each level, making a multilevel aluminium back end
  manufacturable.[^uttecht-1991]
* **Enough oxide over the capacitors.** On our reading of
  {ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>` and {ref}`CAPTIW2 <step-151>` a `cap2m` plate
  top lies of the order of 0.1–0.15 µm above the metal-4 top; with a
  finished 0.505 µm above metal 4[^pdk-04] about 0.35–0.4 µm of oxide
  would remain over a plate (our arithmetic from estimated thicknesses),
  including the cap to come. An over-polish that thins the field
  approaches the plates first (inference). Ng et al. review MiM
  integration in Al–Cu and copper back ends.[^ng-2005]
* **A uniform via-4 depth.** The {ref}`VIM4E <step-160>` etch must clear
  the full dielectric over every metal-4 landing while stopping on the
  shallower `cap2m` plates, and the aluminium deposition that follows
  fills the hole directly; the post-polish thickness range is both the
  etch's {term}`over-etch` budget and the spread in the depth the metal-5
  aluminium must cover (inference, on the fill reading at
{ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`).
* **Pattern-density control.** Oxide over an isolated line is removed
  faster than over a dense array or a large capacitor, so the surface
  follows the layout density on the scale of the {term}`planarisation length`
  (Ouma et al.[^ouma-2002]); Stine et al. showed how metal fill reduces
  the effect and what it costs in capacitance.[^stine-1998-ted] The
  `cmm4` fill, the 0.7 oxide-density rule and the 700 µm
  windows[^pdk-periph] are that practice in SKY130's rules; the UMC and
  TSMC dummy-pattern patents show it at other fabs.[^pat-dummy-umc][^pat-dummy-tsmc]
* **Capacitance.** The oxide over metal 4 sets the metal-4-to-metal-5
  capacitance in the PDK's extraction tables (68.33 aF/µm² plate
  term).[^pdk-08]

Without `CMPM4` the via-4 level would be printed and etched on a surface
that followed the metal-4 pattern, and the depth metal 5 must fill would
vary across every die.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic ILD polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's
recipe is not public); the sequence is that of {ref}`CMPM <step-116>`.

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher[^pat-cmp-mirra] with a
   stacked pad, a multi-zone carrier head and diamond conditioning;
   optical thickness endpoint through a pad window is available on this
   class of tool.[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm]
2. **Slurry.** Fumed or colloidal silica in KOH or NH₄OH at pH 10–11
   (industry-typical values[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]); Cook's
   mechanism of hydration and abrasive removal of a glass
   surface[^cook-1990] and Krishnan, Nalaskowski and Cook's review of
   slurry chemistry[^rev-02] describe it.
3. **Recipe.** Removal follows Preston's law,[^preston-1927] modified by
   the pad's contact mechanics;[^runnels-1994] Nanz and Camilletti review
   the models.[^nanz-1995] A first platen removes the bulk, a second trims
   to target, a buff platen removes residue. The target is the remaining
   oxide over metal 4 plus an allowance for the {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>`
   cap, so that the sum reaches the 0.505 µm of the PDK;[^pdk-04]
   down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens of rpm are
   typical.[^txt-05]
4. **Endpoint.** By time calibrated on monitors, or by in-situ optical
   thickness;[^pat-cmp-window] the polish must stop well above the
   capacitor plates (inference from the construction).
5. **{term}`Post-CMP clean <post-CMP clean>`.** Double-sided brush scrub with dilute NH₄OH
   (Philipossian and Sun on brush design[^philipossian-2009]), perhaps a
   dilute HF dip, spin-rinse-dry; Devriendt et al. relate oxide-CMP
   defects to the clean.[^devriendt-1998]
6. **Metrology.** Remaining oxide over metal-4 test pads by reflectometry
   or ellipsometry at many sites; die-level thickness maps on density
   structures (Stine et al.'s method[^stine-1998]); scratch and particle
   inspection on patterned-wafer inspection tools (industry
   practice[^txt-07]); via-4 chain resistance later at {term}`e-test`.

## Open questions

* The slurry, pad, removal amount, target oxide over metal 4 and the
  endpoint method are not public.
* How the 0.505 µm via-4 height[^pdk-04] is split between the polished
  {ref}`NILD6 <step-156>` and the {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` cap is not
  public.
* How much oxide remains over a `cap2m` plate depends on unpublished
  plate and dielectric thicknesses; 0.35–0.4 µm is our estimate.
* Whether a brush scrubber is used after the polish, and which tool, is
  not public.
