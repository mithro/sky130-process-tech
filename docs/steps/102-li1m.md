## What this step is

`LI1M` is the lithography for the {term}`local interconnect`. On the blanket
titanium nitride of {ref}`LITIN <step-101>` it coats, exposes and
develops a resist whose remaining pattern — lines and pads, 0.17 µm
wide at minimum — protects the TiN that {ref}`LI1ME <step-103>` will
leave standing as the `li1` wiring level; everything else is etched
away down to the {term}`cap oxide` and the tops of the tungsten plugs. It is
the first lithography step in the flow to print a *conductor*
pattern over a metallised surface, and the first to print on a
substrate that is itself a strong absorber.

The PDK's mask table lists "Local Intrcnct 1, LI1M" as used in
SKY130;[^pdk-05] the drawn layer is `li1` (67:20, "Local
interconnect") and the generated mask layer `cli1m` (56:0, "Local
interconnect mask") with "mask add" (115:43) and "mask drop" (115:42)
purposes.[^pdk-06] The rules head the `li` section "Defines local
interconnect to diff/tap and poly" and give: 0.170 µm width (li.1),
0.140 µm inside cells named `s8rf2_xcmvpp_hd5_*` (li.1a), a maximum
length-to-width ratio of 10 for {term}`LI` without a licon or mcon (li.2),
0.170 µm spacing (li.3; 0.140 µm in the same RF cells, li.3a),
0.080 µm enclosure of a licon on one of two adjacent sides (li.5), a
minimum area of 0.0561 µm² (li.6) and a minimum LI-resistor width of
0.290 µm (li.7).[^pdk-periph] The minimum-CD table carries
0.14/0.14 µm (core) and 0.17/0.17 µm entries (`LI1MCD`,
`LI1MCDSP`),[^pdk-03] and the "other criteria" table two entries
that bear directly on this step: "LI1CD add/drop" of 0.01 and
0.04 µm and a "Li1 proximity correction" space of 0.25 µm.[^pdk-03]
The latter is, we infer, public evidence that the `cli1m` mask
carries an optical proximity correction applied to lines closer than
0.25 µm.

## Step category

`LI1M` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *critical line* type — printed at the same 0.17 µm
feature and space as the contact layer ({ref}`LICM1 <step-093>`) but
as lines rather than holes, which makes the task easier: a line's
aerial image has markedly higher contrast than an equal-width hole,
and the
0.14 µm core variant (li.1a) is within reach of 248 nm with
{term}`OPC`. What is specific to this instance is the substrate.
Titanium nitride is a low-reflectivity, nitrogen-bearing surface:
the low reflectivity reduces the need for a bottom anti-reflective
coating (TiN is itself used as an {term}`ARC` on aluminium — see
{ref}`category-deposition`), but the nitrogen makes it a classic source of
chemically amplified resist poisoning — the substrate-contamination
"footing" Sturtevant et al. characterised for {term}`DUV` resists.[^sturtevant-1994]

## Why this step exists

The local interconnect is a wiring level, and this is the step that
gives it a pattern. The reasons the rules look as they do:

* **Density.** 0.17 µm lines on a 0.34 µm pitch (li.1, li.3) are
  what let a standard cell connect its transistors below metal 1;
  the tighter 0.14 µm rules inside the RF cells (li.1a, li.3a)[^pdk-periph]
  show the layer being pushed as far as the lithography allows for
  one family of cells. ITRS 2001 sets the half-pitch targets and
  lists the exposure options for the node.[^itrs-03]
* **Enclosure of plugs.** li.5 (0.080 µm) and licon.4 ("Licon1 must
  overlap li1")[^pdk-periph] are the {term}`overlay` budget of this layer to
  the contact layer plus the {term}`etch bias` of the TiN; a plug left
  uncovered is etched by {ref}`LI1ME <step-103>` and, later, exposed
  to the oxide etch of {ref}`CTME <step-108>`. Levinson's book has a
  chapter on overlay,[^levinson-2005] and van Haren et al. treat the
  alignment-mark side of it.[^van-haren-2019]
* **Proximity correction.** At k₁ ≈ 0.41–0.48 (0.17 µm at 248 nm,
  {term}`NA` 0.6–0.7), isolated and dense lines print at different widths
  and line ends pull back; the PDK's "Li1 proximity correction"
  entry and the add/drop mask purposes[^pdk-03][^pdk-06] are, we
  infer, the marks of a rules- or model-based OPC of the kind Otto
  et al., Rieger and Stirniman, and Cobb and Zakhor
  describe.[^otto-1994][^rieger-1994][^cobb-1995] Wikipedia
  summarises the technique.[^wiki-opc]
* **Resist over TiN.** The resist has to survive a chlorine or
  fluorine TiN etch and must not foot or scum on the nitride
  surface; a thin oxide or organic barrier layer, a BARC, or a
  resist formulated against base poisoning are the usual answers.
  He et al. describe an inorganic ARC process for deep-UV
  lithography,[^he-1998] the kind of film that also isolates the
  resist from the substrate.

Without `LI1M` the TiN would remain a blanket sheet and the local
interconnect could not exist.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic critical-line lithography sequence on TiN for a
200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Surface preparation.** The TiN is planar and fresh from the
   sputter tool; a dehydration bake and, if a BARC is not used,
   {term}`HMDS` prime on the track.[^txt-02] {term}`Queue time <queue time>` from
   {ref}`LITIN <step-101>` is limited because TiN oxidises slowly in
   air, which changes both its reflectivity and its etch behaviour.
2. **BARC and resist.** An organic {term}`BARC` or a thin inorganic
   ARC, then a 248 nm chemically amplified positive resist of the
   order of 0.4–0.6 µm (industry-typical for a critical line
   layer[^txt-05][^mack-2007]); the resist must be tolerant of a
   nitride substrate or isolated from it.[^sturtevant-1994]
3. **Exposure.** At 0.17 µm feature and space an i-line {term}`stepper` at
   NA 0.6 would need k₁ ≈ 0.28, below the "0.4 for production"
   limit,[^wiki-litho] so i-line is excluded; a 248 nm tool at NA
   0.6–0.7 gives k₁ ≈ 0.41–0.48, workable for lines with OPC and,
   for the 0.14 µm cells, off-axis illumination. ITRS 2001 lists
   "248 nm + PSM" and "193 nm" as the exposure options for the 130 nm
   node, and only 248 nm had "a mature infrastructure".[^itrs-03] We infer a
   **DUV (KrF) exposure with OPC** for `LI1M`; whether on a stepper
   or a scanner is not public — SkyWater lists both.[^skw-01]
   Brunner sets out how lens aberrations enter the {term}`CD` budget of
   dense lines.[^brunner-1997]
4. **Alignment.** To the contact layer ({ref}`LICM1 <step-093>`) for
   plug coverage and, through it, to poly and active; the
   licon-enclosure rule (li.5) is the tightest overlay-driven rule
   on the layer.[^pdk-periph]
5. **Post-exposure bake**, **develop** in 2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH,[^txt-02]
   rinse, dry.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** After-develop {term}`CD-SEM` of dense and
   isolated lines and line ends; overlay on box-in-box targets to
   the contact layer; the dose/focus window characterised as
   Bossung and Ausschnitt describe;[^bossung-1977][^ausschnitt-1999]
   after-develop inspection for bridges (which become LI shorts) and
   breaks (opens).

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`DUV (KrF, 248 nm) stepper or scanner <machine-duv-krf-stepper>`**, 200 mm: ASML PAS 5500/300–/750
  series,[^asml-750e][^asml-800] Nikon NSR-S20x, Canon FPA-3000EX
  ({ref}`category-lithography`).
* **{ref}`Coat/develop track <machine-coat-develop-track>`** (TEL, DNS/SCREEN, Sokudo) with BARC and DUV
  resist modules and {term}`PEB` plates.
* **{ref}`Overlay metrology <machine-cd-sem-overlay-metrology>`** (KLA-Tencor 5xxx/Archer) and **CD-SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML DUV stepper / DUV scanner.** SkyWater lists "ASML DUV
  stepper" and "ASML DUV scanner".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for
  the existence of the tools; the assignment of `LI1M` to DUV is an
  **inference** from the 0.17 µm and 0.14 µm rules.
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ Lithius**.[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence.
* **Overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer; CD — AMAT Verity/VeraSEM**.[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence (SkyWater statement); use at this
  mask is an inference.

## Resources required

* **248 nm chemically amplified positive photoresist** ({ref}`lithography materials <material-lithography-materials>`) and **organic
  BARC** (or an inorganic ARC); SkyWater's 2021 S-1 names Dow, JSR and
  Tokyo Ohka Kogyo as resist suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **HMDS**, **2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH developer**,[^txt-02] edge-bead remover,
  rinse solvents, {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>` and {ref}`nitrogen <material-process-gases>`.
* **The LI1M reticle** — generated from `cli1m` (56:0) with its
  add/drop corrections,[^pdk-06] on a quartz blank (SEMI P1
  class[^semi-p1]).

## Open questions

* The exposure tool, wavelength, illumination, ARC scheme and resist
  are not public; the DUV-with-OPC reading is an inference from the
  rules and the PDK's proximity-correction entry.
* How the "LI1CD add/drop" values of 0.01 and 0.04 µm are applied
  (mask bias, OPC, or sizing) is not stated.
* Whether the resist sits directly on the TiN or on a barrier/ARC
  layer is not public.
