## What this step is

`TIN3` deposits the liner of the via-1 plug: a thin titanium nitride
film sputtered over the whole wafer — across the
{ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>` cap oxide and down into every 0.15 µm via
opened at {ref}`VIME <step-119>` — coating the oxide sidewalls and the
titanium–tungsten floor. The liner is described here as {term}`IMP`
TiN (ionised-metal-plasma physical vapour deposition, in which the
sputtered titanium is ionised and drawn into the hole by a wafer
bias[^rossnagel-1998]). SkyWater lists "Imp TiN" among its AMAT PVD
films,[^skw-01] and ionised PVD was the 200 mm-era way to coat the
floor of a hole this deep (inference). {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>`
describes the same liner at the metal contact. The film is
the surface on which {ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` will nucleate tungsten,
the barrier that keeps WF₆ and its fluorine away from the metal-1 cap
and from any aluminium an unlanded via has exposed, and the adhesion
layer that holds the plug in its hole; it is removed from the field
again, with the tungsten, at {ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>`. Its thickness
is not public; a 200 mm, 130 nm-era via liner of this kind is
typically a few tens of nanometres on the field and much less on the
via floor (industry-typical value[^txt-05][^rossnagel-1998]).

What the liner covers differs from the contact level in the floor
and the geometry. The floor is the top of metal 1 — on the public
description of the S8 process, 300 Å of TiW over
Al–0.5%Cu[^cyp-qtp-113005] — rather than the titanium-nitride local
interconnect, so this is a TiN-on-TiW interface; and the hole is
0.15 µm wide and 0.27 µm deep,[^pdk-periph][^pdk-04] an
{term}`aspect ratio` of about 1.8:1, shallower and wider than the
0.17 µm contact of {ref}`CTME <step-108>` with its 0.09 µm
bottom.[^pdk-03] The PDK's "min. etch and fill capability for via" of
0.18 µm[^pdk-03] is the public statement that the liner-plus-fill is
qualified down to that opening.

## Step category

`TIN3` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *PVD* class; the category page's account of magnetron sputtering,
its line-of-sight flux and poor {term}`step coverage` in holes, and of
the IMP remedy applies, and {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>` sets out the
physics of ionised PVD at length. Within SKY130 it is the third of the
IMP TiN liners ({ref}`TI/TIN1 <step-097>`, {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>`,
here, {ref}`TIN4 <step-131>`, {ref}`TIN5 <step-146>`), each followed by
a tungsten fill and polish.
What is specific to this instance is that it is the first liner to
land on an aluminium-alloy wiring level rather than on silicide or
TiN, which makes the pre-clean and the barrier function — not the
bottom coverage — the delicate parts.

## Why this step exists

A tungsten via cannot be grown directly into the hole
{ref}`VIME <step-119>` leaves:

* **Nucleation and adhesion.** CVD tungsten nucleates poorly and
  adheres badly on oxide; the liner gives the WF₆-based process a
  surface to start on. Srinivas et al. studied tungsten nucleation on
  TiN,[^srinivas-1992] Ireland reviews the liner-plus-plug process as
  practised at the 0.25 µm generation,[^ireland-1997] and Wang et al.
  describe optimising a Ti/TiN liner for a 0.35 µm tungsten
  plug.[^wang-1996-liner]
* **Barrier against the precursor.** WF₆ and the HF it releases attack
  titanium and aluminium; tungsten is inert to them, but the titanium
  of a Ti:W cap is not, and an unlanded via exposes bare Al–Cu
  sidewall. A continuous TiN film on the floor and sidewalls keeps
  the fluorine off both — the barrier role Wittmer reviewed for
  refractory nitrides[^wittmer-1985] — and prevents the blistered
  "volcano" defects and fluoride-bearing interfaces of an unprotected
  via (industry experience[^txt-05]).
* **Coverage at the bottom.** Ordinary sputtering delivers little
  metal to the floor of a hole above about 2:1; IMP makes the flux
  directional.[^rossnagel-1993][^rossnagel-1998][^hamaguchi-1996] At
  1.8:1 the via is less demanding than the contact, but the liner
  must still be continuous over the corner where the sidewall meets
  the TiW floor, which is where the tungsten fill would otherwise
  meet oxide. Mao and Hopwood's model of ionised PVD of TiN gives the
  coverage such a process achieves.[^mao-2004]
* **Via resistance and reliability.** The liner sits in series with
  every `via` — 4 500 mΩ in the PDK's tables[^pdk-08] — and the
  W/TiN/TiW interface it forms is where a tungsten-plug via fails
  under electromigration: Kwok et al. and Hu, Small and Ho describe
  damage forming at the stud in two-level Al–Cu/W
  structures,[^kwok-1990][^hu-1993] and Bui et al. found the cap
  beneath the plug controls the via's lifetime.[^bui-1994]

Without `TIN3` the tungsten of {ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` would not stick
to the oxide, its precursor would attack the metal-1 cap, and the vias
would be voided, resistive or open.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic IMP TiN via-liner deposition for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Cluster tool.** A multi-chamber PVD platform under one vacuum
   with degas, sputter pre-clean and IMP chambers; SkyWater lists
   "Sputter etch, degas" beside its PVD films,[^skw-01] and Applied
   Materials' Endura is the 200 mm-era example of the
   architecture.[^amat-endura]
2. **Degas.** A vacuum bake to drive out the water the open vias have
   absorbed since the {ref}`VIME <step-119>` clean; this is the first
   defence against the poisoned via, whose origin in dielectric
   outgassing Kobayakawa et al. and Romero et al.
   documented.[^kobayakawa-1991][^romero-1991]
3. **Sputter pre-clean.** A low-energy argon (or Ar/H₂) plasma etch
   removes a few nanometres from the exposed surfaces — the
   titanium- and tungsten-fluoride and oxide residue on the TiW
   floor — with care not to sputter oxide from the via edge onto the
   floor or to thin the 300 Å cap[^cyp-qtp-113005] (industry
   practice[^txt-05]). Boumerzoug et al. report a plasma treatment
   that removes the oxide grown on a TiN cap and lowers via
   resistance,[^boumerzoug-1997] the same concern one level down.
4. **IMP TiN deposition.** Reactive sputtering of a titanium target in
   Ar/N₂ — Berg and Nyberg's hysteresis governs the N₂ flow
   window[^berg-2005] — in an IMP chamber whose RF coil sustains a
   dense secondary plasma[^hopwood-1992] that ionises the titanium; a
   wafer bias draws the ions down the hole at a pressure of tens of
   mTorr.[^rossnagel-1998] Collimated sputtering[^rossnagel-1991] is
   the older route, and SkyWater lists "Collimated Ti" beside "Imp
   TiN".[^skw-01]
5. **Thickness.** Not public; of the order of 10–30 nm on the field
   for a 130 nm-era via liner (industry-typical value[^txt-05]). The
   0.15 µm via[^pdk-periph] leaves room only for a thin film on the
   sidewalls before the tungsten must close the hole.
6. **Optional treatment.** Some flows follow the liner with a short
   N₂/H₂ plasma or a rapid thermal step to densify the TiN and reduce
   its fluorine uptake; whether SKY130 does is not public.
7. **Metrology.** Sheet resistance by {term}`four-point probe` on
   monitors; thickness by X-ray fluorescence or reflectometry; stress
   by wafer bow; bottom and sidewall coverage by cross-section SEM
   during development; via-chain resistance later at {term}`e-test`.

## Machines typically used

* **PVD cluster tool with an IMP (ionised PVD) TiN chamber**, 200 mm:
  Applied Materials Endura with IMP chambers,[^amat-endura][^pat-imp-amat]
  Novellus INOVA with hollow-cathode-magnetron ionised
  PVD[^pat-hcm-novellus] ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **Degas and sputter pre-clean chambers** on the same platform.
* **Four-point probe**, **XRF thickness gauge**, **stress gauge**,
  **cross-section SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **AMAT PVD Metal platform with "Imp TiN".** SkyWater lists "AMAT
  PVD Metal" with "Sputter etch, degas", "Aluminum both pure and Cu
  doped", "TiW", "ESC TiN", "Imp TiN", "Collimated Ti", "WN", "Cobalt"
  and "Niobium".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the vendor and for
  an IMP TiN capability. The platform model (Endura is the natural
  reading[^amat-endura]) and the use of the "Imp TiN" chamber for the
  via liners are **inferences** from that capability and from the
  via's aspect ratio.
* **Novellus INOVA** is not on SkyWater's list; the "Lam/Novellus"
  entries there are for CVD.[^skw-01]

## Resources required

* **Titanium sputter target** (high-purity Ti; SkyWater's filings name
  Honeywell Electronic Materials and JX Metals as sputter-target
  suppliers[^sec-01][^sec-02]).
* **Argon** (sputter and pre-clean) and **nitrogen** (reactive
  gas);[^berg-2005] possibly **hydrogen** in the pre-clean.
* **Chamber consumables** — shields, coil, clamp or electrostatic
  chuck parts — and **target changes** on a kilowatt-hour schedule.
* **Monitor wafers** for sheet resistance and particles.

  film list;[^skw-01] no public source names an Endura at SkyWater.
