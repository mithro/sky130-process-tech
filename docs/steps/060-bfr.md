## What this step is

`BFR` strips the films that have accumulated on the *back* of the wafer,
immediately before the most critical lithography of the flow. Every furnace
step coats both faces of a wafer: the thermal oxidations of the isolation
and gate modules grew oxide on the back as well as the front, the
{term}`LPCVD` nitride of {ref}`ISONIT <step-003>` was deposited on both
sides (and removed from both in the hot-phosphoric bath of
{ref}`NS19 <step-013>`, a batch immersion, so both faces — inferred on
those pages), and the amorphous-silicon gate film of {ref}`SAGD <step-048>`
— plus the gate nitride and oxide cap of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` and
{ref}`POC <step-059>`, if either was deposited in a furnace — now covers
the backside too. `BFR` etches that stack off the back while leaving the
front-side gate stack untouched, so that {ref}`P1M <step-061>` prints on a
flat, clean, properly chucked wafer.

The step is generic to furnace-based processing rather than specific
to SKY130, and no public SkyWater document describes it. SkyWater's
capability list, however, names the class of tool it needs: among the
single-wafer wet tools it lists "SEZ223, Davinci, HF, DSP+HF,
titration controlled",[^skw-01] and a SkyWater maintenance profile
mentions a technician replacing "a motor for a robot on a SEZ etcher
tool".[^skw-07] The SEZ Spin-Processor 223 was introduced in 1999 as a
dual-chamber spin-processing system for "high throughput cleaning and
film removal applications";[^sez-223-pr] a used-equipment listing
describes the 200 mm tool and names "Backside Film Removal" among its
applications.[^sez-223-moov]

## Step category

`BFR` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step — a *wet*, single-wafer,
single-sided etch, unlike the plasma etches of the module. The
category page lists single-wafer spin etchers for backside film
removal and bevel clean as a tool class of their own; this is the
one step in the {term}`FEOL` that uses it. What is being etched (undoped
silicon, nitride and oxide) is ordinary; the constraint that the
front side must not see the chemistry is not.

## Why this step exists

A film on the wafer back does harm in four ways, all of which bear on
the gate lithography that follows:

* **Flatness and focus.** A stressed film bows the wafer (Stoney's
  relation[^stoney-1909]), and a thick, rough or non-uniform backside
  film prevents the wafer from seating on the exposure tool's chuck.
  Liauw, Muray and Chen showed how wafer flatness limits submicron
  optical lithography,[^liauw-1987] Zhang, Wagner and Golbutsov why
  flatness matters for lithography and {term}`CMP`,[^zhang-1997] and Une et
  al. how chucking itself eats into the focus margin for fine
  patterns.[^une-2000] At the 0.15 µm gate level the depth of focus is
  a few hundred nanometres (industry-typical,
  {ref}`category-lithography`), so a backside particle or film step of
  that height is a printed defect.
* **Particles and "hot spots".** Backside films, especially deposited
  poly, shed particles onto chucks and into tools; Lederer et al. and
  Carlson and Le correlate backside defects with lithography hot
  spots and describe the inspection used to find them.[^lederer-2003][^carlson-2006]
* **Chucking and cooling in plasma tools.** The gate etch
  ({ref}`P1ME <step-062>`) holds the wafer on an electrostatic chuck
  with helium backside cooling; a dielectric-on-conductor stack on the
  back changes both the clamping force and the thermal contact, and a
  peeling backside film contaminates the chamber.
* **Contamination and film integrity.** Undoped poly on backside
  oxide crystallises and roughens in later furnace steps and can
  flake; a bare, uniform backside is what the later steps (and the
  final backgrind) expect. Broussous et al. describe single-sided
  backside cleaning of silicon, nitride and oxide surfaces in a spin
  tool for exactly these reasons.[^broussous-2005]

The step's position — after the last furnace deposition of the gate
stack and before the gate mask — is the earliest point at which all
of the module's backside films can be removed at once and the latest
at which the gate lithography still benefits.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic single-wafer backside etch for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab:

1. **Tool.** A spin processor holds the wafer face-up on a chuck that
   contacts only the edge (or floats it on a nitrogen cushion), spins
   it, and dispenses chemistry onto the *back* through the chuck while
   a nitrogen purge across the front keeps liquid and vapour away
   from the gate stack; the SEZ 223 is a dual-chamber tool of this
   kind that "can process 80 or more wafers per hour and use up to
   three chemistries".[^sez-223-pr] The front side is protected only
   by gas flow and geometry, which is why the oxide cap of
   {ref}`POC <step-059>` on top of the nitride is welcome.
2. **Chemistry.** The stack is removed from the top down. Silicon
   (the a-Si film) is etched isotropically in HF/HNO₃ mixtures — the
   classic chemistry characterised by Schwartz and
   Robbins[^schwartz-1976] and reviewed by Kern and
   Deckert[^kern-1978] — in which nitric acid oxidises the silicon and
   HF dissolves the oxide. Oxide is removed in HF.[^wiki-hf] Nitride
   etches slowly in HF-based solutions[^deckert-1978][^knotter-2001]
   and much faster in hot phosphoric acid,[^vgh-1967] so a nitride
   layer on the back is either taken in a longer HF/HNO₃ exposure or
   is absent because the cap was deposited by {term}`PECVD` (front side only).
   SkyWater lists its single-wafer wet tools as "SEZ223, Davinci, HF,
   DSP+HF, titration controlled",[^skw-01] so the chemistries belong
   to the pair rather than to either tool. Broussous et al. report a
   published single-sided backside recipe on an SEZ spin processor —
   an H₂O:H₂O₂:H₂SO₄:HF mixture chosen because it lets the etch rate
   on bare silicon, nitride and oxide be set independently without
   roughening the silicon[^broussous-2005] — a public example of the
   tool class and chemistry SkyWater's list implies, not SkyWater's
   own recipe.
3. **{term}`Endpoint <endpoint>`.** Timed, with the etch rate held by titration-controlled
   bath concentration;[^skw-01] a used-equipment description of the
   tool mentions film-removal endpoint detection as an
   option.[^sez-223-moov]
4. **Rinse and dry.** DI rinse on the spin chuck and spin dry.
5. **Inspection.** Backside inspection for residual film and
   particles;[^lederer-2003] front-side particle scan to confirm no
   wrap-around damage.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`Single-wafer spin etcher / spin processor <machine-single-wafer-spin-processor>`** for backside and
  bevel processing: SEZ (now Lam) 200-series and Da Vinci, SCP/Akrion
  spin tools (category page).
* **{ref}`Backside inspection <machine-defect-inspection>`** tool — e.g.
  the KLA-Tencor Surfscan SP1's Back Side Inspection Module (BSIM), as
  used in industry practice[^lederer-2003] — and a **front-side
  particle scanner**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **SEZ 223 spin processor.** Named on SkyWater's capability list with
  its chemistries ("SEZ223, Davinci, HF, DSP+HF, titration
  controlled")[^skw-01] and, as "a SEZ etcher tool", in a SkyWater
  staff profile.[^skw-07] Strength: **strong** for the tool; the
  assignment to this step is an inference from its application class
  (the SEZ press release and the used-equipment listing both describe
  film removal and backside applications[^sez-223-pr][^sez-223-moov]).
* **Da Vinci** — the other single-wafer wet tool on the list, which
  names no vendor for it ("Davinci").[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence.
* **Particle and backside scans — KLA-Tencor Surfscan SP1 (our
  reading).** A SkyWater *Defect Technician 2* posting reads "General
  operation of semiconductor defect metrology tools:
  SEM/AIT/KLA/SP1/EV300/1X";[^job-06] we read "SP1" as KLA-Tencor's
  Surfscan SP1, and the posting expands none of the abbreviations, does
  not mention a backside module, and gives no models or counts.
  Strength: medium.

## Open questions

* The chemistry, sequence and etch times of the SKY130 backside
  removal are not public; HF/HNO₃ for silicon and HF for oxide are
  the generic choices.
* Whether the backside nitride (if the gate nitride is a furnace
  film) is removed here, in a separate hot-phosphoric step, or not at
  all, is unknown.
* Whether SkyWater's SEZ 223 or its Da Vinci tool runs this step is not
  stated.
* Whether other backside strips occur elsewhere in the flow (for
  example after the {term}`spacer` nitride) is not stated publicly;
  this reference describes only this one.
