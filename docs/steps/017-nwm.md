:::{admonition} At a glance

* **Does:** opens resist windows for the N-well implants (`NWI`,
  `NWI2`) and the P-channel threshold implant (`LVTPI`).
* **Why:** defines the N-well that every PMOS, the deep-N-well ring
  and the extended-drain drift regions need.
* **Public numbers:** minimum N-well width 0.840 µm (nwell.1); minimum
  spacing between two N-wells 1.270 µm (nwell.2a).[^pdk-periph]
* **Likely SkyWater tool:** ASML i-line stepper or scanner — strong
  (existence); inference (assignment).[^skw-01]
* **Not public:** whether the three implants truly share one resist
  (→ Open questions).

## What this step is

`NWM` prints the *N-well mask*.

A photoresist (thick, we infer) is
coated on the planar, oxide-covered wafer, exposed through the N-well
{term}`reticle` and developed, opening windows over every region that is to
become N-type well. These are the body of all PMOS transistors, the ring that
ties the deep N-well to the surface, and the N-type {term}`drift regions <drift region>` of the
extended-drain devices.

Three implants are then placed through the same
resist without an intervening strip: the MeV N-well implants
{ref}`NWI <step-018>` and {ref}`NWI2 <step-019>` and the P-channel
threshold implant {ref}`LVTPI <step-020>`. The resist is removed at
{ref}`LVTPIS <step-021>`. This reference describes the three implants
as sharing one mask (inference): it is the normal way to build a
{term}`retrograde well` plus channel implant
with a single lithography.[^txt-01]

The PDK's mask table lists "N-Well*, NWM" as used in SKY130.[^pdk-05]
The drawn layer is `nwell` (GDS 64:20, "N-well region") and the
generated mask is `cnwm` (GDS 21:0, "Nwell mask").[^pdk-06]

### Key numbers

Its design
rules are among the loosest in the front end, looser than any layer
except the deep N-well (`dnwell.2` minimum width 3.000 µm, `dnwell.3`
minimum spacing 6.300 µm[^pdk-periph]):

| Rule[^pdk-periph] | Constrains | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| nwell.1 | minimum width | 0.840 µm |
| nwell.2a | minimum spacing between two N-wells | 1.270 µm |
| nwell.4 | every N-well "will contain metal-contacted tap" | — |
| nwell.5 | a deep N-well "must be enclosed by nwell" | — |
| nwell.6 | minimum enclosure of an N-well hole by deep N-well | 1.030 µm |
| nwell.7 | spacing between an N-well and a deep N-well on separate nets | 4.500 µm |

The PDK's process assumptions add what the well looks like in the
silicon:

| Quantity[^pdk-03] | Value |
|---|---:|
| "n-well peak concentration" | 6.0 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ |
| "background concentration" | 8.0 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³ |
| N-well vertical dimension | 1.1 µm |
| "min n-well width to guarantee 90 % peak concentr." | 0.55 µm |
| N-well/P-well junction, from the drawn edge | 0.034 µm |

## Step category

`NWM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *thick-resist implant-block* kind, like
{ref}`DNM <step-007>`.

**Specific to this step:**

* The resist has to stop phosphorus ions at several
  hundred keV to about 1 MeV, we infer (see {ref}`NWI <step-018>`), so it
  is thicker than the etch resists, and its edge profile matters more than
  its {term}`CD`.
* It aligns to the {term}`STI` pattern.

## Why this step exists

CMOS needs two kinds of body: "The N device is manufactured on a p-type
substrate while the P device is manufactured in an n-type well
(n-well)".[^wiki-cmos] On SKY130's bulk p-type wafers (substrate
"Bulk")[^skw-02] the N-well is therefore where every PMOS lives —
`pfet_01v8` and its low- and high-Vt variants, the 5 V `pfet_g5v0d10v5`,
and the varactors.[^pdk-07] It also does three more jobs that the PDK
documents:

* it completes the deep N-well "tub" — nwell.5 requires the deep N-well
  to be enclosed by N-well, giving the isolated P-well its side
  walls[^pdk-periph] (see {ref}`DNM <step-007>`);
* it provides the drain extension of the high-voltage NMOS: the PDK's HV
  methodology states that "The VHV devices need to be designed with
  drain extentions (DE) fabricated by lightly doped Nwells and Pwells
  respectively".[^pdk-hv]

  A Texas Instruments patent describes
  precisely this use of "the n-well region … as the lightly doped drain
  extension region surrounding the heavily doped n-type drain
  region";[^pat-demos-ti]
* it forms the base of the vertical PNP and the collector contact of the
  NPN.[^pdk-07]

Latch-up is the other reason the N-well matters: in CMOS "the n-p-n-p
structure is formed by the source of the NMOS, the p-substrate, the
n-well and the source of the PMOS".[^wiki-latchup] The PDK's
assumptions give a "Minimum n+ or p+ - nwell spacing to prevent
latch-up" of 0.23 µm.[^pdk-03] A retrograde N-well with a heavy buried
peak lowers the well resistance and the parasitic bipolar gain (see
{ref}`NWI <step-018>`).

## How it is typically performed

*An industry-generic well-mask lithography for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab:*

1. **Track preparation.** {term}`HMDS` prime; no {term}`BARC` for a layer whose minimum
   feature is 0.84 µm (inference).[^wiki-litho]
2. **Thick resist coat.** The resist must exceed the range of the
   highest-energy well implant with margin.

   Published figures for comparable wells:

   :::{table} Published thick-resist thicknesses for comparable retrograde wells, as each source gives them
   :widths: 30 70

   | Source | Resist thickness |
   |---|---|
   | IBM[^pat-well-ibm] | "typically 1800–2500 nm in thickness", for phosphorus implants up to 850 keV |
   | IBM mask-edge study[^hook-2003] | "2.3 µm-thick pwell mask" |
   | Zilog[^pat-resist-zilog] | "minimum thicknesses of 3.4 µm … with the preferred thickness for the resist between 3.8 µm and 4.2 µm", for a 1.3–1.5 MeV phosphorus N-well |
   :::

   For an N-well whose junction lies 1.1 µm deep[^pdk-03] a resist of
   roughly 2–3 µm is the plausible range (inference).[^txt-02]
3. **Exposure.** i-line (365 nm): at 0.84 µm minimum width the process
   factor is {term}`k₁ <k1>` ≈ 1.4 at {term}`NA` 0.6,[^wiki-litho] so any i-line {term}`stepper`
   prints it easily.

   A thick resist favours i-line over {term}`DUV` because
   chemically amplified DUV resists are seldom formulated at
   2–3 µm.[^txt-05] We infer an **i-line layer**.
4. **Alignment** to the STI/active pattern. The well edge must sit
   inside the isolation between the NMOS and PMOS active areas; the
   PDK's 0.034 µm junction offset from the drawn edge[^pdk-03] and the
   0.23 µm latch-up spacing[^pdk-03] show how tightly the well edge is
   budgeted.
5. **Develop, hard bake, inspect.** 2.38 % TMAH[^microchemicals-dev]
   (0.26 N; our arithmetic from TMAH's molar mass of 91.15 g/mol,[^wiki-tmah]
   taking the solution's density as about 1 g/mL)
   develop.

   A hard bake or UV cure
   so that the resist survives the long, hot MeV implant without flowing
   or outgassing excessively (industry practice).[^txt-02] {term}`Overlay <overlay>` to
   STI on the overlay tool.

**{term}`Well proximity effect <well proximity effect>`.** A thick implant resist has a consequence for
design: "Some of the ions scattered out of the edge of the photoresist
are implanted in the silicon surface near the mask edge, altering the
threshold voltage of those devices". The same source gives
"threshold voltage shifts of up
to 100 mV … over a lateral distance on the order of a
micrometer",[^hook-2003] as modelled by Sheu et al.[^sheu-2006] Rules
such as the 0.380 µm enclosure of `lvtn` by `nwell`
(lvtn.10)[^pdk-periph] are the kind of layout guard that follows.

## Open questions

* **Shared resist.** That `NWI`, `NWI2` and `LVTPI` all use this one
  resist is this reference's reading, following the usual single-mask
  retrograde-well practice;[^txt-01] no public document says so.
* **Resist thickness and exposure tool.** Both are inferred from the
  design rules and the implant energies implied by the 1.1 µm well
  depth.
* **Reticle modification.** Whether the N-well reticle is modified
  (Boolean add/drop) around the high-voltage and deep N-well regions is
  not public.
