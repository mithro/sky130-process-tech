## What this step is

`NWM` prints the *N-well mask*: a thick photoresist is coated on the
planar, oxide-covered wafer, exposed through the N-well reticle and
developed, opening windows over every region that is to become
N-type well — the body of all PMOS transistors, the ring that ties
the deep N-well to the surface, and the N-type drift regions of the
extended-drain devices. Three implants are then placed through the
same resist without an intervening strip: the MeV N-well implants
{ref}`NWI <step-018>` and {ref}`NWI2 <step-019>` and the P-channel
threshold implant {ref}`LVTPI <step-020>`. The resist is removed at
{ref}`LVTPIS <step-021>`. That three implants share one mask is our
inference from the step order (mask, implant, implant, implant,
strip); it is the normal way to build a retrograde well plus channel
implant with a single lithography (TXT-01, ch. 2).

The PDK's mask table lists "N-Well*, NWM" as used in SKY130 (PDK-05).
The drawn layer is `nwell` (GDS 64:20, "N-well region") and the
generated mask is `cnwm` (GDS 21:0, "Nwell mask") (PDK-06). Its design
rules are the loosest of any front-end layer: minimum width 0.840 µm
(nwell.1), minimum spacing between two N-wells 1.270 µm (nwell.2a),
every N-well "will contain metal-contacted tap" (nwell.4), a deep
N-well "must be enclosed by nwell" (nwell.5), minimum enclosure of an
N-well hole by deep N-well 1.030 µm (nwell.6) and 4.500 µm spacing
between an N-well and a deep N-well on separate nets (nwell.7)
(PDK-PERIPH). The PDK's process assumptions add what the well looks
like in the silicon: "n-well peak concentration" 6.0 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ over
a "background concentration" of 8.0 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³, a baseline N-well
vertical dimension of 1.1 µm, a "min n-well width to guarantee 90 %
peak concentr." of 0.55 µm, and an N-well/P-well junction 0.034 µm
from the drawn edge (PDK-03).

## Step category

`NWM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *thick-resist implant-block* kind, like
{ref}`DNM <step-007>`: the resist has to stop phosphorus ions of
several hundred keV to about 1 MeV, so it is thicker than the etch
resists, and its edge profile matters more than its CD. It aligns to
the STI pattern.

## Why this step exists

CMOS needs two kinds of body: "The N device is manufactured on a
p-type substrate while the P device is manufactured in an n-type well
(n-well)" (WIKI-CMOS). On SKY130's bulk p-type wafers (SKW-02:
substrate "Bulk") the N-well is therefore where every PMOS lives —
`pfet_01v8` and its low- and high-Vt variants, the 5 V
`pfet_g5v0d10v5`, and the varactors (PDK-07). It also does three more
jobs that the PDK documents:

* it completes the deep N-well "tub" — nwell.5 requires the deep
  N-well to be enclosed by N-well, giving the isolated P-well its side
  walls (PDK-PERIPH; see {ref}`DNM <step-007>`);
* it provides the drain extension of the high-voltage NMOS: the PDK's
  HV methodology states that "The VHV devices need to be designed
  with drain extentions (DE) fabricated by lightly doped Nwells and
  Pwells respectively" (PDK-HV), and a Texas Instruments patent
  describes precisely this use of "the n-well region … as the lightly
  doped drain extension region surrounding the heavily doped n-type
  drain region" (PAT-DEMOS-TI);
* it forms the base of the vertical PNP and the collector contact of
  the NPN (PDK-07).

Latch-up is the other reason the N-well matters: in CMOS "the n-p-n-p
structure is formed by the source of the NMOS, the p-substrate, the
n-well and the source of the PMOS" (WIKI-LATCHUP), and the PDK's
assumptions give a "Minimum n+ or p+ - nwell spacing to prevent
latch-up" of 0.23 µm (PDK-03). A retrograde N-well with a heavy buried
peak lowers the well resistance and the parasitic bipolar gain (see
{ref}`NWI <step-018>`).

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic well-mask lithography for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab:

1. **Track preparation.** HMDS prime; no BARC for a layer whose
   minimum feature is 0.84 µm (inference; WIKI-LITHO).
2. **Thick resist coat.** The resist must exceed the range of the
   highest-energy well implant with margin. Published figures for
   comparable wells: IBM used a photoresist "typically 1800–2500 nm in
   thickness" for phosphorus implants up to 850 keV (PAT-WELL-IBM);
   an IBM study of mask-edge scattering modelled a "2.3 µm-thick
   pwell mask" (HOOK-2003); and Zilog needed "minimum thicknesses of
   3.4 µm … with the preferred thickness for the resist between 3.8 µm
   and 4.2 µm" for a 1.3–1.5 MeV phosphorus N-well (PAT-RESIST-ZILOG).
   For an N-well whose junction lies 1.1 µm deep (PDK-03) a resist of
   roughly 2–3 µm is the plausible range (inference; TXT-02).
3. **Exposure.** i-line (365 nm): at 0.84 µm minimum width the process
   factor is k₁ ≈ 1.4 at NA 0.6 (WIKI-LITHO), so any i-line stepper
   prints it easily; a thick resist favours i-line over DUV because
   chemically amplified DUV resists are seldom formulated at 2–3 µm
   (TXT-05). We infer an **i-line layer**.
4. **Alignment** to the STI/active pattern. The well edge must sit
   inside the isolation between the NMOS and PMOS active areas; the
   PDK's 0.034 µm junction offset from the drawn edge (PDK-03) and the
   0.23 µm latch-up spacing (PDK-03) show how tightly the well edge
   is budgeted.
5. **Develop, hard bake, inspect.** TMAH develop; a hard bake or UV
   cure so that the resist survives the long, hot MeV implant without
   flowing or outgassing excessively (industry practice, TXT-02);
   overlay to STI on the overlay tool.

**Well proximity effect.** A thick implant resist has a consequence
for design: "Some of the ions scattered out of the edge of the
photoresist are implanted in the silicon surface near the mask edge,
altering the threshold voltage of those devices", with "threshold
voltage shifts of up to 100 mV … over a lateral distance on the order
of a micrometer" (HOOK-2003; modelled by SHEU-2006). Rules such as the
0.380 µm enclosure of `lvtn` by `nwell` (lvtn.10, PDK-PERIPH) are the
kind of layout guard that follows.

## Machines typically used

* **i-line stepper**, 200 mm (ASML PAS 5500/100–/275, Nikon NSR-2205i,
  Canon FPA-3000i); a KrF tool could print it but is not needed
  (TXT-05).
* **Coat/develop track** with thick-resist capability.
* **Overlay metrology** tool.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **ASML i-line stepper or i-line scanner** (SKW-01). Strength:
  **strong** for existence; assignment to `NWM` is an **inference**
  from the 0.84 µm rules and resist thickness.
* **Tracks — DNS 80B, Sokudo RF3, TEL ProZ/Lithius** (SKW-01).
  Strength: strong for existence.
* **Overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer** (SKW-01). Strength: strong.

## Resources required

* **Thick positive i-line implant resist** (2–3 µm class, inference);
  suppliers named in SEC-01 include Dow, JSR and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo.
* **HMDS**, **TMAH developer**, edge-bead remover, DI water, nitrogen.
* **The NWM reticle** (generated from `cnwm`, PDK-06).

## Open questions

* That `NWI`, `NWI2` and `LVTPI` all use this one resist is inferred
  from the step order; no public document says so.
* Resist thickness and exposure tool are inferred from the design
  rules and the implant energies implied by the 1.1 µm well depth.
* Whether the N-well reticle is modified (Boolean add/drop) around the
  high-voltage and deep N-well regions is not public.
