## What this step is

`P1IS` removes the resistor-protect resist printed at
{ref}`RPM <step-049>` after it has masked the gate implant
{ref}`P1I <step-050>`, and cleans the wafer so that the next mask,
{ref}`RRPM <step-052>`, can be coated. This reference describes a
strip after each implant mask; this is
the first of three in the gate module ({ref}`PRIS <step-054>` and
{ref}`UPRIS <step-057>` follow).

Two things make this strip different from the well-module strips
({ref}`LVTNIS <step-016>` and its companions). First, the resist has
taken a *heavy* implant — of the order of 10¹⁵ cm⁻² or more of
phosphorus or arsenic (industry-typical, {ref}`P1I <step-050>`) —
which is a hundred to a thousand times the dose of a channel implant,
so the carbonised {term}`crust <implant crust>` on the resist is fully developed. Second, the
surface under and around the resist is bare silicon: the undoped
resistor bodies under the islands and the freshly implanted
amorphous-silicon gate film everywhere else. There is no oxide to
absorb an aggressive clean, and the film must come out of the strip
clean, smooth and only negligibly thinned, because the next resist is
coated straight onto it.

## Step category

`P1IS` is a {ref}`Resist strip / clean <category-strip>` step of the
*post-high-dose-implant* type. The category page describes the crust
that a high-dose implant forms on resist and the {term}`popping` that follows
if the crust is heated too fast; the well-module strips could largely
ignore that problem, whereas here it is the whole problem. The
source/drain strips ({ref}`PDIS <step-084>`,
{ref}`NSDIS <step-087>`) are the other members of this class.

## Why this step exists

The resist must be gone before the next coat, and it must be gone
completely: any organic residue left on the future gate film would be
sealed under the {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` cap and printed into every
device below it. A crusted, implanted resist is much harder to remove
than a fresh one. Orvek and Huffman measured the carbonised layer that
forms in implanted resist masks,[^orvek-1985] and Fujimura et al.
showed why such a layer {term}`ashes <ash>` slowly and pops when the bulk resist
beneath it outgasses.[^fujimura-1989] Popped flakes are the classic
yield hazard of a high-dose strip: they land elsewhere on the wafer as
particles that survive later cleans.

The clean that follows the ash also has to restore a surface fit for
lithography. The gate film has been bombarded through its whole open
area; the ash leaves a thin oxide on it; the implanted species and
sputtered beam-line metals sit in the resist crust and on the exposed
film. A wet clean removes the organic remnant and the metals, and
leaves a controlled, thin chemical oxide.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic strip after a high-dose implant on a bare-silicon
surface, for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab:

1. **Two-step plasma ash.** Downstream (remote) O₂ plasma, starting
   *cold* — the patent literature specifies "a low-temperature
   (<220 °C) environment"[^pat-strip-mosel] — with a little N₂ or
   {term}`forming gas` until the crust is consumed, then a hotter bulk step.
   TSMC's ashing patent describes the "carbonized crust" that motivates
   the sequence,[^pat-strip-tsmc] and Fujimura et al. explain why
   nitrogen is added to the oxygen.[^fujimura-1990] SkyWater's ashers
   have exactly this configuration: "Gasonic PEP, remote microwave
   plasma, N2, O2, 120C – 270C", "Iridia RF microwave, N2, O2, H2, CF4,
   NH3, H2/N2, 40C-270C" and "Mattson Aspen2, RF plasma, O2, CF4,
   H2>N2, up to 250C".[^skw-01] The temperatures are far below the
   crystallisation range of the amorphous film ({ref}`SAGD <step-048>`),
   so the ash does not change the film. Fluorine additions (CF₄) are
   avoided on a silicon surface: they would etch it.
2. **Wet strip and clean.** Sulphuric–peroxide ({term}`SPM`) at typically 120–150 °C to
   dissolve the remaining organics and lift the crust remnants
   (composition on the category page[^wiki-piranha]), then {term}`SC-1`
   (NH₄OH/H₂O₂/H₂O) for particles and, optionally, {term}`SC-2`
   (HCl/H₂O₂/H₂O) for the sputtered metals.[^wiki-rca] On silicon these steps
   are not inert: SPM and SC-1 both leave a chemical oxide of about a
   nanometre,[^kern-1990] and SC-1 etches silicon slowly — Lee measured
   the silicon and oxide etch rates in ammonia–peroxide
   solutions[^lee-kt-1999] — so the SC-1 time and temperature are limited
   to keep the 0.18 µm film within its thickness budget. SkyWater's
   Akrion Gamma bench lists "Sulfuric, SC1" among its chemistries.[^skw-01]
   Alternatives to the ash-plus-SPM route exist for high-dose resist:
   Visintin et al. describe liquid formulations for stripping high-dose
   implanted resist,[^visintin-2006] and sulphur-trioxide wet stripping
   has been reported.[^bergman-2009]
3. **Rinse and dry.** Cascade DI rinse and IPA (Marangoni) or spin dry.
4. **Inspection.** Patterned-wafer inspection for popped flakes and
   residue; a residue here is a killer defect for every gate under it.

Whether SKY130 uses an oxide-removing (dilute HF) step at this point is
not public. We infer that it does not: the chemical oxide left by the
clean is thin, will be repeated by the two strips that follow, and is
buried under the nitride cap of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`, where a thin
interfacial oxide is harmless.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`Downstream plasma asher <machine-downstream-plasma-asher>`**, 200 mm: GaSonics (Novellus) Aura/PEP,
  Mattson Aspen, Axcelis/Fusion ES series, PSK (category page).
* **{ref}`Batch wet bench <machine-wet-bench>`** with SPM and SC-1 tanks and a rinser/dryer
  (Akrion, DNS/SCREEN, SCP), or a **{ref}`spray processor <machine-wet-bench>`** (FSI Mercury).
* **{ref}`Patterned-wafer inspection <machine-defect-inspection>`** (KLA-Tencor AIT class).

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **GaSonics PEP, Iridia and Mattson Aspen II ashers** — all named on
  SkyWater's facilities page with their gases and
  temperatures.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for existence; the
  assignment of this strip to any one of them is an inference, the
  Iridia's 40 °C floor and hydrogen chemistries fitting a crust step
  best, as at {ref}`PDIS <step-084>` and {ref}`NSDIS <step-087>`.
* **Akrion Gamma batch wet bench** ("Sulfuric, SC1, phosphoric,
  BOE")[^skw-01] for the SPM/SC-1 sequence. Strength: strong for
  existence.
* **DNS wet bench and FSI Mercury** ("industry standard
  HF/SC1/SC2")[^skw-01] as alternatives. Strength: strong for existence.
* **KLA-Tencor AIT** patterned-wafer inspection, our reading of "AIT"
  in a SkyWater job posting's "SEM/AIT/KLA/SP1/EV300/1X".[^job-01]
  Strength: medium.

## Resources required

* **Oxygen, nitrogen, forming gas (H₂/N₂)** for the ash; **CF₄**,
  listed on the Iridia and Mattson ashers,[^skw-01] would etch the
  exposed silicon film and is avoided (recipe outline above).
* **Sulphuric acid and hydrogen peroxide** ({ref}`wet chemicals <material-wet-chemicals>`) for SPM; **ammonium
  hydroxide** for SC-1; **hydrochloric acid** for an optional SC-2[^wiki-rca]
  (category page).
* **Ultrapure DI water**, **isopropanol**, nitrogen.
* **Abatement** for the ash exhaust, which carries the implanted
  species out of the crust (arsenic, if used, is a regulated waste).
* Chemical suppliers named in SkyWater's filings: KMG Chemicals (2021
  S-1), EMD Performance Materials (fiscal 2023 10-K).[^sec-01][^sec-02]
