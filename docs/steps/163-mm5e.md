## What this step is

`MM5E` is the metal-5 etch, the last metal etch of the flow. Through the
resist of {ref}`MM5 <step-162>` a chlorine plasma removes the metal-5
stack of {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>` — on this reference's reading a TiW
cap, about 1.2 µm of Al–Cu and a refractory underlayer, the cap read as
TiW because the February 2014 S8P metal-stack change excluded "top
metal layers"[^cyp-qtp-123907] ({ref}`overview-metal-cap`) — everywhere
outside the drawn `met5` shapes (GDS 72:20[^pdk-06]), stopping on the
{ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` cap oxide. What remains is the top-level wiring,
the bond and probe pads and the inductor turns, at 1.600 µm minimum width
and space (m5.1, m5.2[^pdk-periph]). The step list used in this reference
has no separate strip step after `MM5E`; this page treats the
passivation, resist strip and clean as part of the etch, as at
{ref}`MM4E <step-155>`. The next deposition is the first film of the
passivation module, {ref}`NFUSOX <step-164>`.

**How thick is the metal?** The PDK's stack diagram labels `metal5`
1.26 µm,[^pdk-04] and Edwards's slides repeat it.[^ann-16] The
assumptions table gives a metal-5 antenna thickness of 1.2 µm for
"S8P*/SP8P* with 1.2um thick metal" and 2 µm for "S8P*/SP8P* with 2um
thick metal";[^pdk-03] the antenna chapter's Table Ig, for
"S8P12-10R*/S8PIR-10R/S8PF-10R*", gives 1.200 µm in its `met5.1` row,
while Table Ie, for "S8P-5R/SP8P-5R/S8P-10R*", has no `met5` row and
gives 2.000 µm in a row labelled `waffle_chip`;[^pdk-11] on the S8PIR\*
reading at {ref}`MM5 <step-162>` the 1.2 µm table applies (inference).
The device page's 28.5 mΩ/sq[^pdk-07] fits about 1.2 µm of Al–Cu (our
arithmetic; see {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`). This page uses the diagram's
1.26 µm for geometry. At 1.26 µm deep and 1.6 µm wide, a minimum space has an
{term}`aspect ratio` of about 0.8:1 (our arithmetic) — shallower than
the 2.8:1 spaces of metals 3 and 4 — but the film to be cleared is
half as thick again as theirs.

## Step category

`MM5E` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *metal,
chlorine-chemistry* class — the category page's "Aluminium–copper with
Ti/TiN or Ti:W caps" entry — and the fifth of the five metal etches.
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` sets out the chemistry: aluminium etches
spontaneously in chlorine, so {term}`anisotropy` comes from a sidewall
passivation film, and the etch products leave the lines liable to
corrode.[^hess-1982][^nojiri-2015] What is specific to this instance is
the depth of metal and what it does to the rest of the process. The etch
runs longer than at any lower level, so the resist budget is tighter —
Krogh et al. followed resist erosion in an aluminium etch plasma by
emission spectroscopy[^krogh-1987] — the wafer heats more, and more
copper-rich residue and chlorinated polymer are left on a larger sidewall
area. The level's pattern density is also low over much of a die: Chang
et al. studied the corrosion and passivation-swelling defects of an
ultra-low pattern-density thick-metal etch.[^chang-2004] Unlike
{ref}`MM3E <step-140>` and {ref}`MM4E <step-155>`, no capacitor
dielectric sits on this metal.

## Why this step exists

* **Pads, power and inductors cut to shape.** The etched metal is what
  the 1.600 µm rules[^pdk-periph] and the 28.5 mΩ/sq[^pdk-07] describe;
  over 1.26 µm of height a small sidewall angle changes the line's cross
  section noticeably. Cooperberg, Vahedi and Gottscho show how the
  Cl₂/BCl₃ balance and the passivant set the sidewall,[^cooperberg-2002]
  and Allen and Rickard obtained a tapered aluminium profile with
  nitrogen additions in a TCP etcher, which eases the later coverage by
  passivation;[^allen-1994] AT&T's tapered-etch patent shows the older
  form of the same choice.[^pat-taper-att]
* **Complete clearing without undercutting.** Al–Cu etches in
  Cl₂/BCl₃,[^schaible-1978][^chen-1989] but copper chlorides are
  involatile and leave copper-rich residues that seed
  corrosion;[^lee-1981] Abraham, Gabriel and Zheng describe the
  {term}`microloading <loading effect>` between dense and open areas on a high-density metal
  etcher,[^abraham-1997] which on a top metal with wide pads and narrow
  lines is large (inference).
* **Corrosion before the passivation.** Chlorine left in the sidewall
  polymer and resist forms HCl with moisture and attacks the
  aluminium;[^lee-1981] Wai and Ling describe the post-etch and
  polymer-clean sequence that prevents it,[^wai-2017] and a top metal
  that waits for its passivation, with {term}`bond pads <bond pad>` that are later reopened,
  is where corrosion is least tolerable (inference).
* **The last antenna.** When metal 5 is cut, every net that reaches the
  top level is isolated in the plasma; the PDK's antenna chapter gives
  metal-5 ratios and design limits for this level,[^pdk-11] Fang and
  McVittie described the thin-oxide damage from {term}`gate charging <plasma charging>` during
  plasma processing,[^fang-1992] and Hwang and Giapis the {term}`notching` at the
  line foot.[^hwang-1997] Second-level capacitor plates connected through
  via 4 are charged in the same way (inference; Wang, Ackaert et al.
  showed the dependence on each plate's antenna[^wang-2004-mim]).
* **Keeping clear of the via-4 fill.** Because the metal-5 aluminium
  also fills every via 4 on this reference's reading, an over-etch
  beside a via would cut into the fill; the 0.310 µm metal-5 enclosure
  of via 4 (m5.3)[^pdk-periph] is the margin that keeps the etch off it
  (inference).

Without `MM5E` metal 5 would remain a sheet shorting every pad, supply
and via 4 together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic thick aluminium top-metal etch for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the chemistry is at
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>`.

1. **Chamber.** A high-density plasma metal etcher — transformer- or
   inductively coupled, with independent bias, a cooled electrostatic
   chuck and helium backside cooling — with integrated passivation and
   strip modules; Lam's {term}`TCP` source[^pat-tcp-lam] and Applied
   Materials' DPS reactor[^pat-dps-amat] are the two 200 mm-era designs,
   and Lam's 10-K describes its metal etch lines.[^lam-10k]
2. **Cap breakthrough.** The cap, read here as TiW, is broken through
   in a short fluorine- or chlorine-bearing step; Liu and Kuo showed TiW etching in
   CF₄-based plasmas.[^liu-2007-tiw]
3. **Main etch.** Cl₂/BCl₃ with N₂ (or CHF₃) at a few to tens of mTorr;
   chlorine removes aluminium as AlCl₃, BCl₃ scavenges oxygen, and the
   nitrogen or carbon species build the sidewall
   film.[^schaible-1978][^hess-1982][^chen-1989] At a few hundred
   nanometres per minute (industry-typical[^txt-02]) the
   1.26 µm stack clears in two to four minutes; the resist and the chuck
   temperature are managed against resist erosion and reticulation over
   that time (industry practice[^nojiri-2015]).
4. **Endpoint.** Optical emission on the atomic aluminium line near
   396 nm or the AlCl band, which falls as the aluminium
   clears;[^bawolek-1987] with most of a top-metal level open, the signal
   is strong.
5. **{term}`Over-etch <over-etch>`.** A timed, more passivating step to clear the underlayer
   and stringers, with {term}`selectivity` to the
   {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` oxide; the over-etch is bounded by the
   0.505 µm of dielectric the stack diagram puts above metal 4[^pdk-04]
   — the thinnest oxide the etch can break through into (inference).
6. **Passivation.** Without an air break, an H₂O-vapour or O₂/CF₄ plasma
   converts residual chlorides and strips chlorine from the
   resist.[^nojiri-2015][^wai-2017]
7. **Strip and clean.** Downstream O₂/N₂ {term}`ash` — SkyWater lists
   three resist-removal tools: "Gasonic PEP, remote microwave plasma,
   N2, O2, 120C – 270C"; "Iridia RF microwave, N2, O2, H2, CF4, NH3,
   H2/N2, 40C-270C"; and "Mattson Aspen2, RF plasma, O2, CF4, H2>N2, up
   to 250C"[^skw-01] — then a solvent polymer clean — SkyWater's batch
   rotational tools are listed with "EKS265, EKC270 solvents, CO2
   injected DI"[^skw-01] — within a {term}`queue-time <queue time>`
   limit; DI rinse and dry.
8. **Metrology.** Line CD by optical CD or {term}`CD-SEM`; profile by
   cross-section SEM on monitors; metal-5 sheet resistance and
   comb/serpentine structures at {term}`e-test` against
   28.5 mΩ/sq;[^pdk-07] via-4 chains against 380 mΩ;[^pdk-08] corrosion
   inspection after a delay. The published SKY130 {term}`test tile`
   measures metal 5 with "S8PIR/PF WM5_Kelvin (Width = 1.6um/ Length =
   223.6um)" and "RSM5_Kelvin (Width = 2.4um/ Length = 353.4um)" lines,
   1.6 µm serpentines "over field" and "over M4 grid", and 1.6 µm
   serpentine/comb pairs "(S8PIR/PF BVM5M5)" and
   "(S8PIR/PF BVM5M4)".[^raw-data-testtile-pads]

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Cl₂ <material-etch-gases>` and BCl₃**, **{ref}`N₂ <material-process-gases>`** (and possibly CHF₃) for the main etch; a
  fluorine- or chlorine-bearing mixture for the cap breakthrough; **Ar**;
  **He** for backside cooling (industry practice;[^nojiri-2015] SkyWater
  lists no gases for its metal etchers, but names Cl₂, CF₄ and CHF₃ on its
  other etchers[^skw-01]).

* **{ref}`Chamber consumables <material-hardware-consumables>`**, replaced more often for the thickest metal;
  **{ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** with the full metal-5 stack; **chlorine abatement**.

## Open questions

* **Metal-5 thickness.** The stack diagram gives 1.26 µm,[^pdk-04] the
  assumptions and antenna tables 1.2 µm or 2 µm by flow;[^pdk-03][^pdk-11]
  that the 1.2 µm "S8PIR-10R" table applies to SKY130 is our reading
  (see {ref}`MM5 <step-162>`), and whether 1.26 µm includes the
  refractory layers is not stated in any public source.
* **Metal-5 films.** No public source gives the metal-5 composition; the
  Ti(W)/Al–Cu/TiW reading is set out at {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`.
* The chemistry, endpoint, over-etch, passivation and queue-time rules,
  and which Lam etcher runs the step, are not public.
* The step list used in this reference has no separate strip step after
  `MM5E`; this page treats the resist strip and clean as part of the
  etch.
