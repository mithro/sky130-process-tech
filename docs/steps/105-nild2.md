## What this step is

`NILD2` deposits the inter-level dielectric between the local
interconnect and metal 1: a blanket silicon dioxide over the
nitride-capped TiN lines of {ref}`LINIT <step-104>`, filling the
0.17 µm spaces between them (li.3[^pdk-periph]) and burying them
under enough oxide for the {ref}`CMPL <step-106>` polish to leave a
planar, controlled thickness on which the `mcon` contacts
({ref}`CTM1 <step-107>`) and metal 1 will be built. This reference's
label follows the PDK's own name for the film: the stack diagram labels the
dielectric between `li` and `metal1` "NILD2 K=4.05" and dimensions
it 0.265 µm, from the top of the LINT cap over `li` to the `metal1`
bottom, which it places at 1.3761 µm above the substrate; the `li`
bottom (0.9361 µm) plus the 0.1 µm `li`, the 0.075 µm LINT and the
0.265 µm NILD2 gives exactly that level (our reading of the drawing
and our arithmetic).[^pdk-04] The PDK's numbering
continues up the stack — NILD3 (4.5), NILD4 (4.2), NILD5 (4.1),
NILD6 (4.0)[^pdk-04] — and the later steps {ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`
to {ref}`NILD6 <step-156>` deposit them. The drawing also carries
"NILD3_C K=3.5" and "NILD4_C K=3.5", which it does not
explain.[^pdk-04]

Two things follow from the numbers. The permittivity of 4.05 is that
of a dense silicon dioxide (thermal oxide is 3.9; the PDK labels its
"PSG" and "FOX" 3.9 and its "NILD3" 4.5[^pdk-04]), so the film is an
undoped or lightly doped oxide, not a low-k material. And 0.265 µm
is the *post-polish* thickness over the {term}`local interconnect`; the
as-deposited film must be thicker by the polish removal plus the
0.1 µm {term}`LI` step plus its 0.075 µm nitride cap[^pdk-04] — of the
order of 0.5–0.7 µm as deposited — a typical figure for a polished
inter-level dielectric at this node[^txt-05] — which is what the
geometry and the polish imply (inferred; not public). The gap it
fills is modest: 0.17 µm wide by about 0.175 µm deep (TiN plus
nitride), an {term}`aspect ratio` near 1:1 that either {term}`HDP-CVD` or a {term}`PECVD`
{term}`TEOS` oxide can fill without voids (category page).

## Step category

`NILD2` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step —
a plasma {term}`CVD` oxide, in the family of {ref}`FILOX <step-011>`,
{ref}`PSG <step-089>` and {ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>` before it and the
higher NILD films after. What distinguishes it from the {term}`PSG` is that
it is undoped and fills a shallow gap; from the {term}`cap oxide`, that it
does have gaps to fill and is thick; and from the later NILDs, that
the conductors it buries are 0.1 µm of TiN rather than 0.36 µm or
more of aluminium.[^pdk-04] The thermal ceiling is unchanged from
{ref}`LINIT <step-104>`: a wafer with TiSi₂ contacts, tungsten plugs
and TiN wiring is deposited on at 350–450 °C (industry-typical;
category page[^txt-02][^txt-09]).

## Why this step exists

Every wiring level needs an insulator above it, and the properties
of this one are set by what sits on either side:

* **Insulation and capacitance.** The film separates every `li1`
  line from every metal-1 line that crosses it; its 0.265 µm
  thickness and 4.05 permittivity, with the 0.075 µm nitride at
  7.3,[^pdk-04] are the LI-to-metal-1 parallel-plate and fringe
  capacitances of the extraction tables.[^pdk-08] The PDK's
  capacitance data spreadsheet carries the same stack.[^pdk-09]
* **Gap fill without voids.** A void between two LI lines would be
  opened by the polish or by a later contact etch and would fill
  with metal, shorting the lines. At an aspect ratio near 1:1 the
  fill is not demanding, but the choice between HDP-CVD (which
  fills by simultaneous deposition and sputtering — Nguyen's review
  of HDP-CVD dielectrics,[^nguyen-1999] the Novellus and Applied
  Materials gap-fill patents[^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat]) and
  a PECVD TEOS oxide (whose conformality Nguyen et al. and Raupp,
  Cale and Hey characterised[^nguyen-1990][^raupp-1992]) still
  matters, because an HDP film's sputter component can erode a thin
  nitride cap at the corners of the lines while a PECVD film can
  pinch off over a narrow gap; Kwok et al. describe the integrated
  PECVD/ozone-TEOS approach used for sub-half-micron gaps,[^kwok-1994]
  and Nishimura et al. the gap-fill choices at 0.13 µm.[^nishimura-2002]
* **A polishable, stable film.** The oxide is polished at
  {ref}`CMPL <step-106>`, so its density, hardness and wet-etch rate
  must be uniform across the wafer; PECVD TEOS oxide and HDP oxide
  both polish well, while a hygroscopic TEOS/ozone film would need
  a cap. The film also sits under the metal-1 contact etch
  ({ref}`CTME <step-108>`), whose fluorocarbon chemistry etches
  dense and porous oxide at different rates.
* **Mechanical protection of the TiN.** The 0.1 µm TiN lines and
  their nitride cap are thin; the oxide fixes them in place against
  the shear of the polish and the thermal cycles of the five metal
  levels above.

Without `NILD2` there would be no dielectric between the local
interconnect and metal 1.

## How it is typically performed

Industry-generic routes for an inter-level oxide over a thin local
interconnect in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not
public):

* **HDP-CVD oxide.** SiH₄/O₂/Ar in an inductively coupled plasma
  with RF bias, 350–450 °C at the wafer, deposition-to-sputter ratio
  tuned for the gap; the standard {term}`inter-metal dielectric` route of
  the era for aluminium processes (category page; Novellus
  SPEED[^lam-speed] and Applied Materials Ultima[^amat-1997]).
  SkyWater lists "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma (HDP) doped and
  phos doped with sputter etch" with the sub-bullet "high aspect
  (5:1) fill capability"[^skw-01] — the "doped" reading of which is
  an open question here — and a SkyWater technician
  profile names a "Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07]
* **PECVD TEOS oxide.** TEOS/O₂ at 350–400 °C, often as a {term}`liner`
  under an HDP fill or as the whole film where the gap allows;
  SkyWater lists "PECVD TEOS, C2 and Producer".[^skw-01] For a
  1:1 gap this is a plausible whole-film route (inference).
* **Which route SKY130 uses.** The 4.05 permittivity[^pdk-04] fits
  either; the PSG-doped HDP entry on SkyWater's list argues that
  HDP is used somewhere in the stack, and the "NILD2" naming
  separate from "PSG" suggests a different, undoped film here. We
  infer an undoped HDP or PECVD TEOS oxide and leave the choice
  open.
* **Thickness.** Not public; of the order of 0.5–0.7 µm as
  deposited (typical for a polished inter-level dielectric[^txt-05])
  to give 0.265 µm over LI after polish (see above).
* **Sequence.** Load from {ref}`LINIT <step-104>` (no wet step
  between, so the nitride is clean); optional thin PECVD liner;
  main deposition; in-situ NF₃ chamber clean between wafers.[^txt-09]
* **Metrology.** Thickness and refractive index by ellipsometry;
  wet-etch rate as a density check; stress by wafer bow;
  cross-section SEM for voids between LI lines during development;
  particles.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus HDP-CVD.** SkyWater lists a Lam/Novellus HDP system
  "with sputter etch" and "high aspect (5:1) fill
  capability"[^skw-01] and a technician profile names a
  "Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07] Strength: **strong**
  for the tool's existence; assignment to `NILD2` is an
  **inference**, and the public entry mentions only doped films.
* **PECVD TEOS "C2 and Producer".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; medium for this step, where the 1:1 gap makes a whole-film
  TEOS oxide plausible and a TEOS liner or overburden is possible
  (inference).
* **PECVD silane oxide "C1"**[^skw-01] as a candidate for the optional
  liner (weak for assignment).

## Open questions

* The deposition route (HDP or PECVD TEOS), the as-deposited
  thickness and whether the film is undoped are not public; the
  4.05 permittivity is the only public constraint on composition.
* Whether a PECVD liner precedes an HDP fill is not public.
