## What this step is

`NILD3` deposits the inter-level dielectric between metal 1 and
metal 2. Over the freshly etched metal-1 lines of
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` — 0.36 µm tall,[^pdk-04] as close as 0.14 µm
apart[^pdk-periph] — a silicon dioxide film is deposited thick enough
to fill every gap between the lines and to bury them under a
continuous layer, which {ref}`CMPM <step-116>` then polishes flat and
{ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>` caps before the via-1 mask
({ref}`VIM <step-118>`). The name follows the PDK: its process stack
diagram labels the dielectric between `met1` and `met2` "NILD3" with
a relative permittivity of 4.5, draws beside it a "NILD3_C" of
permittivity 3.5 and thickness 0.030 µm, and gives the via-1 height
through it as 0.27 µm.[^pdk-04] The diagram's levels put the bottom
of `met1` at 1.3761 µm and the bottom of `met2` at 2.0061 µm,[^pdk-04]
so the finished dielectric between the two metals — after this
deposition, the polish and the cap — is on our reading about
0.63 µm from metal-1 bottom to metal-2 bottom, or 0.27 µm above the
metal-1 top.

Two public hints shape our reading of the film. The permittivity of
4.5 is above the 3.9–4.2 of the other oxides in the diagram (FOX 3.9,
NILD2 4.05, NILD4 4.2, NILD5 4.1, NILD6 4.0[^pdk-04]), which is what
a dense, slightly nitrogen- or hydrogen-containing plasma oxide, or
one measured with its {term}`liner` and cap, would show (inference); and the
separately named "NILD3_C" at 3.5 and 0.030 µm[^pdk-04] suggests, on
our reading, that the {term}`inter-metal dielectric` is a *stack* of at least
two films, of which one is thin and has the permittivity of a
fluorinated oxide.[^denison-1996][^usami-1993] Whether NILD3_C is a
liner under the fill, a cap over it, or the extraction model's way of
describing a graded film is not public; we describe the gap-fill film
here and note the candidates under *Open questions*.

## Step category

`NILD3` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *dielectric gap-fill* class. Its nearest relatives in the flow
are the {term}`STI` fill {ref}`FILOX <step-011>` and the earlier back-end
dielectrics {ref}`PSG <step-089>` and {ref}`NILD2 <step-105>`; its
successors are {ref}`NILD4 <step-126>` and above. What is specific
to this instance is the gap: 0.14 µm wide, 0.36 µm deep — an
{term}`aspect ratio` of about 2.6:1 — between *aluminium* lines that
must stay below roughly 400–450 °C (industry-typical limit for Al–Cu
metallisation[^txt-05]) and that a plasma can charge and damage. The
category page sets out the two routes a 130 nm-era fab had for such
a gap: {term}`HDP-CVD`, with simultaneous sputtering by biased argon
to keep the gap mouth open, and {term}`PECVD` {term}`TEOS` oxide, more
conformal but liable to pinch off above a narrow gap.

## Why this step exists

An inter-metal dielectric does four jobs, and its deposition
decides how well:

* **Insulation without voids.** A void between two 0.14 µm-spaced
  lines is a place where the {ref}`VIME <step-119>` {term}`via` etch or the
  next metal can find a path, and a moisture trap that corrodes the
  aluminium. Nguyen's review of HDP-CVD explains how the deposition/
  sputter balance fills a re-entrant gap without a
  seam,[^nguyen-1999] and the Applied Materials and Novellus
  patents give production recipes for high-aspect-ratio
  gaps.[^pat-hdp-amat][^pat-hdp-novellus] Novellus's later press
  release still calls HDP "the preferred gapfill dielectric
  technology for advanced geometries".[^lam-speed]
* **Capacitance.** The film's permittivity sets the line-to-line
  and level-to-level capacitance that the PDK's extraction tables
  encode;[^pdk-08] the ITRS 2001 interconnect chapter tracked the
  move from 4.1 oxide to fluorine-doped oxide (κ 3.7) and lower-k
  dielectrics,[^itrs-02] and fluorine-doped oxide by HDP-CVD or
  PECVD was the 130 nm-era way to reach the 3.5 the PDK labels for
  NILD3_C.[^denison-1996][^usami-1993]
* **Mechanical and chemical protection of the metal.** The oxide
  must not crack, must not stress the lines into voiding — Yue,
  Funsten and Taylor described stress-induced voids in aluminium
  interconnects during processing[^yue-1985] — and must not let the
  aluminium form {term}`hillocks <hillock>` during deposition, the phenomenon
  Chaudhari analysed,[^chaudhari-1974] which is one reason for the
  refractory cap of {ref}`TIAL6 <step-112>` — TiW or TiN, which the
  public record does not settle ({ref}`overview-metal-cap`) — and for a
  moderate deposition temperature.
* **A polishable overburden.** {ref}`CMPM <step-116>` needs enough
  oxide above the highest metal to reach a flat surface with margin
  for the pattern-density dependence of the polish; the PDK's "Oxide
  Bias for MM1" of 0.6 and "Min pattern density for oxide" of 0.75
  in its assumptions table[^pdk-03] are, on our reading, the fill and
  density controls that keep that {term}`overburden` uniform.

Without `NILD3` there is nothing to carry metal 2, and metal 1 is
exposed.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic inter-metal dielectric deposition over 0.14 µm
aluminium spaces in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not
public):

1. **Liner.** A thin PECVD oxide (silane or TEOS based) at 350–400 °C
   (industry-typical[^txt-05][^raupp-1992]) over the metal, deposited
   without high-energy ion bombardment so that the aluminium and the
   gate oxides beneath its antennas are not charged; Hwang and
   Giapis explained the charging mechanism during HDP oxide
   deposition,[^hwang-1998] and Byun et al. how a liner and a modified
   HDP recipe reduce the damage.[^byun-2005] Whether the PDK's
   0.030 µm "NILD3_C"[^pdk-04] is such a liner is an inference.
2. **Gap fill by HDP-CVD.** SiH₄/O₂/Ar in an inductively coupled
   plasma with RF bias on the wafer, at a few mTorr and a
   sputter-to-deposition ratio tuned so that the argon sputtering
   keeps the mouth of each 0.14 µm gap open until it fills from the
   bottom.[^nguyen-1999][^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat] The wafer is
   heated by the plasma; backside helium and a cooled chuck hold it
   at a temperature compatible with aluminium. SiF₄ can be added for
   a fluorinated film.[^denison-1996] Lieberman and Lichtenberg treat
   the ion-bombardment physics.[^lieberman-2005] SkyWater lists
   "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma (HDP) doped and phos doped with
   sputter etch",[^skw-01] and a maintenance-page photograph names "a
   Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07]
3. **Gap fill by PECVD TEOS (alternative).** TEOS/O₂ plasma oxide at
   about 400 °C,[^raupp-1992][^wiki-teos] in a deposition–etch–deposition
   sequence that etches back the overhang between steps; more
   conformal than silane oxide but at 2.6:1 it needs the etch-back to
   avoid a seam ({ref}`category-deposition`). SkyWater's "PECVD TEOS,
   C2 and Producer"[^skw-01] is the capability.
4. **Overburden.** After the gaps are full, deposition continues —
   HDP or a faster PECVD TEOS cap — until the oxide over the metal is
   thick enough for the polish; several hundred nanometres of
   overburden is typical (industry-typical value[^txt-05]). The
   finished, polished and capped dielectric above metal 1 is the
   0.27 µm via-1 height of the PDK.[^pdk-04]
5. **Chamber clean.** NF₃ remote-plasma clean between wafers or lots.
6. **Metrology.** Thickness and refractive index by ellipsometry on
   monitors and on test pads; stress by wafer bow; gap-fill by
   cross-section SEM on dense metal-1 arrays; charging damage by
   antenna test structures at {term}`e-test`; particles.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus HDP-CVD.** SkyWater lists "Lam/Novellus High Density
  Plasma (HDP) doped and phos doped with sputter etch" with
  "high aspect (5:1) fill capability"[^skw-01] — the 2.6:1 gap here is
  well inside it — and its maintenance page names "a Novellus high
  density plasma tool".[^skw-07] Strength: **strong** for the tool
  class; the assignment to this step is an **inference** from the gap
  geometry, and the public entry mentions only doped films; the model
  (SPEED is the Novellus HDP product[^novellus-history]) is not
  public.
* **PECVD TEOS "C2 and Producer".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; medium for depositing the liner and/or the overburden
  here (inference); as the gap fill itself it would need the
  deposition–etch–deposition sequence above.
* **PECVD silane oxide "C1"**[^skw-01] as a liner candidate (weak
  for assignment).

* **{ref}`Silane <material-precursors>`, {ref}`oxygen <material-process-gases>` and argon** for HDP-CVD;[^nguyen-1999] **SiF₄** if the
  film is fluorinated;[^denison-1996] **TEOS and oxygen** (with helium or

  SkyWater's filings: Air Products and Praxair (2021 S-1), Linde and Airgas
  (fiscal 2023 10-K).[^sec-01][^sec-02]

* **{ref}`Chamber consumables <material-hardware-consumables>`** — ceramic domes, coils, showerheads, liners.
* **{ref}`Monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** for thickness, index and stress.

## Open questions

* The route (HDP-CVD, PECVD TEOS with etch-back, or a combination),
  the liner, the deposited thickness and whether the film is
  fluorinated are not public.
* What the PDK's "NILD3_C" (k 3.5, 0.030 µm)[^pdk-04] represents — a
  liner, a cap, or a modelling construct — is not public; the
  extraction tables treat it as a separate dielectric.
* Why the NILD3 permittivity is labelled 4.5[^pdk-04] rather than the
  4.0–4.2 of the other inter-metal oxides is not public.
* The tool model behind SkyWater's "Lam/Novellus High Density
  Plasma" entry[^skw-01] is not public.
