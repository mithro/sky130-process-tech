## What this step is

`POC` deposits a (thin, we infer) silicon dioxide film on top of the
gate nitride of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`, completing the gate stack —
gate oxide, 0.18 µm poly,[^pdk-03] nitride, oxide — that is patterned at
{ref}`P1M <step-061>` and etched at {ref}`P1ME <step-062>`. This
reference labels it a *protective* oxide cap. The cap's thickness,
deposition method and precise function are not public. The
oxide is not a device layer: whatever survives the gate etch sits on top
of the nitride cap and is, we infer, consumed or removed by the {term}`spacer`
etch ({ref}`SPE <step-077>`) and the nitride cut
({ref}`NPCM <step-078>`) before the contacts are made.

What can be said with confidence is where it sits and what it is made
of. It is an oxide on a nitride on a doped silicon film, deposited
immediately before the {term}`backside film removal` ({ref}`BFR <step-060>`)
and the most critical lithography of the process. SkyWater's
capability list offers three ways to deposit such an oxide: "LPCVD
silane oxide" in the Aviza furnaces, "PECVD TEOS, C2 and Producer" and
"PECVD silane oxide/nitride/oxynitride, C1".[^skw-01] Which is used is
not stated.

## Step category

`POC` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step —
an {term}`LPCVD` or {term}`PECVD` oxide like the later spacer and cap oxides
({ref}`SPOX <step-080>`, {ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>`). What is specific
to it is that it is the *top* of a lithography stack: its thickness
enters the reflectivity of the stack at the exposure wavelength, its
surface is what the resist (or {term}`BARC`) is coated on, and its chemistry
is what the resist touches.

## Why this step exists

An oxide-on-nitride cap can protect in several ways, and the public
literature supports each of them. They are not exclusive; a thin
oxide can do all of them at once.

* **Protecting the resist from the nitride.** Chemically amplified
  {term}`DUV` resists are poisoned by bases: traces of airborne organic
  bases — amines and amides such as the N-methylpyrrolidone whose
  uptake Hinsberg et al. measured at the 10 ppb level[^hinsberg-1992] —
  neutralise the photo-generated acid and leave an insoluble skin,
  and nitrogen-rich
  substrates can do the same from below. Sturtevant et al. documented
  substrate contamination effects in the processing of chemically
  amplified DUV resists,[^sturtevant-1994] and later work on
  BARC–resist compatibility and {term}`footing` over nitrogen-containing
  layers[^yoshino-1998][^lin-2001] shows the problem persisted into
  the 248 nm generation. A thin oxide between the nitride and the
  resist is the standard cure: it is inert to the resist chemistry
  and blocks the nitrogen. If {ref}`P1M <step-061>` is printed with a
  chemically amplified KrF resist, as its 0.15 µm gate length
  suggests, this reading is the most likely one.
* **Completing an anti-reflective stack.** An inorganic {term}`ARC` is a
  thin-film interference filter, and a two-layer oxide/{term}`oxynitride` or
  oxide/nitride stack has more freedom to reach low reflectivity at
  248 nm than a single film; Dijkstra and Juffermans treat the
  optimisation of such layers,[^dijkstra-1993] and He et al. describe
  a production inorganic ARC process.[^he-1998] On this reading the
  oxide thickness is tuned to the nitride's index and thickness.
* **Protecting the nitride.** The next step is a wet backside etch in
  HF-containing chemistry ({ref}`BFR <step-060>`); a {term}`top oxide <blocking oxide>` takes
  any front-side attack on itself. It also protects the nitride
  during the BARC/resist-open step of the gate etch and lets the
  etch sequence use an oxide {term}`hard mask`, whose sidewall chemistry Bell
  and Joubert analysed.[^bell-1996]
* **Serving as a stop.** An oxide on nitride is the classic
  stop/{term}`selectivity` pair: the spacer nitride etch
  ({ref}`SPE <step-077>`) can stop on it, and a {term}`CMP` over poly
  ({ref}`CMPP <step-090>`) can use the stack as its stop.

Without the cap, the gate resist would sit directly on the nitride
with the poisoning risk above, the ARC stack would have one fewer
degree of freedom, and the nitride would be exposed to the backside
etch.

## How it is typically performed

Industry-generic routes for a thin cap oxide in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab:

* **PECVD {term}`TEOS` oxide.** Tetraethyl orthosilicate with O₂ in a
  single-wafer chamber at about 350–400 °C (typical industry values,
  category page[^wiki-teos][^wiki-pecvd]); Raupp, Cale and Hey describe
  the plasma chemistry,[^raupp-1992] and Chapple-Sokol et al. how RF
  power sets the film's properties.[^chapple-sokol-1989] Low
  temperature, front-side only, good thickness control for a film of
  tens of nanometres; the film is less dense than a thermal oxide and
  etches faster in HF.
* **PECVD silane oxide.** SiH₄ + N₂O at similar temperatures on the
  same class of tool — SkyWater's "C1" tool lists it.[^skw-01]
* **LPCVD oxide.** Silane–oxygen at roughly 400–450 °C or TEOS
  pyrolysis at 650–750 °C in a furnace (category
  page[^adams-1979][^becker-1987]); conformal, both sides of the
  wafer, and, for TEOS, hot enough to crystallise the gate film.
* **Thickness.** Not public. A resist-poisoning barrier or a
  reflectivity trim layer is a few tens of nanometres; a hard mask is
  thicker. The PDK's "poly cap after SPE" of 0.2 µm[^pdk-03] is, if
  our reading of it is right, the whole cap stack after the spacer
  etch.
* **Metrology.** Thickness and index by ellipsometry; for an ARC
  stack, reflectivity at 248 nm on a monitor; particles.

## Open questions

* The deposition method (PECVD TEOS, PECVD silane oxide or LPCVD),
  temperature and thickness of the cap are not public.
* Which of the four functions above the cap serves — resist-poisoning
  barrier, ARC layer, nitride protection, etch/polish stop — is
  inferred; the word "protective" is this reference's label, not a
  description from a public source.
* Whether any of the cap survives to the finished device is not
  public; our reading is that it is removed by the spacer etch and
  the nitride cut where contacts are made.
