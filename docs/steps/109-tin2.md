## What this step is

`TIN2` deposits the {term}`liner` of the metal contact: a thin titanium
nitride film sputtered over the whole wafer — across the planarised
{ref}`NILD2 <step-105>` oxide and down into every 0.17 µm contact hole
opened at {ref}`CTME <step-108>`, coating the sidewalls and the
titanium nitride local-interconnect floor. The liner is described
here as {term}`IMP` TiN (ionised-metal-plasma physical vapour
deposition, the variant of sputtering in which the metal flux is
ionised and drawn vertically into the hole by a wafer
bias[^rossnagel-1998]). SkyWater lists "Imp TiN" among its AMAT PVD
films,[^skw-01] and ionised PVD was the 200 mm-era way to coat the
floor of a hole this deep (inference). The film
is not a device layer; it is the surface on which
{ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>` will nucleate tungsten, the barrier that keeps
the tungsten precursor away from what lies beneath, and the adhesion
layer that holds the plug in its hole. It is removed from the field
again, with the tungsten, at {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`.

What the liner covers is, on our reading of the previous two steps, a
hole about 0.34 µm deep with a 0.17 µm top and a bottom that the
PDK's assumptions put at 0.09 µm,[^pdk-03][^pdk-04] whose floor is the
0.10 µm TiN `li`[^pdk-04] — so this is a TiN-on-TiN contact, unlike
the local contact of {ref}`TI/TIN1 <step-097>`, whose liner lands on
{term}`silicide` and is described in this reference as Ti/TiN. That
difference is, we infer, why
this step needs no titanium: there is no silicon to make ohmic
contact to, and no native oxide of the kind a Ti layer reduces. The
liner thickness is not public; a 200 mm, 130 nm-era contact liner of
this kind is typically a few tens of nanometres on the field and
much less at the hole bottom (industry-typical;[^txt-05][^rossnagel-1998]
see below).

## Step category

`TIN2` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *PVD* class — the category page's account of magnetron
sputtering, its line-of-sight flux and poor {term}`step coverage` in
holes, and of the IMP remedy applies directly. In this reference it
is one of a family of liners: Ti/TiN at {ref}`TI/TIN1 <step-097>`,
TiN here and at {ref}`TIN3 <step-120>`, {ref}`TIN4 <step-131>`,
{ref}`TIN5 <step-146>` for the {term}`vias <via>`, each followed by a {term}`CVD` tungsten
fill and a tungsten polish. What is specific to this instance is the
substrate — TiN {term}`local interconnect` rather than silicide — and the
hole size, the smallest of the tungsten-filled holes above the local
contact level.

## Why this step exists

A tungsten plug cannot be grown directly into an oxide hole:

* **Nucleation and adhesion.** CVD tungsten nucleates poorly and
  adheres badly on oxide; a metal or nitride liner gives the
  WF₆-based process a surface to start on. Srinivas et al. studied the
  nucleation of tungsten on TiN under hydrogen reduction of
  WF₆,[^srinivas-1992] and the plug scheme itself — tungsten studs in
  holes, planarised — is Kaanta et al.'s.[^kaanta-1987]
* **Barrier against the precursor.** WF₆ attacks titanium and silicon,
  and the fluorine it releases can lift or blister a Ti-only liner
  (the "volcano" failure); TiN is inert to it. Wittmer reviewed why
  refractory-metal nitrides make good barriers,[^wittmer-1985] and
  Sundgren the structure of sputtered TiN.[^sundgren-1985] On a TiN
  `li` the barrier role is smaller than over silicide, but the plug
  still needs a continuous, fluorine-tolerant surface on the oxide
  sidewalls.
* **Coverage at the bottom of a narrow hole.** Ordinary sputtering
  delivers little metal to the floor of a hole with an {term}`aspect
  ratio` above about 2:1; ionising the sputtered metal and biasing the
  wafer makes the flux directional, so the bottom is coated. That is
  the point of IMP, first demonstrated by Rossnagel and Hopwood in
  1993[^rossnagel-1993] and reviewed by Rossnagel in 1998;[^rossnagel-1998]
  Hamaguchi and Rossnagel modelled the liner conformality it
  gives,[^hamaguchi-1996] and Mao and Hopwood built a deposition model
  for ionised {term}`PVD` of TiN specifically.[^mao-2004] Applied Materials'
  patents on alternating IMP and conventional sputtering show how
  sidewall and bottom coverage were traded.[^pat-imp-amat]
* **Contact resistance.** The liner is in series with every contact;
  the PDK's 152 000 mΩ (152 Ω) per `mcon`[^pdk-08] includes the TiN
  liner, the W/TiN and TiN/TiN interfaces and the tungsten itself.

Without `TIN2` the tungsten of {ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>` would not stick
to the oxide, the WF₆ would attack whatever it reached, and the plugs
would be voided or missing.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic IMP TiN liner deposition for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Cluster tool.** A multi-chamber PVD platform under one vacuum
   with a {term}`degas` chamber, a sputter pre-clean chamber and one or more
   PVD chambers; SkyWater's list names "Sputter etch, degas" alongside
   its PVD films,[^skw-01] and Applied Materials' Endura is the
   200 mm-era example of the architecture.[^amat-endura]
2. **Degas.** The wafer is heated under vacuum to drive out the water
   the oxide and the post-etch clean have left, so that it does not
   oxidise the liner or poison the plasma.
3. **Sputter pre-clean.** A low-energy argon (or Ar/H₂) plasma etch
   removes a few nanometres from the exposed surfaces — the native
   oxide and etch residue on the TiN floor — with care not to sputter
   oxide from the hole edge onto the floor (industry
   practice[^txt-05]).
4. **IMP TiN deposition.** {term}`Reactive sputtering <reactive sputtering>` of a titanium target
   in Ar/N₂: Berg and Nyberg describe the reactive-sputtering
   hysteresis that the N₂ flow must be controlled
   against.[^berg-2005] In an IMP chamber an RF coil between target
   and wafer sustains a dense secondary plasma (Hopwood reviews the
   inductively coupled sources[^hopwood-1992]) that ionises a large
   fraction of the sputtered titanium; a bias on the wafer draws the
   ions down the hole. Pressure is higher than in conventional PVD —
   tens of mTorr rather than a few — to lengthen the ionisation
   path.[^rossnagel-1998] Bottom coverage of tens of per cent of the
   field thickness results, against a few per cent for
   line-of-sight sputtering.[^rossnagel-1998][^hamaguchi-1996]
   {term}`Collimated sputtering <collimated sputtering>` — a honeycomb filter that passes only
   near-normal atoms — is the older route to the same
   end,[^rossnagel-1991] and SkyWater lists "Collimated Ti" beside
   "Imp TiN".[^skw-01]
5. **Thickness.** Not public. A 130 nm-era TiN contact liner is
   typically of the order of 10–30 nm on the field (industry-typical
   value[^txt-05]); the 0.09 µm bottom {term}`CD`[^pdk-03] leaves room for only
   a thin film on the sidewalls before the tungsten must close the
   hole.
6. **Optional treatment.** Some flows follow the liner with a short
   N₂/H₂ plasma or a rapid thermal step to densify the TiN and reduce
   its fluorine uptake; whether SKY130 does is not public.
7. **Metrology.** {term}`Sheet resistance <sheet resistance>` by {term}`four-point probe` on
   monitor wafers; thickness by X-ray fluorescence or reflectometry;
   stress by wafer bow; bottom and sidewall coverage by cross-section
   SEM during development; particles.

## Machines typically used

* **PVD cluster tool with an IMP (ionised PVD) TiN chamber**, 200 mm:
  Applied Materials Endura with IMP chambers,[^amat-endura][^pat-imp-amat]
  Novellus INOVA with hollow-cathode-magnetron ionised
  PVD[^pat-hcm-novellus] ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **Degas and sputter pre-clean chambers** on the same platform.
* **Four-point probe**, **XRF thickness gauge**, **stress gauge**,
  **cross-section SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **AMAT PVD Metal platform with "Imp TiN".** SkyWater lists "AMAT
  PVD Metal" with the films "Sputter etch, degas", "Aluminum both pure
  and Cu doped", "TiW", "ESC TiN", "Imp TiN", "Collimated Ti", "WN",
  "Cobalt", "Niobium".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the vendor and
  for an IMP TiN capability. The platform model (Endura is the
  natural reading[^amat-endura]) and the use of the "Imp TiN" chamber
  for the contact liner are **inferences** from that capability and
  from the contact's aspect ratio.
* **Novellus INOVA** is not on SkyWater's list; the "Lam/Novellus"
  entries there are for CVD.[^skw-01]

## Resources required

* **Titanium sputter target** (high-purity Ti; SkyWater's filings name Honeywell Electronic Materials (2021
  S-1 and fiscal 2023 10-K) and JX Metals (fiscal 2023 10-K)
  as sputter-target suppliers[^sec-01][^sec-02]).
* **Argon** (sputter and pre-clean) and **nitrogen** (reactive
  gas);[^berg-2005] possibly **hydrogen** in the pre-clean.
* **Chamber consumables** — shields, coil, clamp or electrostatic
  chuck parts — and **target changes** on a kilowatt-hour schedule.
* **Monitor wafers** for sheet resistance and particles.

## Open questions

* The liner thickness, whether the film is TiN alone or TiN over a
  thin Ti, the bias and pressure of the IMP process, and whether a
  post-deposition plasma or thermal treatment is used are not public.
* Whether the "Sputter etch" pre-clean SkyWater lists[^skw-01] is used
  before this liner, and its depth, is not public.
* The PVD platform model is inferred from "AMAT PVD Metal" and the
  film list;[^skw-01] no public source names an Endura at SkyWater.
* The contribution of the liner to the 152 Ω `mcon`[^pdk-08] is not
  separated in any public source.
