## What this step is

`NWI2` is the second N-well implant, performed through the same resist
windows as {ref}`NWI <step-018>` and immediately after it. This
reference describes it as a second phosphorus dose at a different
energy, i.e. it treats the SKY130 N-well as a *chained* implant. That
is an inference from practice; no public document describes the two
implants. Chaining is the universal way of building a
{term}`retrograde well`: a deep, heavy implant sets the buried peak and one or
two shallower, lighter implants fill in the profile between the peak and
the surface.[^txt-01] Morris and Rubin's implanter comparison assumes
"an advanced CMOS process containing multiple modulated well implant
steps".[^morris-2000] The category page draws the same inference for
the `NWI2`/`PWI2` pairs.

## Step category

`NWI2` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*well* class, sharing the mask, the tool class and the anneal of
{ref}`NWI <step-018>`. Depending on its energy it may run on the same
high-energy batch implanter or on a medium-current tool.

## Why this step exists

A single Gaussian implant cannot make a good well. Its peak
concentration N_max ≈ 0.4 Φ/ΔR_p (category page) falls off on both
sides, so a lone MeV implant leaves the region between its peak and the
surface nearly at the background doping of 8 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³.[^pdk-03] That
region is where the PMOS channel, its source/drain junctions and the
field between neighbouring devices sit, and each needs its own doping
level:

* **Under the field oxide**, a "field stop" raises the doping so that
  the trench bottom does not invert and short adjacent devices; a Hynix
  triple-well patent uses a dedicated "p channel field stop" phosphorus
  implant "of about 150 kev to about 300 keV" at 5 × 10¹¹–2 ×
  10¹³ cm⁻².[^pat-dnw-hynix]
* **Under the channel**, a moderate peak a few tenths of a micrometre
  down suppresses {term}`punch-through` — the retrograde-well *raison
  d'être*.[^itrs-01][^pat-umc-dc]
* **At the well edge**, the shallower implants set the lateral junction
  with the P-well and hence the n⁺/p⁺ spacing rules[^rubin-2002] (the
  PDK gives a 0.034 µm junction offset).[^pdk-03]

Published twin-well flows therefore use two or three energies per well:
500/275/130 keV,[^pat-twin-harris] 700 keV + 120 keV,[^pat-well-hynix]
850/550/50 keV.[^pat-well-ibm] SKY130's pair, `NWI` + `NWI2`, is the
two-energy version of the same idea, and the PDK's statement that a
0.55 µm wide well is needed "to guarantee 90 % peak concentr."[^pdk-03]
is the lateral-straggle consequence of implanting the peak deep.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic second N-well implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (illustrative values only):

* **Species.** Phosphorus, as for `NWI`.[^wiki-implant] Arsenic is
  occasionally used for the shallowest member of a chain because its
  shorter range and lower diffusivity keep the surface profile
  sharp,[^txt-01] but phosphorus is the norm.
* **Energy.** Lower than the deep implant: the published shallow members
  lie between about 50 and
  300 keV.[^pat-well-ibm][^pat-well-hynix][^pat-dnw-hynix][^pat-twin-harris]
  Whether SKY130's second implant is the shallower or the deeper of its
  pair is not stated publicly.
* **Dose.** Typically a few 10¹² cm⁻², lower than the deep implant (2.0
  × 10¹² cm⁻² at 120 keV against 2.0 × 10¹³ cm⁻² at
  700 keV).[^pat-well-hynix]
* **Tilt/twist.** As for `NWI`; a shallower implant is more sensitive to
  {term}`channelling`, so 7° with twist is the safer
  choice.[^wiki-implant][^txt-02]
* **Sequence.** The step list places `NWI2` directly after `NWI`; it
  does not say which tool runs either implant.[^steps-sheet]
  Resist temperature is the budget shared between the two implants.
* **Anneal.** {ref}`RTAI <step-034>`, with `NWI`.[^pat-03]

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`High-energy batch implanter <machine-high-energy-implanter>`** (Axcelis GSD/HE, Varian VIISta 3000)
  if the energy is above the medium-current ceiling.
* **{ref}`Medium-current implanter <machine-medium-current-implanter>`** (Axcelis 8250 class, Varian E500) for
  energies below a few hundred keV; the Eaton 8250HT is rated "3keV to
  750keV".[^axcelis-8250]
* **{term}`Four-point probe <four-point probe>` / {ref}`thermal-wave <machine-sheet-resistance-metrology>`** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD high-current/high-energy implanter** ("B11, BF2, P, As,
  10-3000kev").[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for existence; assignment is
  an inference. Both N-well implants, at the energies published for
  well implants of this kind (50–850 keV in the examples above), fall
  within the GSD's listed 10–3000 keV range,[^skw-01] so one tool could
  run both (inference); the step list does not say which tool runs
  either implant.[^steps-sheet]
* **Axcelis 8250** is listed without phosphorus ("B11, BF2,
  As"),[^skw-01] which argues against it for a phosphorus implant.
  Strength: strong for existence.

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Phosphine <material-dopant-sources>` (PH₃)** or solid phosphorus; support gases; cryopump
  and source consumables; {ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>` (category page).
* No additional lithography materials: the resist is the
  {ref}`NWM <step-017>` resist.

## Open questions

* That `NWI2` is a second energy of the same species is an inference
  from retrograde-well practice;[^txt-01] its energy, dose and purpose ({term}`field stop`,
  punch-through, or lateral profile) are not public.
* Whether it runs on the same implanter as `NWI` is unknown.
