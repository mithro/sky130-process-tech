## What this step is

`NWI2` is the second N-well implant, performed through the same resist
windows as {ref}`NWI <step-018>` and immediately after it. The step
list gives it no descriptive name beyond "NWI2 implant"; the numbering
and its position between `NWI` and the strip tell us that it is a
second phosphorus dose at a different energy, i.e. that the SKY130
N-well is a *chained* implant. That is our inference from the naming;
no public document describes the two implants. Chaining is, however,
the universal way of building a retrograde well: a deep, heavy implant
sets the buried peak and one or two shallower, lighter implants fill
in the profile between the peak and the surface (TXT-01, ch. 2;
MORRIS-2000). The category page draws the same inference for the
`NWI2`/`PWI2` pairs.

## Step category

`NWI2` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*well* class, sharing the mask, the tool class and the anneal of
{ref}`NWI <step-018>`. Depending on its energy it may run on the same
high-energy batch implanter or on a medium-current tool.

## Why this step exists

A single Gaussian implant cannot make a good well. Its peak
concentration N_max ≈ 0.4 Φ/ΔR_p (category page) falls off on both
sides, so a lone MeV implant leaves the region between its peak and
the surface nearly at the background doping of 8 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³
(PDK-03). That region is where the PMOS channel, its source/drain
junctions and the field between neighbouring devices sit, and each
needs its own doping level:

* **Under the field oxide**, a "field stop" raises the doping so that
  the trench bottom does not invert and short adjacent devices; a
  Hynix triple-well patent uses a dedicated "p channel field stop"
  phosphorus implant "of about 150 kev to about 300 keV" at
  5 × 10¹¹–2 × 10¹³ cm⁻² (PAT-DNW-HYNIX).
* **Under the channel**, a moderate peak a few tenths of a micrometre
  down suppresses punch-through — the retrograde-well *raison d'être*
  (ITRS-01; PAT-UMC-DC).
* **At the well edge**, the shallower implants set the lateral
  junction with the P-well and hence the n⁺/p⁺ spacing rules
  (RUBIN-2002; PDK-03 gives 0.034 µm junction offset).

Published twin-well flows therefore use two or three energies per
well: 500/275/130 keV (PAT-TWIN-HARRIS), 700 keV + 120 keV
(PAT-WELL-HYNIX), 850/550/50 keV (PAT-WELL-IBM). SKY130's pair,
`NWI` + `NWI2`, is the two-energy version of the same idea, and the
PDK's statement that a 0.55 µm wide well is needed "to guarantee
90 % peak concentr." (PDK-03) is the lateral-straggle consequence of
implanting the peak deep.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic second N-well implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (illustrative values only):

* **Species.** Phosphorus, as for `NWI` (WIKI-IMPLANT). Arsenic is
  occasionally used for the shallowest member of a chain because its
  shorter range and lower diffusivity keep the surface profile sharp
  (TXT-01, ch. 8), but phosphorus is the norm.
* **Energy.** Lower than the deep implant: the published shallow
  members lie between about 50 and 300 keV (PAT-WELL-IBM,
  PAT-WELL-HYNIX, PAT-DNW-HYNIX, PAT-TWIN-HARRIS). Whether SKY130's
  second implant is the shallower or the deeper of its pair is not
  stated; the numbering suggests only order.
* **Dose.** Typically a few 10¹² cm⁻², lower than the deep implant
  (PAT-WELL-HYNIX: 2.0 × 10¹² cm⁻² at 120 keV against 2.0 × 10¹³ cm⁻²
  at 700 keV).
* **Tilt/twist.** As for `NWI`; a shallower implant is more
  sensitive to channelling, so 7° with twist is the safer choice
  (WIKI-IMPLANT; TXT-02).
* **Sequence.** Run back-to-back with `NWI` in the same tool if the
  energies permit, so the wafers stay in the batch; otherwise the
  cassette moves to a medium-current tool. Resist temperature is the
  budget shared between the two implants.
* **Anneal.** {ref}`RTAI <step-034>`, with `NWI` (PAT-03).

## Machines typically used

* **High-energy batch implanter** (Axcelis GSD/HE, Varian VIISta 3000)
  if the energy is above the medium-current ceiling.
* **Medium-current implanter** (Axcelis 8250 class, Varian E500) for
  energies below a few hundred keV; the Eaton 8250HT is rated "3keV
  to 750keV" (AXCELIS-8250).
* **Four-point probe / thermal-wave** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD** (SKW-01: "B11, BF2, P, As, 10-3000kev"). Strength:
  **strong** for existence; assignment is an inference. Because the
  GSD's energy range starts at 10 keV, both N-well implants can be
  run on it without changing tools, which is the simplest reading of
  the back-to-back steps.
* **Axcelis 8250** is listed without phosphorus ("B11, BF2, As",
  SKW-01), which argues against it for a phosphorus implant.
  Strength: strong for existence.

## Resources required

* **Phosphine (PH₃)** or solid phosphorus; support gases; cryopump
  and source consumables; monitor wafers (category page).
* No additional lithography materials: the resist is the
  {ref}`NWM <step-017>` resist.
