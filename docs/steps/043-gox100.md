## What this step is

`GOX100` is the first of SKY130's two gate oxidations: the *thick*
gate oxide. With the ONO stack now confined to the memory cells and
the logic silicon cleared at {ref}`ONOME <step-042>`, the wafer is
oxidised to grow a gate-quality silicon dioxide over every active
area. Three steps later this oxide is stripped from the 1.8 V regions
({ref}`LVOM <step-044>`, {ref}`GOXETCH <step-046>`) and a thin oxide
grown in its place ({ref}`LVGOX <step-047>`); where it is *not*
stripped it becomes the gate oxide of the 5 V transistors and of the
10–20 V drain-extended devices, thickened slightly by the second
oxidation.

The PDK gives the finished thickness: "All high voltage devices use
110A gate oxide thickness just like low voltage (0 to Vcc) devices"
and "All VHV devices use 110A gate oxide thickness just like standard
5.0V Vcc devices",[^pdk-hv] and the public SPICE model of the 5 V NMOS
carries `toxe = 1.16e-8` (11.6 nm) as its oxide thickness for
electrical purposes.[^pdk-model-nfet5v] Because the final thick oxide
is the sum of this growth and the increment added at
{ref}`LVGOX <step-047>` ({ref}`category-oxidation`, *Dual gate oxide
processes*), the oxide grown *here* is somewhat thinner than 11 nm.
The "100" in the step code is not explained in any public source and
this reference does not interpret it.

The oxidation also acts on the ONO islands. Cypress's integration
patent sequences the ONO "prior to formation of the logic MOS gate
insulator so that formation of the logic MOS gate insulator with a
thermal process has the advantage of additionally serving to reoxidize
the ONO charge trapping dielectric stack", which "may have the effect
of densifying a CVD formed blocking layer";[^pat-03] in its later cell
patent the oxidation "is adapted to oxidize the first cap layer 232a
to form the blocking oxide layer 238 while simultaneously or
concurrently oxidizing at least a portion of the surface 216 of the
substrate 204 in the second region 208 to form a first gate oxide
240".[^pat-04] A third Cypress patent explains the hazard of that
coupling for thick-oxide flows: "In some process flows, HV MOS gate
oxide and NVM blocking oxide are formed concurrently. While the HV
MOS gate oxide may be formed/grown to its required thickness, since
blocking or top oxide of the NVM transistor is subjected to the same
environment during oxidation of the gate oxide of HV MOS
transistor(s), it may be grown to be too thick", and it therefore
grows the thick gate oxide *before* forming the ONO.[^pat-ono-thick-cyp]
The step list used in this reference follows the former ordering
(ONO first); how SKY130's blocking oxide is protected during `GOX100`
is not public.

## Step category

`GOX100` is a {ref}`Thermal oxidation <category-oxidation>` step of
the *gate oxide* class — grown dry or in dilute steam at 800–900 °C to
a thickness controlled to a few per cent, in contrast to the
mechanical pad and liner oxides of {ref}`BOX <step-002>` and
{ref}`LINOX <step-010>`. Among the gate oxidations it is the thick,
first-pass one; {ref}`LVGOX <step-047>` is the thin, second-pass one.
It is the first step in the flow whose film survives into a finished
transistor.

## Why this step exists

Every MOSFET needs a gate dielectric; the 5 V and high-voltage
transistors of SKY130 need a thick one. A gate oxide "serves as the
dielectric layer so that the gate can sustain as high as 1 to 5 MV/cm
transverse electric field",[^wiki-gate-oxide] and the PDK's HV rules
set the failure point of the thick oxide at "7.3 V @ 25C" for HV NMOS
and "8.1 V @ 25C" for HV PMOS gate-to-channel[^pdk-hv] — voltages that
a 4 nm oxide could not hold. The thick oxide is therefore what makes
the "5.0V I/O"[^pdk-10] and the "20+V, Drain-Extended CMOS (16V)"[^skw-02]
devices possible. ITRS 2001 shows the same split in another form: a
2.0–2.8 nm equivalent oxide thickness for 130 nm-node low-power logic
beside 5 nm for the DRAM transfer device of the same year.[^itrs-01]

**Why the thick oxide is grown first.** Three reasons, all generic:

* **Thermal budget.** The thin oxide is the most sensitive film in the
  logic flow and must see as little heat as possible after it is
  grown; the thick oxide needs the longer or hotter cycle, so it goes
  first and the thin oxide last ({ref}`category-oxidation`).
* **Etch exposure.** The oxide that is grown first is the one that
  gets masked and etched; a thin oxide would not survive being the
  masked layer, whereas a thick oxide survives the small additional
  growth of the second oxidation.[^togo-1998]
* **Additive growth.** Under the Deal–Grove law an existing oxide
  simply continues to grow during the second oxidation, at the slower
  parabolic-regime rate, so the increment added to the thick oxide is
  small and predictable, while the bare silicon of the 1.8 V regions
  receives the full thin-oxide growth.[^deal-1965] Both must be
  modelled together ({ref}`category-oxidation`).

Growing the thick oxide right after the ONO also serves the memory:
it reoxidises and densifies the blocking oxide[^pat-03] and provides
part of the thermal budget that activates the {ref}`PTSI <step-037>`
and {ref}`DEPI <step-038>` implants.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic thick-gate oxidation for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
with an embedded ONO (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Pre-clean.** The last oxide over the logic silicon must be gone
   and the surface pristine. Two Cypress approaches are public: a
   non-HF clean because "Conventional HF-based gate insulator
   pre-cleans will etch or otherwise degrade the quality of the ONO
   charge trapping dielectric stack",[^pat-03] with ozonated water in
   place of SC-1;[^pat-03] or a BOE "GOX preclean" that removes a
   sacrificial cap over the ONO together with "any oxide, such as an
   oxide tunnel dielectric 228 and pad oxide 209, remaining … in the
   second region 208 to prepare the substrate 204 in that region for
   gate oxide growth".[^pat-04] Which SKY130 uses is not public.
2. **Oxidation.** In a vertical furnace at 800–900 °C. Cypress
   describes "a dilute wet oxidation" in which "the H₂:O₂ ratio is
   between 1 and 1.3", "at a temperature of between 800° C. and
   900° C.", for a duration "sufficient to grow between 5.0 nm and
   15.0 nm of silicon dioxide";[^pat-03] the alternative is dry O₂ with
   a chlorine source (HCl or DCE) for mobile-ion gettering and
   interface quality ({ref}`category-oxidation`).[^txt-01] Growth in
   this thickness range is in the transition from the linear to the
   parabolic Deal–Grove regime,[^deal-1965] with the thin-oxide
   enhancement still contributing.[^massoud-1985] A single-wafer
   radical or ISSG oxidation is the other public option — H₂ and O₂
   reacting "at a temperature approximately in the range of
   700-800° C. at a pressure approximately in the range of 0.5-5 Torr"
   for "1-5 minutes for a single substrate using an ISSG process, or
   10-15 minutes" in a batch tool[^pat-04] — and Cypress notes that its
   results hold "whether the thick gate oxide is formed by a furnace
   process or RTO process".[^pat-ono-thick-cyp]
3. **Post-oxidation anneal.** A short inert (N₂ or Ar) anneal in the
   same tube reduces fixed charge and interface traps, the charges of
   Deal's standard terminology;[^deal-1980] a nitridation may be
   deferred to the thin-oxide step ({ref}`LVGOX <step-047>`).
4. **Metrology.** Spectroscopic ellipsometry on monitor wafers
   (thickness to ±0.1 nm), and later C–V on test structures; ITRS 2001
   asks for EOT control of "<± 4" % 3σ.[^itrs-01]

## Machines typically used

* **Vertical oxidation furnace**, 200 mm, with dry O₂, pyrogenic or
  dilute-steam H₂/O₂ and HCl/DCE delivery: ASM A400, TEL Alpha-8,
  Aviza/Thermco ({ref}`category-oxidation`).
* **Rapid thermal oxidation / ISSG** chamber as the single-wafer
  alternative: Applied Materials RTP Centura, AG Associates Heatpulse
  ({ref}`category-oxidation`).[^nulman-1985][^yu-1999]
* **Spectroscopic ellipsometer**; **C–V** test.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Aviza vertical furnaces.** "Furnaces are all made by Aviza: wet
  oxidation to 1150C, dry oxidation to 1150C".[^skw-01] Strength:
  **strong** for the tool; **inference** for the assignment of
  `GOX100` (the step is a furnace-class oxidation by every public
  description).
* **AG Associates Heatpulse 8808** ("NH3, Ar, N2, O2, up to
  1200C")[^skw-01] — an RTO alternative. Strength: strong for
  existence; weak for assignment.

## Resources required

* **Oxygen**; **hydrogen** for dilute-steam or ISSG
  oxidation;[^pat-03][^pat-04] **nitrogen/argon** for purge and
  anneal.[^skw-01]
* **HCl or trans-1,2-dichloroethylene** as chlorine source
  ({ref}`category-oxidation`).
* **Ozonated water, SC-1/SC-2 or BOE** for the pre-clean.[^pat-03][^pat-04]
* **Quartz tubes, boats, liners**; **monitor wafers**.
* Gas suppliers named by SkyWater: Air Products, Praxair, Linde,
  Airgas.[^sec-01][^sec-02]

## Open questions

* The thickness grown at `GOX100` (as opposed to the 110 Å final
  thick oxide) is not public, and the meaning of "100" in the step
  code is not explained publicly.
* Dry versus dilute-steam versus RTO/ISSG, temperature and time are
  not public.
* Whether the pre-clean is HF-free (protecting the exposed ONO) or
  BOE-based with a sacrificial cap is not public.

  too thick during this oxidation is not public.
