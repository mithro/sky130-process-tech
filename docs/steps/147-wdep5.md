## What this step is

`WDEP5` fills the via-3 holes with tungsten. A blanket
chemical-vapour-deposited tungsten film is grown over the whole wafer
— on the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN5 <step-146>`, across the field and
into every 0.20 µm via — thick enough that the holes close from their
sidewalls inward and the surface above them is continuous metal. The
film is polished away from the field at {ref}`WCMP5 <step-148>`,
leaving tungsten only in the holes: the {term}`W plug` that the PDK
calls `via3`, "Contact from metal 3 to metal 4".[^pdk-06] It is the
fifth and last tungsten fill in the step list used in this reference,
after {ref}`WDEP <step-099>`, {ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>`,
{ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` and {ref}`WDEP4 <step-132>`; via 4 above
({ref}`VIM4 <step-159>`, 0.8 µm wide (via4.1[^pdk-periph])) is
followed directly by the metal-5 stack.

The holes being filled are 0.20 µm wide (via3.1[^pdk-periph]) and, to
metal 3, 0.39 µm deep[^pdk-04] — an {term}`aspect ratio` of about
1.95:1, narrowed by the liner — and, over the {term}`MiM capacitors <MiM capacitor>`,
shallower holes that land on the top plates (TiW, as assumed at
{ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>`; the PDK's `cap_mim`
cross-section draws vias from metal 4 landing on "CAPM"[^pdk-07]). Since a conformal fill
closes a hole by growing inward from its sidewalls, the closing
thickness is set by the hole width, not its depth, so one film fills
both (inference from the growth mode[^ireland-1997]). The deposited
thickness is not public; a blanket fill must exceed half the hole
width plus an over-fill margin, so a film of the order of 0.25–0.35 µm
is the industry norm for a via of this size (industry-typical
value[^txt-05]). SkyWater describes its capability as "Lam/Novellus
PECVD Tungsten – plug fill – PNL option for high aspect ratio (up to
10:1)".[^skw-01] The PDK's `via3` is 3 410 mΩ,[^pdk-08] the plug
included.

## Step category

`WDEP5` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *CVD tungsten* class; the category page and {ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>`
set out the process — a silane- or diborane-reduced
{term}`nucleation layer`, then hydrogen reduction of WF₆ at roughly
400–450 °C and tens of Torr, almost perfectly conformal — and its
kinetics.[^rev-03][^mcconica-1986] {ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` and
{ref}`WDEP4 <step-132>` describe what changes between aluminium levels.
What is specific to this instance is what the plugs connect: metal 3
to metal 4, the two thick 0.845 µm levels of the stack
diagram,[^pdk-04] and the top plates of the first MiM capacitor to
metal 4. A plug on a capacitor plate sits on a TiW film that itself
rests on a few tens of nanometres of dielectric, so the plug's stress
and the WF₆ chemistry reach closer to a device dielectric than at any
earlier tungsten level (inference from the construction).

## Why this step exists

Tungsten plugs are how an aluminium back end connects its levels
through holes it cannot fill with sputtered metal, since Kaanta et
al.'s tungsten studs of 1987.[^kaanta-1987] The reasons are those of
{ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>`; the via-3 level adds:

* **Conformal fill of two kinds of hole.** Surface-reaction-limited CVD
  coats the sidewalls at the field rate, so each via fills from the
  outside in; Ireland's review of the plug process[^ireland-1997] and
  Saito et al.'s reliability study of blanket-tungsten filling of
  high-aspect-ratio holes[^saito-1993] describe the practice. The {term}`PNL`
  option SkyWater names[^skw-01] — Novellus's pulsed nucleation layer
  for improved step coverage[^pat-pnl-novellus] — is not needed at
  under 2:1 but may be used if the tool runs it by default (inference);
  Kim et al. describe its benefit for plug fill.[^kim-2004]
* **Nucleation on the liner, not in the via.** Tungsten nucleates on
  the TiN of {ref}`TIN5 <step-146>`[^srinivas-1992][^mcconica-1988]
  through a thin nucleation layer;[^hegde-1997] where the sidewall
  outgasses or carries residue the nucleation stalls and the via is
  poisoned ({term}`via poisoning`).[^kobayakawa-1991][^pat-via-poison-umc]
* **Protecting the capacitor plate from the precursor.** WF₆ and the
  HF it releases would attack a TiW plate through any gap in the
  liner; the liner's continuity over the plate vias is what prevents
  it (inference from the chemistry; Wittmer's barrier role for
  refractory nitrides[^wittmer-1985]).
* **Current and reliability.** Metal 3 and metal 4 carry the
  block-level power and long signals of a five-metal design (0.3 µm
  width, 47 mΩ/sq[^pdk-periph][^pdk-08]); under current the plug is the
  flux-divergence point where the aluminium on either side
  voids by {term}`electromigration`.[^kwok-1990][^hu-1993] Kawasaki and
  Hu model tungsten-plug via
  lifetimes,[^kawasaki-1996] Le, Banerjee and McPherson show the
  dependence on via size,[^le-1996] Li analysed the reservoir effect
  and allowed via misalignment for Al–Cu lines with tungsten
  plugs,[^li-2003] and Walls and Domae et al. report
  {term}`stress voiding <stress-induced voiding>` in
  tungsten-plug and stacked-via systems[^walls-1995][^domae-1998] —
  relevant where via 3 stacks on via 2.
* **Stress.** CVD tungsten is strongly tensile — Shioya et al.
  measured it at temperature[^shioya-1987] — and a blanket film over
  three aluminium levels bows the wafer; the film is kept to the
  minimum that fills.

Without `WDEP5` the lined vias would be left for the sputtered
aluminium of {ref}`WTIAL4 <step-149>` to fill, which cannot reach
their bottoms; metal 4 would be open to metal 3 and to the capacitor
plates.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic blanket tungsten via fill for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the chemistry is set out in full
at {ref}`WDEP2 <step-110>`.

1. **Chamber.** A single-wafer or multi-station CVD reactor with a
   heated pedestal at about 400–450 °C, showerhead delivery and remote
   NF₃ clean (industry-typical values[^txt-01][^wiki-wf6]); Novellus's
   multi-station Altus family[^novellus-history] is the reference
   design for the "Lam/Novellus" tool SkyWater lists.[^skw-01]
   SkyWater's wording "PECVD Tungsten"[^skw-01] we read as a label
   rather than a statement of plasma-driven deposition (inference).
2. **Nucleation layer.** WF₆ reduced by SiH₄ (or B₂H₆) for a few
   nanometres, pulsed if the PNL option is used;[^pat-pnl-novellus][^wiki-wf6]
   the layer is resistive and is kept thin.[^hegde-1997]
3. **Bulk fill.** Hydrogen reduction, WF₆ + 3H₂ → W + 6HF, at tens of
   Torr with a large H₂ excess,[^rev-03][^mcconica-1986] at several
   hundred nanometres per minute (industry-typical[^txt-01]).
4. **Thickness.** More than 0.10 µm on each sidewall of a 0.20 µm via
   plus an over-fill; of the order of 0.25–0.35 µm on the field
   (industry-typical value[^txt-05]). The 0.8 µm via3 allowed inside
   e-test modules (via3.1a[^pdk-periph]) cannot be closed by such a film
   and is left as a lined, partly filled hole (inference).
5. **Edge and clean.** Edge exclusion or bevel removal where the liner
   is absent; NF₃ chamber clean (industry practice[^txt-05]).
6. **Metrology.** Thickness and sheet resistance on monitors by
   {term}`four-point probe`; stress by wafer bow; cross-section SEM for
   seams; particles; via-3 chain resistance to metal 3 and to plates,
   and capacitor leakage, at {term}`e-test`.

## Open questions

* The nucleation chemistry, bulk thickness, temperature and pressure
  of the via-3 fill are not public.
* Whether the PNL option[^skw-01] is used at this level is not public.
* Whether plugs landing on capacitor plates need any special treatment
  (plug density limits, stress control) is not public.
* No public source confirms our inference that the 0.8 µm e-test via3
  (via3.1a)[^pdk-periph] is left as a lined, partly filled hole.
* SkyWater's phrase "PECVD Tungsten"[^skw-01] is read here as a label
  for the tungsten CVD tool; no public source clarifies it.
