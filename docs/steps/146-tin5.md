## What this step is

`TIN5` deposits the {term}`liner` of the via-3 plug: a thin titanium
nitride film sputtered over the whole wafer — across the
{ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` cap oxide and down into every 0.20 µm via
opened at {ref}`VIM3E <step-145>` — coating the oxide sidewalls and
the floors, which are of two kinds: the titanium–tungsten cap of a
metal-3 line, or the top plate of a {term}`MiM capacitor` (TiW, as
assumed at {ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>`; the PDK's `cap_mim`
cross-section draws vias from metal 4 landing on
both[^pdk-07]). The liner is described here as {term}`IMP` TiN
(ionised-metal-plasma physical vapour deposition, in which the
sputtered titanium is ionised and drawn into the hole by a wafer
bias[^rossnagel-1998]). SkyWater lists "Imp TiN" among its AMAT PVD
films,[^skw-01] and ionised PVD was the 200 mm-era way to coat the
floor of a hole this deep (inference). {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>`–{ref}`TIN4 <step-131>`
describe the same liner below. The film is
the nucleation surface for the tungsten of {ref}`WDEP5 <step-147>`,
the barrier that keeps WF₆ and its fluorine off the TiW floors and off
any aluminium or plate edge an unlanded via has exposed, and the
adhesion layer for the plug; it is removed from the field, with the
tungsten, at {ref}`WCMP5 <step-148>`. Its thickness is not public; a
200 mm, 130 nm-era via liner is typically a few tens of nanometres on
the field and much less on the via floor (industry-typical
value[^txt-05][^rossnagel-1998]).

The via to metal 3 is 0.20 µm wide (via3.1[^pdk-periph]) and 0.39 µm
deep — the via-3 height of the PDK's stack diagram[^pdk-04] — an
{term}`aspect ratio` of about 1.95:1, between via 1's 1.8:1 and
via 2's 2.1:1. Its floor is, on the public description of the S8 metal
stacks, a 300 Å TiW cap over Al–Cu;[^cyp-qtp-113005] a via over a
capacitor is shallower by the plate and dielectric thickness (on our
reading 0.1–0.2 µm, estimated at {ref}`CAPILD <step-135>` and
{ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>`), so the same deposition coats holes of two
aspect ratios. The PDK's `via3` is 3 410 mΩ,[^pdk-08] liner and
interfaces included.

## Step category

`TIN5` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *PVD* class; {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>` sets out the physics of
ionised PVD at length and {ref}`TIN3 <step-120>` what changes when the
liner lands on an aluminium-alloy level. It is the fifth and last
IMP TiN liner described in this reference; via 4
above it ({ref}`VIM4 <step-159>`) is 0.8 µm wide (via4.1[^pdk-periph])
and is followed by a different deposition. What is specific to this
instance is the second floor: a capacitor electrode on a thin
dielectric, onto which the sputter pre-clean and the ion-bombarded
IMP deposition both act. The TiW plate is several times thicker than
the metal-3 cap on our reading, which is what lets it absorb a light
pre-clean (inference), and the plate edge must not be reached by
either.

## Why this step exists

A tungsten via cannot be grown directly into the hole
{ref}`VIM3E <step-145>` leaves:

* **Nucleation and adhesion.** CVD tungsten nucleates poorly and
  adheres badly on oxide; the liner gives the WF₆ process a surface to
  start on.[^srinivas-1992] Ireland reviews the liner-plus-plug process
  for high-aspect-ratio holes,[^ireland-1997] and Wang et al. the
  optimisation of a Ti/TiN liner for a tungsten plug.[^wang-1996-liner]
* **Barrier against the precursor.** WF₆ and the HF it releases attack
  titanium-bearing films and aluminium; a continuous TiN film on floor
  and sidewalls keeps the fluorine off the TiW cap, off the capacitor
  plate and off any exposed Al–Cu sidewall — Wittmer's barrier role
  for refractory nitrides.[^wittmer-1985]
* **Coverage at the bottom of a 2:1 hole.** Ordinary sputtering
  delivers little metal to the floor of a hole of this aspect ratio;
  IMP makes the flux directional.[^rossnagel-1993][^rossnagel-1998]
  Hamaguchi and Rossnagel modelled liner conformality in ionised
  sputtering,[^hamaguchi-1996] Mao and Hopwood built a deposition model
  for ionised PVD of TiN,[^mao-2004] and Applied Materials' patent on
  alternating IMP and conventional sputtering shows how sidewall and
  bottom coverage were traded.[^pat-imp-amat]
* **Via resistance and reliability.** The liner is in series with
  every `via3`,[^pdk-08] and the W/TiN/TiW interface is where a
  tungsten-plug via fails under {term}`electromigration`;[^kwok-1990][^hu-1993]
  Bui et al. found the cap beneath the plug controls the via's
  lifetime,[^bui-1994] and Kawasaki and Hu modelled tungsten-plug via
  lifetimes.[^kawasaki-1996] For a capacitor contact the same series
  resistance adds to the plate's 5.8 Ω/sq[^pdk-07] in the capacitor's
  {term}`quality factor` (inference).

Without `TIN5` the tungsten of {ref}`WDEP5 <step-147>` would not stick
to the oxide, its precursor would attack the metal-3 caps and the
capacitor plates, and the vias would be voided, resistive or open.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic IMP TiN via-liner deposition for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the sequence is that
of {ref}`TIN3 <step-120>` and {ref}`TIN4 <step-131>`.

1. **{term}`Cluster tool <cluster tool>`.** A multi-chamber PVD platform under one vacuum
   with degas, sputter pre-clean and IMP chambers; SkyWater lists
   "Sputter etch, degas" beside its PVD films,[^skw-01] and Applied
   Materials' Endura is the 200 mm-era example.[^amat-endura]
2. **Degas.** A vacuum bake to drive out the water the open vias have
   absorbed — the first defence against the poisoned via, whose origin
   in dielectric outgassing Kobayakawa et al. and Romero et al.
   documented.[^kobayakawa-1991][^romero-1991]
3. **Sputter pre-clean.** A low-energy argon (or Ar/H₂) plasma etch
   to remove fluoride and oxide residue from the TiW floors, kept
   light so as not to thin the 300 Å metal-3 cap[^cyp-qtp-113005] or
   the capacitor plate, and not to redeposit oxide from the via edge
   (industry practice[^txt-05]); Boumerzoug et al. report a plasma
   treatment that removes oxide from a refractory cap and lowers via
   resistance.[^boumerzoug-1997]
4. **IMP TiN deposition.** {term}`Reactive sputtering <reactive sputtering>` of a titanium target in
   Ar/N₂[^berg-2005] in an IMP chamber whose RF coil sustains a dense
   secondary plasma[^hopwood-1992] that ionises the titanium; a wafer
   bias draws the ions into the hole.[^rossnagel-1998]
   {term}`Collimated sputtering <collimated sputtering>`[^rossnagel-1991]
   is the older route; SkyWater lists
   "Collimated Ti" beside "Imp TiN".[^skw-01]
5. **Thickness.** Not public; of the order of 10–30 nm on the field
   for a 130 nm-era via liner (industry-typical value[^txt-05]).
6. **Metrology.** Sheet resistance by {term}`four-point probe` on
   monitors; thickness by X-ray fluorescence; stress by wafer bow;
   coverage by cross-section SEM during development; via-3 chain
   resistance to metal 3 and to capacitor plates later at
   {term}`e-test`.

## Machines typically used

* **PVD cluster tool with an IMP TiN chamber**, 200 mm: Applied
  Materials Endura with IMP chambers,[^amat-endura][^pat-imp-amat]
  Novellus INOVA with hollow-cathode-magnetron ionised
  PVD[^pat-hcm-novellus] ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **Degas and sputter pre-clean chambers** on the same platform.
* **Four-point probe**, **XRF thickness gauge**, **stress gauge**,
  **cross-section SEM**.
