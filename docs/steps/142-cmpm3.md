| **Category** | {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>` |

## What this step is

`CMPM3` polishes the inter-level dielectric ({term}`ILD`) over
metal 3 flat. The {ref}`NILD5 <step-141>` oxide arrives following the
metal-3 topography — the 0.845 µm step of the lines[^pdk-04], softened,
and on top of some lines the smaller step of a {term}`MiM capacitor` plate —
thick over wide lines, capacitor arrays and dense buses, thinner over
open field. The wafer is pressed against a pad flooded with alkaline
silica slurry (industry-standard for ILD; SKY130's is not public)
until that topography is gone and a planar oxide surface remains at a
controlled height above the metal. As at {ref}`CMPM <step-116>` and
{ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>` it is an *oxide-on-oxide* polish stopped by
removal amount, not by a stop layer; the cap of
{ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` then restores a fixed thickness before the
via-3 mask ({ref}`VIM3 <step-144>`) is printed. The finished
dielectric above metal 3 is, per the PDK's stack diagram, the 0.39 µm
via-3 height.[^pdk-04]

"CMP over metal3" is the third of the four back-end oxide polishes
({ref}`CMPM <step-116>`, {ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>`, here,
{ref}`CMPM4 <step-157>`). SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP" with "oxide"
first among its applications.[^skw-01] The PDK's metal-3 rules are
written around it: a "Min MM3_oxide_Pattern_density" of 0.7 (m3.pd.1)
checked in 700 µm square windows (m3.pd.2a) stepped by 70 µm
(m3.pd.2b), and a `cmm3 waffleDrop` fill algorithm that flags any
700 µm × 700 µm window whose metal density falls below stated levels
for its fill coverage;[^pdk-periph] the assumptions table gives an
"Oxide Bias for MM3" of 1.15 — the same as for MM4, against 0.6 for
MM1 and MM2 — a "Min pattern density for oxide" of 0.75 and a "Min
MM* PD range" of 0.3.[^pdk-03]

## Step category

`CMPM3` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *inter-level dielectric* type: plasma oxide, no stop
layer, silica slurry at pH 10–11, with thickness non-uniformity,
scratches and pattern-density steps as the failure modes.
{ref}`CMPM <step-116>` sets out the mechanism, the pattern-density
models and the recipe at length. Two things are specific to this
instance. The step to be planarised is more than twice that at the
lower levels (0.845 µm against 0.36 µm[^pdk-04]), so more oxide must
be deposited and removed and the {term}`planarisation length` of the
pad matters over longer distances. And the finished surface is
closer to a *device* than any earlier oxide polish: the capacitor top
plates stand above the metal-3 lines inside the 0.39 µm of via-3
dielectric, so the oxide left over a plate is less than that over a
bare line (inference from the construction; see below).

## Why this step exists

A subtractive aluminium back end accumulates topography with every
level; this polish removes the metal-3 topography and fixes:

* **Planarity for lithography.** The {ref}`VIM3 <step-144>` and
  {ref}`MM4 <step-154>` exposures need a surface flat within their
  depth of focus ({ref}`category-lithography`); Sivaram et al.
  modelled the removal rate and planarity of ILD CMP,[^sivaram-1992]
  Burke gave an early semi-empirical planarisation model,[^burke-1991]
  and Boning et al. and Chang et al. the statistical metrology of the
  thickness variation that results.[^boning-1994][^chang-1995] IBM's
  four-level fully planarised interconnect of 1991 demonstrated that
  this polish, repeated at each level, makes a multilevel aluminium
  back end manufacturable.[^uttecht-1991]
* **Enough oxide over the capacitors.** On our reading of
  {ref}`CAPILD <step-135>` and {ref}`CAPTIW1 <step-136>` the TiW plate
  top lies of the order of 0.1–0.2 µm above the metal-3 top; with a
  finished 0.39 µm above metal 3,[^pdk-04] only about 0.2–0.3 µm of
  oxide would remain over a plate (our arithmetic from
  estimated thicknesses). An over-polish that thins the field
  therefore approaches the plates first, and a polish that touched a
  plate would scratch it and expose its edge (inference). Ng et al.
  review MiM integration in Al–Cu and Cu back ends.[^ng-2005]
* **A uniform via-3 depth.** The {ref}`VIM3E <step-145>` etch must
  clear the full dielectric over every metal-3 landing while stopping
  on the shallower plates; the post-polish thickness range over metal 3
  and over the capacitors is that etch's over-etch budget.
* **{term}`Pattern-density <pattern density>` control.** Oxide over an isolated line is removed
  faster than over a dense array or a large capacitor, so the surface
  follows the layout density on the scale of the planarisation length
  (Ouma et al.[^ouma-2002]); Stine et al. showed how metal fill reduces
  the effect and what it costs in capacitance.[^stine-1998-ted] The
  `cmm3` fill, the 0.7 oxide-density rule and the 700 µm
  windows[^pdk-periph] are that practice in SKY130's rules; the UMC
  and TSMC dummy-pattern patents show it at other
  fabs.[^pat-dummy-umc][^pat-dummy-tsmc] The PDK's larger "Oxide Bias"
  for metals 3 and 4[^pdk-03] is, on our reading, the rule-deck trace
  of the thicker metal (inference).
* **Capacitance.** The oxide over metal 3 sets the metal-3-to-metal-4
  capacitance in the PDK's extraction tables (84.03 aF/µm² plate
  term).[^pdk-08]

Without `CMPM3` the via-3 chain would be open in some places and
shorted to capacitor plates in others, and metal 4 could not be
printed reliably.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic ILD polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's
recipe is not public); the sequence is that of {ref}`CMPM <step-116>`.

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher[^pat-cmp-mirra] with a
   stacked pad, a multi-zone carrier head and diamond conditioning;
   optical thickness endpoint through a pad window is available on
   this class of tool.[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm]
2. **Slurry.** Fumed or colloidal silica in KOH or NH₄OH at pH 10–11
   (industry-typical values[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]); Cook's
   mechanism of hydration and abrasive removal of the glass
   surface[^cook-1990] and Krishnan, Nalaskowski and Cook's review of
   slurry chemistry[^rev-02] describe it.
3. **Recipe.** Removal follows Preston's law,[^preston-1927] modified
   by the pad's contact mechanics;[^runnels-1994] Nanz and Camilletti
   review the models.[^nanz-1995] A first platen removes the bulk —
   more than at the lower levels, since the step is larger — a second
   trims to target, a buff platen removes residue. The target is the
   remaining oxide over metal 3 plus an allowance for the
   {ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` cap, so that the sum reaches the 0.39 µm of
   the PDK;[^pdk-04] down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens
   of rpm are typical.[^txt-05]
4. **Endpoint.** By time calibrated on monitors, or by in-situ
   optical thickness;[^pat-cmp-window] the polish must stop well above
   the capacitor plates, the highest metal features at this level
   (inference from the construction).
5. **{term}`Post-CMP clean <post-CMP clean>`.** Double-sided brush scrub with dilute NH₄OH
   (Philipossian and Sun on brush design[^philipossian-2009]), perhaps
   a dilute HF dip, spin-rinse-dry; Devriendt et al. relate oxide-CMP
   defects to the clean.[^devriendt-1998]
6. **Metrology.** Remaining oxide over metal-3 test pads and, on our
   reading, over capacitor test structures, by reflectometry or
   ellipsometry at many sites; die-level thickness maps on density
   structures (Stine et al.'s method[^stine-1998]); scratch and
   particle inspection on the KLA AIT class SkyWater's job posting
   names;[^job-01] via-3 chain resistance later at {term}`e-test`.

## Machines typically used

* **Rotary multi-platen CMP polisher**, 200 mm: Applied Materials
  Mirra,[^pat-cmp-mirra][^chiphistory-mirra] Ebara EPO/F-REX,[^ebara-frex]
  SpeedFam-IPEC, Strasbaugh ({ref}`category-cmp`).
* **Post-CMP brush scrubber** (OnTrak/Lam Synergy, SEZ/Lam Da Vinci).
* **Thin-film thickness metrology** (reflectometer, ellipsometer),
  **patterned-wafer inspection**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Applied Materials Mirra CMP.** SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP"
  with "oxide" among its applications.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong**
  for the tool and its oxide polish (SkyWater statement); assignment to
  this step is an **inference** from the film, since the list names no
  steps.
* **Post-CMP clean — SEZ 223 / Da Vinci** single-wafer tools, and the
  Mirra entry also lists "Track ammonia clean" and "IPA clean";[^skw-01]
  a "SEZ etcher tool" on the maintenance page.[^skw-07] A brush
  scrubber is not named (open question).
* **Defect inspection — KLA AIT / SP1.**[^job-01] Strength: medium.

## Resources required

* **Silica-based oxide CMP slurry** (KOH- or NH₄OH-stabilised fumed or
  colloidal silica).[^rev-02][^wiki-cmp]
* **Polishing pads** (stacked polyurethane, IC1000/Suba IV class;
  category page) and **diamond conditioners**, consumed faster
  for the larger removal.[^wiki-cmp]
* **DI water**, **dilute NH₄OH** (category page), possibly **dilute
  HF**, for the post-CMP clean; **PVA brushes**.
* **Carrier-head consumables**; **slurry supply and waste
  treatment**.[^txt-07]

## Open questions

* The slurry, pad, removal amount, target oxide over metal 3 and the
  endpoint method of `CMPM3` are not public.
* How the 0.39 µm via-3 height[^pdk-04] is split between the polished
  {ref}`NILD5 <step-141>` and the {ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` cap is not
  public.
* How much oxide remains over a capacitor top plate depends on the
  unpublished dielectric and plate thicknesses; about 0.2–0.3 µm is our
  estimate.
* Why the "Oxide Bias" is 1.15 for metals 3 and 4 against 0.6 below[^pdk-03]
  is not public.
* The post-CMP cleaning tool is not identified beyond the SEZ/Da Vinci
  single-wafer tools.[^skw-01]
