## What this step is

`MM4E` is the metal-4 etch, and with it the step that separates the
second-level {term}`MiM capacitors <MiM capacitor>` from one another. Through the resist of
{ref}`MM4 <step-154>` a chlorine plasma removes everything down to the
via-3 level: first whatever remains of the {ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>`
dielectric outside the capacitor plates, then the metal-4 stack of
{ref}`WTIAL4 <step-149>` — a TiW cap, a thick Al–Cu layer and a thin
refractory bottom layer on our reading — stopping on the
{ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` cap oxide and on the tops of the via-3
{term}`tungsten plugs <W plug>` of {ref}`WCMP5 <step-148>`. What remains is the `met4`
wiring (GDS 71:20[^pdk-06]) at 0.300 µm minimum width and space (m4.1,
m4.2[^pdk-periph]) and, under each `cap2m` plate, a metal-4 bottom
electrode — the "M4 (plate 2)" of the PDK's stacked `cap_mim`
cross-section.[^pdk-07] The step list used in this reference has no
separate strip step after `MM4E`; this page treats the passivation,
resist strip and clean as part of the etch, as at
{ref}`MM3E <step-140>`.

**How thick is the metal?** The PDK's stack diagram labels `metal4`
0.845 µm,[^pdk-04] and Edwards's slides repeat it;[^ann-16] the
antenna-rule table uses 0.8 µm for the S8P*/SP8P* flows and 2 µm for
the S8Q*/SP8Q flows.[^pdk-03] Unlike metal 3, metal 4 has no public
film-by-film description: the Cypress qualification reports that list
S8 metal stacks describe three-metal processes.[^cyp-qtp-113005][^cyp-qtp-123907]
The same Fab 4 reports show that 7 200 Å and 8 000 Å Al metal-3 stacks
have both existed there,[^cyp-qtp-113005][^cyp-qtp-014807] which is why
the 0.8–0.845 µm difference is left open for metal 3 at
{ref}`MM3E <step-140>`; we carry that question over to metal 4 and use
the PDK's 0.845 µm for geometry. The electrical data do not decide it:
`RSM4` is 47 mΩ/sq (limits 38–56),[^pdk-07][^pdk-08] consistent with
0.72–0.80 µm of Al–Cu at 3.4–3.8 µΩ·cm (our arithmetic; typical
industry value[^txt-02]).

## Step category

`MM4E` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *metal,
chlorine-chemistry* class — the category page's "Aluminium–copper with
Ti/TiN or Ti:W caps" entry — and the fourth of the five metal etches.
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` sets out the chemistry: aluminium etches
spontaneously in chlorine, so {term}`anisotropy` comes from a sidewall
passivation film, and the etch products leave the lines liable to
corrode.[^hess-1982][^nojiri-2015] Its geometry is metal 3's: 0.30 µm
spaces 0.845 µm deep, an {term}`aspect ratio` of about 2.8:1 (our
arithmetic from the PDK values[^pdk-periph][^pdk-04]). And like
{ref}`MM3E <step-140>` — one of the two metal etches in the flow that
begin with a capacitor dielectric — it must first break through a thin
silicon-based film on the TiW cap outside the plates.

## Why this step exists

* **Line width and profile in a thick film.** The etched width is what
  the PDK's 0.300 µm[^pdk-periph] describes; over 0.845 µm of height a
  small sidewall angle becomes a large difference between line top and
  bottom. A vertical profile gives {ref}`NILD6 <step-156>` its best
  chance of filling a 2.8:1 gap; Cooperberg, Vahedi and Gottscho show
  how the Cl₂/BCl₃ balance and the passivant set the
  sidewall,[^cooperberg-2002] and Allen and Rickard obtained a tapered
  aluminium profile in a TCP etcher with nitrogen additions — the
  opposite choice.[^allen-1994]
* **Breaking through the capacitor dielectric.** Wherever the
  {ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>` film remains on the metal, a chlorine plasma
  that etches aluminium readily makes little progress; a
  fluorine-containing breakthrough removes it and the TiW cap together —
  fluorocarbon etching of silicon oxide is described by Oehrlein et
  al.,[^oehrlein-1994b] and Liu and Kuo showed TiW etching in CF₄-based
  plasmas.[^liu-2007-tiw] Dielectric left in a space masks the metal and
  leaves a stringer that shorts adjacent lines (inference from the
  stack; the Newport Fab patent removes its dielectric outside the
  capacitor before patterning the bottom-plate metal[^pat-mim-newportfab]).
* **Complete clearing of thick Al–Cu.** Al–Cu etches in
  Cl₂/BCl₃,[^schaible-1978][^hess-1982][^chen-1989] but copper chlorides
  are involatile and are removed only by ion bombardment, leaving
  copper-rich residues that seed corrosion;[^lee-1981] Abraham, Gabriel
  and Zheng describe cap-layer etch chemistries and microloading on a
  high-density metal etcher.[^abraham-1997]
* **Corrosion.** Chlorine in the sidewall polymer and resist forms HCl
  with moisture and attacks aluminium at copper precipitates;[^lee-1981]
  Wai and Ling describe the post-etch and polymer-clean sequence that
  prevents it.[^wai-2017]
* **Charging of both capacitors.** Until this etch every `capm` top
  plate contacted by via 3 is tied to a continuous metal-4 sheet
  (inference from the PDK's stacked cross-section[^pdk-07]); here those
  connections are cut into separate nets, and each `cap2m` bottom plate
  is cut free under a top plate that stays floating until via 4 is made.
  Wang, Ackaert et al. showed that {term}`plasma-charging <plasma charging>` damage of floating
  MiM capacitors depends on the antenna areas connected to each
  plate,[^wang-2004-mim] Fang and McVittie described the thin-oxide
  damage charging causes,[^fang-1992] and Hwang and Giapis the notching
  at the line foot.[^hwang-1997] The PDK's antenna chapter[^pdk-11]
  exists because of steps such as this.

Without `MM4E` metal 4 would remain a sheet shorting every via-3 plug,
every second-level capacitor and every fuse together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic thick aluminium-stack etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the chemistry is at
{ref}`MM1E <step-114>` and the capacitor-level differences at
{ref}`MM3E <step-140>`.

1. **Chamber.** A high-density plasma metal etcher — transformer- or
   inductively coupled, with independent bias, a cooled electrostatic
   chuck and helium backside cooling — with integrated passivation and
   strip modules. Lam's {term}`TCP` source[^pat-tcp-lam] and Applied
   Materials' DPS reactor[^pat-dps-amat] are the two 200 mm-era designs;
   Lam's 10-K describes its metal etch lines.[^lam-10k]
2. **BARC open and dielectric breakthrough.** The organic {term}`BARC`
   is opened in an O₂/N₂ or Cl₂-containing step; the capacitor dielectric
   and TiW cap are then broken through with a fluorine-bearing mixture
   such as CF₄ or CHF₃ with Cl₂ (industry
   practice;[^oehrlein-1994b][^liu-2007-tiw] SKY130's is not public).
3. **Main etch.** Cl₂/BCl₃ with N₂ (or CHF₃) at a few to tens of mTorr;
   chlorine removes aluminium as AlCl₃, BCl₃ scavenges oxygen and breaks
   the native oxide, and the nitrogen or carbon species build the
   sidewall film.[^schaible-1978][^hess-1982][^chen-1989] At the hundreds
   of nanometres per minute of the class (industry-typical[^txt-02]) the
   0.845 µm stack[^pdk-04] clears in one to two minutes.
4. **Endpoint.** Optical emission on the atomic aluminium line near
   396 nm or the AlCl band, which falls as the aluminium
   clears;[^bawolek-1987] the open area of a metal level is large, so the
   signal is strong.
5. **{term}`Over-etch <over-etch>`.** A timed, more passivating step to remove the bottom
   refractory layer and stringers at plug edges, with {term}`selectivity`
   to the {ref}`NCAPOX5 <step-143>` oxide and the tungsten plug tops;
   {term}`notching` from charging appears here.[^hwang-1997] The
   over-etch is bounded by the 0.39 µm of via-3 oxide beneath[^pdk-04]
   (inference).
6. **Passivation.** Without an air break, an H₂O-vapour or O₂/CF₄ plasma
   that converts residual chlorides and strips chlorine from the
   resist.[^nojiri-2015][^wai-2017]
7. **Strip and clean.** Downstream O₂/N₂ {term}`ash` — SkyWater lists
   "Gasonic PEP", Iridia and Mattson Aspen II strippers with N₂, O₂, CF₄ and
   H₂/N₂ options[^skw-01] — then a solvent polymer clean ("EKS265, EKC270
   solvents"[^skw-01]) within a {term}`queue-time <queue time>` limit, benign to the exposed
   plate edges (inference); DI rinse and dry.
8. **Metrology.** Line CD by {term}`CD-SEM`; profile by cross-section
   SEM on monitors; metal-4 sheet resistance and comb/serpentine
   structures at {term}`e-test` against 47 mΩ/sq;[^pdk-07] capacitance,
   leakage and breakdown of the now separate second-level capacitors
   against `CMIM2A` and `CMIM2P`;[^pdk-07] corrosion inspection after a
   delay. The published SKY130 {term}`test tile` measures metal 4 with
   "S8P WM4_Kelvin (Width = 0.3um/ Length = 418.375um), # of squares =
   1394.58" and "S8P RSM4_Kelvin" lines, and 0.3 µm serpentine/comb
   pairs "over field (S8P BVM4M4)" and "over M3 grid (S8P
   BVM4M3)".[^raw-data-testtile-pads]

## Resources required

* **Cl₂ and BCl₃**, **N₂** (and possibly CHF₃) for the main etch; **CF₄
  or CHF₃** for the dielectric and cap breakthrough; **Ar**; **He** for
  backside cooling (industry practice;[^nojiri-2015] SkyWater lists no
  gases for its metal etchers, but names Cl₂, CF₄ and CHF₃ on its other
  etchers[^skw-01]).
* **H₂O vapour or O₂/CF₄** for passivation; **O₂/N₂** for the ash.[^skw-01]
* **Amine or semi-aqueous polymer-removal solvent** ({ref}`wet chemicals <material-wet-chemicals>`) and {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>`.[^skw-01]
* **{ref}`Chamber consumables <material-hardware-consumables>`**; **{ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** with the full metal-4
  stack; **chlorine abatement**.

## Open questions

* **Metal-4 thickness and composition.** No public source describes the
  metal-4 films; the PDK gives 0.845 µm in its stack diagram[^pdk-04] and
  0.8 µm in its P-flow antenna entry,[^pdk-03] the same unresolved
  difference as for metal 3 ({ref}`MM3E <step-140>`).
* How much of the {ref}`CAPILD2 <step-150>` dielectric remains outside
  the plates, and how the breakthrough is set, depend on
  {ref}`CAP2ME <step-153>` and are not public.
* The chemistry, endpoint, over-etch, passivation and queue-time rules,
  and which Lam etcher runs the step, are not public.
* Whether fuse lines in metal 4[^pdk-periph] need any etch treatment
  different from the wiring is not public.
* The step list used in this reference has no separate strip step after
  `MM4E`; this page treats the resist strip and clean as part of the
  etch.
