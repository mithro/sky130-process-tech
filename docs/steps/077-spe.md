## What this step is

`SPE` is the spacer etch-back. The conformal nitride of
{ref}`SPNIT <step-076>` is etched anisotropically, without a mask,
until it has been removed from every horizontal surface — the tops of
the gate caps, the source/drain silicon, the field oxide — and remains
only where it was thickest in the vertical direction: on the sidewalls
of the gate lines, the poly interconnect and the resistor bodies.
Those remnants are the sidewall {term}`spacer`s. Their width is set
by the deposited thickness and the etch's profile; their height by the
gate stack; and everything that follows — the {term}`nitride cut`, the deep
source/drain implants, the contact placement — takes them as a fixed
part of the topography.

The PDK gives the step a public foothold in its assumptions table: the
entry "poly cap after SPE" of 0.2 µm.[^pdk-03] This reference reads
"SPE" there as the spacer etch described on this page (inference; see
{ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`), meaning that 0.2 µm of the nitride/oxide
cap stack is still standing on the gates when this etch is finished.
That is the crucial constraint on the recipe. The etch must clear the
spacer nitride over the source/drain — stopping on a thin oxide (on
our reading), under which is the silicon of the extensions — while
over the gates it eats first through the same nitride, then into the
cap, and must not take so much of it that the cap fails later as an
implant block ({ref}`PSDI <step-082>`, {ref}`NSDI <step-086>`) and
polish stop ({ref}`CMPP <step-090>`).

The film being etched is not the only nitride in play. The
{ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` cap is nitride too, with an oxide
({ref}`POC <step-059>`) on top; whether the oxide cap is still there
at the start of this etch, after the gate etch and the tip module, is
not public. The {ref}`POC <step-059>` page reads the oxide cap as
being consumed here or at the nitride cut.

## Step category

`SPE` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *dielectric,
fluorine-chemistry* class — a nitride etch in CF₄/CHF₃/O₂ or similar
(category page[^txt-01]) — and the first unmasked, blanket anisotropic
etch of the front end. It differs from the masked nitride etches
({ref}`STINITE <step-005>`, {ref}`NPCME <step-079>`) in that there is
no resist to protect anything: what survives is decided entirely by
geometry and by the etch's {term}`anisotropy` and {term}`selectivity`.
It is also the etch whose over-etch is most exposed to the
transistor: the surface it stops on lies directly over the
extensions, and any silicon recess or damage there ends up in the
source/drain.

## Why this step exists

The spacer is what offsets the deep source/drain from the gate edge
in a self-aligned way, as Tsang et al. set out when they introduced
the sidewall-spacer process[^tsang-1982] for the LDD
transistor of Ogura et al.[^ogura-1980] The etch-back is the step
that turns a blanket film into that offset. Three of its properties
carry directly into device parameters:

* **Spacer width** (deposited thickness minus lateral etch) sets the
  distance between extension and deep junction, and with it the
  extension series resistance that Ng and Lynch analysed[^ng-1986]
  and the hot-carrier field distribution that the LDD was invented to
  soften.[^ogura-1980]
* **Spacer profile** — whether the spacer is a quarter-round, a
  triangle or a vertical slab — decides how the later high-current
  implants are scattered at the spacer foot and how much dopant lands
  under it; Krieger et al. showed how a tilted arsenic source/drain
  implant is shadowed by the gate stack and spacer,[^krieger-1989] and
  the PDK's own "High current 0 deg" implant-angle entry[^pdk-03] is,
  we infer, one response to that.
* **Residual damage and recess** in the source/drain silicon after
  the over-etch: Reinhardt and Dumesnil measured the damage from an
  LDD spacer etch with thermal-wave reflectance,[^reinhardt-1992] and
  Om et al. found the junction leakage of LDD NMOS to depend on the
  spacer material and its processing.[^om-1995]

A second purpose is often served by the same etch: the spacer nitride
that lands on the vertical sidewalls of the *resistor* bodies seals
them, and spacers along the poly interconnect sidewalls keep the later
contact etch ({ref}`LICM1E <step-094>`) from reaching the gate edge.
Goss and Thornburg describe what a nitride-spacer module has to get
right in production — selectivity, residues, stringers and the
{term}`etch-stop layer` — for a 0.35 µm technology.[^goss-1997]

Without `SPE` the nitride would remain everywhere: no source/drain
implant could reach the silicon, no contact could reach anything, and
there would be no offset between extension and deep junction.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic nitride spacer etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Chamber.** Single-wafer plasma etcher with independent source
   and bias power — a high-density TCP/ICP tool or a medium-density
   RIE — with helium backside cooling and optical emission endpoint
   ({ref}`category-etch`).
2. **Main etch.** Fluorocarbon chemistry: CF₄ or CHF₃ with O₂ (and
   often Ar or N₂) at a few tens of mTorr. Fluorine etches nitride as
   SiF₄ and N₂; the carbon from CHF₃ polymerises preferentially on
   oxide, which is how selectivity to the oxide beneath is
   obtained;[^flamm-1981][^winters-1992] Lee and Chen worked out the
   mechanism and anisotropy of the CF₄/O₂ system on
   silicon,[^lee-chen-1983] and Kastenmeier et al. quantified nitride
   and oxide etch rates in CF₄/O₂/N₂ mixtures[^kastenmeier-1996] and
   how to make nitride etch selectively over both silicon and
   oxide[^kastenmeier-1999] (both in downstream, ion-free reactors:
   their rates and selectivities describe the chemistry, not the
   ion-driven etch used here). The bias is set high enough that the
   etch is ion-driven and vertical, so the film clears from
   horizontal surfaces while the sidewall film, seen by the ions
   edge-on, survives.
3. **Endpoint.** Optical emission on the 387 nm CN band, which falls
   as the nitride clears from the open areas — "a strong peak at 387
   nm indicates that CN is present in the plasma, usually indicating
   that nitride is being etched"[^pat-cn-tel] — in a
   double-endpoint scheme of the kind Tokyo Electron patented for
   layered stacks.[^pat-cn-tel]
4. **Over-etch.** A timed step, often with the chemistry moved toward
   CHF₃-rich or CH₃F-based mixtures for higher selectivity to
   oxide,[^regis-1997] long enough to remove nitride stringers at
   the step down from field oxide to active area and to clear the
   spacer foot, while the oxide over the extensions survives: Regis
   et al. report a production recipe "with high selectivity to oxide"
   and its process window.[^regis-1997] Any oxide punch-through here
   recesses the source/drain silicon and leaves the plasma damage
   Oehrlein reviewed.[^oehrlein-1989]
5. **Loading.** Because the wafer is mostly open nitride at the
   start and mostly oxide at the end, the {term}`loading effect` is
   large and the endpoint is sharp;[^gottscho-1992] the over-etch
   must still cover dense poly arrays, where the nitride between
   lines clears last.
6. **Post-etch.** Resist is not involved, so no strip; a wet clean
   (SC-1 class) removes fluorocarbon residue, and we infer no HF is
   used because the stop oxide is still needed as an implant screen.
7. **Metrology.** Spacer width and profile by cross-section SEM on
   monitors; remaining oxide over active by ellipsometry on test pads;
   cap thickness on poly test pads (the "poly cap after SPE" of the
   PDK[^pdk-03] is precisely such a number); particles and defect
   inspection.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`Dielectric/nitride plasma etcher <machine-plasma-etcher-dielectric>`**, 200 mm single-wafer: Lam TCP 9400
  (the Stanford user guide describes the class and its gas
  set[^snf-9400]), Lam 4520XLE/Exelan, Applied Materials MxP/eMax and
  DPS with fluorocarbon gases, TEL DRM ({ref}`category-etch`).
* **Optical emission endpoint** system on the etcher.
* **{ref}`Cross-section SEM <machine-cross-section-sem-profilers>`**, **{ref}`ellipsometer <machine-film-thickness-metrology>`**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam 9400 TCP.** SkyWater lists "Lam 9400 TCP, poly/nitride, HBr,
  CF4, SF6, O2".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the tool and for
  the word "nitride" and the CF₄/O₂ gas set; assignment to the spacer
  etch is an **inference**, since the list names no steps.
* **AMAT DPS II** ("HBR, Cl2, NF3, CF4, CHF3, O2 – gate, trench,
  W/WN"[^skw-01]) carries CF₄ and CHF₃ and could run a nitride
  etch-back. Strength: **medium** for this step, since its CF₄ and
  CHF₃ etch nitride although the entry names no nitride application.
* **Lam 4400** ("HBr, Cl2, C2F6, CF4, SF6, O2"[^skw-01]). Strength:
  **weak**, as the entry names no application.
* Wet clean: **DNS wet bench, FSI Mercury, Akrion Gamma**.[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence.

  **{ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** with blanket nitride and oxide for rate and
  selectivity checks.

## Open questions

* The SKY130 spacer etch chemistry, endpoint scheme, over-etch and the
  resulting spacer width and profile are not public.
* What the etch stops on over the source/drain — the
  {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>` oxide, a separately deposited liner, or
  bare silicon after an intentional oxide breakthrough — is not
  public; our reading is that an oxide survives and serves as the
  implant screen.
* Whether the {ref}`POC <step-059>` oxide cap is consumed here, and
  how much of the {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` nitride the "poly cap
  after SPE" 0.2 µm[^pdk-03] represents, is inferred.
* Which etcher on SkyWater's public list runs the step is inferred
  from the "poly/nitride" label on the Lam 9400 TCP.[^skw-01]
