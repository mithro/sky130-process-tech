## What this step is

`WCMP4` is the tungsten polish that turns the blanket film of
{ref}`WDEP4 <step-132>` into via-2 plugs. The wafer is pressed
face-down on a rotating pad flooded with an oxidising, abrasive slurry
until the tungsten and the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN4 <step-131>` are
gone from the field and the polish lands on the
{ref}`NCAPOX4 <step-128>` cap oxide; tungsten survives only inside the
vias. What is left is a planar oxide surface studded with 0.20 µm
tungsten squares[^pdk-periph] — the `via2` plugs, "Contact from
metal 2 to metal 3"[^pdk-06] — on which the metal-3 stack of
{ref}`WTIAL3 <step-134>` will be sputtered. It is the fourth tungsten
polish of the flow, after {ref}`WCMPLI <step-100>`,
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` and {ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>`;
{ref}`WCMP5 <step-148>` follows at via 3.

SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP" with the applications "oxide",
"nitride", "niobium", "aluminum", "tungsten", "high selectivity
tungsten" and "copper";[^skw-01] which tungsten process runs this step
is not public. What differs from the via-1 polish is the film and
the surface that follows. The blanket film is thicker — of the order
of 0.25–0.35 µm for a 0.20 µm via (industry-typical value[^txt-05])
— so there is more to remove; the dielectric it stops on is 0.42 µm
thick[^pdk-04] rather than 0.27 µm, so oxide loss is a smaller
fraction of the level-to-level spacing; and the metal deposited next
is the thick metal 3 — 0.845 µm,[^pdk-04] or 2 µm in the S8TM
option[^pdk-03] — which is sputtered, not grown, and so cannot fill a
recessed plug top (inference). The PDK's `via2` is 3 410 mΩ,[^pdk-08]
which on our reading is the finished plug resistance after this
step's polish (inference: the PDK publishes the value, not the
process stage it was taken from).

## Step category

`WCMP4` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *metal-clearing* type: it removes two metals completely
and stops on a third material, with plug recess, oxide {term}`erosion`
in dense arrays, tungsten coring and corrosion as the failure modes.
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` sets out the slurry electrochemistry and
endpoint methods in full and {ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>` what the via
level changes; Kaufman et al.'s oxidation-and-abrasion mechanism is
the physics.[^kaufman-1991] What is specific to this instance is that
it is the last polish before the flow's thick metal, and — if the
thick-metal option's 0.8 µm via 2[^pdk-03] is in use — the first
tungsten polish over a hole a blanket film cannot close, where the
polish would leave a lined recess rather than a plug (inference; see
*Open questions*).

## Why this step exists

The plug process needs the tungsten removed from everywhere but the
vias, and the result flat:

* **Isolation.** Blanket tungsten shorts every via-2 plug and every
  metal-2 line together; the polish makes them separate conductors,
  as IBM's metal/insulator CMP patents first
  described.[^pat-cmp-ibm-1988][^pat-cmp-ibm-1990]
* **A flat base for a thick metal.** The metal-3 stack of
  {ref}`WTIAL3 <step-134>` is sputtered onto this surface and, before
  it is patterned, carries the MiM capacitor module of
  {ref}`CAPILD <step-135>`; a recessed plug under a 0.3 µm line is a
  step the 0.845 µm sputter[^pdk-04] must cover, and any tungsten or
  TiN residue between plugs is a short under a level that
  {ref}`MM3 <step-139>` cannot etch away. Wang et al. characterised an
  integrated tungsten CMP process for via plugs,[^wang-2002-wcmp] and
  Lee et al. the consumables for a plug polish.[^lee-2001-wcmp]
* **Via resistance.** Over-polish recesses the plug — Vlassak's
  contact-mechanics model of {term}`dishing` and erosion[^vlassak-2001]
  and Nguyen et al.'s dishing model[^nguyen-2000] describe the
  geometry — and reduces the area the metal-3 bottom layer contacts;
  under-polish leaves residue. The 3 410 mΩ per `via2` of the PDK's
  tables[^pdk-08] is, on our reading, the finished plug resistance
  after this step's polish (inference).
* **Oxide loss over dense vias.** Via-2 arrays under bus crossings
  and in the seal ring erode faster than the field, the
  pattern-density dependence Stine et al. characterised;[^stine-1998]
  whatever is lost thins the 0.42 µm via-2 dielectric[^pdk-04]
  locally and changes the metal-2-to-metal-3 capacitance of the
  extraction tables.[^pdk-08] Shiratani and Kumazawa describe a
  low-erosion tungsten CMP process,[^shiratani-2006] and Grover et al.
  how slurry rheology shifts the oxide-to-tungsten balance.[^grover-1998]

Without `WCMP4` there would be no via-2 plugs, only a tungsten sheet
shorting metal 2 together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic tungsten via-plug polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public); the sequence is that of
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`.

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher — the Mirra
   architecture[^pat-cmp-mirra] — with a carrier head, retaining ring
   and multi-zone membrane, a stacked polyurethane pad, a diamond
   conditioner and an in-situ endpoint window on some
   platens.[^pat-cmp-window]
2. **Slurry.** Acidic (pH 2–4, industry-typical[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]),
   with alumina or silica abrasive and an oxidiser — ferric nitrate,
   hydrogen peroxide with an iron catalyst, or potassium
   iodate;[^kaufman-1991][^kneer-1997][^stein-1999][^pat-cmp-cabot]
   Krishnan, Nalaskowski and Cook review the chemistry.[^rev-02] The
   "high selectivity tungsten" of SkyWater's list[^skw-01] we read as
   a slurry formulated to remove tungsten tens of times faster than
   oxide (inference).
3. **Recipe.** A first platen removes the bulk tungsten at high rate
   — a longer step here than at via 1, for the thicker film; a second
   clears the last tungsten and the TiN liner and lands on the cap
   oxide with a short, low-pressure over-polish; a third platen
   buffs. Down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens of rpm
   are typical.[^txt-05] Elbel et al. give a production description of
   tungsten CMP and its defects.[^elbel-1998]
4. **Endpoint.** Motor-current (friction) or optical endpoint as the
   pad passes from tungsten to oxide;[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm]
   Sue et al. describe tungsten CMP endpoint detection.[^sue-1999]
   The over-polish is then timed.
5. **Post-CMP clean.** Immediately, before the slurry dries: a
   double-sided brush scrub with dilute NH₄OH or a TMAH-based
   chemistry — Jolley reports TMAH as a post-tungsten-CMP
   clean[^jolley-1998] — then spin-rinse-dry; Sun, Han and Keswani
   review brush scrubbing.[^sun-2017] The clean also passivates the
   plugs against corrosion in the wet, oxidising slurry.
6. **Metrology.** Plug recess and oxide erosion by profilometry or AFM
   on via-2 array test structures; remaining oxide thickness over
   metal-2 pads by reflectometry; residual-metal and scratch
   inspection on the KLA AIT class (our reading of "AIT" in SkyWater's job posting);[^job-01] via-2 chain resistance at {term}`e-test`.

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Tungsten CMP slurry <material-cmp-consumables>`** — alumina or silica abrasive with ferric
  nitrate, hydrogen peroxide ({ref}`wet chemicals <material-wet-chemicals>`) or iodate oxidiser (industry
  practice[^rev-02][^pat-cmp-cabot]).
* **Polishing pads** (stacked polyurethane, IC1000/Suba IV class;
  category page) and **diamond pad conditioners**.[^wiki-cmp]
* **{ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>`**, **dilute NH₄OH** or **TMAH**,[^jolley-1998] possibly
  **citric acid** or **dilute HF** (category page), for the post-CMP
  clean; **PVA brushes**.
* **Carrier-head consumables** — membranes, retaining rings.
* **Slurry supply and {ref}`waste treatment <material-hardware-consumables>`** for metal-laden acidic CMP
  waste.[^txt-07]
