## What this step is

`VIM` is the via-1 lithography: the mask step that defines the holes
through which metal 2 will reach metal 1. On the cap oxide of
{ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>`, which seals the polished
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>` dielectric, a resist is coated, exposed and
developed so that a small square opening stands over every place the
layout draws a `via` — GDS 68:44, "Contact from metal 1 to
metal 2".[^pdk-06] The holes are etched at {ref}`VIME <step-119>`,
lined at {ref}`TIN3 <step-120>`, filled with tungsten at
{ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` and polished at {ref}`WCMP3 <step-122>`. The
PDK's mask table lists "Via, VIM" as used in SKY130,[^pdk-05] so the
step code is the mask name. It is the module's first step and, with
{ref}`CTM1 <step-107>` below it, the second hole mask of the
{term}`BEOL`.

The public geometry is again complete. The periphery rules give a
fixed size — "Min and max L and W of via outside areaid.mt" 0.150 µm
(via.1a), with three permitted sizes of 0.150, 0.230 and 0.280 µm
inside the `areaid.mt` region (via.1b) — a spacing of 0.170 µm
(via.2), the rule that "Only min. square vias are allowed except die
seal ring" (via.3), and enclosures by metal 1 of 0.055 µm (via.4a) and
0.085 µm on one of two adjacent sides (via.5a); metal 2 must enclose
the via by the same 0.055 µm and 0.085 µm (m2.4, m2.5).[^pdk-periph]
The minimum-CD table repeats the numbers as `VIMCD` 0.15 µm and
`VIMCDSP` 0.17 µm, adds a "min. etch and fill capability for via" of
0.18 µm and a "Via1 slope" of 0.02, and gives the nominal resist
thickness of 1.14 µm used for antenna calculations.[^pdk-03] The
extraction tables put a finished `via` at 4 500 mΩ (4.5 Ω), against
152 000 mΩ for the `mcon` below it.[^pdk-08]

What lies under the resist is different from the contact level in two
ways that shape the whole module. First, the hole is shallower and of
lower aspect ratio, though it is narrower: the stack diagram gives the
via-1 height through NILD3 as 0.27 µm,[^pdk-04] so a 0.15 µm via
(via.1a)[^pdk-periph] has an {term}`aspect ratio` of about 1.8:1 as
drawn, against roughly 2:1 for the 0.17 µm contact (ct.1)[^pdk-periph]
of {ref}`CTM1 <step-107>`. Second, the floor is not a titanium-nitride
local-interconnect line but the top of the metal-1 stack — on the
public description of the S8 process, a 300 Å titanium–tungsten cap
over 3 200 Å of Al–0.5%Cu[^cyp-qtp-113005] — which is both the etch
stop for {ref}`VIME <step-119>` and a mirror under the resist.

## Step category

`VIM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *contact-hole* type, like {ref}`CTM1 <step-107>`: an
isolated small square transmits little light and has the smallest
depth of focus of any feature at its pitch.[^mack-2007] At 0.15 µm it
is the smallest drawn hole in the flow, on a 0.32 µm pitch; on a
248 nm tool of NA 0.70 (ASML's PAS 5500/750E[^asml-750e]) the process
factor is {math}`k_1 = 0.15 \times 0.70 / 0.248 \approx 0.42`, and
NA 0.80[^asml-800] lifts it only to 0.48. The ITRS 2001 lithography
chapter lists "248 nm + PSM" and "193 nm" as the exposure options for
the 130 nm node, noting that "only 248 nm lithography has a mature
infrastructure",[^itrs-03] and the category page reads via 1 as a
critical layer; we infer that `VIM` is exposed on
the "ASML DUV stepper" or "ASML DUV scanner" SkyWater lists,[^skw-01]
with a chemically amplified resist ({term}`CAR`) and, on our reading
of the geometry, an {term}`attenuated PSM` with {term}`OPC`. Nothing
public states the tool, resist or reticle type for this layer.

What distinguishes it from `CTM1` is the substrate. The resist sits on
a thin, transparent oxide over a patterned reflective metal; light
that passes the resist is reflected by the TiW cap wherever a
metal-1 line lies beneath and by nothing in particular elsewhere, so
the effective dose and the swing-curve phase vary with the metal
pattern under each via. This is the classic reason a bottom
anti-reflective coating ({term}`BARC`) is used on via levels, and
Brunner's swing-curve analysis is the basis for choosing its
thickness.[^brunner-1991][^wiki-arc]

## Why this step exists

Every connection between the first two wiring levels passes through a
`via`; the step fixes what the plug that follows inherits:

* **Hole size and profile.** The drawn 0.150 µm is the top opening;
  the "Via1 slope" of 0.02 in the PDK's assumptions[^pdk-03] is, on
  our reading, the taper allowed between top and bottom, and the
  "etch and fill capability" of 0.18 µm[^pdk-03] the size at which the
  liner and tungsten fill ({ref}`TIN3 <step-120>`,
  {ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>`) are qualified. A 0.15 µm hole must
  therefore print close to size: a resist opening that closes down
  leaves an under-filled plug, one that opens up eats the metal-1
  enclosure.
* **Placement.** With 0.055 µm of metal-1 enclosure (via.4a) and the
  same for metal 2 (m2.4),[^pdk-periph] the {term}`overlay` of this
  layer to {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` and of {ref}`MM2 <step-124>` to it
  is looser than the 0.030 µm the contact level allows (m1.4), but a
  via that slips off its metal-1 line lands on oxide and etches a
  trench beside the line — the "unlanded via" the etch must tolerate.
  Levinson's book has a chapter on overlay.[^levinson-2005]
* **Uniformity through pitch.** Because every via is the same square
  (via.1a, via.3),[^pdk-periph] the layer prints one feature at many
  pitches, from isolated vias to dense arrays in bus crossings; the
  proximity behaviour of contact arrays is why hole layers received
  {term}`OPC` and assist features early.[^yamamoto-2000][^socha-2004][^wiki-opc]
* **Resistance.** The 4.5 Ω the PDK's extraction tables give for a
  `via`[^pdk-08] is, on our reading, the finished plug resistance,
  whose bottom area this mask sets (inference: the PDK publishes the
  value, not the structure it was taken from); the electromigration
  lifetime of a tungsten-plug via also depends on its size, as Le,
  Banerjee and McPherson showed.[^le-1996]

Without `VIM` there would be no holes for {ref}`VIME <step-119>` to
etch and metal 2 would float above metal 1 on unbroken oxide.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic 248 nm via-hole lithography sequence for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's is not public); the contact-level page
({ref}`CTM1 <step-107>`) describes the shared steps at greater length.

1. **Surface preparation.** The wafer arrives from the
   {ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>` deposition with a fresh oxide surface; a
   dehydration bake and {term}`HMDS` prime, then an organic
   {term}`BARC` of a few tens of nanometres, spun and baked, to
   suppress the pattern-dependent reflection from the buried
   metal.[^brunner-1991][^wiki-arc]
2. **Resist coat.** A positive-tone chemically amplified
   resist[^ito-2005][^wallraff-1999] of a few hundred nanometres,
   chosen for compatibility with an attenuated phase-shift mask if one
   is used;[^lu-1999] the PDK's 1.14 µm[^pdk-03] is an antenna-rule
   assumption and need not describe this layer. Chemically amplified
   resists are degraded by airborne amines, so the track environment
   must exclude them (industry practice[^hinsberg-1992]); SkyWater
   does not say how its tracks are filtered.
3. **Exposure.** KrF at NA 0.7–0.8.[^asml-750e][^asml-800] For a
   0.15 µm hole the era's toolkit was an {term}`attenuated PSM`
   (ITRS 2001 names "attenuated shifter" as a primary PSM
   choice[^itrs-03][^wiki-psm]) — Chen, Wang and Chu describe its
   optimisation for contact-hole printing[^chen-1999-psm] — with
   {term}`OPC` and assist features,[^yamamoto-2000][^socha-2004]
   focus-latitude techniques such as FLEX,[^fukuda-1987] or a chemical
   shrink after develop to reach a hole smaller than the printed
   one.[^toyoshima-1998] Which of these SKY130 uses is not public.
4. **Post-exposure bake and develop.** {term}`PEB` on a hot plate,
   2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH develop, rinse and dry.[^txt-02]
5. **Alignment.** To the {ref}`MM1 <step-113>` metal-1 pattern, on
   box-in-box or grating targets etched into the metal at
   {ref}`MM1E <step-114>` and now buried under 0.27 µm of oxide; the
   0.055 µm enclosure (via.4a)[^pdk-periph] is the registration that
   matters.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** Hole CD by {term}`CD-SEM`, overlay on
   the KLA 5200/5300/Archer class SkyWater lists,[^skw-01]
   after-develop inspection for missing or scummed holes; dose and
   focus separated by Ausschnitt's method.[^ausschnitt-1999] Rework is
   possible since nothing has yet been etched.

## Resources required

* **248 nm positive chemically amplified photoresist** ({ref}`lithography materials <material-lithography-materials>`); SkyWater's 2021 S-1
  names Dow, JSR and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo as photoresist
  suppliers.[^sec-01]
* **Organic BARC**, **HMDS**, **2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH developer** (the 2021 S-1
  names "Air Products & Chemicals, Inc., Moses Lake" for
  developer[^sec-01]), edge-bead remover, rinse
  solvents, {ref}`DI water <material-ultrapure-water>` and {ref}`nitrogen <material-process-gases>`.[^txt-02]
* **The VIM reticle** — typically a 6-inch quartz reticle, on our
  reading an attenuated phase-shift type with OPC, generated from the
  drawn `via` layer (68:44).[^pdk-06]
* **KrF excimer laser gas** (Kr/F₂/Ne fills) for the exposure tool.

## Open questions

* The exposure tool, wavelength, resist, BARC and reticle type
  (binary or attenuated PSM, with or without OPC) for `VIM` are not
  public; DUV with an attenuated PSM and OPC is an inference from the
  0.15 µm/0.17 µm rules and industry practice of the period.
* Why the PDK's "min. etch and fill capability for via" is 0.18 µm
  when the drawn via is 0.15 µm[^pdk-03] is not explained publicly;
  we read the 0.18 µm as the qualified fill size after the etch's
  positive bias, but this is an inference.
* The larger via sizes permitted inside `areaid.mt` (via.1b) — the
  layer the PDK defines as the "Location of e-test modules within the
  frame"[^pdk-06] — are, on our reading, confined to test structures;
  whether they are printed with the same process as product vias is
  not public.
* Whether a chemical-shrink or resist-trim step is used to reach the
  printed via size is not public.
