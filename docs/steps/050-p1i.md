## What this step is

`P1I` is the implant that turns the undoped amorphous-silicon film of
{ref}`SAGD <step-048>` into a conductor. With the
{ref}`RPM <step-049>` resist islands covering the future resistor
bodies, the whole remaining film — every future gate, every poly
interconnect and every varactor plate — receives a heavy n-type dose.
"Poly1" is the film's name in a naming scheme that allows for a second
poly; SKY130 has only one, so `P1I` is *the* gate implant.

The polarity is public. The PDK's generic resistor list describes the
poly layer as "N+ doped gate poly",[^pdk-07] and the extraction table
gives the finished poly a sheet resistance of 48.2 Ω/sq.[^pdk-08] For a
0.18 µm film[^pdk-03] that is a resistivity of about 0.9 mΩ·cm, the
value of degenerately phosphorus- or arsenic-doped polysilicon.[^kamins-1998]
Whether the species is phosphorus or arsenic, and the energy and dose,
are not public; both species were used for gate pre-doping at this
node, and the discussion below is generic.

Because the film is amorphous when implanted, there is no crystal
channelling and the as-implanted profile follows the amorphous-target
range statistics of {term}`LSS theory` almost exactly — one of the
practical conveniences of an a-Si gate film. The dopant is placed in
the upper part of the film and is redistributed through its whole
thickness by the later thermal steps, in which grain-boundary
diffusion in poly is orders of magnitude faster than diffusion in
single-crystal silicon.[^kamins-1972]

## Step category

`P1I` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*high-dose, low-energy* class — the category page groups it with the
resistor implants as "poly and resistor implants" and notes that "the
gate must be degenerately doped (high 10¹⁵ cm⁻²) to avoid poly
depletion". It is the first high-dose implant of the flow; everything
before it (wells, channels, punch-through stops) was in the
10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻² range. It is also the first implant into a deposited
film rather than into the substrate, and the first performed over a
resist mask on bare silicon rather than on oxide.

## Why this step exists

A polysilicon gate has to do three jobs that all depend on heavy
doping:

* **Be a good conductor.** We infer that SKY130 does not silicide its
  gates: the contact silicidation ({ref}`CSIL <step-098>`) happens
  inside the local-interconnect contact holes (the step name is "Contact
  silicidation"), and the 48.2 Ω/sq poly sheet resistance[^pdk-08] — an
  unsilicided value — is the evidence. The gate RC of a long poly line
  is therefore set entirely by this implant. ITRS 2001 lists gate sheet
  resistance among the front-end requirements for the node.[^itrs-01]
* **Not deplete.** When the gate is biased into inversion, a lightly
  doped poly gate develops a depletion layer at the oxide interface
  that adds to the effective oxide thickness. Schuegraf, King and Hu
  quantified the penalty for thin oxides,[^schuegraf-1993] and Arora,
  Rios and Huang modelled its impact on circuit
  performance;[^arora-1995][^rios-1994] the only cure is dopant
  concentration at the interface above about 10²⁰ cm⁻³, hence a dose in
  the 10¹⁵ cm⁻² range (typical industry value).[^txt-04]
* **Set the work function.** n⁺ poly on an NMOS gives the classic
  surface-channel device; on a PMOS it gives a buried-channel device
  unless the channel is counter-doped. See the open question below.

The reason the implant comes *before* gate patterning — rather than
doping the gate with the source/drain implants as a classic dual-gate
flow does[^wong-1988] — follows from what comes next in the step list
used in this reference: the poly is capped with nitride and oxide
({ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`, {ref}`POC <step-059>`) before it is
etched, and that cap is only cut open under the contacts much later
({ref}`NPCM <step-078>`). We infer that a capped gate cannot be doped
by the source/drain implants, so the gate must be fully doped now.
The PDK's "N+ doped gate poly"[^pdk-07] and the position of `P1I`
directly after deposition are consistent with this reading.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic gate pre-doping implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130 values are not public):

* **Species.** Phosphorus (³¹P⁺, from PH₃[^wiki-ph3]) or arsenic
  (⁷⁵As⁺, from AsH₃[^wiki-ash3]). Phosphorus diffuses readily along
  grain boundaries and gives a uniform gate; arsenic stays nearer the
  surface and segregates to grain boundaries, and needs more thermal
  budget to reach the oxide interface.[^mandurah-1981][^kamins-1972]
* **Energy.** Chosen so that the peak lies in the upper half of the
  0.18 µm film and the tail does not reach the gate oxide: an implant
  that penetrates the oxide would dope the channel. Tens of keV for
  phosphorus, somewhat more for arsenic, are typical (category page;
  range tables[^srim]).
* **Dose.** Of the order of 10¹⁵ cm⁻² and upwards (typical industry
  value for gate pre-doping[^txt-04]); SkyWater's high-dose implanter
  is specified to 5 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻².[^skw-01]
* **Tilt.** Normal incidence or a small tilt; channelling is irrelevant
  in an amorphous film, and a large tilt would shadow at the resist
  edges of {ref}`RPM <step-049>` (the mask-proximity effect analysed by
  Hook et al.[^hook-2003]).
* **Wafer handling.** High-current batch end station with wafer
  cooling: a 10¹⁵–10¹⁶ cm⁻² implant deposits enough energy to flow an
  uncooled resist, and the {ref}`RPM <step-049>` resist must survive.
  Charge neutralisation by electron flood to protect the gate oxide
  under the film (category page).
* **Anneal.** None dedicated; activation and redistribution happen in
  {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>`, {ref}`TIPRTAD <step-075>` and
  {ref}`RTAD <step-088>`. The film crystallises during the same steps
  ({ref}`SAGD <step-048>`).
* **Monitoring.** Four-point-probe sheet resistance on monitor wafers
  after a monitor anneal; thermal-wave dose monitoring is less useful
  on an amorphous film.

## Machines typically used

* **High-current ion implanter**, 200 mm, batch or single-wafer:
  Axcelis/Eaton GSD/HC and NV-GSD series, Varian VIISta 80/HC, Applied
  Materials xR LEAP and Quantum series (category page).
* **Four-point probe** for sheet resistance.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD high-dose implanter.** SkyWater lists "Axcelis GSD Hi
  dose B11, BF2, P, As 2-180kev, 5e12 to 5e16",[^skw-01] the only tool
  on its public list that reaches gate-doping doses; the medium-current
  8250 is specified only to 10¹⁴ cm⁻².[^skw-01][^axcelis-8250]
  Strength: **strong** for the tool; the assignment to this step is an
  inference from the dose. Axcelis describes the GSD family as the
  longest-supported batch implanter line.[^axcelis-gsd-page]
* A SkyWater maintenance-technician profile shows implanters being
  serviced in house[^skw-07] — corroboration, not tool evidence.

## Resources required

* **Phosphine (PH₃)** or **arsine (AsH₃)** source gas, or a solid
  source charge;[^wiki-ph3][^wiki-ash3] toxic gases held in gas
  cabinets with monitoring.
* **Source consumables** — filaments, arc-chamber liners, extraction
  electrodes; high-dose sources wear quickly.
* **Wafer-cooling water and helium**; **liquid nitrogen** for cryopump
  regeneration; nitrogen vent gas.
* **Monitor wafers** for sheet resistance.

## Open questions

* **n⁺ or p⁺ PMOS gates?** The PDK calls the poly "N+ doped gate
  poly",[^pdk-07] and the step list used in this reference dopes the
  whole film here with only the resistors protected and then caps it
  before etch. That reading implies n⁺ gates on both NMOS and PMOS
  (buried-channel PMOS). The channel-implant pages
  ({ref}`LVTPI <step-020>`, {ref}`PNCHI <step-024>`) took the opposite
  reading — surface-channel PMOS with a p⁺ gate — as their preferred
  interpretation of the Vt-implant module. No public source settles
  the question; a p⁺ gate would require the PMOS gate to be counter-
  doped through the cap by {ref}`PSDI <step-082>`, which the design
  rules neither confirm nor exclude. The two readings are recorded here
  as an open inconsistency.
* Species (P or As), energy, dose and tilt are not public.
* Whether `P1I` is a single implant or two (for example a shallow and
  a deep component) is not stated.
* Which implanter runs it is inferred from dose capability only.
