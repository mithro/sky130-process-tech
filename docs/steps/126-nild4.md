## What this step is

`NILD4` deposits the inter-level dielectric between metal 2 and
metal 3. Over the freshly etched metal-2 lines of
{ref}`MM2E <step-125>` — 0.36 µm tall,[^pdk-04] as close as 0.14 µm
apart[^pdk-periph] — a silicon dioxide film is deposited thick enough
to fill every gap between the lines and to bury them under a
continuous layer, which {ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>` then polishes flat
and {ref}`NCAPOX4 <step-128>` caps before the via-2 mask
({ref}`VIM2 <step-129>`). The name follows the PDK: its process stack
diagram labels the dielectric between `met2` and `met3` "NILD4" with
a relative permittivity of 4.2, draws beside it a "NILD4_C" of
permittivity 3.5 and thickness 0.030 µm, and gives the via-2 height
through it as 0.42 µm.[^pdk-04] The diagram's levels agree: the bottom
of `met2` is at 2.0061 µm and the bottom of `met3` at 2.7861 µm, a
difference of 0.78 µm that is exactly the 0.36 µm metal plus the
0.42 µm via height.[^pdk-04]

What differs from {ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`, the dielectric one level
down, is not the gap — metal 2's width, space and thickness are
metal 1's — but the finished thickness above the lines: 0.42 µm
against 0.27 µm.[^pdk-04] Nothing public says why. On our reading the
thicker dielectric goes with what sits on top of it: a via 2 of
0.20 µm rather than 0.15 µm (via2.1a[^pdk-periph]), which tolerates a
deeper hole, and a metal 3 of 0.3 µm width and space (m3.1,
m3.2[^pdk-periph]) and 0.845 µm thickness[^pdk-04] — or 2 µm in the
thick-metal option the assumptions table describes[^pdk-03] — whose
larger, longer wires benefit from more spacing to the level below
(inference). The PDK's permittivity of 4.2 for NILD4, against 4.5 for
NILD3 and 4.0–4.1 for the levels above,[^pdk-04] and the separately
named NILD4_C at 3.5,[^pdk-04] repeat the pattern discussed at
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`: a stack of at least two films, one thin and
with the permittivity of a fluorinated oxide,[^denison-1996][^usami-1993]
whose identity is not public.

## Step category

`NILD4` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *dielectric gap-fill* class, the direct repeat of
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`, which sets out the two routes a 130 nm-era
fab had for a 0.14 µm gap of 2.6:1 {term}`aspect ratio` between
aluminium lines: {term}`HDP-CVD`, with simultaneous argon sputtering
to keep the gap mouth open, and {term}`PECVD` {term}`TEOS` oxide with
etch-back. SkyWater's list describes its HDP tool as having "high
aspect (5:1) fill capability",[^skw-01] comfortably above what this
gap needs. What is specific to this instance is the thicker
overburden the 0.42 µm target implies, and the fact that the wafer
now carries two aluminium levels and a longer thermal and plasma
history: the temperature limit of Al–Cu (roughly 400–450 °C,
industry-typical[^txt-05]) and the charging of longer antennas both
bind more tightly.

## Why this step exists

The inter-metal dielectric does the four jobs set out at
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`; the via-2 level sharpens three of them:

* **Insulation without voids.** A void between two 0.14 µm-spaced
  metal-2 lines is a path for the {ref}`VIM2E <step-130>` etch and a
  moisture trap; Nguyen's review explains how the HDP
  deposition/sputter balance fills such a gap without a
  seam,[^nguyen-1999] the Applied Materials and Novellus patents give
  production recipes,[^pat-hdp-amat][^pat-hdp-novellus] and Yoo and
  Swope show a PECVD fluorine-doped oxide filling inter-metal gaps of
  this class.[^yoo-1996] Novellus still calls HDP "the preferred
  gapfill dielectric technology for advanced geometries".[^lam-speed]
* **Capacitance to two neighbours.** The film sets both the
  line-to-line capacitance within metal 2 and the level-to-level
  capacitance to metal 3, which the PDK's extraction tables
  encode;[^pdk-08] the ITRS 2001 interconnect chapter tracked the move
  from 4.1 oxide to fluorine-doped oxide (κ 3.7),[^itrs-02] and
  Stamper, Fuselier and Tian set out why the RC of these levels
  matters at the sub-0.25 µm generation.[^stamper-1998] A thicker
  NILD4 lowers the metal-2-to-metal-3 term.
* **Protection of the metal without damage.** The oxide must not
  stress the lines into voiding[^yue-1985] or let them hillock during
  deposition,[^chaudhari-1974] and the plasma must not charge the
  gates the metal-2 antennas reach through metal 1 and the contacts:
  Hwang and Giapis explained the charging mechanism during HDP oxide
  deposition,[^hwang-1998] Roche and McVittie applied a charging probe
  to a production HDP-CVD tool,[^roche-1996] and Byun et al. and Kim,
  Lee and Seo describe recipe and liner measures that reduce the
  damage.[^byun-2005][^kim-2004-hdp]
* **A polishable overburden.** {ref}`CMPM2 <step-127>` needs enough
  oxide above the highest metal to reach a flat surface at the 0.42 µm
  target with margin for the polish's pattern-density dependence; the
  PDK's "Oxide Bias for MM2" of 0.6 and "Min pattern density for
  oxide" of 0.75[^pdk-03] are, on our reading, the fill and density
  controls that keep that overburden uniform.

Without `NILD4` there is nothing to carry metal 3, and metal 2 is
exposed.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic inter-metal dielectric deposition over 0.14 µm
aluminium spaces in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not
public); the sequence is that of {ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`.

1. **Liner.** A thin PECVD oxide (silane or TEOS based) at
   350–400 °C (industry-typical[^txt-05][^raupp-1992]) over the metal,
   deposited without high-energy ion bombardment so that the
   aluminium and the gates beneath its antennas are not
   charged.[^hwang-1998][^byun-2005] Whether the PDK's 0.030 µm
   "NILD4_C"[^pdk-04] is such a liner is an inference.
2. **Gap fill by HDP-CVD.** SiH₄/O₂/Ar in an inductively coupled
   plasma with RF bias on the wafer, at a few mTorr and a
   sputter-to-deposition ratio tuned so that argon sputtering keeps
   the mouth of each 0.14 µm gap open until it fills from the
   bottom;[^nguyen-1999][^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat] backside
   helium and a cooled chuck hold the wafer at a temperature
   compatible with aluminium. SiF₄ can be added for a fluorinated
   film.[^denison-1996] Lieberman and Lichtenberg treat the
   ion-bombardment physics.[^lieberman-2005] SkyWater lists
   "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma (HDP) doped and phos doped with
   sputter etch – high aspect (5:1) fill capability",[^skw-01] and a
   maintenance-page photograph names "a Novellus high density plasma
   tool".[^skw-07]
3. **Gap fill by PECVD TEOS (alternative).** TEOS/O₂ plasma oxide at
   about 400 °C,[^raupp-1992][^wiki-teos] in a
   deposition–etch–deposition sequence; SkyWater's "PECVD TEOS, C2 and
   Producer"[^skw-01] is the capability.
4. **Overburden.** After the gaps are full, deposition continues — HDP
   or a faster PECVD TEOS cap — until the oxide over the metal is
   thick enough for the polish; with a 0.42 µm finished
   thickness[^pdk-04] the overburden here is larger than at NILD3,
   and a TEOS cap over an HDP fill is the usual way to deposit it
   quickly (industry practice[^txt-05]).
5. **Chamber clean.** NF₃ remote-plasma clean between wafers or lots.
6. **Metrology.** Thickness and refractive index by ellipsometry on
   monitors and test pads; stress by wafer bow; gap fill by
   cross-section SEM on dense metal-2 arrays; charging damage by
   antenna test structures at {term}`e-test`; particles.

## Machines typically used

* **HDP-CVD system**, 200 mm: Novellus SPEED,[^novellus-history][^lam-speed]
  Applied Materials Ultima HDP-CVD,[^pat-hdp-amat] Lam/Trikon
  ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **PECVD TEOS system**: Applied Materials Producer / Centura
  DxZ,[^amat-10k] Novellus Concept One/Two, Sequel.[^novellus-history]
* **Ellipsometer**, **stress gauge**, **cross-section SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus HDP-CVD.** SkyWater lists "Lam/Novellus High Density
  Plasma (HDP) doped and phos doped with sputter etch – high aspect
  (5:1) fill capability"[^skw-01] and its maintenance page names "a
  Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07] Strength: **strong**
  for the tool class; the assignment to this step is an **inference**
  from the gap geometry, and the public entry mentions only doped
  films; the model (SPEED is the Novellus HDP
  product[^novellus-history]) is not public.
* **PECVD TEOS "C2 and Producer".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; medium for depositing the liner and/or the overburden
  here (inference); as the gap fill itself it would need the
  deposition–etch–deposition sequence above.
* **PECVD silane oxide "C1"**[^skw-01] as a liner candidate (weak for
  assignment).

## Resources required

* **Silane, oxygen and argon** for HDP-CVD;[^nguyen-1999] **SiF₄** if
  the film is fluorinated;[^denison-1996] **TEOS and oxygen** (with
  helium or nitrogen carrier) for PECVD TEOS.[^wiki-teos] Gas
  suppliers per SkyWater's filings.[^sec-01]
* **Helium** for backside cooling; **NF₃** for the chamber clean.
* **Chamber consumables** — ceramic domes, coils, showerheads, liners.
* **Monitor wafers** for thickness, index and stress.

* The route (HDP-CVD, PECVD TEOS with etch-back, or a combination),
  the liner, the deposited thickness and whether the film is
  fluorinated are not public.
* Why the finished dielectric above metal 2 is 0.42 µm where that
  above metal 1 is 0.27 µm[^pdk-04] is not public; the association
  with the larger via 2 and the thicker metal 3 is our inference.
* What "NILD4_C" (k 3.5, 0.030 µm)[^pdk-04] represents, and why NILD4
  is labelled k 4.2 against NILD3's 4.5,[^pdk-04] are not public.

  Plasma" entry[^skw-01] is not public.
