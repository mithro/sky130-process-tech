## What this step is

`NTM` is the first mask of the source/drain-extension module and the
first lithography step after the gate has been etched. The wafer
arriving from {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>` carries the patterned polysilicon
gates from {ref}`P1ME <step-062>`, re-covered — on this reference's
reading of {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>` — by a thin {term}`screen oxide` grown over the exposed
silicon and the poly sidewalls. `NTM` coats this now-topographic surface
with photoresist, exposes it through the *N-tip implant* {term}`reticle` and
develops it, leaving resist windows over the transistors that are to
receive the standard n-type {term}`extension` ("tip") and its
{term}`halo`. The arsenic tip implant {ref}`ASTI <step-065>` and the
boron halo implant {ref}`BHI <step-066>` are then made through the
same resist, which is removed at {ref}`ASTIS <step-067>` (inference:
the tip and its halo belong to the same transistors, so one mask
serves both).

The PDK's mask table lists "N-tip Implant, NTM" as a mask used in
SKY130.[^pdk-05] The generated mask layer is `cntm` (GDS 27:0, "N-tip
implant mask") with a drawing purpose (26:20) and "mask add" (26:21) and
"mask drop" (26:22) purposes;[^pdk-06] there is no designer-drawn `ntm`
layer in the layer list, so we infer that the reticle is derived
entirely by Boolean operations from the drawn device layers (active,
poly, the n-type source/drain layer `nsdm` and the thick-oxide identifier
`hvi`), with the add/drop purposes providing manual corrections. The two
other tip masks in the same module *are* drawn by designers: `hvntm`
(125:20, "High voltage N-tip implant") and `ldntm` (11:44, "N-tip
implant on SONOS devices").[^pdk-06]

Unusually for an implant-block mask, the PDK's *Criteria & Assumptions*
page publishes several parameters that describe this mask's job: a tip
implant angle of 7° ("Angle for tip implant"), an "NTM shadowing"
distance of 0.16 µm, a "Min width of tip implant opening" of 0.1 µm, a
"pseudo-shadowing" allowance of 0.045 µm, and a lateral "N Tip (As)"
extent of 0.01 µm in its junction-depth table (variable
`LDNTIP`).[^pdk-03] These are the quantities a mask-generation flow
needs to size the resist opening so that a tilted implant reaches the
gate edge without being {term}`shadowed <shadowing>` by the resist wall.

## Step category

`NTM` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *implant-block* type, like {ref}`LVTNM <step-014>` and the
well masks, but with one difference that matters: the implants it
admits are *self-aligned to the polysilicon gate*, not to the resist. The
resist only selects which transistors are implanted; the gate edge sets
where the tip begins. The mask therefore has generous device-scale
features and coarse tolerances, yet its {term}`overlay` to the poly pattern
still matters because a resist edge that comes too close to a gate
shadows the tilted implant (the "NTM shadowing" allowance
above).[^pdk-03] It is the first mask that must be printed over the
gate-stack topography — 0.18 µm of poly[^pdk-03] plus the roughly
0.2 µm cap read on the {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` page[^pdk-03] — rather
than on a planar surface.

## Why this step exists

The lightly doped drain, introduced by Ogura and co-workers in 1980,
places a lightly doped n⁻ region between the channel and the heavily
doped n⁺ drain so that the drain field is spread out and hot-carrier
generation is reduced;[^ogura-1980] Codella and Ogura added the halo — a
p-type {term}`pocket` implanted around the extension — to hold the threshold
voltage up as channels shortened.[^codella-1985] By the 130 nm node the
"LDD" had become the "extension": no longer lightly doped, but the
shallowest and most abrupt junction in the transistor. ITRS 2001 asks
for a drain-extension junction depth of 27–45 nm, a lateral abruptness
of 7.2 nm/decade and a {term}`spacer` thickness of 48–95 nm for the 2001
technology year, and defines the extension junction depth at the channel
as 0.55 times the physical gate length.[^itrs-01]

Three tip masks exist because three transistor families need three
different extensions:

* **1.8 V NMOS** (`nfet_01v8`, its low-Vt variant and the SRAM
  transistors) need a shallow, heavily doped arsenic tip for
  short-channel control at 0.15 µm gate length and low series
  resistance.[^pdk-07] This is the `NTM` tip.
* **5 V NMOS** (`nfet_g5v0d10v5` and the {term}`drain-extended <DEMOS>` devices that
  share its 110 Å gate oxide)[^pdk-hv] must survive a 5.5 V drain: they
  need a deeper, more graded, lightly doped drain of the original {term}`LDD`
  kind. That is the {ref}`HVNTM <step-068>` tip, whose "Angle for HV tip
  implant" of 40° in the same assumptions table[^pdk-03] confirms it is
  a different implant altogether.
* **{term}`SONOS` memory transistors** get their own tip through
  {ref}`LDNTM <step-071>`, the "N-tip implant on SONOS devices"
  layer.[^pdk-06]

Without `NTM`, every NMOS would receive the same extension: the 5 V
devices would fail hot-carrier lifetime with a 1.8 V-style abrupt tip,
or the 1.8 V devices would lose drive current and short-channel margin
with a 5 V-style graded one. Separate LDD implants for different
transistor types on one chip are a recurring theme of the patent
literature of the era.[^pat-multi-ldd-amd][^pat-ldd-micron]

There is no P-tip mask in the PDK's mask table.[^pdk-05] How the PMOS
extensions are formed is therefore not stated publicly; we return to
this under *Open questions*.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic tip-mask lithography sequence for a 200 mm, 130
nm-era fab:

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake and vapour {term}`HMDS` prime. The
   surface is now oxide (the screen oxide from {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>`)
   over silicon and over the capped poly gates.
2. **Resist coat.** A single-layer positive resist of about 1 µm; the
   PDK's nominal "Photoresist thickness" is 1.14 µm.[^pdk-03] The tip
   and halo implants that follow are at keV to tens of keV, which such a
   resist stops with a large margin (industry-typical).[^txt-02] The
   poly steps cause resist-thickness variation and reflective notching
   at poly edges; because the features are large this is tolerated
   without a bottom anti-reflective coating (inference from the
   feature sizes).
3. **Exposure.** The openings enclose whole transistors, so the critical
   dimensions are of the order of the 0.7 µm width and spacing rules
   the PDK gives for the comparable `hvntm` layer[^pdk-periph] — well
   within an i-line {term}`stepper`'s reach ({term}`k₁ <k1>` = 0.7 × 0.6 / 0.365 ≈ 1.2 at
   {term}`NA` 0.6).[^wiki-litho] ITRS 2001 reserves 248 nm for the critical
   layers only,[^itrs-03] so we infer that `NTM`, like the other
   implant-block masks, is an i-line layer.
4. **Alignment.** To the poly pattern (inferred; the alignment tree is
   not public). The overlay budget is set not by the printed feature
   but by shadowing: the PDK's 0.16 µm "NTM shadowing" and 0.1 µm "Min
   width of tip implant opening"[^pdk-03] are the distances the mask
   generator keeps between a resist edge and a gate so that the 7°
   implant is not blocked.
5. **Develop** in 2.38 % (0.26 N) TMAH,[^txt-02] rinse and dry; a post-develop
   bake or UV cure hardens the resist against the two implants that
   follow (industry practice).[^txt-02][^lee-1996]
6. **Inspection.** Overlay to poly on box-in-box targets, {term}`CD` sampling,
   after-develop defect inspection.

## Open questions

* **PMOS extensions.** The mask table has no P-tip mask,[^pdk-05] yet
  the *Criteria & Assumptions* page lists an "HVPTM shadowing" value of
  0.089 µm alongside the NTM and HVNTM values,[^pdk-03] and the *High
  Voltage Methodology* page says hv p-channel devices "get the extra
  junction grading implant into the ringed gate with the HVPDM
  mask"[^pdk-hv] — a mask name absent from `masks.csv`.[^pdk-05]
  Whether the 1.8 V PMOS extension is implanted under the P⁺
  source/drain mask ({ref}`PSDM <step-081>`) before the spacer, or under
  a mask absent from the PDK's mask table, cannot be settled from
  public data.
* The exact Boolean recipe that generates `cntm` from the drawn layers
  is not public; the absence of a drawn `ntm` layer is our only
  evidence that it is derived.
* Resist thickness, exposure tool and any resist-hardening step are
  inferred from the PDK's assumptions and general practice.
* Whether the screen oxide from {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>` is the only
  film under the resist, or whether a {term}`poly re-oxidation` adds to it, is
  not public.
