## What this step is

`ALLY1` is the first of the two alloy anneals described in this
reference — the other,
{ref}`ALLY <step-170>`, is the conventional final forming-gas anneal
after the last metal and passivation. An {term}`alloy anneal` in the
industry's vocabulary is a low-temperature (350–450 °C, typical;
category page[^txt-01]) furnace anneal in hydrogen-bearing
{term}`forming gas` whose job is to passivate the Si/SiO₂ interface
with hydrogen and to sinter metal-to-silicon contacts. What is
unusual here is the position: `ALLY1` comes *before* any metal is on
the wafer, with the contact holes open and the silicon at their
bottoms freshly cleaned by {ref}`SACETCH <step-095>`, and just
before the titanium {term}`liner` of {ref}`TI/TIN1 <step-097>`. There is
nothing to sinter. The step list used in this reference does not
explain the anneal's purpose; SkyWater's capability list shows the
process exists — "H2 and forming gas alloy" among the Aviza furnace
processes[^skw-01] — but does not say where it is used.

We read the step, as an inference, as a hydrogen anneal that
(a) passivates the plasma damage and interface traps created by the
contact etch and the earlier plasma steps before they are sealed
under titanium, tungsten and nitride, and (b) drives residual water
and hydrogen-bearing species out of the phosphosilicate glass and
the {term}`cap oxide` so that they are not released onto the liner or into
the tungsten chamber. A third possibility — that the anneal is
there to condition the freshly HF-cleaned silicon surface before
sputtering — is discussed below. Which, if any, is the actual reason
is not public.

## Step category

`ALLY1` is an {ref}`Anneal / thermal processing <category-anneal>`
step of the *alloy / forming-gas* type — the lowest-temperature
anneal so far in the flow and the first in a reducing ambient. Its
{term}`thermal budget` is negligible for the junctions: at 400 °C dopant
diffusion is immeasurable and the junctions of {ref}`RTAD <step-088>`
and {ref}`RTAD2 <step-092>` are untouched. What it *can* change is
hydrogen: hydrogen diffuses readily through oxide at these
temperatures, passivates dangling bonds at the Si/SiO₂
interface[^reed-1988][^cartier-1993] and, less welcome, can
neutralise boron acceptors in silicon[^sah-1983][^pankove-1983]
and alter the charge-trapping nitride of the {term}`SONOS` cells
({ref}`ONO <step-040>`; Maes, Usmani and Heyns measured the effect
of a hydrogen anneal on MNOS retention[^maes-1981]). Those
side-effects are the reason the temperature, time and hydrogen
content of an alloy anneal are recipe parameters rather than
defaults.

## Why this step exists

The physics of a forming-gas anneal is settled; the reasons for
placing one here are inferred.

* **Interface-trap passivation.** Wikipedia summarises the purpose:
  "a high-temperature anneal in forming gas assists in
  silicon-silicon dioxide interface passivation".[^wiki-fg] Reed and
  Plummer set out the chemistry of interface-trap annealing,[^reed-1988]
  Cartier, Stathis and Buchanan the passivation and depassivation of
  dangling bonds by atomic hydrogen,[^cartier-1993] Brower the
  dissociation kinetics of the passivated defects[^brower-1990] and
  Stesmans the passivation of P_b0 and P_b1 centres by molecular
  hydrogen[^stesmans-1996] — the defects whose density sets the
  threshold-voltage stability and the 1/f noise of the analogue
  transistors in the PDK. Deal's terminology for the charges
  involved is standard.[^deal-1980]
* **Plasma damage before it is buried.** The contact etch
  ({ref}`LICM1E <step-094>`) is the most damaging plasma exposure the
  gate oxides have seen — charging through the contact holes and
  the borderless nitride (Cacciato et al.[^cacciato-2003]) and the
  electron-shading mechanism Hashimoto identified[^hashimoto-1994]
  — and Rangan, Krishnan and Ashok showed that hydrogen (or
  deuterium) passivation repairs process-induced damage.[^rangan-1998]
  Once tungsten and titanium nitride are on the wafer, hydrogen has
  a harder path to the interface; an anneal now reaches every
  transistor through the open contacts and the still-thin oxide
  stack (inference).
* **Outgassing the dielectric.** The HDP glass and the {term}`PECVD` cap
  hold water and hydrogen (Levin[^levin-1982]; Adams et al. for
  plasma oxide[^adams-1981-pecvd]); a 400 °C anneal after the wet
  clean drives them out before the sputtered liner seals the surface
  and before the wafer enters the tungsten chamber, where water
  reacts with WF₆.[^wiki-wf6] A {term}`degas` is standard practice before
  {term}`PVD` (category page; SkyWater's "AMAT PVD Metal" entry lists
  "Sputter etch, degas"[^skw-01]); a furnace alloy does the same
  for a whole lot at once (inference).
* **Surface conditioning.** The {term}`HF-last` silicon of
  {ref}`SACETCH <step-095>` is hydrogen-terminated;[^cerofolini-1998]
  a hydrogen anneal keeps it so and removes adsorbed fluorine and
  hydrocarbons before the titanium arrives — the surface that the
  {term}`silicide` reaction of {ref}`CSIL <step-098>` depends on. Whether
  this is a design intent or a side-effect is not public.

"Alloy" is the industry's generic word for a forming-gas anneal —
SkyWater's list uses it so[^skw-01] — and, on this reading, implies no
alloying at this step.

Without `ALLY1`, on this reading, the interface damage of the
contact module would be sealed in and passivated only by the final
{ref}`ALLY <step-170>`, through five metal levels.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic forming-gas alloy anneal for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

* **Tool and ambient.** Vertical batch furnace with a forming-gas
  supply — "typical forming gas formulations (5% H2 in N2)"[^wiki-fg]
  — or pure hydrogen diluted at the tool; SkyWater lists "H2 and
  forming gas alloy" on its Aviza furnaces.[^skw-01] The tube is
  purged of oxygen before hydrogen is admitted; hydrogen
  concentrations below the 4 % flammability limit in nitrogen are
  used where the tool is not rated for pure hydrogen.
* **Temperature and time.** 350–450 °C for 20–60 min (typical
  industry values; category page[^txt-01][^txt-02]). Before any
  aluminium is on the wafer the ceiling is not the Al–Si eutectic
  but the stability of the surfaces: the bare silicon and poly at
  the contact bottoms must not oxidise, which the reducing ambient
  guarantees, and the hydrogen must not deactivate the boron in the
  P⁺ source/drains, which limits time and temperature
  (Sah, Sun and Tzou[^sah-1983]).
* **Sequence.** Load; nitrogen purge; ramp; hydrogen admitted at
  temperature; soak; hydrogen purged before ramp-down; unload. Queue
  time to the liner deposition kept short so that the native oxide
  does not regrow on the contact bottoms.[^morita-1990]
* **Single-wafer alternative.** A forming-gas anneal in an {term}`RTP`
  chamber, or the degas station of the PVD {term}`cluster tool`, does the
  outgassing part of the job; SkyWater's Heatpulse 8808 lists
  NH₃, Ar, N₂ and O₂ but not H₂,[^skw-01] which argues for the
  furnace here (inference).
* **Metrology.** Interface-trap density by charge pumping or C–V on
  test structures ({ref}`category-test`); contact-chain resistance
  after the plug module.

* **{ref}`Vertical furnace <machine-vertical-furnace-anneal>`** with forming-gas or hydrogen capability:
  Aviza/SVG/Thermco AVP,[^aviza-avp] ASM A400,[^asm-vf] TEL Alpha-8,

  measurement.

* **Aviza furnaces, "H2 and forming gas alloy".** SkyWater states
  that "Furnaces are all made by Aviza" and lists "H2 and forming gas
  alloy" among the furnace processes.[^skw-01] Strength: **strong**
  for the existence of a furnace alloy process; assignment to
  `ALLY1` is an **inference**: the furnace process is the only anneal
  listed with H₂ or forming gas (the furnaces' "H2 and forming gas
  alloy"; the Heatpulse lists NH₃ but no H₂ or
  forming gas, below); whether it is used here as well as at
  {ref}`ALLY <step-170>` is not public. Dealer documentation
  describes the Aviza/SVG AVP-8000 as a vertical batch furnace for
  150–200 mm wafers.[^aviza-avp]
* **AG Associates Heatpulse 8808** — no H₂ or forming gas among its
  listed gases ("NH3, Ar, N2, O2");[^skw-01]
  weak.

## Resources required

* **Forming gas** (H₂ in N₂; "5% H2 in N2" is the typical formulation
  Wikipedia gives[^wiki-fg]) or **hydrogen** and **nitrogen** supplied
  separately; gas suppliers named in SkyWater's 2021 S-1: Air Products,
  Praxair.[^sec-01]
* **Quartz tube, boats and baffles**; hydrogen-rated gas panel with
  leak detection and purge interlocks.
* **Monitor wafers** (SEMI M8 class)[^semi-m8] with MOS capacitors
  for interface-trap tracking.

## Open questions

* The purpose of an alloy anneal before any metal is deposited is
  not public; the passivation, outgassing and surface-conditioning
  readings above are inferences.
* The ambient (forming gas or hydrogen), temperature and time are
  not public.
* Whether the step is a furnace batch anneal or a single-wafer
  degas/anneal on the PVD platform is not public; the furnace
  reading rests on SkyWater's "H2 and forming gas alloy" entry.
* How the SONOS nitride's trap population responds to a hydrogen
  anneal at this point, and whether the recipe is constrained by it,
  is not public.
