## What this step is

`PWI2` is the second P-well implant, run through the same
{ref}`PWBM <step-026>` resist as {ref}`PWI <step-027>` and directly
after it. Like its N-well twin {ref}`NWI2 <step-019>`, it has no public
description; this reference describes it as a second boron dose at a
different energy, making the SKY130 P-well a two-energy
{term}`chained implant` (inference). No public source describes the
pair; chaining is, however, how every published {term}`retrograde P-well <retrograde well>` of the
era is built.[^pat-well-ibm][^pat-well-hynix][^pat-twin-harris][^txt-01]
The category page draws the same inference.

At PWI2 a second P-well implant goes through the same block-mask opening, directly after PWI; the drawing is the state at this step, which is the same as before it. That PWI2 is a second boron dose at another energy is this reference's inference;[^txt-01] whether it is the shallower or the deeper member of the pair is not public, so the drawing does not divide the well between the two implants. The arrows are drawn vertical because the tilt is not public. The NMOS channel implant, the PMOS channel implant and the liner oxide are drawn faded. Not to scale.

The P-well is characterised in the PDK by a single peak of 4 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³
at 0.42 µm with a "y.char" of 0.13 µm;[^pdk-03] a two-implant well fits
such a description if the second implant is either shallower and lighter
(broadening the profile towards the surface) or deeper and lighter
(extending it towards the 0.75 µm vertical dimension).

## Step category

`PWI2` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*well* class, sharing mask, species and anneal with
{ref}`PWI <step-027>`; its implanter depends on its energy.

## Why this step exists

A single boron implant leaves the region between its peak and the
surface too lightly doped for three purposes that a P-well must
serve, each of which has a public design rule attached:

* **Field isolation.** Under the trench oxide between two n⁺ diffusions
  the doping must be high enough to prevent inversion and {term}`punch-through`;
  the PDK's assumption for "n+ - n+" punch-through spacing at 3.3 V is
  0.23 µm.[^pdk-03] A Hynix triple-well flow uses a separate "n channel
  field stop" boron implant at "about 50 keV to about 80 keV" and 5 ×
  10¹¹–1 × 10¹³ cm⁻² for this;[^pat-dnw-hynix] in a chained well the
  shallower member does the same job.
* **Punch-through under the channel.** A moderate peak a few tenths of a
  micrometre below the gate is the retrograde-well mechanism for
  short-channel control;[^itrs-01] an LSI Logic patent describes
  "punch-through barriers" built from chained boron implants at 100, 70
  and 50 keV — there placed into an n-well, as a counter-doped barrier
  for a p-channel device, but the same chaining arithmetic applies in a
  p-well.[^pat-vt-lsi]
* **Lateral profile at the well edge.** The shallower members set the
  N-well/P-well junction position — 0.034 µm from the drawn edge in the
  PDK's assumptions[^pdk-03] — and hence the n⁺/p⁺ isolation
  rules.[^rubin-2002]

Published examples of the split: IBM's 550/150/45 keV boron
chain;[^pat-well-ibm] Hynix's 350 keV at 1.5 × 10¹³ cm⁻², 150 keV at 6.0
× 10¹² cm⁻² and 80 keV at 1.0 × 10¹² cm⁻²;[^pat-well-hynix] Harris's
360/185/55 keV.[^pat-twin-harris] SKY130's `PWI` + `PWI2` would, on our
inference, be the two-member version.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic second P-well implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(illustrative values only):

* **Species.** Boron (¹¹B⁺) from BF₃,[^wiki-implant] as for `PWI`.
* **Energy.** Different from `PWI`. The published shallow members lie
  between about 45 and
  185 keV.[^pat-well-ibm][^pat-well-hynix][^pat-twin-harris] Whether
  SKY130's second implant is the shallow or the deep one is not stated.
* **Dose.** Lower than the main implant — a few 10¹² cm⁻² is typical
  (the Hynix patent's shallower members are 6.0 × 10¹² and 1.0 ×
  10¹² cm⁻²).[^pat-well-hynix]
* **Tilt/twist.** 7° with twist; a shallower boron implant is the most
  channelling-prone case in the module, so the pad oxide's role as a
  randomising screen matters (category page).[^txt-02]
* **Sequence.** The step list places `PWI2` directly after `PWI`; it
  does not say which tool runs either implant.[^steps-sheet] The two
  implants share the resist's {term}`thermal budget`.
* **Anneal.** {ref}`RTAI <step-034>`; the published support for that
  placement is a Cypress patent that may still be in force, in the
  collapsed note below this list.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`High-energy batch implanter <machine-high-energy-implanter>`** (Axcelis GSD/HE, Varian VIISta 3000)
  or **{ref}`medium-current implanter <machine-medium-current-implanter>`** (Axcelis 8250HT "3keV to
  750keV"[^axcelis-8250]; Varian E500), depending on energy.
* **{term}`Four-point probe <four-point probe>` / {ref}`thermal-wave <machine-sheet-resistance-metrology>`** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis GSD high-current/high-energy implanter** ("B11 …
  10-3000kev").[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for existence; assignment is
  an inference. Both P-well implants, at the energies published for
  well implants of this kind (45–550 keV in the examples above), fall
  within the GSD's listed 10–3000 keV range,[^skw-01] so one tool could
  run both (inference); the step list does not say which tool runs
  either implant.[^steps-sheet]
* **Axcelis 8250** ("B11, BF2, As … 1e11 to 1e14")[^skw-01] is equally
  capable of a boron implant in the 100–200 keV band. Strength: strong
  for existence, weak for assignment.

## Resources required

* **{ref}`Boron trifluoride <material-dopant-sources>` (BF₃)**; support gases; cryopump and source
  consumables; {ref}`monitor wafers <material-substrates>` (category page).
* No new resist: the {ref}`PWBM <step-026>` resist is reused.

* That `PWI2` is a second boron energy is an inference from

  public.
* Whether it runs on the same implanter as `PWI` is unknown.
