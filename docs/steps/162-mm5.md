## What this step is

`MM5` is the metal-5 lithography, the last metal mask of the flow. A
resist is coated, exposed and developed on the blanket metal-5 stack of
{ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>` so that resist remains wherever the layout
draws `met5` (GDS 72:20, "Metal 5"[^pdk-06]) and is cleared everywhere
else; the exposed metal is removed at {ref}`MM5E <step-163>`. The
pattern holds the top-level power grid and wide buses, the {term}`bond pads <bond pad>`
that the later `pad` opening ("Passivation cut (opening over
pads)"[^pdk-06]) will expose, inductor turns and the landings of every
via 4.

The PDK's mask table lists "Metal 5, MM5" once, flagged as used in
SKY130.[^pdk-05] The periphery rules give "Min width of met5" 1.600 µm
(m5.1), "Min spacing between two met5" 1.600 µm (m5.2), a 4.000 µm²
minimum area (m5.4) and a via-4 enclosure of 0.310 µm
(m5.3);[^pdk-periph] the minimum-CD table gives `MM5CD`/`MM5CDSP`
0.8 µm for "All flows except S8PF*/S8PIR*" and 1.6 µm for
"S8PF*/S8PIR*".[^pdk-03] The background page lists the technology's
features as "5 levels of metal (p - penta)", "Inductor or
Inductor-Capable (i)", "Poly resistor (r)", "SONOS shrunken cell (s)"
and "Supports 10V regulated supply (10R)",[^pdk-02] which is the
letter-by-letter reading of the antenna chapter's "S8PIR-10R"
heading;[^pdk-11] the published periphery rules give "Min width of
met5" as 1.600 µm with no flow qualifier (m5.1),[^pdk-periph] which
agrees with the minimum-CD table only in its "S8PF*/S8PIR*"
row;[^pdk-03] and no metal-5 structure in the published test tile is
narrower than 1.6 µm — the widths it gives are 1.6, 2.0 and 2.4 µm —
every one labelled "s8pf" or "S8PIR/PF".[^raw-data-testtile-pads] We therefore read SKY130 as an
S8PIR* flow (inference), so both tables give 1.6 µm,
and the m5.4 exemption for probe pads "exactly 1.42um by 1.42um", which
applies "For all flows except SKY130PIR*/SKY130PF*", does not apply
here (inference; the corresponding metal-4 rule, m4.4, carries the same
1.42 µm probe-pad exemption without a flow qualifier[^pdk-periph]). For
example, the test tile carries "s8pir/pf M5 (w=1.6) serp / comb over
field",[^raw-data-testtile-pads] consistent with the 1.6 µm reading.
The metal under the resist is 1.26 µm thick on the
stack diagram,[^pdk-04] with a sheet resistance of 28.5 mΩ/sq.[^pdk-07]

## Step category

`MM5` is a {ref}`Photolithography (mask step) <category-lithography>`
step of the *line-and-space* type printed on metal, like
{ref}`MM1 <step-113>` to {ref}`MM4 <step-154>`, and the coarsest of them.
At 1.6 µm on an i-line (365 nm) stepper of {term}`NA` 0.6, the process factor
{term}`k₁ <k1>` is {math}`k_1 = 1.6 \times 0.6 / 0.365 \approx 2.6`, and even at the
0.8 µm the CD table gives other flows {math}`k_1 \approx 1.3` (our
arithmetic) — far from any
resolution limit, and ASML describes older exposure tools that "migrate
to the lithography of choice for less critical layers".[^asml-30] We
infer that
`MM5` is exposed on the "ASML I-line stepper" or "ASML I-line scanner"
SkyWater lists,[^skw-01] with a DNQ/novolac resist; nothing public states
the tool.

What is specific to this instance is thickness rather than resolution.
The resist must survive the etch of a 1.26 µm stack,[^pdk-04] and
aluminium etches in chlorine plasmas only a few times faster than
photoresist (industry-typical[^nojiri-2015]) — Krogh et al. monitored
resist erosion spectroscopically in an aluminium etch
plasma[^krogh-1987] — so the resist is thicker than at the lower metal
levels, and a thick resist in turn needs depth of focus and a careful
bake to hold its profile (industry practice[^mack-2007]).
The surface is flat — the metal was sputtered onto the polished via-4
level of {ref}`CMPM4 <step-157>` and {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>` — except,
on the fill reading of {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`, for shallow dimples
over the via-4 holes (inference).

## Why this step exists

Metal 5 is the top routing level and the package interface; the mask
fixes:

* **Width, spacing and resistance of the power grid.** At 28.5 mΩ/sq
  and 1.6 µm minimum width[^pdk-07][^pdk-periph] metal 5 carries the
  lowest-resistance routing of the flow; Bohr's argument that
  interconnect limits performance[^bohr-1995] and Stamper, Fuselier and
  Tian's account of wiring RC delay[^stamper-1998] are why an aluminium
  back end reserves its thickest level for global wiring. The spacing
  sets the gap the passivation of {ref}`NFUSOX <step-164>` and the
  steps after it must cover (inference).
* **Bond pads and probe pads.** The pads that wire bonds and probe
  needles touch are drawn in this layer; the `pad` spacing rule (pad.2,
  1.270 µm)[^pdk-periph] is the public trace. Hess et al. studied the reliability of
  bond-over-active pad structures, with circuitry under the pad, for
  0.13 µm CMOS.[^hess-2003]
* **Inductors.** A thick, wide top metal is the classic inductor level,
  as Chu et al. showed;[^chu-2001] the PDK calls the technology "Inductor
  or Inductor-Capable".[^pdk-02] The width and spacing of each turn are
  set here.
* **Enclosure of via 4.** Every via-4 opening must be enclosed by
  0.310 µm of metal 5 (m5.3)[^pdk-periph] — a larger margin than the
  lower levels need, which on the fill reading of
  {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>` keeps the metal that fills the via continuous
  with the line above it (inference). The overlay of this mask to
  {ref}`VIM4 <step-159>` is the registration that matters; Levinson's
  book has a chapter on overlay.[^levinson-2005]
* **Reflectivity.** The cap of the stack — read as TiW at
  {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`, on the February 2014 metal-stack change
  having excluded "top metal layers" ({ref}`overview-metal-cap`) — is a
  partial
  anti-reflective layer — the role Rocke and Schneegans documented for
  a titanium-nitride cap on aluminium and which a Ti:W cap plays in the
  same way (inference)[^rocke-1988] — and a thick resist on a
  reflective metal shows the {term}`swing-curve <swing curve>` effects Brunner
  analysed.[^brunner-1991] At these feature sizes a dyed resist or BARC
  is a latitude choice rather than a necessity (inference).
* **No fill for a polish.** The periphery rules list no {term}`pattern-density <pattern density>`
  or fill rule for metal 5, where metals 3 and 4 have `cmm3`/`cmm4` fill
  checks;[^pdk-periph] the PDK gives no reason. The step list records no
  polish after {ref}`CMPM4 <step-157>`,[^steps-sheet] and a density rule
  at this level would have nothing to planarise; both are arguments
  from an absence, and neither is positive evidence (inference). A low
  metal density does matter to the etch: Chang et al.
  studied corrosion defects in an ultra-low pattern-density thick-metal
  etch.[^chang-2004]

Without `MM5` metal 5 would remain a sheet shorting every via 4 and every
pad together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic thick-metal lithography sequence for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's is not public).

1. **Surface preparation.** Dehydration bake; no {term}`HMDS` is needed
   on metal; optionally a {term}`BARC` or a dyed resist (Brunner's
   swing-curve analysis[^brunner-1991]).
2. **Resist coat.** A positive DNQ/novolac i-line
   resist[^dammel-1993][^reichmanis-1989][^wiki-dnq] thick enough, at the
   etch's resist selectivity, to outlast the 1.26 µm stack (industry
   practice[^nojiri-2015]); the PDK's 1.14 µm "Photoresist
   thickness"[^pdk-03] is an antenna-rule assumption.
3. **Exposure.** i-line on a 5× or 4× reduction tool — the ASML PAS 5500
   family spans i-line and DUV models on one platform[^asml-30][^chiphistory-pas5500]
   — with conventional illumination and a binary reticle; the focus is
   set for the full resist thickness (industry practice[^mack-2007]).
4. **Post-exposure bake, develop and hard bake.** {term}`PEB`, 2.38 % (0.26 N)
   TMAH develop, rinse and dry;[^txt-02] a hard bake raises the resist's
   resistance to the chlorine etch (industry practice[^txt-02]). Allen
   and Rickard found that the slope of an etched aluminium line need not
   follow the slope of the resist,[^allen-1994] so the bake is set for
   etch resistance rather than profile.
5. **Alignment.** To the {ref}`VIM4 <step-159>` pattern, whose holes print
   shallow features into the metal surface (inference from the fill
   reading at {ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`), with the 0.310 µm enclosure
   (m5.3)[^pdk-periph] as the governing margin.
6. **Inspection and metrology.** Line {term}`CD` by optical CD or
   {term}`CD-SEM` on the AMAT Verity/VeraSEM class; overlay on the KLA
   5200/5300/Archer class;[^skw-01] after-develop inspection for resist
   defects over pads; dose and focus tracked by Ausschnitt's
   method.[^ausschnitt-1999]

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`i-line stepper or scanner <machine-i-line-stepper>`**, 200 mm: ASML PAS 5500 i-line
  class,[^asml-30][^chiphistory-pas5500] Nikon NSR-i and Canon FPA i-line
  systems ({ref}`category-lithography`).
* **{ref}`Coat/develop track <machine-coat-develop-track>`** capable of thick resist (TEL ACT/Lithius,[^tel-act]

  migration of older exposure tools to "less critical layers" that ASML
  describes.[^asml-30]

* **CD — AMAT Verity, VeraSEM; overlay — KLA 5200/5300/Archer.**[^skw-01]
  Strength: strong for existence (SkyWater statement); use at this
  mask is an inference.

## Open questions

* **Minimum width.** That SKY130 is an S8PIR* flow, under which the
  minimum-CD table and periphery rules both give 1.6 µm,[^pdk-03][^pdk-periph]
  is our reading of the background page;[^pdk-02] neither table names
  SKY130's flow.
* The exposure tool, resist type and thickness, BARC and bake are not
  public.
* Whether metal-5 fill or density rules exist for the aluminium flow is
  not stated in the public rule tables; this reference describes none.
* Whether the via-4 dimples, if any, affect alignment or pad flatness is
  not public.
