## What this step is

`NILD6` deposits the inter-level dielectric between metal 4 and metal 5.
Over the freshly etched metal-4 lines of {ref}`MM4E <step-155>` —
0.845 µm tall on the PDK's stack diagram,[^pdk-04] as close as
0.300 µm apart (m4.2[^pdk-periph]) — and over the second-level
{term}`MiM capacitors <MiM capacitor>` on some of them, a silicon
dioxide film — the diagram's k = 4.0 is within a few per cent of
thermal oxide's 3.9[^pdk-04] and the step list calls the step an "ILD
oxide deposition",[^steps-sheet] though neither is a statement of the
film's composition (inference) — is deposited thick
enough to fill every gap and bury the lines and the `cap2m` plates;
{ref}`CMPM4 <step-157>` polishes it flat and {ref}`NCAPOX6 <step-158>`
caps it before the via-4 mask ({ref}`VIM4 <step-159>`). The PDK's stack
diagram labels the dielectric between `metal4` and `metal5` "NILD6" with
a relative permittivity of 4.0, draws no separate "_C" film beside it,
and places the bottom of `metal5` 1.35 µm above the bottom of `metal4`
(5.3711 µm against 4.0211 µm) — the 0.845 µm metal plus a labelled
0.505 µm of dielectric above it, the via-4 height.[^pdk-04] This page
uses "NILD6" as the PDK does; the name is the diagram's label.

The finished dielectric is thicker than the 0.39 µm above metal 3,[^pdk-04]
and the extraction tables agree: they give a metal-4-to-metal-5 plate
capacitance of 68.33 aF/µm²,[^pdk-08] which for {math}`k = 4.0`
corresponds to about 0.52 µm of oxide (our arithmetic,
{math}`d = \varepsilon_0 k / C`), close to the diagram's 0.505 µm. The
gap to be filled is metal 3's: a 0.30 µm space between 0.845 µm lines,
an {term}`aspect ratio` of about 2.8:1 (our arithmetic). The surface
also carries the second capacitor: every `cap2m` plate — of the order
of 0.1 µm thick on the capacitor dielectric, on our reading at
{ref}`CAPTIW2 <step-151>` — stands on its metal-4 bottom plate, and the
oxide deposited here surrounds the plate edge, the site of the `CMIM2P`
periphery capacitance (0.19 fF/µm).[^pdk-07]

## Step category

`NILD6` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *dielectric {term}`gap-fill <gap fill>`* class and the last repeat of
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`, which sets out the two routes a 130 nm-era fab
had between aluminium lines: {term}`HDP-CVD`, with simultaneous argon
sputtering to keep the gap mouth open, and {term}`PECVD` {term}`TEOS`
oxide with etch-back. SkyWater lists "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma
(HDP) doped and phos doped with sputter etch" with "high aspect (5:1)
fill capability";[^skw-01] the entry names only doped films, so its use
for an undoped ILD here is an inference. The 5:1 figure is above the
2.8:1 needed here. What is specific to this instance, next to
{ref}`NILD5 <step-141>`, is the larger finished thickness above the
metal (0.505 µm against 0.39 µm[^pdk-04]) and so a larger total
deposition before the polish, and the fact that the wafer now carries
two levels of MiM capacitors, the lower ones connected through via 3 to
metal-4 nets and the upper ones with floating top plates.

## Why this step exists

The {term}`inter-metal dielectric` does the jobs set out at
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`; metal 4, its capacitors and the large via 4
above sharpen them:

* **Insulation without voids.** A 2.8:1 space between 0.845 µm lines
  must fill without a seam; Nguyen's review explains how the HDP
  deposition/sputter balance fills such a gap,[^nguyen-1999] the Novellus
  and Applied Materials patents give production recipes for
  high-aspect-ratio gaps,[^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat] and Yoo and
  Swope show a PECVD fluorinated oxide filling inter-metal
  gaps.[^yoo-1996] A seam in a metal-4 space is a path for the
  {ref}`VIM4E <step-160>` etch and a moisture trap beside a capacitor.
* **Protecting the capacitors from the plasma.** A MiM capacitor whose
  plates connect to exposed conductors is charged by the plasma across
  its thin dielectric: Wang, Ackaert et al. showed how {term}`plasma-charging <plasma charging>`
  damage of floating MiM capacitors depends on the antenna areas
  connected to each plate,[^wang-2004-mim] and Cheung analysed the
  charging mechanism during plasma-enhanced dielectric
  deposition.[^cheung-2000] Hwang and Giapis's mechanism for charging
  during interlevel HDP oxide deposition[^hwang-1998] and Byun et al.'s
  and Kim, Lee and Seo's damage-reduction measures[^byun-2005][^kim-2004-hdp]
  apply (inference that the capacitors, not only the gates, are the
  concern).
* **Capacitance to two neighbours.** The film sets the line-to-line
  capacitance within metal 4 and the level-to-level capacitance to
  metal 5 encoded in the extraction tables (68.33 aF/µm² plate
  term);[^pdk-08] Stamper, Fuselier and Tian set out why these RC terms
  matter,[^stamper-1998] and the ITRS 2001 interconnect chapter the
  permittivities of the era.[^itrs-02]
* **The dielectric that via 4 passes through.** Via 4 is a
  0.800 µm square (via4.1)[^pdk-periph] etched through the 0.505 µm this
  film leaves above metal 4,[^pdk-04] an aspect ratio of about 0.63:1
  (our arithmetic), far below the roughly 2:1 of vias 2 and 3; the film's thickness uniformity after polish is what fixes that
  aspect ratio, which matters to how the via is later filled
  ({ref}`WTIAL5 <step-161>`).
* **Protection of the metal without damage.** The oxide must not stress
  a thick aluminium line into voiding[^yue-1985] or let it {term}`hillock` during
  deposition[^chaudhari-1974] and must stay below the Al–Cu temperature
  limit (roughly 400–450 °C, industry-typical[^txt-05]).
* **A polishable {term}`overburden`.** {ref}`CMPM4 <step-157>` needs enough oxide
  above the highest features — the `cap2m` plates — to reach a flat
  surface at the 0.505 µm target with margin; the PDK's "Oxide Bias for
  MM4" ({term}`oxide bias`) of 1.15 and 0.7 minimum oxide pattern density for metal 4[^pdk-03][^pdk-periph]
  are, on our reading, the fill and density controls that keep the
  overburden uniform.

Without `NILD6` there is nothing to carry metal 5, metal 4 is exposed,
and the second capacitor's plates have no dielectric around them.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic inter-metal dielectric deposition over 0.3 µm
spaces between thick aluminium lines in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's recipe is not public); the sequence is that of
{ref}`NILD3 <step-115>` and {ref}`NILD5 <step-141>`.

1. **Liner.** A thin PECVD oxide (silane or TEOS) at 350–400 °C
   (industry-typical[^txt-05][^raupp-1992]), deposited without
   high-energy ion bombardment so that the aluminium, the gates at the
   far end of the antennas and the floating capacitor plates are not
   charged before they are covered;[^hwang-1998][^byun-2005] the PDK draws
   no liner at this level.[^pdk-04]
2. **Gap fill by HDP-CVD.** SiH₄/O₂/Ar in an inductively coupled plasma
   with RF bias, the sputter-to-deposition ratio tuned so that each
   0.3 µm gap fills from the bottom;[^nguyen-1999][^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat]
   backside helium and a cooled chuck hold the wafer at an
   aluminium-compatible temperature, and the recipe is tuned against
   charging damage.[^byun-2005][^kim-2004-hdp] Lieberman and Lichtenberg
   treat the ion-bombardment physics.[^lieberman-2005] SkyWater's
   maintenance-page photograph names "a Novellus high density plasma
   tool".[^skw-07]
3. **Gap fill by PECVD TEOS (alternative).** TEOS/O₂ plasma oxide at about
   400 °C[^raupp-1992][^wiki-teos] in a deposition–etch–deposition
   sequence; SkyWater lists "PECVD TEOS, C2 and Producer".[^skw-01]
4. **Overburden.** Deposition continues — HDP or a faster PECVD TEOS cap —
   until the oxide over the tallest features is thick enough to polish:
   of the order of the metal thickness plus the finished 0.505 µm and a
   polish margin (industry practice;[^txt-05] the exact thickness is not
   public).
5. **Chamber clean.** NF₃ remote-plasma clean between wafers or lots.
6. **Metrology.** Thickness and refractive index by ellipsometry on
   monitors; stress by wafer bow; gap fill by cross-section SEM on dense
   metal-4 arrays; charging by antenna and MiM test structures at
   {term}`e-test`; particles.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus HDP-CVD.** SkyWater lists Lam/Novellus HDP "with sputter
  etch" and 5:1 fill capability,[^skw-01] and its maintenance page names
  "a Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07] Strength: **strong** for
  the tool class; the assignment to this step is an **inference** from
  the gap geometry, and the public entry mentions only doped films;
  the model (SPEED is the Novellus HDP product[^novellus-history]) is
  not public.
* **PECVD TEOS "C2 and Producer".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; medium for depositing the liner and/or the overburden
  here (inference); as the gap fill itself it would need the
  deposition–etch–deposition sequence above.
* **PECVD silane oxide "C1"**[^skw-01] as a liner candidate (weak for
  assignment).

## Open questions

* The deposition route (HDP, PECVD TEOS or both), the liner and the
  as-deposited thickness are not public.
* Why the PDK's diagram gives the via-4 dielectric 0.505 µm against
  0.39 µm for via 3,[^pdk-04] and whether the difference is set by this
  deposition or by the polish and cap that follow, is not public.
* How much oxide remains over a `cap2m` plate after polish depends on
  unpublished plate and dielectric thicknesses; about 0.35–0.4 µm is our
  estimate from the 0.505 µm via-4 height[^pdk-04] and the plate
  thickness inferred at {ref}`CAPTIW2 <step-151>`.
* Whether plasma-damage controls specific to the capacitors are applied
  is not public.
