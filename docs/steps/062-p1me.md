## What this step is

`P1ME` is the gate etch. It transfers the resist pattern printed at
{ref}`P1M <step-061>` through the whole gate stack — the oxide cap of
{ref}`POC <step-059>`, the nitride cap of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`
and the 0.18 µm doped poly of {ref}`SAGD <step-048>`[^pdk-03][^pdk-04]
— and stops on the gate dielectric beneath, leaving free-standing
gate lines, poly interconnect and resistor bodies on a wafer whose
source/drain silicon is now exposed under a few nanometres of oxide.
The resist and any organic {term}`ARC` are stripped as part of the sequence,
since no separate strip step follows in the step list used in this
reference, and the next step is the re-oxidation
{ref}`IOX45 <step-063>`.

The stop layer is the thinnest film in the process. The 1.8 V
transistor model carries `toxe = 4.148e-9` (4.148 nm) as its oxide
thickness for electrical purposes,[^pdk-model-nfet01v8] against
11.6 nm for the 5 V device;[^pdk-model-nfet5v] under the {term}`SONOS` cells
the etch lands on the {term}`blocking oxide` of the {term}`ONO` stack
({ref}`ONO <step-040>`). All three surfaces are exposed on the same
wafer to the same {term}`over-etch`, and the 4 nm one must survive it. The
lines being cut are 0.150 µm wide at minimum (poly.1a) on a 0.210 µm
space (poly.2), and the resistor bodies are at least 0.330 µm wide
(poly.3);[^pdk-periph] the etched width, after whatever {term}`etch bias` the
recipe has, is the physical gate length. ITRS 2001 lists an MPU/ASIC
gate etch bias of 25 nm for the 130 nm node ({ref}`category-etch`;
[^itrs-01]) — an industry figure, not a SkyWater one.

One feature of this etch is peculiar to SKY130's module order. The
poly being etched is not uniformly doped: the gates and interconnect
are n⁺ ({ref}`P1I <step-050>`), the 300 Ω/sq resistor bodies are
p-type at a much lower level ({ref}`PRI <step-053>`) and the 2000 Ω/sq
bodies lower still ({ref}`UPRI <step-056>`). Silicon etch rates in
chlorine and bromine chemistries depend on doping — Ogryzlo, Flamm,
Ibbotson and Mucha measured the effect for doped poly in molecular
chlorine[^ogryzlo-1988] and for Cl-atom etching of silicon[^ogryzlo-1990]
— so the resistor bodies clear later than the gates, and the over-etch
must cover the slowest region while the 4 nm oxide under the fastest
survives.

## Step category

`P1ME` is an {ref}`Etch <category-etch>` step of the *silicon/poly*
class — in HBr/Cl₂/O₂ chemistry in a high-density plasma, we infer from
SkyWater's DPS II gas list[^skw-01] — the same family as the trench etch
{ref}`STIE <step-006>` but with the opposite priorities: `STIE` is a
timed etch into a semi-infinite substrate, `P1ME` is an endpointed etch
that must stop, with {term}`selectivity` of the order of 100:1, on a film
thinner than the roughness of the surface it is clearing
({ref}`category-etch`[^nojiri-2015][^txt-01]). It is the most
CD-critical and most selectivity-critical etch in the flow, and the one
whose profile every later self-aligned step depends on.

## Why this step exists

The etched gate is the master reference for the transistor. Its
width is the gate length; its sidewall angle sets where the tip
implants ({ref}`ASTI <step-065>`) land relative to the channel and how
wide the {term}`spacers <spacer>` ({ref}`SPE <step-077>`) come out; its foot decides
whether the gate edge is sharp (a "footing" leaves a wedge of poly
that shifts the junction outward and degrades the field at the drain
edge) or notched (a "notch" undercuts the gate and thins it at the
oxide, the classic charging-induced failure). The literature on
HBr/Cl₂/O₂ gate etching in high-density plasmas is largely about
controlling exactly these features: Tuda, Shintani and Ootera
followed the profile evolution through the etch,[^tuda-2001] Detter
et al. showed how the gas chemistry controls the profile of
resist-masked gates,[^detter-2003] Joubert et al. how linewidth is
held to nanometre precision,[^joubert-2003] Kim, Kim and Lee how HBr
and oxygen set the selectivity and the post-etch profile in an HBr/O₂
over-etch,[^kim-2007] and Bell and Joubert what the sidewall
passivation film is actually made of under resist and under oxide
masks.[^bell-1997][^bell-1996] Desvoivres, Vallier and Joubert set out
where the plasma technology reaches its limits for sub-0.1 µm
gates.[^desvoivres-2000]

Two failure modes are specific to stopping on a thin gate oxide.
**{term}`Punch-through <punch-through>` and recess**: if the over-etch consumes the oxide at
the gate periphery, the plasma etches the source/drain silicon and
the gate-oxide edge is damaged — Kraft and Krishnan measured the gate
oxide loss at the periphery of a MOSFET after a poly gate etch in a
high-density tool,[^kraft-1995] and Krishnan et al. the damage that a
high-density-plasma etch does to thin gate oxide.[^krishnan-1995]
**Charging**: as the poly clears, isolated gate lines connected to
large areas of poly act as antennas, and the electron-shading
mechanism that Hashimoto described[^hashimoto-1994] charges the
oxide through the gate — Noguchi et al. tied thin-oxide reliability
to this topography-dependent charging during the poly etch,[^noguchi-1997]
which is one reason gate etches end with a low-bias over-etch.

Without a clean, vertical, selective gate etch there is no gate
length, no self-aligned junction and no intact gate oxide. Everything
in the PDK's device tables assumes it.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic gate etch for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab (SKY130's
recipe is not public):

1. **Chamber.** High-density inductively coupled or
   transformer-coupled plasma etcher (Applied Materials DPS, Lam {term}`TCP`)
   with separate source and bias power, an electrostatic chuck with
   helium backside cooling, optical emission spectroscopy and often
   laser interferometry for endpoint ({ref}`category-etch`).
2. **{term}`Resist trim <resist trim>` (optional).** A short O₂- or HBr/O₂-based isotropic
   trim of the resist line before any film is opened, to bring the
   gate length below the printed {term}`CD` ({ref}`P1M <step-061>`); Kang,
   Joung and Mun describe such a CD-control step on an organic
   {term}`BARC`.[^kang-2007] Whether SKY130 trims is not public.
3. **ARC and cap open.** The organic BARC, if present, is opened in
   O₂/N₂ or HBr/O₂; the oxide cap and the nitride cap are opened in a
   fluorocarbon step (CF₄/CHF₃ with O₂ or Ar — the DPSII gas list
   includes both[^skw-01]), which stops on the poly by endpoint.
   Whether the resist is then stripped and the caps used as a hard
   mask for the poly etch, or the resist is kept through the poly
   etch, is a design choice: Bell and Joubert compared the sidewall
   chemistry of the two cases,[^bell-1997] and a {term}`hard-mask <hard mask>` etch gives
   a cleaner profile and removes the resist's contribution to
   {term}`line-edge roughness <LER>`.[^desvoivres-2000] Which SKY130 does is not
   public ({ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`).
4. **Breakthrough.** A few seconds of CF₄ or Cl₂ to clear the native
   oxide on the poly.
5. **Main etch.** HBr with Cl₂ and a few percent O₂ at a few mTorr,
   high source power and moderate bias. Bromine and chlorine remove
   silicon as volatile SiBrₓ/SiClₓ while a thin SiOₓBrᵧ passivation
   forms on the sidewalls and keeps the profile vertical;
   Vallier et al. analysed the chemical topography of gates etched in
   these chemistries[^vallier-2003] and Cunge et al. the ion flux
   composition of industrial high-density HBr/Cl₂/O₂
   plasmas.[^cunge-2002] The etch is run to an {term}`endpoint`: the
   emission of a silicon-halide product line falls as the poly clears,
   or an interferometer on a monitor pad counts the film down
   ({ref}`category-etch`;[^hsu-2004]). Because clearing is not
   simultaneous across the wafer, across pattern densities
   ({term}`loading effect`; Gottscho, Jurgensen and Vitkavage on
   micro-uniformity[^gottscho-1992]) or across doping levels
   ({ref}`RPM <step-049>` bodies versus gates[^ogryzlo-1988]), the
   endpoint is set to trigger when the first regions clear.
6. **{term}`Soft landing <soft landing>`.** As the endpoint approaches the bias power is
   reduced so that the ions arriving at the oxide have too little
   energy to sputter it, and the chemistry moves toward HBr/O₂.
7. **Over-etch.** A timed HBr/O₂ step at low bias, with selectivity to
   SiO₂ of the order of 100:1 (industry-typical;[^nojiri-2015]), long
   enough to remove poly stringers along the field-oxide step at the
   active edge — the 0.07 µm step of the PDK's assumptions
   table[^pdk-03] — and to clear the slower-etching resistor bodies,
   while the 4 nm oxide under the n⁺ gates survives. The oxygen
   builds the passivation that gives the selectivity; too much of it
   causes {term}`footing` at the oxide interface.[^kim-2007][^tuda-2001]
8. **Resist strip and post-etch clean.** In-situ O₂ plasma in the
   etcher or a downstream asher (SkyWater lists Gasonics PEP, Iridia
   and Mattson Aspen 2[^skw-01]), then a wet clean ({term}`SPM`/{term}`SC-1` on the
   Akrion, DNS or FSI benches[^skw-01]) to remove the bromine- and
   chlorine-containing sidewall residue. We infer that no HF step is
   used here, because the gate oxide over the source/drain is exposed
   and is needed as the base for {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>`.
9. **Metrology.** After-etch CD on the {term}`CD-SEM` (AEI), compared with
   the after-develop CD to track the etch bias;[^itrs-01]
   cross-section SEM for profile and foot; electrical gate length and
   {term}`gate-oxide integrity <gate oxide integrity>` at {ref}`category-test`.
10. **Damage.** Ion bombardment leaves a few nanometres of damaged
    silicon and oxide wherever the plasma reached the substrate
    (Oehrlein's review[^oehrlein-1989]); the next step, the
    re-oxidation {ref}`IOX45 <step-063>`, is where that damage is
    healed or consumed.

## Machines typically used

* **High-density poly etcher**, 200 mm, single wafer: Applied
  Materials Centura DPS / DPS II (decoupled plasma source, the
  reactor of Yin et al.[^pat-dps-amat]); Lam TCP 9400 series
  (transformer-coupled plasma[^pat-tcp-lam][^snf-9400]); TEL
  DRM/Unity; Hitachi microwave ECR etchers ({ref}`category-etch`).
* **Downstream asher** and **wet bench** for the strip and clean.
* **CD-SEM** and **cross-section SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **AMAT DPS II.** SkyWater lists "AMAT DPSII, HBR, Cl2, NF3, CF4,
  CHF3, O2 – gate, trench, W/WN".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** —
  the tool, the HBr/Cl₂/O₂ chemistry and the word "gate" are all
  SkyWater's; that this step runs on it is nevertheless an inference,
  since the list does not name process steps.
* **Lam 9400 TCP** ("poly/nitride, HBr, CF4, SF6, O2"[^skw-01]) is a
  poly etcher of the same generation on the same list; Stanford's user
  guide describes the TCP 9400 as a poly etcher running HBr, Cl₂, O₂,
  CF₄ and C₂F₆.[^snf-9400] Strength: **medium** — capable, but without
  the Cl₂ and the "gate" label.
* **Lam 4400** ("HBr, Cl2, C2F6, CF4, SF6, O2"[^skw-01]). Strength:
  weak; an older-generation tool.
* **Ashers — Gasonics PEP, Iridia, Mattson Aspen 2; wet benches —
  Akrion Gamma, DNS, FSI Mercury.**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence.
* **CD metrology — AMAT Verity, VeraSEM.**[^skw-01] Strength: strong.
* A SkyWater job advertisement for a plasma-etch process development
  engineer confirms the function in house but names no
  tools.[^job-03]

## Resources required

* **HBr, Cl₂, O₂** for the poly etch; **CF₄, CHF₃** for the cap open
  and breakthrough[^skw-01] ({ref}`category-etch`).
* **Helium** for backside cooling; **argon** or nitrogen as diluent.
* **NF₃** (listed on the DPSII[^skw-01]) or SF₆/O₂ for chamber
  cleaning.
* **O₂/N₂/{term}`forming gas`** for the strip; **H₂SO₄/H₂O₂ and SC-1** for
  the post-etch clean.[^wiki-rca]
* **Chamber consumables** (ceramic liners, focus rings, electrostatic
  chuck), **endpoint optics**, **monitor wafers** for etch-rate and
  selectivity checks.

  method, soft-landing and over-etch times — is not public.
* Whether the poly is etched with the resist in place or with the
  nitride/oxide caps as a hard mask after an early strip is not

  etched gate length relative to the 0.150 µm drawn width, is not
  public.

  the three stop surfaces (4 nm oxide, 11.6 nm oxide, ONO) on one
  wafer is not public.

  from SkyWater's "gate" label, not stated.
