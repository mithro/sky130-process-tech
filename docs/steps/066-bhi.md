## What this step is

`BHI` is the boron {term}`halo` (or "pocket") implant of the standard
1.8 V NMOS transistors. It is made through the same resist windows as
the arsenic tip of {ref}`ASTI <step-065>` — we infer that the two share
the {ref}`NTM <step-064>` resist, since the halo belongs to the same
transistors as the tip (see below) — but with a tilted beam, so that boron
ions pass beneath the edge of the polysilicon gate and land in the
silicon *around* the tip, on the channel side of it. After the anneal
at {ref}`TIPRTAD <step-075>` each tip is wrapped in a {term}`pocket` of p-type
doping several times higher than the p-well background: a local
increase in channel doping at the two ends of the channel, and nowhere
else.

This reference describes the halo as boron, the usual p-type pocket
species for an NMOS (industry-typical[^txt-04]); no public source gives
the SKY130 halo's species, energy, dose, tilt or rotation. The PDK does show that
large-tilt implants are part of this module: its assumptions page lists
a 40° "Angle for HV tip implant" with a 23° twist for the sibling
{ref}`HVASTI <step-069>` step, next to the 7° tip angle.[^pdk-03] What
we describe below is the industry-generic halo of a 130 nm-era process.

## Step category

`BHI` is an {ref}`Ion implantation <category-implant>` step of the
*channel-engineering* class — a low-dose (10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻², typical)[^txt-01]
boron implant at tens of keV, like the threshold and {term}`punch-through`
implants of the well module — but placed by geometry rather than by a mask:
the tilt puts it under the gate edge, and the gate itself keeps it out of
the middle of the channel. It is the module's tilt-sensitive implant and,
with the halos of {ref}`LDBHI <step-073>`, one of the two tilted implants
of the flow whose angle is not public.

## Why this step exists

Codella and Ogura introduced the halo in 1985 as a p-type pocket
around the {term}`LDD` that lets a sub-micrometre NMOS hold its threshold
voltage as the channel shortens.[^codella-1985] Three effects make it
indispensable at 0.15 µm:

* **Punch-through and threshold roll-off.** The drain's depletion
  region widens into a lightly doped channel until the source barrier
  collapses ("the depletion regions of the source and drain begin
  overlapping underneath the channel").[^wiki-sce] Raising the doping
  uniformly would cure this at the cost of mobility, body effect and
  junction capacitance; raising it only next to the junctions — the
  halo — narrows the depletion regions exactly where they threaten the
  channel. Hori and Kurimoto's large-tilt-angle punch-through stopper
  is the same idea placed deeper.[^hori-1988]
* **{term}`Reverse short-channel effect <reverse short-channel effect>`.** Lu and Sung observed threshold
  voltage *rising* as channels shortened in salicided
  devices,[^lu-1989-rsce] which Rafferty and co-workers explained by boron
  piling up near the source/drain under the gradient of implant
  defects;[^rafferty-1993] Machala et al. traced the role of boron
  segregation and transient enhanced diffusion.[^machala-1997] A
  deliberately implanted halo turns this accident into a design
  variable: its dose is chosen so that the roll-off of the short
  device and the roll-up of the halo cancel over the range of gate
  lengths the design rules allow.
* **Lower channel doping elsewhere.** With the halo carrying the
  short-channel burden, the rest of the channel can be doped more
  lightly for higher mobility and lower junction capacitance. Taur,
  Wann and Frank's "super-halo" analysis showed how far this can be
  pushed;[^taur-1998] the price is a drain-side pocket that adds
  junction capacitance and band-to-band leakage, which is why halo
  design is a compromise.[^pat-halo-chartered]

SKY130's 1.8 V NMOS has a threshold that rises as the gate shortens. In
the published test-tile measurements it is 0.534 V at 7/8 µm and
0.707 V at 7/0.15 µm; at W = 0.42 µm it is 0.53–0.54 V for L = 8–25 µm (the 20 and 25 µm devices are in deep
N-well),
0.60–0.64 V at L = 0.5 µm and 0.72 V at L = 0.15 µm
(maximum-transconductance extrapolation at V_DS = 0.1 V, less half the
drain bias; a constant current of 100 nA × W/L gives the same trend; our
extraction from the published measurements). The PDK's e-test nominals
rise the same way, from 0.541 V at 7/8 µm to 0.700 V at
7/0.15 µm.[^raw-data-lv-mosfets][^pdk-07] A halo is one mechanism
for such a rise, but the measurements do not isolate it, and the 1.8 V
PMOS in the same data shows the ordinary fall, from a threshold
magnitude of 1.065 V at 7/8 µm to 0.798 V at
7/0.15 µm.[^raw-data-lv-mosfets]

The halo shares the tip's resist because it belongs to the same
transistors: the 5 V devices ({ref}`HVNTM <step-068>`) have a different
drain design, and the {term}`SONOS` transistors get their own halo at
{ref}`LDBHI <step-073>`. Making the halo under its own mask would cost a
lithography step for no device benefit (inference).

Without `BHI`, the 1.8 V NMOS would need a uniformly heavier channel
implant to reach the same punch-through margin, with worse drive
current, and its threshold would fall sharply at the short end of the
allowed gate-length range.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic NMOS halo implant for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era fab
(SKY130's values are not public):

* **Species and source.** ¹¹B⁺ from boron trifluoride
  (BF₃),[^wiki-bf3] or BF₂⁺ from the same gas for a shallower placement
  at a controllable beam energy (only 11/49 of the BF₂ energy is
  carried by the boron atom).[^txt-01] Indium is the heavier
  alternative that gives a steeper pocket,[^shahidi-1993] but SkyWater
  lists no indium on any implanter.[^skw-01]
* **Energy and dose.** Tens of keV and 10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻² (typical);[^txt-01]
  an AMD channel-engineering patent of the era gives "approximately
  10-20 KeV for boron or 45-90 KeV for BF₂ at a concentration of about
  1.0 to 2.5×10¹³ ions/cm²" for a channel implant,[^pat-vt-amd] and a
  halo sits in the same range. The energy is chosen so that the pocket
  peaks just below and beside the tip rather than at the surface.
* **Tilt and rotation.** Large tilts — 20–45° are typical[^txt-04] —
  push the pocket under the gate edge; the beam is {term}`shadowed <shadowing>` on one side
  of each gate, so the implant is split into two or four rotations of
  the wafer ("quad" implants) to make source and drain
  symmetric,[^pat-quad-tsmc] unless an asymmetric device is
  wanted.[^shin-1999][^buti-1989] For PMOS a 0° halo has been used
  instead.[^pat-halo-amd-zero] Chen et al. quantified the shadowing of
  tilted drain implants by neighbouring gates.[^chen-1995] The PDK's
  "NTM shadowing" allowance of 0.16 µm is close to what a 1.14 µm
  resist casts at the published 7° tip angle (1.14 µm × tan 7° ≈
  0.14 µm),[^pdk-03] which suggests the mask is sized for the tip; a
  halo at a much larger tilt through the same resist would be shadowed
  further from the resist edge, so either the halo tilt is moderate or
  the shadowing near window edges is accepted — we cannot tell which
  from public data.
* **Order relative to the tip.** This reference describes the arsenic
  first. The halo boron then enters silicon already
  amorphised by the arsenic, which suppresses {term}`channelling` and, on
  regrowth, activates the boron well; a solid-phase-epitaxy activation
  of {term}`extension` and halo together is the subject of an AMD
  patent.[^pat-spe-amd] The interstitials released when the damage
  anneals, and those injected as arsenic deactivates,[^rousseau-1994]
  drive transient enhanced diffusion of the halo
  boron[^eaglesham-1994][^stolk-1997] — the reason
  {ref}`TIPRTAD <step-075>` must be short and hot.[^agarwal-1999]
* **Charge control and cooling.** Electron shower and cooled
  electrostatic chuck, both features SkyWater lists for its
  medium-current tool ("ESC chuck, E shower").[^skw-01]
* **Monitoring.** Thermal-wave measurement on monitor wafers, since
  the dose is too low for {term}`sheet resistance`;[^smith-1985] tilt-angle
  calibration is a specific control item for halo implants.

## Machines typically used

* **{ref}`Medium-current ion implanter <machine-medium-current-implanter>`**, 200 mm, single-wafer with a tilting
  platen: Axcelis (Eaton) 8250 class, Varian E220/E500 and VIISta 810,
  Applied Materials xR LEAP ({ref}`category-implant`). The 8250HT covers
  "3keV to 750keV" with beam currents "between 4µA and
  3,500µA".[^axcelis-8250]
* **{ref}`Thermal-wave <machine-sheet-resistance-metrology>`** metrology.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Axcelis 8250 medium-current implanter.** SkyWater lists "Axcelis
  8250 Mid current B11, BF2, As, ESC chuck, E shower, 1e11 to 1e14,
  0-60 deg tilt".[^skw-01] Boron and BF₂, a dose window that spans the
  whole halo range, and a tilt range that reaches 60° — everything a
  halo needs. Strength: **strong** for the tool and its tilt
  capability; **inference** for the assignment to `BHI`.
* **Axcelis GSD implanters (high-current/high-energy and high-dose)**
  ("10-3000kev, 1e11 to 5e15, tilt/twist" and "2-180kev, 5e12 to 5e16,
  tilt/twist")[^skw-01] — batch tools that SkyWater lists with tilt and
  twist capability, so a rotated halo is possible on them too, although the
  dose sits at the bottom of their range. Strength: strong for existence;
  weak for assignment.

## Resources required

* **Boron trifluoride (BF₃)** for ¹¹B⁺ or BF₂⁺;[^wiki-bf3] BF₃ is
  toxic and corrosive and is handled in gas cabinets.
* **Source-support gases**, source consumables (filaments, arc-chamber
  liners), high-purity **nitrogen** for venting.
* **Helium** for platen cooling; **liquid nitrogen** or cryopump
  regeneration.
* **{ref}`Monitor wafers <material-substrates>`** for thermal-wave dose and tilt control.
* Gas suppliers named in SkyWater's filings: Air Products and Praxair
  (2021 S-1), Linde and Airgas (fiscal 2023 10-K).[^sec-01][^sec-02]

## Open questions

* The halo species (B⁺ or BF₂⁺), energy, dose, tilt and number of
  rotations are not public.
* Whether the PDK's 0.16 µm "NTM shadowing" allowance is computed for
  the tip alone, or also bounds the halo tilt, is our inference from
  the resist thickness and tip angle on the same page.[^pdk-03]
* Whether the 5 V NMOS receives any halo at all is not stated
  publicly; this reference describes halos only with the `NTM` and
  `LDNTM` tips.
* Whether the PMOS transistors receive a halo — and if so under which
  mask — is not stated publicly either; see {ref}`NTM <step-064>`.
