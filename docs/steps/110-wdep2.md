## What this step is

`WDEP2` fills the metal-contact holes with tungsten. A blanket
chemical-vapour-deposited tungsten film is grown over the whole wafer
— on the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>`, across the field and
into every 0.17 µm contact — thick enough that the holes close from
their sidewalls inward and the surface above them is continuous
metal. The film is then polished away from the field at
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`, leaving tungsten only in the holes: the
{term}`W plug` that the PDK calls `mcon`.[^pdk-06] The "2" in the
step code distinguishes it from the local-contact fill
{ref}`WDEP <step-099>`; the same operation recurs for every via level
({ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` and after).

The holes being filled are, on our reading of {ref}`CTME <step-108>`
and the PDK's assumptions, roughly 0.3–0.37 µm deep with a 0.17 µm
top and a 0.09 µm bottom,[^pdk-03][^pdk-04] narrowed further by the
liner. The PDK's "min. etch and fill capability for mcon" of
0.14 µm[^pdk-03] is the public statement that the fill process is
qualified down to that opening. The deposited thickness is not
public; a blanket fill must exceed half the largest hole width plus
an over-fill margin, so a few hundred nanometres is the industry norm
for a hole of this size (industry-typical value[^txt-05]). SkyWater
describes its capability as "Lam/Novellus PECVD Tungsten – plug fill
– PNL option for high aspect ratio (up to 10:1)".[^skw-01]

## Step category

`WDEP2` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step of
the *CVD tungsten* class; the category page's summary — a silane- or
diborane-reduced nucleation layer followed by hydrogen reduction of
WF₆ at roughly 400–450 °C and tens of Torr, almost perfectly conformal
— is the process. Unlike the dielectric depositions of the module
({ref}`NILD3 <step-115>`, {ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>`), its product is
sacrificial everywhere except in the holes, and the film property that
matters most is not thickness but the absence of a void or an open
seam at the hole centre.

## Why this step exists

Tungsten plugs are the standard way to connect metal levels through
small holes, and have been since Kaanta et al. presented tungsten
studs with planarisation at IEDM 1987.[^kaanta-1987] The reasons the
industry chose CVD tungsten, and why SKY130's flow uses it at every
level, are all in play here:

* **Conformality.** Surface-reaction-limited CVD coats the sidewalls
  of a hole at the same rate as the field, so the hole fills from the
  outside in; sputtered aluminium cannot reach the bottom of a
  0.17 µm hole of this depth ({ref}`category-deposition`).
  Broadbent and Ramiller's kinetics of the WF₆/H₂ and WF₆/SiH₄
  reactions,[^rev-03] McConica and Krishnamani's single-wafer
  kinetics[^mcconica-1986] and Levy and Green's early LPCVD tungsten
  work[^levy-1986] are the foundations.
* **Nucleation.** Tungsten does not nucleate on oxide, and the
  nucleation on the TiN liner is what Srinivas et al.[^srinivas-1992]
  and McConica and Cooper[^mcconica-1988] studied; a thin silane-
  reduced nucleation layer, whose properties Hegde et al.
  characterised,[^hegde-1997] makes the bulk hydrogen-reduction step
  start uniformly. Novellus's pulsed nucleation layer ("PNL") —
  alternating pulses of reducing gas and WF₆ so that the nucleation
  film grows one thin layer at a time with better step
  coverage[^pat-pnl-novellus] — is the "PNL option" SkyWater
  names.[^skw-01]
* **Filling without voids.** Because the film grows from every
  surface, the last place to fill is the hole axis, and any
  re-entrant profile from {ref}`CTME <step-108>` or overhang from
  {ref}`TIN2 <step-109>` leaves a seam or "keyhole" that
  {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` may open. The taper the PDK's 0.09 µm bottom
  CD implies[^pdk-03] is, on our reading, part of why the fill works.
* **Resistance and reliability.** Tungsten's resistivity is high, but
  the plug is short; the PDK's 152 000 mΩ per `mcon`[^pdk-08] is
  dominated by the interfaces and the small bottom area rather than
  the tungsten itself. Tungsten does not electromigrate at the
  current densities of a contact, which is why Al lines with W plugs
  fail at the plug interface (the {term}`W plug` acts as a
  flux-divergence point) rather than in the plug.
* **Stress.** CVD tungsten is strongly tensile — Shioya et al.
  measured its stress at temperature[^shioya-1987] — and a thick
  blanket film bows the wafer; the film thickness is kept to the
  minimum that fills.

Without `WDEP2` the lined holes would be filled by the aluminium of
{ref}`TIAL6 <step-112>`, which cannot reach their bottoms; the contacts
would be open or voided.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic blanket tungsten plug fill for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Chamber.** A single-wafer or multi-station warm-wall CVD reactor
   with a heated pedestal at about 400–450 °C, showerhead gas delivery
   and a remote NF₃ plasma clean (industry-typical values, category
   page[^txt-01][^wiki-wf6]). Novellus's multi-station Altus
   family[^novellus-history] is the reference design for the
   "Lam/Novellus" tool SkyWater lists.[^skw-01] Note that SkyWater's
   wording "PECVD Tungsten"[^skw-01] is unusual: plug tungsten is
   normally a thermal CVD, and we read the phrase as a label rather
   than a statement that the deposition is plasma-driven
   (inference).
2. **Nucleation layer.** WF₆ reduced by SiH₄ (or B₂H₆),
   2WF₆ + 3SiH₄ → 2W + 3SiF₄ + 6H₂,[^wiki-wf6] for a few nanometres;
   in the pulsed variant the reducing gas and WF₆ are alternated so
   that each pulse adds only what the surface has adsorbed, which
   gives near-ideal step coverage in high-aspect-ratio
   holes.[^pat-pnl-novellus] The silane-reduced layer is
   fine-grained and more resistive than the bulk,[^hegde-1997] so it
   is kept thin.
3. **Bulk fill.** Hydrogen reduction, WF₆ + 3H₂ → W + 6HF,[^wiki-wf6]
   at tens of Torr with a large H₂ excess, at a rate of several
   hundred nanometres per minute (industry-typical[^txt-01]). The
   reaction is surface-limited, hence conformal;[^rev-03][^mcconica-1986]
   the by-product HF is pumped away and the liner keeps the WF₆ from
   the oxide and from any titanium.
4. **Thickness.** To close a 0.17 µm hole, more than 0.085 µm on each
   sidewall plus an over-fill; a few hundred nanometres on the field
   is typical (industry-typical value[^txt-05]). Too thick a film
   costs polish time at {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` and adds stress.[^shioya-1987]
5. **Chamber clean.** Tungsten deposits on the chamber walls are
   removed by an NF₃ (or SF₆/ClF₃) plasma clean between wafers or
   lots; fluorine residues from the clean are a known cause of
   nucleation delay on the next wafer.
6. **Backside and edge.** Tungsten that reaches the wafer edge and
   backside — where the liner is absent — adheres poorly and flakes;
   an edge-exclusion clamp or a subsequent edge-bevel removal is
   usual (industry practice[^txt-05]).
7. **Metrology.** Thickness and sheet resistance on monitor wafers
   by {term}`four-point probe`; stress by wafer bow; cross-section
   SEM for seams and voids; particle counts, since tungsten flakes
   are a classic defect source.

## Machines typically used

* **Blanket tungsten CVD reactor**, 200 mm: Novellus Altus
  (multi-station),[^novellus-history] Applied Materials Centura WxZ,
  Genus/ULVAC and Tokyo Electron tungsten CVD chambers
  ({ref}`category-deposition`).
* **Remote plasma clean** (NF₃) on the reactor.
* **Four-point probe**, **stress gauge**, **cross-section SEM**,
  **particle inspection**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus tungsten CVD with PNL.** SkyWater lists
  "Lam/Novellus PECVD Tungsten – plug fill – PNL option for high
  aspect ratio (up to 10:1)".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for vendor,
  process and the pulsed nucleation option; the tool model (Altus is
  the Novellus tungsten platform[^novellus-history]) is an
  **inference**. Whether the PNL option is used on this contact
  level, whose aspect ratio is far below 10:1, is not public.
* **Post-deposition metrology** is not named on SkyWater's public
  page for this film.

## Resources required

* **Tungsten hexafluoride** (WF₆)[^wiki-wf6] and **hydrogen**; **silane**
  (or diborane) for the nucleation layer; **argon** and **nitrogen**
  carrier and purge gases (SkyWater's filings name Air Products,
  Praxair, Linde and Airgas as gas suppliers[^sec-01][^sec-02]).
* **NF₃** for the chamber clean; HF-tolerant exhaust and abatement for
  the by-products.
* **Heater, showerhead and pedestal consumables**; **monitor wafers**
  with TiN for thickness and resistance checks.
