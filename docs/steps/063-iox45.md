## What this step is

`IOX45` is a (thin, we infer) thermal oxidation performed immediately
after the gate etch and before the first tip-implant mask. The wafer
arrives from {ref}`P1ME <step-062>` with free-standing gate lines whose
poly sidewalls are bare, whose tops are still covered by the nitride and
oxide caps of {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>` and {ref}`POC <step-059>`, and
whose source/drain regions are covered only by what is left of the gate
oxide after the {term}`over-etch`. `IOX45` puts the wafer into an oxidising
ambient long enough to grow a few nanometres of oxide on every exposed
silicon surface: on the poly sidewalls, on the source/drain silicon
(thickening the residual gate oxide there), on the gate-oxide edge under
the foot of the gate, and — if it is a furnace step — on the wafer back.

This reference describes the oxide as the *{term}`screen oxide`* for
the tip and {term}`halo` implants that follow ({ref}`NTM <step-064>`,
{ref}`ASTI <step-065>`, {ref}`BHI <step-066>` and their high-voltage
and lightly doped counterparts) — an inference from its place in the
sequence this reference describes, after the gate etch, and from the
screening role a thin oxide plays for such implants (see *Why this step exists*[^txt-01]). Neither
the thickness of the oxide, its ambient, its temperature nor the tool
are public; the numbers below are industry-typical values with their
sources.

In the industry literature the same step goes by several names —
{term}`poly re-oxidation`, gate re-oxidation, post-gate-etch oxidation,
sidewall oxidation, implant screen oxide — and the reading that
follows treats it as all of these at once, because a single thin
oxidation after the gate etch does all of these jobs.

## Step category

`IOX45` is a {ref}`Thermal oxidation <category-oxidation>` step of the
*thin oxide* class — the category page's "pad, liner and screen
oxides" of 5–30 nm — grown in the regime where the {term}`Deal–Grove model`
under-predicts the rate and recipes are calibrated on the
tool.[^wiki-dg][^massoud-1985] What distinguishes it from every other
oxidation in the flow is the surface it oxidises: not bare single
crystal but a mixture of heavily doped polysilicon sidewalls, lightly
doped single-crystal source/drain silicon, a thin existing gate oxide
and, in the {term}`SONOS` cells, the edge of an {term}`ONO` stack, all on the same
wafer and all with different oxidation rates. It is also the first
furnace or {term}`RTP` step the gate stack sees after patterning, so it is
where the gate edge takes its final shape.

## Why this step exists

Four things that a thin oxidation does after a gate etch are each
documented in the public literature; we infer that `IOX45` is there
for some combination of them.

* **It repairs the etch.** The gate etch leaves a few nanometres of
  ion-damaged silicon and oxide wherever the plasma reached the
  substrate (Oehrlein's review of dry-etch damage[^oehrlein-1989]) and
  can thin or breach the gate oxide at the gate periphery, as Kraft
  and Krishnan measured after a high-density poly etch.[^kraft-1995]
  A thermal oxidation consumes the damaged surface layer into the new
  oxide, regrows oxide wherever it was lost, and anneals the plasma
  charging damage.
* **It seals the gate and rounds its foot.** Oxidising the poly
  sidewall encapsulates the gate against the implants, cleans and
  strips that follow, and grows oxide into the corner where the gate
  meets the gate oxide — a small "bird's beak" at the gate edge that
  lowers the field there. Its size is set by the two-dimensional
  kinetics that Kao et al. measured and modelled: oxidation of curved
  silicon is *retarded* at low temperatures and sharp curvatures, more
  severely on concave than on convex surfaces,[^kao-1987][^kao-1988]
  so the corner grows less oxide than a flat surface would, and the
  field-lowering effect shrinks as the re-oxidation temperature falls.
  The gate-edge oxide is where
  hot-carrier and gate-induced leakage effects live: Chan, Wu, Ko and
  Hu showed how the gate-to-drain overlap region governs MOSFET
  characteristics,[^chan-1987] Pan, Ng and Kwong compared gate-edge
  effects on the hot-carrier degradation of {term}`LDD` n- and p-channel
  devices,[^pan-1994] Lai et al. reported the reliability improvement
  from an N₂O polysilicon-gate re-oxidation,[^lai-1998] and Doyle and
  Philipossian optimised p-channel hot-carrier lifetime through the
  re-oxidation step of a re-oxidised {term}`nitrided oxide <oxynitride>`.[^doyle-1993] For a
  nitrided gate oxide — which SkyWater's "Nitrided gate oxide" special
  module makes likely for SKY130[^skw-01] ({ref}`LVGOX <step-047>`) —
  the re-oxidation step has its own literature: Hori, Iwasaki and
  Tsuji on RTP re-oxidised nitrided oxides[^hori-1989] and Joshi et
  al. on the effect of rapid thermal re-oxidation on rapidly nitrided
  thin gate oxides.[^joshi-1992]
* **It screens the tip implants.** A thin oxide over the source/drain
  silicon randomises the incoming ions and suppresses {term}`channelling`,
  keeps sputtered resist and beam-line metal off the silicon, and
  moves the implant peak toward the surface by its own thickness
  ({ref}`category-implant`;[^txt-01]). Its cost is {term}`knock-on <knock-on implantation>`: the
  arriving ions recoil oxygen from the screen oxide into the silicon,
  a multilayer-target effect that Christel, Gibbons and Mylroie
  computed.[^christel-1981] A screen oxide of a few nanometres is the
  usual compromise. For the well and channel implants that function
  was served, we infer, by the pad oxide (see {ref}`LVTNI <step-015>`);
  the active areas now carry gate oxide and a gate stack instead, and
  `IOX45` supplies the screen for the implants that follow.
* **It contributes {term}`thermal budget`.** Whatever its ambient, this is a
  furnace or RTP step at several hundred degrees, and it acts on the
  doped but unannealed poly of {ref}`P1I <step-050>`,
  {ref}`PRI <step-053>` and {ref}`UPRI <step-056>` — redistributing
  dopant and, if the film is still amorphous, crystallising it
  ({ref}`SAGD <step-048>`) — and on the channel implants beneath the
  gate oxide.

Without it the tip implants would be shot into bare, plasma-damaged
silicon through a gate-oxide remnant of unknown thickness, the poly
sidewalls would be bare through three implant masks and strips, and
the gate-edge oxide would be as the etch left it.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic post-gate-etch re-oxidation for a 200 mm,
130 nm-era fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Pre-clean.** The post-etch clean of {ref}`P1ME <step-062>` ({term}`SPM`
   and {term}`SC-1`, no HF) leaves a clean, hydrophilic surface; a further
   dilute-HF step is avoided because the residual gate oxide over the
   source/drain is wanted as part of the screen oxide and because HF
   would undercut the gate-oxide edge (inference).
2. **Ambient and temperature.** Dry O₂ at roughly 800–900 °C for a
   few nanometres of oxide (industry-typical range for thin dry
   oxides[^txt-01][^wiki-thox]), in a vertical furnace under
   nitrogen load and ramp, or a rapid thermal oxidation of seconds to
   a minute in a single-wafer RTP chamber[^nulman-1985][^roozeboom-1990]
   — the rapid thermal route is attractive here because it limits the
   diffusion of the channel and poly dopants while still growing the
   oxide. N₂O or NO ambients, which grow and nitride at once, are the
   documented alternative for a poly re-oxidation.[^lai-1998] Which
   SKY130 uses is not public.
3. **What grows where.** Heavily phosphorus-doped poly oxidises much
   faster than lightly doped silicon at these temperatures — the
   effect that Sunami, Kamins, and Saraswat and Singh characterised
   for doped poly films[^sunami-1978][^kamins-1979][^saraswat-1982]
   and that Ho and Plummer modelled for high substrate doping
   levels[^ho-1979] — so the n⁺ gate sidewalls grow the thickest
   oxide, the p-type resistor bodies less, the lightly doped
   source/drain silicon least, and the gate-oxide edge under the poly
   foot thickens by lateral diffusion of oxidant. The poly *top* does
   not oxidise, because it is still capped by the nitride of
   {ref}`GATENIT <step-058>`; on this reading the cap is what keeps
   the gate height and the later polish stop ({ref}`CMPP <step-090>`)
   intact. A sidewall oxide of this kind is later covered by the
   {term}`spacer` nitride ({ref}`SPNIT <step-076>`) and becomes the first
   layer of the spacer.
4. **Post-oxidation anneal.** A short N₂ or Ar anneal in the same
   tool, the standard step for settling the fixed and interface charge
   of a fresh oxide (typical industry practice,
   {ref}`category-oxidation`;[^txt-01]) — Deal's standard names those
   charges (Q_f, Q_it, Q_ot, Q_m) but does not itself prescribe an
   anneal.[^deal-1980]
5. **Metrology.** Thickness by spectroscopic ellipsometry on
   monitor wafers and on large test pads; the sidewall oxide is
   checked by cross-section TEM during development; gate-oxide
   integrity and the electrical gate length at
   {ref}`category-test`, since the re-oxidation moves both.

## Open questions

* The thickness, ambient (O₂, N₂O or NO), temperature, time and tool
  (furnace or RTP) of the oxidation are not public.
* Whether the step is intended primarily as a screen oxide, a
  sidewall seal, a gate-edge re-oxidation or an etch-damage repair is
  inferred from its place in the sequence this reference describes,
  after the gate etch, and from the
  literature cited above; all four are consistent with it.
* How the SONOS cell edge — the ONO stack under the memory gate —
  behaves in this oxidation is not public.
* Whether the poly top is fully protected by the nitride cap during
  the oxidation, as read here, is an inference from the cap's
  presence.
* Whether the backside oxide grown here (if a furnace step) is ever
  removed is not stated publicly.
