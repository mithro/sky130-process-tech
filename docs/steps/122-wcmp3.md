## What this step is

`WCMP3` is the tungsten polish that turns the blanket film of
{ref}`WDEP3 <step-121>` into via-1 plugs. The wafer is pressed
face-down on a rotating pad flooded with an oxidising, abrasive slurry
until the tungsten and the IMP TiN liner of {ref}`TIN3 <step-120>` are
gone from the field and the polish lands on the
{ref}`NCAPOX3 <step-117>` cap oxide; tungsten survives only inside the
vias. What is left is a planar oxide surface studded with 0.15 µm
tungsten squares[^pdk-periph] — the `via` plugs, "Contact from metal 1
to metal 2"[^pdk-06] — on which the metal-2 stack of
{ref}`TIAL12 <step-123>` will be sputtered. It is the third tungsten
polish of the flow, after {ref}`WCMPLI <step-100>` and
{ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`; the same polish recurs at
{ref}`WCMP4 <step-133>` and above.

SkyWater lists "AMAT Mirra CMP" with the applications "oxide",
"nitride", "niobium", "aluminum", "tungsten", "high selectivity
tungsten" and "copper".[^skw-01] The distinction between "tungsten"
and "high selectivity tungsten" is, on our reading, between a
conventional tungsten slurry that also removes oxide at a useful rate
and one that stops sharply on it; which is used at this step is not
public. What differs from the contact polish of {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>`
is what lies beneath the oxide the polish stops on: the dielectric
between metal 1 and metal 2 is only 0.27 µm thick,[^pdk-04] so oxide
lost here is lost from the level-to-level spacing that sets the
metal-1-to-metal-2 capacitance of the extraction tables,[^pdk-08] and
0.36 µm[^pdk-04] of aluminium lies a quarter-micron below the pad.

## Step category

`WCMP3` is a {ref}`Chemical-mechanical planarisation <category-cmp>`
step of the *metal-clearing* type: it removes two metals completely
and stops on a third material. The category page's comparison table
sets it against the oxide polishes — an acidic slurry with an
oxidiser, a stop on oxide, failure modes of plug recess, oxide
{term}`erosion` in dense arrays, tungsten coring and corrosion — and
Kaufman et al.'s description of tungsten CMP as the oxidation of the
metal to a soft WO₃ that the abrasive strips[^kaufman-1991] is the
mechanism. {ref}`WCMP2 <step-111>` sets out the slurry
electrochemistry and the endpoint methods in full; this page dwells on
what the via level changes.

## Why this step exists

The plug process needs the tungsten removed from everywhere but the
vias, and the result flat:

* **Isolation.** Blanket tungsten shorts every via to every other,
  and every metal-1 line to its neighbours through the plugs; the
  polish makes them separate conductors. Before CMP the industry used
  a plasma etch-back, which left recessed plugs and stringers;
  polishing, which IBM patented for metal/insulator structures in the
  late 1980s,[^pat-cmp-ibm-1988][^pat-cmp-ibm-1990] gives a plug flush
  with the oxide.
* **A flat base for metal 2.** The 0.14 µm lines[^pdk-periph] of
  {ref}`MM2 <step-124>` must be printed and etched on this surface
  and cross the vias; a plug standing proud or a recessed via under
  a line is a focus error, a reflective notch, a thinned line or —
  where the metal-2 sputter cannot fill a recess — a resistive
  interface. Wang et al. characterised an integrated tungsten CMP
  process for via plugs specifically,[^wang-2002-wcmp] and Lee et al.
  the consumable choices for a plug polish.[^lee-2001-wcmp]
* **Via resistance.** Over-polish recesses the plug (Vlassak's
  contact-mechanics model of {term}`dishing` and
  erosion[^vlassak-2001] describes the geometry; Nguyen et al. model
  dishing for metal CMP[^nguyen-2000]), which reduces the area the
  metal-2 bottom layer contacts; under-polish leaves TiN or tungsten
  residue that shorts neighbouring vias and lines. The 4 500 mΩ per
  `via` of the PDK's tables[^pdk-08] is, on our reading, the finished
  plug resistance after this step's polish (inference: the PDK
  publishes the value, not the process stage it was taken from).
* **Oxide loss over dense vias.** Via arrays — under bus crossings
  and in the die seal ring, where the periphery rules allow via
  bars[^pdk-periph] — erode faster than the field, the
  pattern-density dependence Stine et al. characterised;[^stine-1998]
  whatever oxide is lost thins the 0.27 µm via-1 dielectric[^pdk-04]
  locally. Shiratani and Kumazawa describe a low-erosion tungsten
  CMP process aimed at exactly this,[^shiratani-2006] and Grover et
  al. how slurry rheology shifts the oxide-to-tungsten
  balance.[^grover-1998]

Without `WCMP3` there would be no via plugs, only a tungsten sheet
shorting metal 1 together.

## How it is typically performed

An industry-generic tungsten via-plug polish for a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

1. **Tool.** A multi-platen rotary polisher — the Mirra
   architecture[^pat-cmp-mirra] — with a carrier head, retaining ring
   and multi-zone membrane, a stacked polyurethane pad, a diamond
   conditioner and an in-situ endpoint window on some
   platens.[^pat-cmp-window]
2. **Slurry.** Acidic (pH 2–4, industry-typical[^steigerwald-1997][^zantye-2004]),
   with alumina or silica abrasive and an oxidiser — ferric nitrate,
   hydrogen peroxide with an iron catalyst, or potassium
   iodate;[^kaufman-1991][^kneer-1997][^stein-1999][^pat-cmp-cabot]
   Krishnan, Nalaskowski and Cook review the chemistry.[^rev-02] The
   "high selectivity tungsten" of SkyWater's list[^skw-01] we read as
   a slurry formulated to remove tungsten tens of times faster than
   oxide, which is what a thin under-lying dielectric calls for
   (inference).
3. **Recipe.** A first platen removes the bulk tungsten at high rate;
   a second clears the last tungsten and the TiN liner — whose
   removal rate differs from tungsten's — and lands on the cap oxide
   with a short, low-pressure over-polish; a third platen buffs.
   Down-force of a few psi and platen speeds of tens of rpm are
   typical.[^txt-05] Elbel et al. give a production description of
   tungsten CMP and its defects.[^elbel-1998]
4. **Endpoint.** Motor-current (friction) or optical endpoint as the
   pad passes from tungsten to oxide;[^pat-cmp-window][^pat-cmp-endpoint-ibm]
   Sue et al. describe tungsten CMP endpoint detection.[^sue-1999]
   The over-polish is then timed, and on a via level it is kept short
   because every nanometre of oxide removed comes off the 0.27 µm
   dielectric.[^pdk-04]
5. **Post-CMP clean.** Immediately, before the slurry dries: a
   double-sided brush scrub with dilute NH₄OH or a TMAH-based
   chemistry — Jolley reports TMAH as a post-tungsten-CMP
   clean[^jolley-1998] — to remove abrasive particles and iron
   residues, then spin-rinse-dry; Sun, Han and Keswani review brush
   scrubbing.[^sun-2017] Tungsten plugs exposed to a wet, oxidising
   slurry can corrode, so the clean also passivates.
6. **Metrology.** Plug recess and oxide erosion by profilometry or AFM
   on via-array test structures; remaining oxide thickness over
   metal-1 pads by reflectometry (the via-1 dielectric after this
   step is what the metal-1-to-metal-2 capacitance tables[^pdk-08]
   see); residual-metal and scratch inspection on the KLA AIT class (our reading of "AIT" in SkyWater's job posting);[^job-06] via-chain resistance at
   {term}`e-test`.
