## What this step is

`PSG` deposits the {term}`pre-metal dielectric`: a thick blanket of
phosphorus-doped silicon dioxide ({term}`PSG`, phosphosilicate glass)
over the finished, annealed transistors. It is the first film of the
middle-of-line ({term}`MOL`) module and the film in which every
local-interconnect contact (`licon1`) will later be cut. Beneath it
lie the capped gate lines with their nitride {term}`spacers <spacer>`, the resistor
bodies, the source/drain silicon under its {ref}`SPOX <step-080>`
oxide, and the field oxide; above it, once it has been polished
({ref}`CMPP <step-090>`), capped ({ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>`) and
annealed ({ref}`RTAD2 <step-092>`), will sit the tungsten plugs and
the titanium-nitride {term}`local interconnect`.

The PDK is explicit that the film exists and what it is made of. The
process stack diagram labels the dielectric between the field oxide
and the local interconnect "PSG K=3.9"; it places the top of the field
oxide at 0.3262 µm above the substrate and the bottom of `li` at
0.9361 µm, and dimensions the film 0.6099 µm from the field-oxide top
to the `li` bottom and 0.4299 µm from the top of the 0.18 µm field
poly to the `li` bottom (0.9361 − 0.3262 = 0.6099 µm and
0.6099 − 0.18 = 0.4299 µm; our arithmetic from the labels and our
reading of the drawing).[^pdk-04] The assumptions table gives a
"Pre-LI ILD thickness" of 0.5 µm.[^pdk-03] SkyWater's capability
list includes "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma (HDP) doped and phos
doped with sputter etch" among its deposition tools.[^skw-01] This
reference describes the film as *sacrificial* in the sense that, on
our reading (inferred from the PDK's cap and pre-LI ILD entries), most
of the as-deposited thickness is removed again by the polish that
follows, which takes
the glass down to, or close to, the tops of the 0.2 µm gate caps
("poly cap after SPE"[^pdk-03]) before the {term}`cap oxide` restores a
planar dielectric over them. The as-deposited thickness is not
public; a film thick enough to bury a roughly 0.4 µm-tall capped gate
(0.18 µm poly plus the 0.2 µm cap[^pdk-03]) with polishing margin —
of the order of 0.6–0.9 µm, a typical industry figure for a
polished pre-metal dielectric[^txt-05] — is what the geometry
implies (inferred).

## Step category

`PSG` is a {ref}`Thin-film deposition <category-deposition>` step —
a doped {term}`CVD` oxide, in the same family as the undoped
{ref}`FILOX <step-011>` trench fill before it and the
{ref}`NILD2 <step-105>` inter-level oxide after. What distinguishes
this instance is the gap it must fill and the surface it lands on.
The gaps are the 0.21 µm spaces between poly lines (poly.2[^pdk-periph])
narrowed by two spacers, roughly 0.4 µm deep, so the {term}`aspect ratio`
approaches 2:1 and a void-free fill needs either a flowable glass or
an {term}`HDP-CVD` deposition with simultaneous sputtering (category
page). The surface is the most delicate the flow will present to a
CVD tool — annealed 0.1 µm junctions,[^pdk-03] thin oxide over the
source/drain, exposed poly heads in the {term}`nitride cuts <nitride cut>` — and the
deposition temperature is limited by the {term}`thermal budget` those
junctions can still absorb.

## Why this step exists

A pre-metal dielectric does three jobs, and phosphorus doping serves
all three:

* **Insulation and planarisation.** The transistors must be buried
  under an insulator thick enough to carry the local interconnect
  and, above it, metal 1. Since the 1970s the standard first-level
  dielectric has been a phosphorus-doped glass because it *flows*:
  Armstrong and Tolliver imaged PSG rounding over steps during a
  high-temperature {term}`reflow`,[^armstrong-1974] Hashimoto et al. set out
  the flow mechanism and its use in MOS devices,[^hashimoto-1977]
  and Adams and Capio quantified how planarisation depends on
  phosphorus content and anneal.[^adams-1981] At 130 nm the flow
  anneal is no longer affordable — the junctions cannot take
  900–1000 °C — so planarity comes from the polish that follows
  ({ref}`CMPP <step-090>`), but the glass composition survived
  because of the next two jobs.
* **Gettering of mobile ions.** Phosphosilicate glass traps sodium
  and other mobile ions that would otherwise drift to the gate oxide
  and shift threshold voltages; Meek and Gibbon studied the
  gettering by ion scattering,[^meek-1974] and Miyazaki et al. showed
  PSG protecting against copper contamination.[^miyazaki-1994] A
  {term}`gettering` layer directly over the gates is the last line of
  defence for the {term}`SONOS` cells ({ref}`ONO <step-040>`) and the analogue
  transistors in the PDK.
* **Contamination and moisture control.** The phosphorus content is
  a compromise: too little and the glass neither getters nor flows;
  too much and it absorbs water and forms phosphoric acid that
  corrodes aluminium, as Paulson and Kirk showed for passivation
  glasses[^paulson-1974] — Levin measured the water absorption and
  densification of PSG films directly.[^levin-1982] This is one reason
  a PSG is sealed under an undoped cap ({ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>`) and
  densified by a later anneal ({ref}`RTAD2 <step-092>`; inference
  from industry practice). A few weight per cent of phosphorus is the
  usual industry compromise.[^txt-02]

Without `PSG` there would be nothing to cut the contacts through and
nothing to carry the local interconnect; every later step of the
flow depends on it.

## How it is typically performed

Industry-generic routes for a pre-metal PSG in a 200 mm, 130 nm-era
fab (SKY130's recipe is not public):

* **HDP-CVD PSG.** An inductively coupled SiH₄/O₂/Ar plasma with a
  PH₃ dopant flow and an RF bias on the wafer, so that argon
  sputtering keeps the gap mouths open while the film grows (category
  page). Vassiliev characterised the properties and gap-fill of
  HDP-CVD phosphosilicate glass for sub-quarter-micron
  devices,[^vassiliev-1999] Lan and Wang the deposition
  mechanism,[^lan-2004] Hsiao, Liu and Wang the film's response to
  thermal budget,[^hsiao-2005] and Chen et al. (TSMC) the plasma
  damage such a process can inflict on gates and how to reduce
  it.[^chen-2002-psg] The Novellus SPEED and Applied Materials Ultima
  chambers were the 200 mm tools of this class;[^lam-speed][^pat-hdp-novellus][^pat-hdp-amat]
  wafer temperature is set by the bias and helium backside cooling,
  typically in the 350–450 °C range (category page[^txt-09]). This is
  the route SkyWater's "HDP … phos doped" entry describes,[^skw-01]
  and we infer it is the SKY130 route.
* **PECVD or SACVD TEOS-based PSG.** {term}`TEOS` with O₂ or O₃ and a
  phosphorus source (TMPO or PH₃), 350–450 °C, followed by an HDP or
  reflow step where {term}`gap fill` demands it; Fujino et al. describe doped
  oxide from TEOS and ozone at atmospheric pressure,[^fujino-1991]
  Becker and Röhl the {term}`LPCVD` TEOS-based PSG/{term}`BPSG`
  films,[^becker-rohl-1987] and Kern and Hartman simultaneous
  deposition and flow of BPSG.[^kern-1991] SkyWater lists "PECVD TEOS,
  C2 and Producer".[^skw-01]
* **Composition and thickness.** Phosphorus of a few weight per cent
  (typical[^txt-02]); an as-deposited thickness that buries the gate
  stack with margin for the polish (see above; not public).
* **Sequence.** Pre-deposition clean ({term}`SC-1` class, no HF, so the
  SPOX/IOX45 oxides survive); an optional thin undoped {term}`liner` — common
  in HDP flows to keep phosphorus away from the silicon and to
  soften the plasma's first contact with the gates (Chen et
  al.[^chen-2002-psg]); the doped fill; an in-situ NF₃ chamber clean
  between wafers.[^txt-09]
* **Metrology.** Thickness and refractive index by ellipsometry;
  phosphorus content by FTIR or XRF on monitor wafers; wet-etch rate
  as a density check; cross-section SEM for voids during
  development; particles.

## Machines typically used

* **HDP-CVD system**, 200 mm: Novellus SPEED,[^lam-speed] Applied
  Materials Ultima/Centura HDP-CVD,[^amat-1997] Lam (Trikon)
  Planar.
* **PECVD/SACVD system** for TEOS-based doped oxides: Applied
  Materials Producer or Centura DxZ, Novellus Concept
  Two/Sequel.[^novellus-history]
* **Ellipsometer / FTIR** for thickness and phosphorus content;
  **cross-section SEM**.

## Machines likely used at SkyWater

* **Lam/Novellus HDP-CVD, phosphorus-doped.** SkyWater lists
  "Lam/Novellus High Density Plasma (HDP) doped and phos doped with
  sputter etch".[^skw-01] Strength: **strong** for the existence of
  an HDP PSG process; assignment to this step is an **inference**
  from the PDK's "PSG" label,[^pdk-04] the gap-fill requirement and
  the fact that no other phosphorus-doped oxide appears in the
  capability list. A SkyWater technician profile mentions a
  "Novellus high density plasma tool".[^skw-07] Strength: strong (a
  SkyWater statement) that such a tool exists; the profile names no
  step.
* **PECVD TEOS "C2 and Producer".**[^skw-01] Strength: strong for
  existence; weak for this step (an undoped TEOS oxide is the cap
  film, {ref}`NCAPOX <step-091>`, on our reading).

## Resources required

* **Silane, oxygen, argon** and **phosphine** (PH₃, typically
  diluted) for HDP PSG;[^wiki-ph3] or **TEOS** with **TMPO/PH₃** and
  **O₂/O₃** for the TEOS route.[^wiki-teos]
* **Helium** backside cooling; **NF₃** (or NF₃/O₂) chamber
  clean.[^txt-09]
* **Chamber consumables** — ceramic domes, liners, nozzles, RF
  matching hardware.
* **Monitor wafers** (SEMI M8 class)[^semi-m8] for thickness,
  phosphorus and particle control.
* Gas suppliers named in SkyWater's 2021 S-1: Air Products,
  Praxair.[^sec-01]

  phosphorus content, the as-deposited thickness and whether an
  undoped liner precedes the doped film are not public; the HDP
  assignment is an inference from SkyWater's capability list.
* That the polish removes most of the film is our inference from the
  0.2 µm cap and the 0.5 µm {term}`ILD` entries in the PDK.
* Whether the {ref}`SPOX <step-080>` oxide is still present under
  the PSG, and so forms part of the "Pre-LI ILD", is not public.
* How the 0.6099 µm and 0.4299 µm labels of the stack diagram map
  onto PSG, cap oxide and gate cap is not stated; the diagram is
  marked "not to scale".[^pdk-04]
